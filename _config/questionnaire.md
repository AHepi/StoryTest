# Setting up the workshop for a writer

A writer's lasting preferences are recorded once, here, and every project draws on them, so no request has to repeat them (the paper this layout follows makes the same point). This questionnaire is how they are gathered. The answers live in `_config/writer.md`, a plain file you can open and change at any time; every stage reads it.

**How it is filled in.** The first time a project starts (stage 01), the agent reads `_config/writer.md`. Any question below with no answer there is asked once, in plain words, with a suggested answer where your earlier words give one; your answer is written into `_config/writer.md` with the date. An answer marked *to confirm* is a stopgap until you confirm it (owner question S16). An answer that holds for one project only goes in that project's brief instead (stage 01), not here. When you change an answer, later runs use the new one; runs already under way say in their project log which answers they used.

**What belongs here, and what does not.** Standing preferences about how you like stories made and how you like the work run. Not rules about craft: those are in the skills, built on your theories, and change only through the workshop's own process.

## About the stories

1. **Medium.** What do you mostly write: feature screenplays, television, stage plays, novels, short stories, games? Several is fine; say which is the default.
2. **Length.** For each medium, the usual length: running time, number of episodes, word count.
3. **Genres.** Genres you are drawn to, and any you want to stay away from. (The genre skill has twelve; a blend is fine.)
4. **Voice and tone.** How you like a story to feel, in your own words. Anything that always rings false to you.
5. **Audience.** Whom you write for. Whether a wide audience matters to you, or only the right one (the premise stage uses this; see its references).
6. **Standing rules.** Things every story of yours should do or never do, whatever the project.
7. **Your own material.** Where your notes, earlier drafts or source documents live, if you want stages to read them. They stay yours: say whether they may be committed to the repository.

## About how the work runs

8. **How often to stop.** After every stage (the default), or only after some.
9. **Helpers at once.** How many helper agents may work at the same time.
10. **Rival versions.** How many rival pitches the premise stage writes, and whether independent reviewers read them before you choose.
11. **Critique.** Which kinds of critic run by default (for example logic and rules; care, suspense and structure; theme and hard to vary; dialogue), and how many rounds before the result comes to you.
12. **Committing story work.** Whether stage outputs are committed to git on this branch. Yes keeps a full history of every version; no keeps your story out of the repository (as with *The Catch*).
13. **Asking first.** What you want to be asked about before it happens.
