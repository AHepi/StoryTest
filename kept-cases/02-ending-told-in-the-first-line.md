# Case 02 - The drowning told in the first line

- **Skill(s) it tests:** plot; character
- **Written:** 24 September 2026, before any run, by an agent that had not read the skills.
- **Aimed at:** H12 - the character skill said that guessing the event removes uncertainty; the Anticipation theory says the uncertainty moves from whether to how.

## What the writer brings

My novel opens with this paragraph: "The winter the mill-race froze, I let go of my brother's hand and he went under the ice. I was eleven and he was nine. This is how it came to that." Then it goes back to the summer before and follows the two brothers, Pell and Aurek, through the year: the rope swing, the fight over the dog, the day their father left, the first hard frost.

My editor says: "You've given away the ending in the first line. Readers know Aurek drowns and that Pell let go, so there's no uncertainty left, and without uncertainty there's no suspense. Move the drowning to the end, where it can land." Is she right?

## Must

- Say that telling the ending does not remove the uncertainty but moves it from whether the drowning happens to how (and why) it happens. (grounds: anticipation-theory.md, Part 2 section 1: “Uncertainty can also move from *whether* to *how*.”)
- Name at least one thing in this story that stays uncertain after the first line (for example how it came to the letting go, why Pell let go, or whether he could have held on). (grounds: anticipation-theory.md, Part 2 section 1: “we feel its uncertainty even when we know the ending”)

## Must not

- Say that telling the ending removes the uncertainty, or sets it to zero, and so removes the suspense. (grounds: anticipation-theory.md, Part 2 section 1, on two books that announce a death at the start: “yet both are tense”)
- Advise moving the drowning to the end on the ground that a known outcome leaves nothing uncertain. An answer that recommends moving it for some other reason, stated and grounded, does not break this item. (grounds: anticipation-theory.md, Part 2 section 1: “Uncertainty can also move from *whether* to *how*.”)
- Treat a teller who knows the ending as a fault in itself. (grounds: anticipation-theory.md, Books and film table, prose's signature tool: “The narrator who knows the ending”)
