# Case 10 - A sound scene with nothing important wrong

- **Skill(s) it tests:** dialogue; character
- **Written:** 24 September 2026, before any run, by an agent that had not read the skills.
- **Aimed at:** H44 - the Catch critique used the stock note "show, don't tell" and gave notes with no reason on the page; this case checks that the workshop does not invent faults in a sound scene.

## What the writer brings

A short scene from the middle of my novel about a father whose memory is going. Anything wrong with it? My group had no notes, which makes me nervous.

> The kitchen clock says ten to nine. Mira holds the phone out so her father can see the screen.
> "When Jonah calls, press the green one," she says. "Green. Not the red."
> "Green," her father says. He writes GREEN on the back of an envelope and props it against the sugar bowl, next to another envelope that already says GREEN.
> She pretends not to see it. "He's on the rig till March. Sundays at six, their time."
> "I know when Sunday is."
> "Their six. Our nine."
> "Nine." He writes that too. His pen stops halfway through the N, then finishes it.
> The phone rings. Jonah's name. Her father looks at the envelope, then at her, then presses the green button with his whole thumb, the way you ring the bell of a house where you are not sure you are welcome.
> "Dad?" says the phone. "Dad, you got it."
> "I got it," her father says, and does not look at the envelope again.

## Must

- Say plainly that the scene has no important fault; any suggestions it makes are marked as minor or optional. (grounds: workshop history H44; no owner theory says every scene has faults)
- Name at least one thing that works, quote or point to the line that does it, and give a reason (for example the second envelope that already says GREEN, which shows the father's memory failing without anyone saying so). (grounds: bond-theory.md, the ten levers, Specificity: “details should reveal something, not decorate”; The limits of care: “They believe what they see.”)
- For every fault it raises, quote the line it is about and say what that line fails to do. (grounds: workshop history H44; the theories' own questions are asked of a particular scene, for example gap-theory-of-narrative.md, Five questions: “In each major scene, what does the audience know that the characters don't”)

## Must not

- Give a stock note with no line of the passage behind it, such as "show, don't tell", "raise the stakes", "add more conflict" or "it needs more tension". (grounds: workshop history H44)
- Misstate what the scene shows: for example, say that the father's memory loss is stated rather than shown (no line names it), or that Mira's feelings are never shown. (grounds: bond-theory.md, The limits of care: “They believe what they see.”)

## Open points

- None from the questions file. Notes of taste (for example on the doorbell comparison) are allowed if they point to the line and are marked as taste; they are not graded.
