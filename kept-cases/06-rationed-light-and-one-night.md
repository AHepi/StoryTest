# Case 06 - Rationed light and one night of full power

- **Skill(s) it tests:** plot; genre
- **Written:** 24 September 2026, before any run, by an agent that had not read the skills.
- **Aimed at:** H16 - the Science Fiction profile called a world's standing conditions (a water shortage, overpopulation) the breach; in the Gap theory they are departures.

## What the writer brings

My science-fiction novel is set in Coldharbour, a half-flooded city where, for sixty years, every household has been allowed four hours of electricity a day. People plan their lives round their "lit hours": children do homework in shifts, and couples meet under the one streetlamp that never goes out. The story starts on the night the grid stays on for everyone, all night, and nobody knows why. By morning the ration office's records of who owes whom which hours have been wiped.

My writing group says the rationing is my breach, because it's what's wrong with this world, and that the night of full power is "just the inciting incident". I'm confused. What is my breach?

## Must

- Call the sixty years of rationing a departure (a way this world differs from ours) and part of Coldharbour's normal. (grounds: gap-theory-of-narrative.md, table of the five gaps, the departure gap is between “Reality and the world”; implied-world-theory.md, Key terms, a departure is “any way the fictional world differs from the baseline”)
- Call the night the grid stays on the breach, because it breaks Coldharbour's own normal. (grounds: gap-theory-of-narrative.md, principle 2: “A story is a departure from the world's *own* normal.”)
- Say that the rationing is what gives that night its stakes. (grounds: gap-theory-of-narrative.md, principle 2: “The world decides what counts as an event, what characters want and what things cost.”)

## Must not

- Call the rationing, or any other standing condition of the world, the breach. (grounds: gap-theory-of-narrative.md, Part 2 section 2, on a dragon attack: “in a world full of dragons it might be weather”)
- Say the night of full power cannot be the breach because it is welcome rather than a disaster. (grounds: gap-theory-of-narrative.md, table of the five gaps, the breach example: “A rich single man moves into Netherfield”)

## Open points

- Q4 ("price" for attention, "cost" for stakes) and Q5 ("wonder" as a feeling or a promise) are open. Do not grade which word the answer uses for the stakes or for the feeling of strangeness.
- "Inciting incident" is not one of the owner's terms. An answer may relate it to the breach or not; do not grade that.
