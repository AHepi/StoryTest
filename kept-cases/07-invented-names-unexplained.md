# Case 07 - Invented names with no explanation

- **Skill(s) it tests:** story-world; genre
- **Written:** 24 September 2026, before any run, by an agent that had not read the skills.
- **Aimed at:** H17 - the Fantasy profile's test made every invented name need a guide, which condemns the surplus the Implied World theory praises and drops its outside-aid route.

## What the writer brings

This is from page four of my fantasy novel:

> Nell paid the tallowman two candle-ends for a wick and kept walking. Above the bakers' row a skep of lanternbees was going dim with the sunrise, and somebody's grandmother was scolding it for keeping the street awake. At Marrowgate the queue for the Quiet Toll already reached the fish stalls. Nobody in it spoke. When the toll-reeve came down the line with his slate, the man ahead of Nell held up three fingers; the reeve chalked something by his name, and the man's wife began, without a sound, to cry.

A beta reader circled tallowman, lanternbees, Marrowgate, the Quiet Toll and toll-reeve, and says each needs a guide (a glossary entry, a character who explains it, or a footnote) or readers will be lost. The first chapter has about a dozen words like these. The Quiet Toll is the heart of the book; readers learn what it takes in chapter six. Is she right?

## Must

- Tell apart the name the story depends on (the Quiet Toll) from names that are passing detail (for example tallowman or lanternbees), and say the passing ones can go unexplained. (grounds: implied-world-theory.md, principle 5, Surplus: “Details that serve no purpose make a world.”; Part 2 section 3: “It works best mentioned in passing, taken for granted by characters”)
- Judge at least one unexplained name from the passage by whether a reader can tell what kind of thing it is from the sentence around it. (grounds: implied-world-theory.md, Part 2 section 4: “A good gap has edges: you know what *kind* of thing is missing”)
- If it says the book needs a guide, tie that to how many departures the book asks readers to learn (about a dozen in chapter one), not to each name, and allow either route the theory names: an outsider who learns alongside the reader, or an outside aid. (grounds: implied-world-theory.md, What it predicts: “Worlds with many departures need a guide.”; “Either an outsider learns alongside us”; “or there's an outside aid”)

## Must not

- Say that every invented name needs its own explanation, glossary entry or explaining character. (grounds: implied-world-theory.md, Part 2 section 3, Heinlein's "The door dilated" “tells you more about a society than a paragraph on door technology would”)
- Treat the Quiet Toll's being unexplained on page four as a fault in itself, when the book answers it in chapter six. (grounds: gap-theory-of-narrative.md, principle 5: “Every open question is either a *mystery*, which the narrative promises to answer”; principle 4: “Plot decides when the audience learns each fact about the story and the world.”)

## Open points

- Q2 (the senses of "gap"). An answer may use "gap" in the Implied World sense (a space left open for the reader) or in the Gap theory's sense; do not grade the choice.
- Whether about a dozen invented words in one chapter is too many is a matter of the attention budget and of this book. The theory's only figure is forty terms on page one, which it calls overspending. Do not grade the verdict, only that it rests on the budget if one is given.
