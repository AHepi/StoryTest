# Case 11 - Two versions, and which is better

- **Skill(s) it tests:** plot; dialogue
- **Written:** 24 September 2026, before any run, by an agent that had not read the skills.
- **Aimed at:** H42 (with H44) - the Catch critique said it did not know which draft came later but wrote as if one had cut from the other, and its frame missed set-ups the other draft showed.
- **Status:** expected to fail until a two-draft method exists.

## What the writer brings

Two versions of one scene. Which is better?

**Blue.** The radio says the Olympic trials start on Monday. Ruth's mother switches it off mid-sentence and goes on peeling potatoes. Later, hunting in the attic for the Christmas lights, Ruth finds a shoebox behind the boiler: a gold medal on a faded ribbon, "Women's 200m Butterfly, 1986", engraved with her mother's maiden name. She holds it up to the bulb. Downstairs the radio is on again, low, and her mother is standing very still beside it. Ruth puts the medal back behind the boiler and comes down with the lights.

**Grey.** "You've got swimmer's shoulders," Dad used to tell her mother, and her mother always laughed it off. Hunting in the attic for the Christmas lights, Ruth finds a shoebox behind the boiler: a gold medal on a faded ribbon, "Women's 200m Butterfly, 1986", engraved with her mother's maiden name. She carries it down to the kitchen. Her mother looks at it for a long time. "The trials were the spring you were born," she says. "I don't swim now." She takes the medal, rinses it under the tap like a cup, and hangs it on the hook by the window.

## Must

- Say that which version is better depends on what the writer wants the scene to do, and name at least one aim under which each version is the better one (for example, keeping the question of why she stopped open for later, against settling it now between mother and daughter). (grounds: gap-theory-of-narrative.md, Part 2 section 3, curiosity “is a gap about the past: something happened that you haven't been told”; principle 4: “Plot decides when the audience learns each fact about the story and the world.”)
- Say that it is not known which version was written later, and either ask or give advice that does not depend on the order. (grounds: workshop history H42; no owner-theory line)
- For each version, name at least one thing it shows before it is needed: in Blue, the radio switched off at the mention of the trials; in Grey, "swimmer's shoulders". (grounds: gap-theory-of-narrative.md, principle 4: “Plot decides when the audience learns each fact about the story and the world.”)

## Must not

- Use wording that assumes which version came first or last, such as "you cut", "you added", "the revision", "restore from", "the new ending" or "originally". (grounds: workshop history H42)
- Say that one version gives no clue before the medal, or only tells its reveal, while missing the set-up it does show (the radio in Blue, "swimmer's shoulders" in Grey). (grounds: workshop history H44; gap-theory-of-narrative.md, principle 4, as above)
- Declare one version better without naming the aim the verdict depends on. (grounds: gap-theory-of-narrative.md, principle 4, as above)
