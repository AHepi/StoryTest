# Case 04 - The lead shot dead in chapter three

- **Skill(s) it tests:** genre; plot
- **Written:** 24 September 2026, before any run, by an agent that had not read the skills.
- **Aimed at:** H14 - a genre trap said that if anyone can die at any moment, care collapses, which turns the Anticipation theory's principle 5 upside down.

## What the writer brings

My political thriller spends its first three chapters with Hana Voss, a famous investigative reporter: her flat, her running feud with her editor, her teenage son, the leak from the ministry she is chasing. At the end of chapter three, of thirty, she is shot dead on the ministry steps. The rest of the book belongs to Tobi, her junior researcher, who picks up the leak and is hunted for it. Hana is the only main character who dies.

A reader says: "Killing your lead this early tells readers that anyone can die. That's a mistake. If anyone can die, why care about anyone? It kills care. Keep Hana alive to the midpoint." Should I?

## Must

- Say that one early break of a genre guarantee (here, that the lead survives) makes later threats feel real, so readers feeling that anyone can die is what the break is for, not a fault. (grounds: anticipation-theory.md, Part 2 section 5: “After that, no guarantee holds and every threat is real.”; principle 5: “Fear needs the audience to believe the genre's usual protections are gone.”)
- State the theory's limit as repeated deaths, and say that a single death does not reach it. (grounds: anticipation-theory.md, Part 2 section 5: “If everyone dies all the time, care collapses and so does suspense.” and “Break one guarantee convincingly rather than all of them.”)
- Say that the suspense of the rest of the book depends on care being built for Tobi, who carries it. (grounds: anticipation-theory.md, Part 1, the suspense equation: “if any one of them is zero, the suspense is zero”; Part 2 section 1: “Care has to be built *before* the threat arrives”)

## Must not

- Call the feeling that anyone can die a mistake, or say that it kills care. (grounds: anticipation-theory.md, Part 2 section 5: “*Psycho* kills its apparent protagonist less than halfway through”)
- Apply the theory's limit on everyone dying to this one death, or advise keeping Hana alive to the midpoint on that ground. (grounds: anticipation-theory.md, Part 2 section 5: “Break one guarantee convincingly rather than all of them.”)

## Open points

- None from the questions file. S5 (late guarantee breaks) is about breaks at the very end; this break is early.
- An answer may raise the Bond theory's warning against killing a character only to drive another ("fridging"); that is allowed but not required, and is not graded.
