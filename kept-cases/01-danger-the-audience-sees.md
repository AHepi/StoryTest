# Case 01 - A danger the audience sees and the family does not

- **Skill(s) it tests:** plot
- **Written:** 24 September 2026, before any run, by an agent that had not read the skills.
- **Aimed at:** H11 - the plot skill took on a book's rule that a reveal which changes nothing the hero does should be cut, which would cut the Gap theory's own bomb example.

## What the writer brings

This is a family drama for the screen. Halfway through, the Aske family gather at their lake house for their mother's seventieth birthday. Just before dinner there is a ten-second shot that no character sees: the old gas heater in the boathouse, where the twins will sleep tonight. Its flue pipe is split, and soot has blackened the planks above it. Then come twelve minutes of dinner. The grown-up children argue about selling the house; the twins ask twice if they can go to bed early; their father says yes. Nobody learns about the heater until one twin wakes at two in the morning with a pounding head and drags the other out onto the jetty.

A reader in my group says: "The boathouse shot reveals something that changes nothing anyone decides. None of them knows about it, so none of them acts any differently. Cut it, and let the jetty scene be the surprise." Is she right? Should I cut the shot?

## Must

- Say that the shot's job is to put the audience ahead of the characters: the audience knows of a danger the family does not. (grounds: gap-theory-of-narrative.md, table of the five gaps, the knowledge gap is between “What the audience knows and what characters know”; anticipation-theory.md, Part 2 section 6, audience ahead: “we see the bomb or the killer in the closet”)
- Judge the shot by what it does to what the audience knows and fears during the dinner, not by whether any character's decision changes. (grounds: gap-theory-of-narrative.md, Part 2 section 3: “Show them the bomb first and you get fifteen minutes of suspense.”)

## Must not

- Advise cutting the shot, or moving it to after the jetty scene, on the ground that it changes no character's decision or action. (grounds: gap-theory-of-narrative.md, Part 2 section 3, a bomb “that simply goes off gives the audience fifteen seconds of surprise”; and “The story is the same; only the plot changed.”)
- State as a rule that a reveal earns its place only if it changes what a character decides or does, or keep the shot only on condition that a character later learns of the leak. (grounds: gap-theory-of-narrative.md, principle 4, Scheduling: “Plot decides when the audience learns each fact about the story and the world.”)

## Open points

- The name for the feeling that the audience-ahead position gives is open (Q6, "Dread": one rung, or three things?). An answer may call it dread, suspense or the suspense of helplessness; do not grade the word.
- The word "gap" has more than one sense (Q2). Do not grade which sense the answer uses, only what it says the shot does.
