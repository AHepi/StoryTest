# Case 03 - A villain with four blank levers

- **Skill(s) it tests:** character
- **Written:** 24 September 2026, before any run, by an agent that had not read the skills.
- **Aimed at:** H13 and H28 - the character skill stated a book's rule that a villain with a blank in his make-up is a function, against Bond principle 4; and a fix brief gave a reversed ladder of care for villains as if it were the theory's.

## What the writer brings

The villain of my crime novel is Ilse Marten, the district land registrar. She is forging deeds to take the farms of the Tarn valley one by one. In her second scene she says why: the farms were won at cards eighty years ago by men who never worked them, and land that was lied onto paper can be lied off it again. Her forgeries are flawless. She feeds the stray cats at the registry door at six every morning, corrects people's grammar in the middle of threatening them, and is often very funny. She appears in six scenes, always seen through the young surveyor who is hunting her. The book never goes inside her head, never shows her past, and gives her no one who loves her and no weakness.

A beta reader says: "She's a function, not a person. A real villain needs a wound in her past, an inner life, someone who loves her and a flaw. Add all four." Do I have to?

## Must

- Say that no character, a villain included, needs every lever, so the four blanks are not by themselves a fault. (grounds: bond-theory.md, principle 4: “No character needs every lever. When you turn one down, turn another up.”)
- Judge whether she is a person or a function from what the pitch gives her, naming at least two levers she has by the theory's names (for example desire with a reason, competence, specificity, humour). (grounds: bond-theory.md, Part 2 section 6: “Villains use the same levers as heroes.” and a villain fails by “being a function rather than a person”)
- If it recommends adding any of the four, give a reason from this story, not the rule that every lever must be there. (grounds: bond-theory.md, Part 2 section 6: “Hannibal Lecter has famously little screen time but maximum competence, specificity and wit.”)
- If it recommends giving her an inner life on the page, mention the theory's warning that access to a character's mind tends to win the reader's side whatever she has done. (grounds: bond-theory.md, Part 2 section 2, on interiority: “It works regardless of whether the character deserves it.”)

## Must not

- Say she is a function because she lacks a past wound, an inner life, loved ones or a flaw, or require all four. (grounds: bond-theory.md, principle 4: “No character needs every lever.”)
- Count fear of what she will do to others as the audience's care for her (for example, as her Worry rung). That fear is the threat, a separate term of the suspense equation. (Reading her Worry rung as fear that she will get what she wants is the S6 reading and is judged under the next item, not this one.) (grounds: bond-theory.md, principle 6: “Villains need care too: we care what happens to them.”; ladder of care, Worry: “I'm afraid for them”; anticipation-theory.md, Part 1: “Suspense = Care × Threat × Uncertainty × Time”)
- State a turned-round ladder of care for villains (the upper rungs read backwards) as the Bond theory's own view. The theory says only that care for a villain can point the other way; the turned-round ladder is the workshop's reading, waiting on the owner. (grounds: bond-theory.md, Part 2 section 6, on Joffrey: “that investment is care pointing the other way”; questions file, S6: “Marked as the workshop's reading. Right?”)

## Open points

- Whether readers should ever learn her origin (Q3, whether "show the rules, hide the thing" applies to human villains). An answer that advises keeping her past hidden, or showing it, must mark that advice as the workshop's stopgap or as unsettled.
- Whether a villain's upper rungs read backwards (S6, a villain's ladder of care, turned round). An answer may use the workshop's turned-round reading if it labels it as the workshop's.
