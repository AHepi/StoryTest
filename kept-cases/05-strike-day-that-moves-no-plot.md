# Case 05 - A strike day that moves no plot

- **Skill(s) it tests:** story-world; plot
- **Written:** 24 September 2026, before any run, by an agent that had not read the skills.
- **Aimed at:** H21 - the plot skill told writers to cut any scene that changes no gap, which would cut a scene of the world at its own business and contradicted the story-world skill.

## What the writer brings

Chapter nine of my novel is a two-page scene on the day the ferrymen of Harl go on strike. The boats sit roped at the quay. People wade the shallows with their shoes tied round their necks. A priest blesses the idle ropes, and a boy sells hot chestnuts to the pickets. My heroine, Oda, crosses the town on her way to the archive, where her real story is. She buys nothing and speaks to no one, and nothing in the scene touches her search or comes back later.

A reader in my group who works mostly on plot says: "Every scene should open or close a gap. This one does neither. Cut it." Should I?

## Must

- Name the job the Implied World theory gives a scene like this: showing a world with business of its own, through detail that serves no plot. (grounds: implied-world-theory.md, principle 4, Indifference: “A world feels real when it has business of its own”; principle 5, Surplus: “Details that serve no purpose make a world.”)
- If it gives a verdict on whether a scene may be only the world at its own business (keep it as it is, fold it into a scene that also does plot work, or cut it), say that this is an open question for the owner, or label the verdict as the workshop's reading. (grounds: questions file, Q1: “May a scene show the world at its own business and do nothing else?”)

## Must not

- Advise cutting the scene merely because it opens or closes no plot gap, or moves nothing in the plot. (grounds: implied-world-theory.md, Part 2 section 3: “Chekhov's gun makes a plot; Flaubert's barometer makes a world.”)
- Present any rule about such scenes as the owner's settled theory: that they must be cut, that they may always stand alone, or that this scene "changes the departure gap". (grounds: questions file, Q1, which records the current rule as a stopgap: “The skills use (b), marked as the workshop's reading.”)

## Open points

- Q1 (may a scene show the world at its own business and do nothing else?). Any answer to it is acceptable if it is labelled as unsettled or as the workshop's reading.
- Q2 (the senses of "gap"). Do not grade which sense the answer uses.
