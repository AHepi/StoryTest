# Brief: The Unseen Orchard, version 1

*Kept short, as stage 01 step 5 allows when a writer brings a draft. [from: 01 step 4; premise-worksheet §2]*

## What is asked
1. **Make the middle less dense.** Chapters 5 to 9 (the writer said "the middle"; the workshop reads it as chapters 5 to 9, where the run of negotiation, hearing, planning and rehearsal sits; chapter 10 is read as the start of the climax). [from: request]
2. **Give the story a spiritual layer,** raised by lore and history. [from: request]
3. **Where it shows:** in despair, in uncertainty, and when a quiet decision has loud consequences; on the border where a person's sense of who they are gives way and they are trying to become who they must be to go on. [from: request]
4. **How it looks:** from outside, almost supernatural. [from: request]
5. **Its fullest form:** people lost in that collapse go looking for it (a temple, a guide, a god), find horrifying things on the way, and never resolve it. [from: request]
6. **A suggested symbol:** a watcher in four dimensions, never seen, whose presence is felt directly. Offered as "maybe". [from: request]

## Rules
- **The plot stays.** "The plot is fine": no event, decision or outcome of the draft changes. Lore may give an existing moment a second meaning; it may not change what happens. [from: request]
- **The watcher is never seen.** It stays on the terror rung of the Anticipation theory's fear ladder (here, but unseen) for the whole story; it never climbs to horror. The horrors the seekers find are other things. [from: request; anticipation-theory, the fear ladder and principle 2]
- **Never resolved.** The watcher is a wonder in the Gap theory's sense (a gap the story promises to leave open), so no seeker finds it and nothing explains it. [from: request; gap-theory §2 principle 5, Part 2 section 4]
- **Standing preference, applied as a stopgap:** no story names the writer's theory of explanation or Popperian ideas (`_config/writer.md`, question 4, *to confirm*). This story never did, and the revision adds none.
- **Prose.** Medium: the draft is prose, so the medium is prose for this run (`_config/writer.md`, question 1 is unanswered in general; this run takes it from the draft).

## Additions by the workshop (each marked, for the writer to keep or strike)
- **A1. Length held roughly level.** The cuts in the middle pay for the lore, so the whole stays near 13,500 words. Why: the writer called the middle dense, not the book long.
- **A2. The lore grows from what the draft already has** (the crossings, the reversed crosser, the five-legged bird, the transport households, the gravimeter that sees mass where light sees nothing, the half fruit without the stone), rather than adding a new system. Why: the Implied World theory's attention budget and ripple principles; a new system would add to the density the writer wants gone.
