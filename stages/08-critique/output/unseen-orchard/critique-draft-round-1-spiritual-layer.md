# Critique: The Unseen Orchard, draft 2, round 1: the spiritual layer

*Stage 08, one critic, one kind of problem: the spiritual layer the writer asked for. Target: `stages/07-dialogue/output/unseen-orchard/draft-v2-part1.md` to `-part3.md`, against the request, `brief-v1.md` and `world-v1.md`. [from: 08 step 3; critique-rounds §2]*

## Overall judgement

The layer is mostly there and mostly good: the Witness is felt and never seen, it stays on the terror rung, and the lore arrives in passing from people who take it for granted. Its weak points are at the end of the middle. In chapter 10 the narration explains what the watcher does and states the lesson Nessa learns from it. By then the "room opened from a side" moment has come so often that the reader expects it, and chapter 10 even lists the earlier times. One drift reading in chapter 9 is close to proof, which spoils the "almost" in "almost supernatural". The writer's "fullest form", the seekers, is real but thin. One lore exchange took the place of a line of Nessa's own.

## Findings, most important first

### 1. Chapter 10 explains the watcher and states the lesson

- **Target:** `draft-v2-part3.md`, ch. 10: "It did not choose. It did not help. It only would not let her see one of them by standing him in front of the rest. / In the field she had wanted it to reach in. It did not reach in now. It left the reaching to her."
- **What is wrong:** the narration gives the watcher a purpose and an act ("would not let her"), then sums up in a sentence what Nessa learns. That turns a wonder into a guide and does the reader's work for them.
- **Grounds:** gap theory, Part 2 section 4: explaining a wonder deflates it, as the midi-chlorians did the Force. Anticipation theory, principle 3: show the rules, hide the thing. `world-v1.md` §2 sets the rule that "it never acts", and its §9 test rejects "a watcher that helps" because the watcher would then stop being Nessa's mirror as inspector. "Would not let her" is helping.
- **Fix:** cut "It only would not let her see one of them by standing him in front of the rest." and the whole next paragraph. Go from "It did not choose. It did not help." straight to "Keep us here." Cost: a reader who did not link chapter 7's "She wanted it to" with this choice loses the link handed to them. The two scenes sit three chapters apart and share the image, so most readers will make it themselves, and making it is the reward.
- **Standing:** bears.
- **Trace:** the file's marker only ([from: 09 step 4; feedback-1; world-v1]); there are no passage markers. `world-v1.md` §6 asks only for "Tomas and the boarders both in view at once, neither hidden behind the other". The purpose and the summing-up are this output's own.

### 2. The same moment five times, and chapter 10 lists them

- **Target:** ch. 6, "as if it had been opened from a side... with nothing in front of anything else"; ch. 7, the hold; ch. 8, "being turned, gently"; ch. 9, "the galley door open from a side she could not face, and all twelve of them in view at once"; ch. 10, "as it had been in the field, and in the assay room, and at the galley door: the room opened from a side."
- **What is wrong:** from chapter 6 the presence comes at every decision Nessa makes, in nearly the same words, so by chapter 10 the reader is waiting for it. The list in chapter 10 confirms the pattern instead of breaking it.
- **Grounds:** anticipation theory, principle 7: tension rises in steps, and the pattern must break before the audience learns it. `world-v1.md` §6 states the same aim: "the presence must not become routine". Its plan put only the drift log in chapter 9, not a felt moment.
- **Fix:** in chapter 9, break the pattern rather than repeat it. After "No other people aboard," Nessa waits for the room to open, and it does not. Something like "She waited for the galley door to open from its side. It stayed a door." Cut "as it had been in the field, and in the assay room, and at the galley door" from chapter 10. Cost: the lie no longer gets the presence, so the writer's "quiet decision" is met there by its absence. Chapter 10 then lands harder, because chapter 9 taught the reader that the presence may not come.
- **Standing:** bears.
- **Trace:** mostly this output (the chapter 9 felt moment and the chapter 10 list are not in the plan). Partly `world-v1.md` §6, which already places a moment in each of chapters 6 to 10.

### 3. One drift reading is close to proof, and cannot be in the log it is in

- **Target:** `draft-v2-part3.md`, ch. 9: "It was Wren's own interior... Another, from the dispatch station on Nearbank, had come in with the ordinary station records: the minute she had selected Tomas's list."
- **What is wrong:** a station reading on another world, at the exact minute of her choice, filed in a log of Wren's own interior, is both a slip in the world's logic and a near-proof that the watcher follows her decisions. That moves it from "almost supernatural" to supernatural, and makes it a mystery the reader will want answered.
- **Grounds:** the request: "look outwardly as almost supernatural". `world-v1.md` §2, item 5: "an instrument reading with an ordinary explanation that never quite covers it". Gap theory, principle 5: a gap pressed as evidence becomes a mystery, and leaving it open then breaks the promise.
- **Fix:** replace the dispatch-station entry with one from Wren's own gravimeter, at the minute of her obstructed crossing in chapter 7, "a little behind her chair", where she was not. It is still uncanny, and "drift" still covers it. Cost: the chapter 6 choice of Tomas's list loses its only touch from the spiritual layer.
- **Standing:** bears.
- **Trace:** this output. `world-v1.md` §6 asks only for "a time Nessa recognises".

### 4. The writer's "fullest form" is a glimpse

- **Target:** ch. 7: "Don't take a rig to an unlit mass to find out. People do." and the *Patience* fragment; ch. 10: "I found an orchard once... It wasn't the one." / "She did not ask which one."
- **What is wrong:** the writer called the seekers the fullest expression of the spiritual layer. On the page there is one man, about fifteen lines, and the thing seekers go to find (the orchard on no side, `world-v1.md` §3) is never named. "The one" leans on the title alone.
- **Grounds:** the request ("The mosr expression of this spirituality is when people get lost... people literally go on a journey"). Implied World theory, Part 2 section 4: a good gap has edges, so you know what kind of thing is missing. Against that, principle 2 (the attention budget), and the brief's rule that the plot stays, which rules out putting the journey on the page.
- **Fix:** one line of lore in passing in chapter 3, at the side place. The child or Sava says who the place is for: whoever is across, and "the one who keeps the orchard", with no more. That gives "It wasn't the one" its edge, and the title its second meaning. Cost: one more lore term (three for the reader to carry, not two), in chapter 3, outside the middle the writer wanted lighter. The other way: keep the glimpse as it is, which keeps the attention budget and the wonder but leaves the "fullest form" thin.
- **Standing:** held if the writer wants the seekers to read as a known practice of this world, not as one man's private past. Both ways are given above; please say which.
- **Trace:** an earlier stage's output. `world-v1.md` §4 chose a single minor character in two fragments, and its §5 lists only "the Witness" and "drift" as terms the reader must decode.

### 5. The last line explains the side place

- **Target:** `draft-v2-part3.md`, ch. 12: "...the way the households here kept a share for whoever was on the other side, and began to eat the other."
- **What is wrong:** the closing image is glossed in the narration's own voice, at the one point where the reader most wants to do the work.
- **Grounds:** Implied World theory, principle 7 and Part 2 section 3: custom works best taken for granted ("The door dilated"). Gap theory, Part 2 section 6: the ending is where the reader settles the gaps. `world-v1.md` §8 wants Tomas, the absent and whatever watches "all three at once", and the gloss narrows the image to the custom.
- **Fix:** cut the clause. End on "Then she set it on a small plate beside her own, and began to eat the other." The way in comes from chapter 3 (finding 4's line, or the existing image of people reaching round the place "as they would round a sleeping dog"). Cost: a reader who missed chapter 3 reads the ending as for Tomas alone, as in the writer's draft 1, which still works.
- **Standing:** bears.
- **Trace:** this output.

### 6. In chapter 6 the lore displaced Nessa's own objection

- **Target:** `draft-v2-part2.md`, ch. 6. Draft 1 had: "'You accepted her berth guarantee,' Nessa said." / "'This is testimony.'" / "'Whose consent did she need to be born?'" Draft 2 puts "There were two witnesses." / "I see one." / "Yes." in their place.
- **What is wrong:** Nessa's one attempt to speak for Rin before she signs is gone, and so is Dema's hardest line. The scene now shows Nessa silent, which makes her signature colder than the writer wrote it.
- **Grounds:** `brief-v1.md`, rule 1: "no event, decision or outcome of the draft changes. Lore may give an existing moment a second meaning; it may not change what happens."
- **Fix:** keep the two-witness exchange, which is the best lore beat in the draft (see below), and restore Nessa's line and the adjudicator's reply before it. Dema's "Whose consent" can go back too, or stay cut for the lighter middle. Cost: two to four lines back in the middle.
- **Standing:** held if the writer counts a protagonist's spoken objection as part of the plot. If so, this bears; if not, it is a matter of character weight, outside this critic's kind.
- **Trace:** this output. `world-v1.md` §6 asks only to add the side mark and "There were two witnesses".

### 7. Tomas's question about the pilot comes from nowhere on the page

- **Target:** `draft-v2-part3.md`, ch. 12: "The guard had said something about him; Nearbank had a word for men like that. / 'Where did he go?' Tomas asked. 'Before.'"
- **What is wrong:** Tomas never touches the lore anywhere in the draft, so his turn towards the seeker reads as random, not as a man whose sense of himself has broken. And "a word for men like that" holds back a word Nessa may have heard.
- **Grounds:** the request (people lost in a collapsed identity go looking). Gap theory, principle 6 and Part 2 section 5: the teller should not hold back what the point-of-view character knows unless the holding back is part of who she is. Implied World theory, Part 2 section 4: a gap needs edges.
- **Fix:** let Tomas give the guard's words, with edges and no name: "He said your pilot went looking, once. That they mostly don't come back." Cost: one line, and the guard's phrase states the seekers' practice outright. That pairs with finding 4, so the writer should decide the two together.
- **Standing:** held if the writer wants Tomas read as a possible next seeker. That is the workshop's addition (`world-v1.md` §4), not the writer's request. If not, cut the exchange; it adds no event.
- **Trace:** an earlier stage's output. `world-v1.md` §4 plans the question but plans no earlier contact between Tomas and the lore.

## Checked and found sound

- **Never seen; no climb to horror.** Every appearance is felt, measured or told (ch. 2, 3, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10). The horrors shown are other things: *Patience*'s crossers, the starved orchard. The "weight of a person" behind the chair (ch. 2) gives the reader a body to imagine without showing one, which is terror.
- **Viewpoint.** No slip found apart from finding 7. Ch. 9, "She had the feeling he had stopped asking", and ch. 7, "It was not unkind", are given as her perceptions. "Later, she never knew in which of the minutes" (ch. 10) looks ahead in time, which close third person allows.
- **Lore as a world, not a dump.** "The Witness" gets one question and a two-sentence answer (ch. 3). "Show your hands" is given as "the way people at home said safe travels". The side mark appears with no explanation. The Transport and its history stay below the surface ("transport descent", "a transport hull"). Nothing reads as an encyclopedia.
- **Plot changes from the layer, other than finding 6:** none found. The longer hold in ch. 7 lengthens what Nessa feels without changing the outcome (she still repins on Wren's side, out of the wreckage).

## What works, and must be kept

- Ch. 3: "the way a room changes when someone comes to stand in the doorway behind you... She wanted, absurdly, to turn towards it, and there was no towards." The four-dimensional watcher in one sentence.
- Ch. 3: the bird, "It's running", and Sava's "You cross out the ones that have gone" and "It's never stopped one." The rules, given in passing.
- Ch. 3: the side place that people "reached round... as they would round a sleeping dog"; and Linet's "It's a socket. It's a chemistry. It's not a sign." A doubter on the page keeps the supernatural at "almost".
- Ch. 6: "There were two witnesses." / "I see one." / "Yes.", and the guard's hand on his chest. The best lore beat in the draft: defiance, history and the world's own business in three lines.
- Ch. 7: "She wanted it to. That was the worst of it." Dread and pull in one beat. Also "she kept her hands where he could see them, though he could not".
- Ch. 7: "Their hands were where they'd been told to keep them. Every one." Ch. 10: "It wasn't the one." / "She did not ask which one."
- Ch. 2: Linet's "Nobody's ever been able to tell me what." Ch. 5: "Pilot will do."
