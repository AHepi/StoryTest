**What the diff shows.** Besides the header notes, six passages changed, and nothing else: ch. 3 Sava's answer (part1 l.287) and Linet's ration line (l.367); ch. 7 a new paragraph in the field (part2 l.235) and the pilot's line in the lock (l.275); ch. 10 the voice saying "Go" (part3 l.195 to 201); ch. 12 the guard's report (l.427). ## Judgement

1. The layer is a real gain: it gives the Witness a history (it once spoke) and gives Nessa's climax a Jaynes shape (the old voice fires once, she decides without it), all on moments the story already had.
2. Jaynes is mostly represented fairly; two slips: on the page the voice talks *during* the Transport and stops after, the reverse of Jaynes's cause, and the two halves of the brain never reach the page.
3. The rules hold: the Witness is not seen, not shown acting and not heard; if anything, ch. 10 now says too plainly that it did not speak.
4. The weakest point is craft in ch. 10: "the word he had always said" has not been set up (the reader meets it two chapters later), and the next line explains the ambiguity outright.
5. "It used to talk" is told three times in almost the same words, and ch. 7 now has seven sets of hands; trim, don't add.

## Findings, ranked

### J1. Ch. 10: "Go" is called a habit the reader has not seen

1. **Target:** part3 l.199: "*The word he had always said.*" Marker: 09 step 4, round 4.
2. **What is wrong:** the line leans on a habit the reader first meets in ch. 12 (part3 l.397, "he had said go, I'll finish this"); in ch. 10 the only recent "Go." the reader has heard is Sava's order at the end of ch. 9 (part3 l.73).
3. **Grounds:** Gap theory, setup before payoff (the plot skill's set-up rule, as used in round 1, DC2 and DC3); world-v2 section 10 itself names ch. 12 as the source, so the plan placed the seed after the harvest.
4. **Fix:** plant it once in ch. 1, on a moment already there: at the end of the lunch (part1 around l.51 to 57), when Nessa reaches for the plates: "Go," he said. "I'll finish this." Cost: one line in ch. 1; ch. 12's sentence becomes a return rather than a first mention, which helps it. Leave Sava's ch. 9 "Go." alone: it quietly lets the ch. 10 voice also carry "go on".
5. **Standing:** bears.
6. **Where it came from:** an earlier stage's output (world-v2 section 10 table, ch. 10 row).

### J2. Ch. 10: the ambiguity is explained, not left

1. **Target:** part3 l.199: "*and it did not say go where: to him, or on.*"
2. **What is wrong:** the clause after the colon tells the reader the two readings, so the choice that follows ("Keep us here") is pre-chewed.
3. **Grounds:** the dialogue skill's on-the-nose family (`flaws-and-fixes.md`); world-v2 section 2, "show the rules, hide the thing"; round 1 SL1 cut a similar explanation from this same passage.
4. **Fix:** end the sentence at "and it did not say where." The next line is the decision, so the reader supplies both meanings. Cost: a reader who skims may take "Go" only as "go to him", which still works.
5. **Standing:** bears.
6. **Where it came from:** this output only.

### J3. Ch. 3: the voice talks during the upheaval, the reverse of Jaynes

1. **Target:** part1 l.287: "*it used to talk to them, in the Transport... Then it stopped.*"
2. **What is wrong:** Jaynes's argument is that upheaval (migration, catastrophe) silenced the voices; the page has the voice speaking through the forced migration and stopping some time after, with no tie to it. World-v2 section 10 claims the Transport is the cause, but the page says otherwise.
3. **Grounds:** feedback 2's request; world-v2 section 10, "The Transport (a forced migration into a strange world) is the kind of upheaval Jaynes blames for the silence."
4. **Fix:** "They used to say it talked them through the first crossings. Told them where to put their hands, which way to turn. By the end of the Transport it had stopped." Cost: loses "Nobody knows when", a nice shrug; keep the mystery with "Nobody knows why."
5. **Standing:** bears (it is the accuracy the writer asked for, and the plan already intends it).
6. **Where it came from:** this output only.

### J4. Ch. 3 and ch. 7: "it used to talk" told three times

1. **Target:** part1 l.287 Sava, "*it used to talk to them, in the Transport*"; part2 l.275 the pilot, "*The old ones say it talked, in the Transport.*"; part3 l.427 the guard, "*listening for something that used to talk.*"
2. **What is wrong:** one piece of lore is told three times in nearly the same words. The pilot repeats to Nessa what Sava told her, and the guard's line partly supplies the reason that the next paragraph says Tomas withholds ("He did not say why he wanted to know").
3. **Grounds:** Implied World theory, glimpses of a larger history, not restatement (world-v2 section 0, last row); world-v2 section 4: the pilot tells "a little... never all of it".
4. **Fix:** pilot: cut "The old ones say it talked, in the Transport." and write "I went to hear it talk. That's what I found instead." Guard: "He said they go to listen." (or cut the sentence; draft 4's line worked). Cost: the reader must carry Sava's line four chapters; Tomas's leaning towards the road becomes quieter, which world-v2 section 4 wants ("Neither is answered").
5. **Standing:** bears.
6. **Where it came from:** an earlier stage's output (world-v2 section 10 places all three lines; none was checked against the others).

### J5. The two halves of the brain are not on the page

1. **Target:** part1 l.367: "*Then he asked if I'd heard anything on the way back. Like I'd been to a concert.*"
2. **What is wrong:** the planned link (a reversed crosser's halves are swapped, so she might hear it) never reaches the page; as written, the joint-shop man's question floats, and the one idea that gives the book its title is missing. Also the plan's fit is looser than it says: a mirror-reversed brain keeps the same relation between its halves, so mirroring does not undo or redo Jaynes's arrangement. As folk lore that is fine, since lore may be wrong.
3. **Grounds:** world-v2 section 10, "Two halves, and a mirror"; feedback 2 ("Pull from Julian Jaynes...").
4. **Fix, both ways:** (a) If the writer wants the book recognisable, give the man half a reason in Linet's mouth: "Then he asked if I'd heard anything on the way back. Says when you come back turned your sides swap, and the quiet one gets a word in. Like I'd been to a concert." Cost: a small lore dump, eased by Linet's scorn. (b) If not, keep the line as is: it plants "heard" for ch. 7 and 10 and stays a wonder.
5. **Standing:** held if: how far the writer wants the book itself visible.
6. **Where it came from:** this output only.

### J6. Ch. 10: "It said nothing at all" shuts the door the plan wanted ajar

1. **Target:** part3 l.201: "*It did not choose. It did not help. It said nothing at all.*"
2. **What is wrong:** together with "She knew he had not said it. It was in her own head", the closing denial settles that the Witness did not speak. The brief asked that the voice read as Tomas's, from inside Nessa, and stay ambiguous; it now reads as only that.
3. **Grounds:** world-v2 section 10, "Rules kept" ("must stay ambiguous", "almost supernatural"), and its rival (a fuller Jaynes reading); section 4, "Why it stays a wonder".
4. **Fix, both ways:** (a) For a trace of the uncanny: cut "It said nothing at all." The voice then sits next to the presence with nothing said about which came from where. Cost: loses the refrain with ch. 7's "It said nothing." and the clean Jaynes beat of the silent god at the decision. (b) To keep the layer strictly psychological, keep the line as is.
5. **Standing:** held if: whether the writer wants the ch. 10 voice wholly explained by grief and stress, or left almost supernatural.
6. **Where it came from:** this output only.

### J7. Ch. 7: the new paragraph comes after the climax and adds a seventh set of hands

1. **Target:** part2 l.235: "*She found she was listening, too, and did not know for what until she caught herself at it: waiting, as if in a moment it would tell her where to put her hands.*"
2. **What is wrong:** it follows "That was the worst of it... it went on looking", so the worst is no longer last. It is also slow ("did not know for what until she caught herself at it"), the "too" has nothing to add to (it is the Witness that looks, not she), and "hands" now comes seven times in the chapter (l.221, 233, 235, 251, 259, 269, 275).
3. **Grounds:** world-v2 section 8 uses hands in sight as a symbol, which repetition wears down; the density round 1 (DC10) trimmed such repeats.
4. **Fix:** shorten in place: "She was listening, as if it might tell her where to put her hands. It said nothing." Keep it: it is the ch. 10 voice's only setup inside Nessa, and it echoes Sava's line. Cost: none that matters.
5. **Standing:** bears.
6. **Where it came from:** this output only.

### J8. Small slips

1. **Target:** part3 l.193 to 195: "*She wanted his hand on her shoulder.*" then "*And then, quite clearly, as if he were standing at her shoulder*", and the next paragraph opens "*Then it was there*". Part1 l.283 and l.287: "*The old crossers said*" twice in five lines.
2. **What is wrong:** "shoulder" twice in two sentences, "then" opening two paragraphs in a row, and Sava's same opener twice.
3. **Grounds:** the round 1 density critique's standard on nearby repeats.
4. **Fix:** "Quite clearly, as if he were standing behind her, she heard him say it." Sava: "They used to say it talked to them..." (as in J3). Cost: none.
5. **Standing:** bears.
6. **Where it came from:** this output only.

## What works

- **The pilot's line in the lock** (part2 l.275) is the best addition. "Their hands were where they'd been told to keep them... I went to hear it. That's what I found instead." puts obedience to the voice and the dead side by side, so the wish to be told what to do becomes the danger. It turns a safety rule into the story's question.
- **Sava's "Told them where to put their hands"** comes a page after Nessa "kept her hands where she had been told to keep them", so the first crossing is read again, quietly.
- **The ch. 10 voice is Tomas's, not a god's**: in Jaynes the voices are those of a king or a parent, and Nessa has acted on Tomas's list all story. The pattern fires once and she decides alone. "Keep us here" is unchanged and now heavier.
- **Linet's "Like I'd been to a concert"** is in her voice and keeps her scorn.
- **Restraint:** nothing was added in ch. 1, 2, 4 to 6, 8, 9 or 11, and the side place was left alone, as planned.
