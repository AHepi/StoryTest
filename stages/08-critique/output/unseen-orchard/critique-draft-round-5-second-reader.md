# The Unseen Orchard, draft 7: critique round 5, second reader (a first-time reader)

**Who wrote this and what they read.** An independent critic who wrote none of the story. I read only the three story files (`stages/07-dialogue/output/unseen-orchard/draft-v7-part1.md`, `-part2.md`, `-part3.md`) in order, every word, and section 2 of `stages/08-critique/references/critique-rounds.md`, the form a finding takes. I opened nothing else: no other drafts, critiques, logs, plans or theories. So the grounds below are what this read did to one reader, plus the story's own earlier pages. No owner theory is cited, because I was told not to open one. Section 3 (tracing a finding to its source) was not read, so "where it came from" is given only as the reading-log entry.

Line numbers are from the files as they stand. The workshop heading and bracketed marker at the top of each file were ignored.

## Reading log, chapter by chapter

**1. The Long Table.** Brother and sister, Tomas and Nessa, pledge the family orchard home to fund a survey voyage. A war is on. The signing scene is warm and exact ("I wanted to eat first"; the half without the stone). Then a cut to the ship. *Confused:* "I'm making it move twice" (l.69). I never worked out what that means; the next line about using the first estimate to reduce the second observation did not settle it. Also briefly unsure that "Wren" was the ship, since it arrives as "Wren's little sleeping compartment". *Felt like a patch:* the service drone carrying a line to a socket while "Nessa watched through the camera until Linet restored reception" (l.91). It reads like an object being put on a shelf for later, and I could not tell what was being repaired or why reception mattered. *Held me:* Moth with one orange foot; the measured mass that no camera can see.

**2. The Invoice.** The crossing rules arrive: a field, an allowance of time, everything carried counts. *Skimmed a little:* l.125 to l.133 ("Everything carried by the field counted ... A lighter load bought more time, not a farther exit"). I took the gist and moved on. Moth vanishes beautifully ("as though part of it had slipped behind a narrow edge that wasn't there"). *Strange element, first touch:* the gravimeter puts a person-sized mass behind Nessa's chair (l.169). Linet's "Drift" shrug makes it creepier, not less. *Moved me:* the fruit, the repaired foot, the invoice, the laughter. "Somebody had sent them a bill" is the best turn in part 1. Three fruit for three people is a lovely question to leave open.

**3. Nearbank.** First crossing by a person: "the sense of being attended to, as when somebody comes to stand in a doorway behind her" (l.253). Felt, not told. Nearbank is vivid at once: wet stone, baking, a whistle, the five-legged bird. The Witness is named by Sava in passing (l.283); it reads as talk, not a lecture, because she is walking and stepping round crates. The ceramic inserts are the trade. The empty place at the long table, "Whoever's across. And the one who keeps the orchard" (l.325), is the image the ending will need; I marked it. Linet comes back reversed and can no longer eat ship food. *Confused briefly:* "The tool she had carried worked as it always had; its home-side socket no longer agreed with it" (l.343). Cleared on the next lines. *Moved me:* Tomas bringing the bowl, then the spoon, then realising the spoon was not the problem; "You can have my share of the crisp bits." Linet's "Looked at" speech (l.363) sounds like the author slipping in a rule, but her own dismissal ("It's a socket. It's not a sign") saves it.

**4. Prize.** A raider under House Kest's commission goes for the convoy's stores carrier; Wren tows the carrier clear. Clear and tense. *Confused:* "They want the cartridges" (l.383). I was not sure whether cartridges were Nearbank's ceramic inserts or something else; I settled on "crossing supplies" later, by guesswork. The prize inventory, with a workshop paid through "the registered owner of its labour", plants the bonded-labour thread without a speech. Good. Ends with Surety setting up a purchasing station.

**5. Surety.** *Confused at the start.* Orra Serrat appears in a room with no word on who she is or why she now holds Nessa's route. I worked out from ch.4 that she must belong to the Serrat purchasing concern, but it took effort. The man "under a carved lintel" who wants "his branch" to name the commander (l.27 to l.39) I never fully placed; nor "the compact permits an allied guarantor" (l.53). I got the outcome: Tomas is made commander because the orchard is pledged. The sister's message ("thirty-one of them sleeping in the house now") is a strong touch; it makes Orra a person. The contract amendments (l.77 to l.107) did not lose me: short lines, real gains. The packing motor (l.113) ties the signature to the orchard. Good.

**6. The Receiving List.** A hearing over Dema's daughter Rin, claimed under an old service register. *Felt like the author stepping in:* l.145 ("The Berth Council had admitted the Serrat agents. In return Serrat recognised ...") and l.153 ("a succession of recognitions by offices that no longer existed"), but both are short and I needed them. *Held me hard:* the slanted stroke; "There were two witnesses." / "I see one." / "Yes."; the guard's hand on his chest; "Whose consent did she need to be born?" Nessa signs. "For a moment the room was very slightly too close" (l.205) is the Witness at her elbow, and I felt it. Then soldiers, Tomas good at it, the mended shirt, and Nessa choosing Tomas's list. "That evening the household's food arrived exactly when she had promised" is a quiet knife. *Slightly unsure:* who the "shipment purchaser" (l.147) is.

**7. The Room That Wasn't There.** Best chapter. The evacuation, the tug operator's fist round the token, machines coming through the wall Nessa's own survey marked clear, Tomas's voice ordering "named personnel ... recovered intact". The held abort (l.351 to l.359) is the centre of the book's strangeness and it works: "She wanted it to. That was the worst of it." *Confused:* "She had repinned on Wren's side" (l.367). Never explained; context carried me through. The rescue by line and drone pays off ch.1's drone, so the earlier plant was right in kind but cold where it stood. The pilot's Patience story (l.411 to l.413) is the best lore in the book: "Their hands were where they'd been told to keep them. Every one." Linet's "Where are you?" hurt.

**8. An Exception.** Nessa refuses Orra's exceptions. Clean, earned. *Skimmed and confused:* the coalition plan (l.491 to l.507): "four drum tracks", "reception axes", "Their old room arrivals become bad guesses", "Surety stopped before it restores the route". I got "move the arrival points so the attackers miss". *Confused:* "Tomas has twenty at the outstation" (l.527) and "Tomas's post was twenty kilometres from the rendezvous" (l.537). When did he go to an outstation, and where is it? It did not clear up; I accepted it as a given. Linet's "You still owe me a day for my tools" is a fine callback.

**9. Maintenance.** *The main skim of the middle:* l.21 to l.33. Frames A and B, four minutes to cool, B left at the relay, "Surety had eight more rigs, in the shop they were trying to capture", the bridge junction and its physical safety cut, the escort still towing the prize. It reads as an operations brief laying track for chapters 10 and 11. I read it fast and had to come back to it during chapter 10's sums. Tomas's recorded reply is strong; "Tomas asked about the orchard motor. It was the last thing he said" is perfect. *Held me:* the pilot's gravimeter list (l.57 to l.65), a mass behind her chair at the minute her frame held her, when she was not on the ship. The strongest single strange moment after ch.7. And "It stayed a door" (l.81).

**10. Twenty-four Minutes.** The boarding goes wrong at the hatch; Tomas's plea arrives. "Please." I felt the choice. *Skimmed:* l.195 ("A's remaining service would finish during those two minutes ... The two crossings themselves would take seconds") and the second sum at l.211. I took the gist (cannot do both) from Nessa's own message, not from the numbers. *Confused for a moment:* "The delegates have put the terms on record" (l.241); delegates are not named until ch.11. The "Go." moment (l.223 to l.227) lands: the voice from behind, the word that does not say where, and the echo of Sava's "Go" and of the behind-the-chair mass.

**11. The Shop.** Action resolves cleanly; the strut failure is felt in the body. *Learned too late:* what the shop is for (l.301, "the bins of consumables that turned individual passages into a working route"). That would have helped before the attack. *Skimmed:* l.337 to l.367, the terms and reports, which sit between "Tomas was not in the report" and his appearance. I was hunting for his name and read the terms as a list. Orra's "I ordered the officers to accept a recorded surrender agreement" is a good surprise buried in it. "He looked exhausted and angry and alive" landed. The pilot's reversed orchard (l.383 to l.385) is lovely and slightly puzzling.

**12. A Place at the Table.** The visit is the best-written scene in part 3: the spectacles, "It's a screw", "I'm not ready to make you feel better about this", the screwdriver. *Felt like a patch:* Tomas asking about the pilot (l.447 to l.461). I could not see why he asks, here, now; it pulls the scene off the two of them. The orchard sold; "It belongs with the rooms." Dema's "No" to the schedule shows Nessa has changed without saying so. *Slip:* "He changed one more time" (l.513), a word missing. The last image lands, with one staging stumble (finding 9).

## Judgements

**Is the middle (chapters 5 to 9) dense or light?** Dense, but alive. Almost every stretch of paperwork ends in a signature or refusal that costs someone something (the access agreement, the lot acceptance, Tomas's list, the refused exceptions), so the forms carry weight and I read them. Chapter 7 is pure story. The stretches that still read as procedure and made me skim are:
- chapter 9, l.21 to l.33 (frames A and B, cooling times, relay, eight rigs, safety cut, escort towing): the clearest remaining operations brief;
- chapter 8, l.491 to l.507 (drum tracks, reception axes, "restores the route");
- chapter 5, l.27 to l.53 (the lintel man, "his branch", "the compact"), more confusing than dull.

Just outside the middle, chapter 10, l.195, and chapter 11, l.337 to l.367, are the two places a reader is most likely to skim at the worst moment.

**Does the strange element feel felt and earned, or explained and bolted on?** Felt and earned. It is built from small, physical, repeated signs: the mass behind the chair (ch.2), the doorway feeling (ch.3), the too-close room (ch.6), the long hold (ch.7), the pilot's list (ch.9), the door that stays a door, and "Go" (ch.10). Each is a sensation or a reading on an instrument, never a lecture, and nobody in the story agrees on what it is. The two spoken pieces of lore (Sava, l.283; Linet, l.363, part 1) come closest to explaining, and both are saved by being in character. The one place it feels bolted on is Tomas's question about the pilot in ch.12.

**Do the lore and history feel like a world with depth?** Yes. The Transport, the registers, the slanted witness stroke, the five-legged bird, the empty place, Patience and the reversed orchard all suggest far more than is shown, and they reach the reader through objects and customs. The war between House Kest and Serrat is the thinnest layer; it works as weather, which is enough.

**Does the ending land?** Yes. The visit to Tomas refuses easy comfort and is better for it. The orchard sold, the long table left behind, Dema's "No", the hearing date entered beside the flight: Nessa pays and changes, and the story does not say so. The half without the stone closes the circle from chapter 1.

**Is anything confusing at the end?** Three small things: why Tomas asks about the pilot (finding 6); the fruit turned towards the place across and then set beside her own plate (finding 9); and "the one" in the pilot's "It wasn't the one", which reaches back to a single line spoken by a child in chapter 3. The last I did not count as a fault (see "dropped").

## Findings, ranked by cost to a reader

**1. The frame arithmetic is planted as a brief and spent as sums.**
- *Target:* part 3, ch.9, l.21 to l.33 ("They marked the two portable frames A and B ..." to "The escort was still towing the disabled prize ..."); and ch.10, l.195 to l.197 and l.211 ("A's remaining service would finish during those two minutes ...").
- *What is wrong:* the facts the climax turns on arrive as a list a reader skims, and are then used as two sums the reader has to do in their head at the story's peak.
- *Grounds:* reading log ch.9 and ch.10: I skimmed both, went back to ch.9 during ch.10, and took the dilemma from Nessa's message ("There isn't time for both", l.237), not from the numbers.
- *Fix and cost:* in ch.9, show the four-minute cooling once in action (a boarder waiting beside a hot frame while Linet counts it down) and cut "Surety had eight more rigs ..." (move it to finding 5's line) and "The escort was still towing ..." (nobody asks about the escort). In ch.10, put l.195 into one line, "Two minutes to pull back, fifteen to fly, four for the frame to cool beside him; it could not cool in flight", and keep l.197 and l.211 as they are. Cost: a reader who checks the mechanics loses a little of the reason the cooling cannot happen en route; the short clause keeps the main point.
- *Standing:* bears.
- *Where it came from:* reading log, ch.9 and ch.10.

**2. The terms and reports stand between "Tomas was not in the report" and Tomas.**
- *Target:* part 3, ch.11, l.337 to l.367 ("Orra wanted her personnel returned ..." to "She did not obtain her ship.").
- *What is wrong:* at the moment of highest dread the reader is given a summary of terms, and skims it hunting for one name, so the wait becomes impatience, not fear.
- *Grounds:* reading log ch.11; Orra's pre-order (l.355 to l.359) and "Is your brother accounted for?" do the dread work; the charter, the departure controls and the mechanic's choice do not.
- *Fix and cost:* keep Orra's question and her pre-order line; cut the terms to two sentences (households released under the charter; the Berth Council's tokens handed over) and move the mechanic's line and "She did not obtain her ship" after Tomas's record. Cost: the political ending is told more briefly; Rin's thread still closes in Dema's message.
- *Standing:* bears for the trim. Moving Tomas's record ahead of the terms is *held if* the writer does not want the reader kept waiting; if the wait is the aim, trim and keep the order.
- *Where it came from:* reading log, ch.11.

**3. Chapter 5 opens on people the reader cannot place.**
- *Target:* part 2, ch.5, l.7 ("Orra Serrat met them ...") and l.27 to l.53 ("a man sitting under a carved lintel ... if his branch could name the expedition's commander"; "The compact permits an allied guarantor").
- *What is wrong:* the hinge where Nessa's survey passes into Serrat's hands is told in shorthand; the reader does not know who Orra is, who the lintel man is, or what the compact is.
- *Grounds:* reading log ch.5. Part 1 names only "a Serrat purchasing concern" (part 1, l.479); I had to guess that Orra runs it.
- *Fix and cost:* one clause at l.7 tying Orra to the concern that qualified Nessa's sample and runs Surety's purchasing; one word for the lintel man's tie to her ("her cousin, from the senior branch", or whatever is true); name the compact once as the agreement Vale's guarantee sits under. Cost: about two lines of telling; the risk is that the scene starts to explain itself, so keep them as clauses, not sentences.
- *Standing:* bears.
- *Where it came from:* reading log, ch.5.

**4. Tomas's outstation appears without being placed, and the coalition plan is in shorthand.**
- *Target:* part 2, ch.8, l.527 ("Tomas has twenty at the outstation") and l.537 ("twenty kilometres from the rendezvous"); l.491 to l.507 ("four drum tracks", "reception axes", "Their old room arrivals become bad guesses", "Surety stopped before it restores the route").
- *What is wrong:* the place where the climax's rescue would go, and the plan the climax serves, are both handed over as if the reader already knew them.
- *Grounds:* reading log ch.8: neither cleared up later; I accepted them as givens, which took some of the weight off ch.10's choice.
- *Fix and cost:* one line in ch.8's Orra scene or Sava's compartment saying Tomas was moved to hold the outstation after the retrieval, and where it lies; in the plan, keep "Their old room arrivals become bad guesses" and "Where we can see them", and give "restores the route" a plain gloss (stopping Surety from sending more rigs). Cost: a line or two; little risk.
- *Standing:* bears.
- *Where it came from:* reading log, ch.8.

**5. What the shop is, and why taking it ends the occupation, is told after it is taken.**
- *Target:* part 1, ch.4, l.383 ("They want the cartridges"); part 3, ch.9, l.25 ("Surety had eight more rigs, in the shop they were trying to capture"); ch.11, l.301 ("the bins of consumables that turned individual passages into a working route").
- *What is wrong:* the boarding's target is clear in place but not in purpose until it is won; "cartridges" also reads at first as Nearbank's ceramic product.
- *Grounds:* reading log ch.4 and ch.11.
- *Fix and cost:* move the sense of l.301 into ch.8 or ch.9 as one line of Sava's or Linet's ("Every rig they send comes out of that shop. Take it and nobody else comes through."); in ch.4 make it "the crossing cartridges". Cost: l.301 then partly repeats; trim it to the sensory detail.
- *Standing:* bears.
- *Where it came from:* reading log, ch.4, ch.9, ch.11.

**6. Tomas's question about the pilot pulls the visit off the two of them.**
- *Target:* part 3, ch.12, l.447 to l.461 ("He asked, without looking up, about the pilot ..." to "She said she would.").
- *What is wrong:* the reader cannot see why Tomas asks this here, so it reads as the author keeping a thread alive inside the most private scene.
- *Grounds:* reading log ch.12; nothing earlier shows Tomas meeting the pilot or hearing of the unlit masses.
- *Fix and cost:* both ways, because it turns on an aim only the writer has. If Tomas is meant to have felt something himself at the surrender, or to be drawn to the unlit masses now, give him one line of his reason. If not, cut l.447 to l.461 and go from "Two need treatment. I've given the names." to the food packet. Cost of the line: a risk of explaining what is better left; cost of the cut: the pilot's thread ends in ch.11, which may be enough.
- *Standing:* held if the writer means Tomas to be drawn to the masses (then add his reason); otherwise cut.
- *Where it came from:* reading log, ch.12.

**7. "Repinned" is a term never met before, at the most intense moment.**
- *Target:* part 2, ch.7, l.367 ("She had repinned on Wren's side.").
- *What is wrong:* a new word stops the eye at the moment the reader most needs to be carried.
- *Grounds:* reading log ch.7; the word appears nowhere else.
- *Fix and cost:* "She had come out on Wren's side." Cost: a little of the trade's flavour.
- *Standing:* bears.
- *Where it came from:* reading log, ch.7.

**8. The ship section of chapter 1 opens on a puzzle and a plant.**
- *Target:* part 1, ch.1, l.69 ("I'm making it move twice") and l.91 ("A service drone moved from a damaged antenna bracket ... until Linet restored reception.").
- *What is wrong:* the first line of the science is unclear, and the drone paragraph reads as an object being put down for later, with a repair the reader cannot follow.
- *Grounds:* reading log ch.1; the drone is paid off in ch.4's towing line and ch.7's rescue, so it should stay, but here it is cold.
- *Fix and cost:* have Tomas say plainly what he sees ("My solution puts it in two places."); give the drone one beat of Nessa's attention (she watches it because she is restless, or she counts how long the clip takes), and cut "until Linet restored reception". Cost: small.
- *Standing:* bears.
- *Where it came from:* reading log, ch.1.

**9. The last gesture is staged two ways.**
- *Target:* part 3, ch.12, l.531 ("She turned the half without the stone towards the place across from her. Then she set it on a small plate beside her own").
- *What is wrong:* the half is offered across the table and then placed beside her own plate, so the reader is not sure who it is for: whoever's across and the keeper (the Nearbank place, part 1, l.321 to l.325) or Tomas (who gave her that half, part 1, l.61).
- *Grounds:* reading log ch.12; I read the line twice.
- *Fix and cost:* both ways. If the writer wants the Nearbank custom, set it at the place across ("She set it on a small plate at the place across from her"). If the writer wants Tomas's absence beside her, drop "towards the place across from her". Cost: either choice closes off the other reading, which may be what the double staging is reaching for; if so, the fix is only to make the two movements read as one deliberate act.
- *Standing:* held if the writer's aim (the custom, Tomas, or both on purpose).
- *Where it came from:* reading log, ch.12.

**10. A missing word.**
- *Target:* part 3, ch.12, l.513 ("He changed one more time and passed it back.").
- *What is wrong:* "changed it" is meant.
- *Fix and cost:* add "it". None.
- *Standing:* bears.

Smaller notes, not ranked: "the shipment purchaser" (part 2, l.147) is unplaced; "the delegates" (part 3, l.241) are named before they are introduced; "rig" and "frame" seem to name the same thing, and I was never sure whether they differ.

## Dropped (failed the flip or swap test)

- *Linet's "Looked at" speech (part 1, l.363) is the author explaining.* Fails the flip: I am about as sure it works, because her dismissal makes it character, and "gets a word in" quietly seeds "Go".
- *The pilot's reversed orchard (part 3, l.383) does not add up* (fruit planted by reversed people should suit them). Fails the flip: the puzzle may be the point, and it moved me.
- *Chapter 6's council sentence (part 2, l.145) is exposition.* Fails the flip: it is short and I needed it.
- *Chapter 5's contract amendments are paperwork.* Fails the flip: I did not skim them.
- *"Too many named people."* Fails the swap: it would fit any novel, and here the cast stayed clear.

## What works

- The opening table and the closing table: the half without the stone frames the book, and the long table left with the rooms is the cost made physical.
- The invoice. A dead-looking mass sends a bill; the laughter is earned.
- Linet's reversal, played through food, a spoon and "the crisp bits", not through rules.
- The hearing in ch.6: the slanted stroke, "I see one." / "Yes.", the guard's hand, "Whose consent did she need to be born?"
- Nessa's complicity built in small signatures: the access agreement, the lot acceptance, Tomas's list, her own survey marking the recess clear. Then the refusal in ch.8 means something.
- The held abort in ch.7: "She wanted it to." The strange element is a feeling, not a system.
- The pilot: nameless, Patience, the list of times "as some people keep a light on in a room nobody uses".
- "Tomas asked about the orchard motor. It was the last thing he said."
- The choice in ch.10 is a real one, and "Go. It did not say where." lets the strange element refuse to choose for her.
- Tomas in ch.12: angry, alive, not ready to comfort her; the screwdriver.
- Dema's "No" in ch.12 shows Nessa's change without a line of telling.
