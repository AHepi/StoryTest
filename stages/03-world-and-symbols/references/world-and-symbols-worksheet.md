## 1. Order of work

Truby starts the world from the designing principle and the cast, not from a map *(built: a world grown from the story expresses it; a world built first tends to become scenery)*. The story-world skill's Building steps give the method; this is the order in which this stage takes them, with stage 02's cast in hand:
1. **The world in one line,** from the premise's designing principle (`world-from-story.md`, section 2).
2. **The arena:** one bounded field for the whole story, and how it holds (section 3 there).
3. **Places from values:** take the values column of the cast sheet (`stages/02-characters/output/<project>/cast-vN.md`) and give opposed values opposed places (section 4 there).
4. **The opening world,** pressing on the hero's flaw (section 5 there).
5. **The departures:** list them, price each, ripple each into ordinary life, let them set the stakes, and make the main one carry the story's question (story-world skill, Building steps 1 to 6).
6. **A place for each turn** of the seven jobs from stage 01 (`world-from-story.md`, section 7).
7. **Business of its own, and surplus** (Building steps 7 and 8).
8. **How much each group says aloud** (section 2 below).
9. **Reserve, surface and gaps; the medium; a guide if the departures are many** (Building steps 9 to 11).
10. **The symbols:** start from the whole story and the hero's change, then the theme, then characters and places (`symbols.md`, sections 2 to 4), and plan each symbol's first appearance, how its meaning moves, and its last return.
11. **Test the load-bearing choices** with `hard-to-vary`, lightly (Building step 12).

## 2. How much each group says aloud

People who share a great deal say less to each other (`voice.md`, section 4, "Shared background", which holds McKee's point and warns against his labels for whole nations). How much each group in this story shares is a fact about the world, so this stage decides it, and stage 07 writes the talk to fit.

**The move.** For each group in the world, write one line: how much its members share, and so how much of their talk to each other stays unsaid. Then sort the group's shorthand into two kinds, since the Implied World theory treats them differently *(the workshop's reading of principles 2, 5 and 7)*:
- **Shorthand the audience need not decode to follow the story.** Leave it unexplained. It is surplus, taken for granted by people who have lived with it, and a gap left open on purpose (principles 5 and 7). The theory's own case is "the door dilated" (Part 2, section 3): no one in the scene explains it, and no one should. Explaining it would be the failure the theory names in Part 2, section 4: characters telling each other what they both already know.
- **Shorthand the audience must decode to follow the story.** Each such term spends attention (principle 2), so give it a way in: something the audience already brings, or someone in the scene with a reason to ask. Only when the departures a newcomer must learn are many does that someone need to be a guide (the theory's prediction "Worlds with many departures need a guide"; the story-world skill's Building step 11, with the gauge in `.claude/skills/story-world/references/diagnosing-a-world.md`, section 3).

## 3. What the output holds

`world-vN.md`, in this order, each part with its marker:
1. The world in one line, and the arena.
2. The baseline, and the list of departures, each with its price, its ripple and its stakes.
3. The places, each with the value or turn it expresses (a table: turn or value, place, what in the place expresses it).
4. The world's own business, and the surplus planned.
5. For each group: how much goes unsaid, which of its shorthand the audience must decode, and the way in for each such term.
6. Reserve, surface and gaps; notes for the medium; the guide, if any.
7. The symbols (a table: symbol, what it is attached to, first appearance, how its meaning moves, last return).
