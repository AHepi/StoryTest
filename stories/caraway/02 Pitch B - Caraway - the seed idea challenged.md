# CARAWAY

*Season pitch B. Ten episodes of about 55 minutes. Serialized drama.*

## Logline

In a walled city that exiles any seventeen-year-old whose tongue tastes the world backwards, a girl who lied her way past the test, and let her twin brother be exiled in her place, finds out three things. The city's holy weight is its own dead. The exile that once saved lives now kills. And the only way to bring her brother home is to get a whole people to say the one word they have spent three hundred years not saying.

## The spine in one line

**The rule was right once.** Exile really was a cure for two hundred years. The city's own dead, piling up where no one could see them, slowly turned it into murder. The needles that measure weight recorded the change faithfully for seventy years, and everyone read the record through the old rule.

## What this pitch changes from the seed, and why

1. **The Passing is not a lie. It was true once, then it went stale.** In the seed, exile always meant death. Here the first Passed really did arrive "where the mint tastes right", and they answered home. The rule was fitted to a world with little weight in it, and the city's own practice changed that world. This makes the Randall right about something. It makes "always dooms" feel earned, because every rule is right until the world moves. And it sets a trap for the audience's own habit of assuming the worst (see "The audience's three traps").
2. **The Randall has already done the sum.** She computed the observed rate forty years ago and has kept it ever since. She reads the rising number as good news: scripture says the Ending comes at "fifty in the hundred". Numbers alone save no one. Any belief can swallow data. What's missing is a new question.
3. **The Changers are a second church.** They recite the forbidden Fable like a catechism. Their fixed belief is that the Order fakes the Answer and the Lift must be destroyed. That belief is as untested as the Order's, and they shift their story when the evidence goes against them. Nobody is "the questioners" to begin with. Questioning has to be built.
4. **The Answer is another congregation's ritual.** For three centuries the reply to Ream's chant has come from the descendants of the first Passed. They kept answering long after arrivals stopped. The one honest channel was honest about who sent the message and silent about whether it was true. The "13" is Wells.
5. **Nobody set the drift slider.** "Fifty in the hundred" was the number the phone page showed before anyone touched it. The end of the world was scheduled by a setting no one chose. In the finale the city learns it now has to set that number itself.

## The world

**Normal.** Ream is a walled stone city of about twenty thousand people. It is built around the Lift, a vast humming device the founders came through three hundred years ago. Its scripture, *the Leaves*, is recited by children: seven Laws, seven Theorems and the "Plots", short case-law passages everyone treats as prophecy. The hand-copying lost the little tags that said which lines were real science, which were a storyteller's guesses and which followed from the others. So "Carvone has two mirror forms" and "the handhold is what did it" sound equally holy. Fossils of the original phone page survive as liturgy. At weddings people tap the shoulder ("the stack at the top right"). The temple chant is "two, three, five, seven". The Tally board in the tower records every crossing and every Turning, and its last line, *Observed rate*, has been left blank for centuries. Priests bless you with "may the animations respect your reduced motion", and nobody knows what an animation is.

**The departures, and what follows from them.** The world has only a few new rules, and each one reaches everywhere.
- *The Tithe.* At seventeen every citizen crosses the Lift once and comes back. Then comes the Mint Test: a leaf from the Sealed Jar, which never crossed. Caraway means Turned: your body came back mirror-reversed.
- *The Turned* starve in a city of bread. Ream's food doesn't fit their bodies, so they can live only on water, spirits and tallow. They catch no disease. Their gut germs are mirror-life to everyone around them, so no one's body can fight them. The Order grows a little "caraway wheat" from seeds that came back turned from the Lift. It feeds the Waiting House, where the Turned wait a few days before the Passing. A leash made of food.
- *The Passing.* The Turned are sent through the Lift, each carrying a Lantern "to light the way home", toward Minthome, "where the mint tastes right". It is a joyful rite. Families sing.
- *The Hands.* Ream's surgeons send one hand through a Lantern, a small, weak Lift, to lift a tumour out without a cut. Old Hands often carry one grey, withered hand that came back turned.
- *The needles.* Weight is the only thing that crosses between worlds. Gravimeters in the temple register the "Weight of the Ancestors" at the Lift. It has risen for as long as anyone has kept records, and people celebrate it as favour.
- *The Chant.* On holy days the congregation hauls the Heart, a forty-tonne stone hung over the temple floor, in time with the Tallykeeper's drum. Two hauls, three, five, seven. Within the hour the needles Answer: two, three, five, seven.

**Surplus the characters take for granted** (every item has a job, revealed later, except where marked). The devout "Thin" fast all year on tallow cakes and spirit and are admired for it. The incorruptible saints lie in the catacomb and never rot. Dogs are "unclean" and rare. The Randall eats alone, as the Leaves command. Tally apprentices climb the 211 steps of the tower; that one is pure surplus, a prime nobody remarks on. Children play the "prime race": one says a prime, the other must say the next.

**Why nobody questions.** It isn't stupidity. Three hard reasons hold the silence in place:
- *Questioning exposes you.* Over three hundred years the Passing has sent away the honest, the ones who confessed "caraway" gladly, while the liars stayed and raised families. Ream has quietly selected for concealment. About one adult in five is secretly Turned, and a person hiding a lie cannot afford a question.
- *The people who know the most have the most to lose.* The office of the Randall has been held by hidden Turned for two centuries. The Randall's table is the only good food a Turned person can get, so every Randall picks an heir who can never afford to talk.
- *The evidence always seems to confirm.* The needles rise, and that is favour. The Answer comes back, and that means the Passed arrived. The one channel nobody can fake keeps confirming the faith.

## The doom engine, step by step

1. **Founding.** The Hundred come through the Lift. The field's own drift is small: about 1 crossing in 25 comes back turned. The Tithe is a measurement. Scripture calls the drift "the most valuable number", so every young adult crosses once and the Accountants count.
2. **A rule that works.** The Turned starve, and they carry germs no one can fight. The Leaves offer "Exile is a cure: home is one leaf over." The first Passed follow the faint pull of the neighbour world, arrive, and answer by moving stone. On the needles an arrival shows as a rise and then a fall, as the weight moves away. The rule is quarantine and cure at once, and it works.
3. **A side effect nobody can see.** A few crossers a year run out of time between worlds and become the Lost: clouds of loose atoms beside the Lift, nothing left but weight.
4. **The rule hardens into ritual.** The tags vanish; the Tithe's purpose is forgotten; the observed rate becomes taboo because scripture already "gives" it: *Field drift, fifty in the hundred*, read as the date of the Ending.
5. **The world moves.** The Lost pile up. Their weight pulls crossers unevenly and raises the chance of turning (the show's stipulation). The drift climbs: 1 in 25, then 1 in 10, and now 1 in 3. More Turned means more Passings.
6. **The cure flips into a killer.** About seventy years ago the dead near the Lift grew heavier than the distant pull of Minthome. The liturgy tells the Passed to "fall toward the Weight of the Ancestors". They do, and they come apart inside it. Each Passing now adds about seventy kilograms to the thing that makes more Passings. On the needles an arrival had always shown as a rise that fell away. A death shows as a rise that stays. For seventy years every record has shown rises that stay.
7. **Re-tuning makes it worse.** The Order answers the rising Turning with more of the same. Crossers pray longer in transit to "come back pure", and that means more time inside the pull. The Lightening, a fast that makes crossers lighter so they can pray longer, adds more time still. The Order Passes more people to "cleanse the city", which adds more weight. It lowers the Tithe age, putting lighter children in the same trap. Every step makes sense inside the old explanation, and every step feeds the loop. No setting of these dials can work, because the rule itself adds the weight.
8. **The other side is dying too.** In Minthome the descendants of the Passed never questioned either. For seventy years no one has arrived, and they have gone on answering the chant "so that Ream remembers us". Each city's unquestioned ritual has confirmed the other's.
9. **Where it ends.** Nell projects the curve. Within twelve years more than half of each Tithe will turn. Within a generation Ream will have fed its young to its own dead.
10. **What breaks the loop** is a new explanation: the weight is the dead, and the dead twist the living. That opens moves the old rule could never reach. The drift can be measured with mint sprigs instead of children, and the Turned can be cured at home by crossing again. The Lost can be balanced with an equal weight of stone. All of it comes from one question nobody had asked: *what changes when things are changed?*

## Main characters

**Nell Abbott (17), apprentice Tallykeeper.** She is quick with numbers and slow to trust, and she would rather be right than be liked.
- *Want:* bring Wells home, without being Passed herself.
- *Wound:* she tasted caraway and said "mint", and her honest brother paid for it.
- *Flaw:* concealment. She keeps the rules on the outside so she can break them in secret. Her lie is the city's lie, told small.
- *Levers:* competence (she counts), interiority (we are inside her hunger), vulnerability (she is starving in a bakery), and a very specific need to eat candles.
- *Arc:* from private knower to public questioner. She begins by hiding one word and ends by saying it in the square, then chooses to stay Turned in the open, last in the queue for the cure.

**Wells Abbott (17), her twin.** Honest, funny, always asking "but what if?" The family's gently mocked questioner. He is always the one who reaches for Nell's hand, and he always gets the prime race wrong at 91.
- *Want:* to come home.
- *Flaw:* he trusts too easily.
- *Arc:* the questioner the city threw away. He survives the Passing because he doubts the liturgy: he feels two pulls and follows the faint one. From exile he asks the question that proves he is alive. He is never a dead boy who exists only to motivate his sister. He has his own episode, his own choices and his own clock.

**The Randall (63), high keeper. Born Ada Ehrenfest.** Gloved, precise, warm with children, and terrifying in her calm.
- *Want:* to carry Ream gently to the Ending, where "none will be parted". Privately, she wants to see Lio again.
- *Wound:* forty-five years ago she crossed holding her best friend Lio's hand. She tasted caraway and lied. Lio tasted mint, and the Randall of that day Passed him by the handhold rule.
- *Flaw:* she is a superb re-tuner. She absorbs every new fact into the old explanation.
- *Right about:* the Passing was once a real mercy, and mirror-life germs are a real danger.
- *Arc:* she saw Nell lie at the Test and did exactly what was done for her: Passed the honest one and took the liar as her heir. In the finale she hands Wells the leaf. When he tastes caraway, she does not Pass him. She says, "Then cross again." She ends reading the observed rate aloud to the whole city every morning. The keeper of the secret becomes the one who must say it.

**Master Tobiah Plattner (70), chief Accountant.** Nell's teacher. He is named, fittingly, after the man in the Wells story who came back mirrored. He is secretly Turned and has lived on candle-ends for fifty years. He is also secretly "Tau" of the Changers.
- *Arc:* he knew the most and changed nothing, because knowing alone never does. To save Nell he confesses. He is Passed, and he spends his death as a measurement. "Watch the needle."

**Theta (34), leader of the Changers. Born Iris Dvali.** A former Hand whose own hand turned. Her sister was Passed.
- *Want:* end the Passings by wrecking the Lift.
- *Flaw:* she has flipped the Order's certainty instead of questioning it. When the evidence goes against her, she moves the goalposts.
- *Levers:* charisma, and grief the audience shares. We side with her until we notice she never tests anything.
- *Arc:* from would-be destroyer to foreman of the wall-chain.

**Pim Sundrum (19), apprentice Accountant.** A true believer, a rival and an informer. His grandfather, Brother Sundrum, the Order's gardener, has known the drift for fifty years without knowing it was a number: "one seed in three comes back right for the caraway beds these days; in my mother's day it was one in twenty."
- *Arc:* the culture learning to question. He repeats Nell's sprig test himself to prove her wrong, and it converts him. In the finale his believer's instinct ("the Weight is the Ancestors' gift") meets the new method and makes the leap Nell can't. One right person is not enough.

**Marta Abbott (45), the twins' mother, a baker.** Devoutly Thin. She never tastes her own dough.
- *Arc:* the silence of love. She knows what Nell is at the first supper and simply leaves tallow cakes at her place. Her silence protects her daughter and keeps the machine running. She is the first to stand after Nell.

**Adamala (87), the last of the Arrived, in Minthome.** She came through at seventeen, seventy years ago, and no one has come since. She keeps the Answering House as a shrine. She is the Randall's mirror: kind, certain, and slowly dying at the end of a ritual.

**Caro, Wells's dog.** An "unclean" animal in a city that has forgotten why dogs are unclean. The first detector.

## The ten episodes

**1. The Tithe.** *A girl lies about a taste, and her twin is exiled for it.*
- *Cold open:* a schoolroom lesson in the Leaves. The show teaches the audience the Laws as its own physics, in clean animated cards: the coin lifted over the wall, the letter R flipping to its mirror. Then the Plot: "Two crossers hold hands. One comes back mirrored. The handhold is what did it."
- *Acts:*
  - The Lightening fast. The bakery. Caro growls at the Randall in the street, and the audience reads it as "the dog senses a villain".
  - The Tithe. Wells reaches for Nell's hand. Blind in the black between worlds, they play the prime race to stay calm: "...83, 89..." "91!" "Ninety-one is seven thirteens, idiot." Back on solid ground, Wells says there were two pulls, "a big one close, and a small one far off, like someone calling from another room." Nell says scripture names only one.
  - The Mint Test. Joyful believers say "caraway" and are garlanded for Minthome. Nell tastes caraway and says "mint". Wells, honestly, says "mint". The Randall opens the Jar, turning the lid a little oddly, and invokes the Plot. "The hand that reached." Wells is Passed that night, with a Lantern "to light the way home".
  - Nell records the needle, and it rises then falls back. The Accountant marks it "Refused": even Minthome didn't want him. The family is shamed.
- *Twist:* we are with the liar, and the audience alone knows the exile was the wrong twin.
- *Hook:* at home, Caro growls at Nell.

**2. Lightening.** *Nell learns to starve in a bakery.*
- *Cold open:* bread tastes of sand.
- *Acts:*
  - Hunger. Marta silently sets a tallow cake at Nell's place and says nothing.
  - Nell starts in the Tally tower. The observed-rate line is forbidden: "the rate is written; the drift is fifty."
  - A field trip to the catacomb of the incorruptible saints.
  - Nell writes her first Tally entry in mirror script without noticing.
- *Twist:* Master Plattner sees the backwards writing, and instead of reporting her he hands her a candle. "Eat. Slowly. Wicks last." The kindest man she knows is Turned, and a Changer.
- *Hook:* he gives her a torn page of the forbidden Fable, with one line on it: *What changes when things are changed?*

**3. Eleven.** *Nell tests the gods.*
- *Cold open:* the Changers' secret meeting. The Fable is recited like a psalm.
- *Acts:*
  - Theta tells Nell the Answer is faked by the Order moving counterweights.
  - Nell sees a test instead. An echo, a fake or a god would all answer the same words the same way. A mind would do something else if the words changed.
  - At the holy chant she beats the drum eleven times. The congregation, trained never to look up, hauls the Heart eleven times.
  - The needle answers **13**.
- *Twist:* something on the other side is thinking. Theta refuses to accept it: "they anticipated you." The Randall tells the Accountants, "The Ancestors add a number. The Ending is near."
- *Hook:* the Randall summons Nell. "You've been counting." She offers to make Nell her own apprentice. Caro has a sore that won't heal.

**4. The Observed Rate.** *(MAJOR REVERSAL 1)* *The number that should change everything changes nothing.*
- *Cold open:* Nell alone in the tower at night, eating a candle and working through three hundred years of Tally entries.
- *Acts:*
  - The Turning has risen from 1 in 25 to 1 in 3.
  - Hand-holders turn exactly as often as everyone else. Forty pairs where both said mint and one was Passed anyway. The handhold rule was a story's example, never a law. Wells was exiled for a sentence.
  - Brother Sundrum's seed beds confirm it without meaning to.
  - Nell brings the number to the Randall as a weapon.
- *Twist:* the Randall opens her private ledger, written in the "keeper's cipher", which is mirror script. It holds the same curve, kept for forty years. "Fifty in the hundred," she says, glowing. "When it reaches fifty, none of us will ever be parted again. Your brother is waiting." Data doesn't break a faith. It feeds it.
- *Hook:* the Order announces its answer to the rising Turning: longer prayers in transit, and a Tithe at sixteen.

**5. Seventy Kilograms.** *A teacher spends his death as an experiment.*
- *Cold open:* Theta and Nell steal the First Leaf, a dead phone in the reliquary, and charge it on the Lift's housing.
- *Acts:*
  - The scripture glows in its original form, tags and all. *Stipulated: the film's own axiom, the place where the science runs out and the story takes over, announced rather than smuggled.* The book knew it was guessing. Its copyists smuggled the guesses in as law.
  - The drift slider sits at 50%. Nell touches it and it moves. The date of the Ending was a setting nobody chose.
  - The show re-runs its own episode-one lesson cards, now stamped *Stipulated*. The audience learns it took a storyteller's guesses for the show's physics.
  - Nell is caught stealing tallow from the Waiting House. Plattner confesses he is Turned, which is true, to cover her, and he is Passed.
  - He whispers, "Watch the needle. Then find the Weight-book."
- *Twist:* the needle rises and stays. Seventy kilograms, slowly spreading. The Weight of the Ancestors is the Passed. The audience's cynicism feels vindicated: "the exile is death, we knew it."
- *Hook:* Nell remembers that Wells's needle fell back. *He didn't stay.* The end credits carry the show's own ledger of stipulations. From here on, every rule the show will use is on the table.

**6. The Weight-Book.** *(MAJOR REVERSAL 2, part one)* *The exile was a cure once, and one boy is proof.*
- *Cold open:* a heist of paper. Nell and the Changers steal the sealed Weight-book, which holds the needle trace from every Passing in three hundred years.
- *Acts:*
  - For two hundred years the traces rise and fall: the Passed arrived. For the last seventy they rise and stay. The one exception in seventy years is Wells.
  - The Changers' certainty breaks. The Passing was never murder until recently. Theta moves the goalposts at once: "Then the Order knew."
  - Nell needs a message only her brother could answer. That takes eighty-nine hauls of a forty-tonne stone, secretly, in one night, by every Changer they can wake. It is the first time the season needs many hands.
- *Twist:* dawn, blistered palms, and the needle answers **91**. Not 97, the right answer. Wells's old mistake. A stranger would have got it right. Nell laughs until she cries.
- *Hook:* the far stone drops hard, once, and then there is silence.

**7. Minthome.** *(MAJOR REVERSAL 2, part two)* *Through the looking-glass, and the same machine is waiting.*
- *Cold open:* we leave Ream for the first time, breaking the show's contract. It is the night of episode one: Wells in the black, feeling two pulls, choosing the faint one. He lands in a bright valley.
- *Acts:*
  - The Arrived: forty people, mostly old, a waterwheel and the Answering House.
  - To them Wells is the wrong hand, and his germs are mirror-life. So they lock him in their own Waiting House. It is Ream's rule, reversed.
  - He lives on water, their spirits and tallow. We see him throw the lever for 13 against the priests, and break back in for 91. The silence was them catching him.
  - Adamala tells him that arrivals stopped seventy years ago. The Arrived kept answering "so Ream remembers us." They feel the weight rising too, and read it as Ream growing proud. Each city's ritual has kept the other certain.
  - "You carry your world inside you," she warns. "So does anyone who crosses."
- *Twist:* Wells can't come home. To reach Ream he must follow its pull, and near Ream the heaviest pull is the dead. His Lantern holds a body for two seconds.
- *Hook:* starving, he talks Adamala into opening the House and sends a countdown: 21, then 20 the next night. In Ream, Nell reads it with joy: *he's coming home at the dark moon.* The audience knows the dead will take him.

**8. The Randall's Table.** *A sacrilege with mint sprigs, and a confession behind a glove.*
- *Cold open:* now the Randall's apprentice, Nell finds the Randall's private kitchen: caraway wheat, tallow, spirit.
- *Acts:*
  - Nell's test. Mint must never cross, because the sample you compare against must stay untouched, but that sound rule long ago grew into "no mint may ever cross."
  - Sprigs through the Lift: one in three come back caraway. Sprigs through a Hand's Lantern at the far wall: one in twenty-five. The Randall's explanation, the impurity of the crosser, says place can't matter. Nell's, the weight of the dead, says the far wall must be safer. It is.
  - Pim runs the test himself to catch her out, and gets the same numbers.
  - Nell sees it: nearness drives the pull, so an equal weight on the far side of the Lift would cancel it and make a still point. That means hundreds of tonnes. Only the city walls are that heavy.
- *Twist:* the Randall ungloves. She is Turned, since her Tithe forty-five years ago. She saw Nell lie and did for her what was done for herself. The office has passed from hidden Turned to hidden Turned for two centuries. "The table is the leash. I've worn it all my life. I was keeping you alive." Then she narrows her faith to survive the sprigs: "Sprigs are not souls. The faithful are carried."
- *Hook:* she seals the temple so Nell can't tell Wells to wait, and warns her: "If you speak, every Thin in Ream is Passed by spring. Your mother first." Countdown: 9.

**9. Caraway.** *A city finds out what it has been hiding from itself.*
- *Cold open:* dawn at the bakery. Marta kneads dough she never tastes. "How long?" "Since my Tithe."
- *Acts:*
  - The Thin: one adult in five. The Lightening is how Ream feeds its hidden Turned. The saints don't rot because nothing on this world can eat mirror flesh. Dogs are "unclean" because dogs smell the Thin. A whole culture grew up around not asking one question.
  - Caro's sore turns grey: mirror-germs, from Nell. The Randall's fear is real.
  - With the Weight-book leaked, the Changers seize the Lift to wreck it: no Lift, no Passing. Nell argues that wrecking it won't lift the dead, Wells will still fall into them, and the Thin can only be cured through the Lift. Theta won't listen.
  - The first Tithe at sixteen: a crowd in the square, Changers on the Lift, the Order in white.
- *Twist:* Nell climbs the Tally board and says, "Caraway." Then, instead of a sermon, a question: "Who else?" Marta stands: "Caraway." Then a Hand, an Accountant, a saint's granddaughter: hundreds. Pim reads the sprig numbers aloud. Theta lowers the hammer.
- *Hook:* alone on the temple steps, the Randall takes off her glove. "Caraway." Countdown: 5.

**10. The Observed Rate.** *(MAJOR REVERSAL 3)* *The weight that killed is the cure, and someone has to hold the dial.*
- *Cold open:* the first stone is pulled from Ream's wall.
- *Acts:*
  - Five days. Twenty thousand hands. Human chains carry the walls to the far side of the Lift. The Randall's forty years of needle traces map where the dead hang. Her skill, turned to a new use.
  - Pim sees what Nell can't. As the stone balances the dead, the drift falls, and that destroys the only cure for the Thin: at one in twenty-five a Turned person needs twenty-five risky crossings, and at one in three, about three. "The Weight *is* the Ancestors' gift. We've been using it wrong."
  - The city builds two cradles and moves stone between them. The drift is a dial. The slider on the First Leaf has finally come true: somebody must set it.
  - They don't finish in time. At the dark moon the balance is only partial: enough to loosen the dead's grip so the faint pull home can be followed, not enough to calm the drift. Wells crosses with his Lantern. The needle rises, and falls. He's home.
  - The Randall hands him the leaf. **Caraway.** It is episode one's moment again, under a new rule. "Then cross again." They swing the stones to the high cradle. He crosses. Mint.
- *Coda:*
  - The first Leaf Tithe: sprigs, not children.
  - The Randall has crossed twice for her own cure, still tastes caraway, and stops. Every morning in the square she reads the observed rate aloud.
  - Nell stays Turned by choice, last in the queue, and eats a candle in daylight.
  - Beneath the filled line on the Tally board she chalks a new one with only a question on it: *The grey?* (the mirror-germs she and every open Turned still carry).
  - Minthome's needle sends something it has not sent in seventy years: **11**.
- *Twist:* the dead were the doom and are also the cure. The drift was never fate. It is a setting, and now it belongs to everyone.
- *Hook:* Wells, laughing at the board, sneezes. He has spent months breathing another world's air.

**Why these twists are hard to swap.** Each is held in place by what came before it.
- **91 (ep 6).** The proof has to be a *mistake*, because any stranger could get a prime right. It has to ride the only channel there is, mass. It has to be planted as a joke in the one scene where the twins held hands. Swap in a birthday and it breaks: the proof would no longer carry his particular history.
- **The rise-and-fall (eps 1, 5, 6).** The needle has to be the witness, because weight is the only honest channel. The same trace that shamed the family in episode 1 proves Wells alive in episode 6. The reading never changed; only the explanation did.
- **The finale's inversion.** It needs all of these together: nearness drives the pull (ep 8), the cure is a dice roll whose odds rise with the drift (the Leaves), and the walls are the only mass big enough. Take one away and there is no dial.

## The audience's three traps

The show gets the viewer to accept rules without questioning them, three times.
1. **The Leaves as physics** (eps 1 to 5). We are taught the Plots as the show's own rules. The episode 5 cards come back stamped *Stipulated*.
2. **Genre cynicism** (eps 1 to 7). Experienced viewers "know" that when a utopia sends people away, they are being killed. Episode 5 flatters that belief, episode 6 breaks it, and episode 7 shows both the faith and the cynicism were unquestioned rules. The truth was a number that moved.
3. **Surplus as mere texture** (eps 1 to 9). The fasting, the saints, the dogs and the lonely table turn out to be the city's way of hiding. Some surplus stays surplus on purpose: the 211 steps, the "reduced motion" blessing.

After episode 5 the show plays fair. Its own ledger is on screen, and every later twist can be worked out from it.

## Mysteries and wonders

**Mysteries (promised an answer, and all answered by episode 10).** Is Wells alive? What is the observed rate? Who answers the chant? What is the Weight? What does the Randall know? Why did the needle fall back? Why does the dog growl?

**Wonders (touched lightly and left open).**
- Where the Hundred came from, "the Before".
- The squared stones in Minthome that are older than any Arrived.
- What a "film" is.
- Who built the first Lift.
- Whether the Lost feel anything.

These are mentioned in passing and never become anyone's goal.

## The ending, and what it settles

Wells comes home and is cured, not exiled. It is the same Mint Test as episode 1, with the opposite outcome, and the only thing that changed is the rule. The Passing and the child Tithe end. The rate is public. The walls are gone. The Randall's office is turned inside out. The dead stay where they are, and now they are used on purpose.

The theme, as the story's answer: a culture survives only while it keeps asking. Ream wasn't doomed by a villain or by bad luck. It was doomed by a correct rule nobody re-examined while its own practice changed the world. Minthome, which had no Lost at all, was dying the same way, of pure repetition.

The residue is the question Nell chalks on the Tally board, *The grey?*, and Wells's sneeze. The new explanation's first unmet change arrives in the moment of triumph, carried by the boy it saved. Season two asks whether Ream will question again, or build a new Passing.

## The show's ledger (declared once, on screen after episode 5)

**Inherited.** All four of the Fourth Direction's stipulations hold, including a neighbour world whose life has the opposite handedness (Minthome).

**Stipulated.**
1. An uneven pull on a crosser in transit raises the chance of turning, more so the heavier and nearer the mass. A balanced pull leaves only the field's own drift, about 1 in 25.
2. The Lost stay beside the Lift as a slowly spreading, still heavy cloud.
3. A crosser can feel the direction of the strongest pulls. Anyone who follows one before their time runs out lands on that world.
4. A Lantern is a weak field. It holds a hand for minutes and a body for about two seconds.

**Derived.**
- An arrival shows on the needle as a rise that falls. A loss shows as a rise that stays.
- Mirror flesh does not rot.
- Anyone who crosses carries their world's germs.
- Stone placed near the Lift sets the drift.
- The higher the drift, the cheaper the cure and the worse the Tithe.

**Declared backstory.** The Hundred came through three centuries ago.

No other rule is used.
