# CARAWAY: Season One master plan

*Ten episodes, about 55 minutes each, serialized drama. This is the one document every episode writer follows. Where it and an episode draft disagree, it wins. If a draft needs to change it, the change is made here first and recorded in the ledger (section 2).*

**Built from:** Pitch A (base), with grafts from Pitch B and Pitch C. The reasons are in `20-judgement.md`.

---

## 0. The show in brief

**Title.** CARAWAY

**Logline.** A starving seventeen-year-old lied about a taste, and her twin brother was exiled in her place. Now she finds that her walled city's most loving rites are what is turning and killing its children. Her brother can come home only if a people who have not asked "why" in three hundred years learn how to.

**The spine in one line.** Every rule in Ream was right once. The city's own love (its funerals) quietly changed the world those rules were made for, and a people who never ask "what changes when things are changed?" can only answer with more of the same.

**The core message, carried by the story rather than spoken.** A culture that never questions keeps doing what used to work, and what used to work changes the world it works on. Sooner or later the world always moves outside what your rules were made for. The only defence is a people who keep asking, even of the answers that saved them.

**Reversals.**
- **Major reversal 1**, episode 4: proof turned into a reason to die.
- **Major reversal 2**, episodes 6 and 7: the holy book was a storyteller's guesses; exile was a cure once; the Ancestors are the killers.
- **Major reversal 3**, episode 10: the promised land has begun the same rite, and the heroine's answer is caught short by a child.

**Tone.** Warm, strange, domestic, frightening. Weddings, bread, debts and dogs, in a city that feeds its dead to a machine. Nobody lectures. Characters discover things by doing: counting, sending, weighing, tasting.

## 1. The world

### 1.1 The normal

**Ream.** A walled hill city of about twelve thousand people in Year 300 of its own calendar.
- It has markets, weddings, debts, a bank, a school, chandlers, and a guild feud about the price of concrete.
- At its heart, inside the Temple of Leaves, stands **the Lift**: 212 bronze plates in a ring, humming. For a few seconds it lifts a body "off the world" into the black between worlds, then sets it down again.
- Beside the Lift stand the **needles**: tall brass gravity meters in glass cases. Needle-Wardens read them day and night.

**What people take for granted.**
- At seventeen, everyone crosses once.
- A leaf of mint tells you whether you came back right.
- The Turned are sent to a better world.
- The dead are Given to the Lift, and they stay with us: you can weigh them.
- The Weight of the Ancestors rises every year, and that means they love us.

### 1.2 The departures and their ripples

There are only four departures from our world. Every other difference in Ream follows from them.

**Departure 1: The Lift.** A machine that unpins a body from its world for a few seconds.
- The lighter you are, the longer you last. The Accountants' rule of thumb: seconds = 700 ÷ kilograms. At 70 kg you get 10 seconds; at 50 kg, 14; a card crate of 5 kg gets over two minutes.
- Ripples:
  - Walls mean nothing to a crosser, so Ream keeps its treasures heavy. The bank stores gold as dust set in concrete, and the lock-makers' guild calls itself the Weight-makers.
  - Crossers train blindfolded, floating in the Cistern, because there is no up in the dark.
  - In the dark a crosser sees only by the light trapped in their own bubble. Within arm's reach, every inside is laid open like a drawing: a heart beating behind ribs, a letter in a sealed envelope. Beyond that, black.

**Departure 2: Turning.** Nobody can feel or steer a turn in the dark. Sometimes a crosser comes back as their own mirror image.
- A Turned body is healthy, but no food in Ream fits it.
- The Turned can live only on water, spirits and tallow, and only for months.
- They catch no disease.
- The germs in their gut are mirror-life to everyone else. Their waste breeds a **grey mould** that nothing in Ream can eat.
- The only test is chemistry: a leaf of mint from the **Sealed Jar**, which has never crossed. A Turned tongue tastes caraway.
- The only cure is another crossing, with the same blind odds.

**Departure 3: Weight crosses; nothing else does.** Light, sound and touch stay on their own world. Only weight is felt across the gap, and it fades very fast with distance.
- The needles can feel a body in the dark beside the Lift, and a heavy stone swung on the neighbouring world, but only one standing almost exactly "overhead", at the same spot on that world.
- That makes the Great Stone a telephone nobody knows is one: a twenty-tonne block hung over the Temple floor and hauled in the holy counts.

**Departure 4: The neighbouring world's life is the other hand.** For a Turned person, the world next door is where the food fits. Ream calls it **Minthome**, "where the mint tastes right".

### 1.3 Scripture and liturgy

**The Leaves.** Ream's holy book is a hand-copied transcript of a single phone web page called "The Fourth Direction: seven laws for a stacked world". Nobody in Ream knows that.

It has three parts:
- **the Seven Laws**;
- **the Theorems**;
- **the Plot**, the "In the plot" examples, read as case law and prophecy.

Every verse carries a small coloured dot. The original page had coloured tags reading *Established*, *Stipulated* or *Derived*. The copyists kept the colours and lost the words. The Order calls the dots **the Three Voices**:
- **Green**, the Voice of the World.
- **Amber**, the Voice of the Ending. These are the holiest verses; in the original they are *Stipulated*, the film's guesses.
- **Grey**, the Voice that Follows.
- The Plot's verses carry no dot at all. The Order calls them **the Silent Voice**: not law, but what *will* happen.

**The First Leaf** is the original: a dead glass tablet (a phone) sealed in a block of stone at the Lift's foot.

**Fossils of the phone page, now liturgy.** Episode writers use these as everyday speech.

**The catechism game.** Children quiz each other: "Leaf four, line three, word six?" "*Mint!*" This is the code the two worlds will use (episodes 5, 8, 9 and 10). Each word is sent as three counts of the stone: leaf, line, word. So it can send only words that appear in the Leaves.

**The Glosses.** The Order's official readings are carved on the Lift's steps beneath the verses. Each time the world disagrees with a verse, a new gloss is carved in smaller letters. The newest, in episode 9, is almost too small to read. Viewers should be able to see the goalposts moving.

**The forbidden book: the Fable.** A second text, sealed at the founding: a dense theory of what an explanation is. Its secret readers, the **Changers**, take Greek letters as names (Theta, Tau, Lambda, Sigma, Pi) and chant its lines like psalms. Their creed is **"What changes when things are changed?"** None of them has ever changed anything to find out.
- Lines a character may quote aloud, and only as quotation: "A failed answer stays failed." "A copy keeps its source's history." "Closing a question is a choice." "A reading is not the thing read." "Nothing is settled."
- The show never puts the Fable's symbols or formulas in any character's mouth, except as a visual on a torn page.

### 1.4 The rites

- **The Tithe.** Every spring, each seventeen-year-old crosses once. They go in pairs and may not touch. Each taps their right shoulder first. Its original purpose, to count how often crossers turn, has been forgotten.
- **The Mint Test.** On return, each crosser tastes a leaf from the Sealed Jar. The Randall unscrews the lid "against the sun" out of reverence. Mint means clean. Caraway means Turned.
- **The Passing, also called the Ending.** The Turned are garlanded, sung to, and sent that night on a long crossing to Minthome. The glosses tell them to "go toward the Ancestors; they will carry you". It is a joyful rite.
- **The Giving.** The dead are laid in the Lift and its time is let run out. They "do not die so much as diffuse". The needles show them staying. Since Year 131 every citizen of Ream has been Given.
- **The Joining.** An elder who hears the Ancestors call may be Given early, alive. It is rare and honoured.
- **The Answering.** On holy days the congregation hauls the Great Stone: two hauls, three, five, seven. Within the hour the needles tremble back: two, three, five, seven. The Order reads this as the Ancestors' reply.
- **Pariahs.** In Nan's bedtime tales, Pariahs walk out of the Old Graves by the river at night, begging to be let in. The law says they are Passed on sight, unheard.

### 1.5 Names and offices

Families are named from the Leaves' list of sources: Abbott, Wells, Carroll, Plattner, Ehrenfest, Dvali, Adamala, Dimopoulos, Arkani, and one poor clan called **Others** (from "K. Adamala and others"). Nobody finds any of this odd.

- **The Randall.** High keeper of the Leaves. Holds one of the two Lift keys. Eats alone, "as the Leaves command". Wears a white braided stole ending in a small silver tooth. *(The stole is the phone's charging cable.)*
- **The Sundrum.** Keeper of the Lift. Holds the second key. Each year recites the Litany of Plates, a numbered chant of all 212 plates. *(It is the founders' assembly order.)*
- **Accountants.** Weigh everyone, set every crossing's time budget, and write the fasting tables. They are led by the First Accountant.
- **Needle-Wardens.** Tend the needles and keep the **Weight-book**, the needle trace of every Passing and Giving since Year 1.
- **Tallykeepers.** Keep the Tally. Apprentices climb the Tally tower's 211 steps. *(Pure surplus: a prime number nobody remarks on.)*
- **The Changers.** Secret readers of the Fable.

**Words the show uses.**
- **Called:** Tithe crossers who never came back.
- **Passed:** the exiled Turned.
- **Given:** the dead.
- **Joined:** those Given early, alive.
- **the Weight:** all of the above, felt on the needles.
- **Pariah:** anyone who comes out of the dark uninvited.
- **Straight:** Minthome's word for an arrival who tastes caraway there.

### 1.6 Everyday surplus

These details exist to make the world real. Most have no plot job, and writers should drop them in passing, never explain them.
- Gold dust in concrete; the Weight-makers' feud with the bank over concrete prices.
- Knots are distrusted: crossers tie nothing ("knots come undone").
- Mirrors are contraband ("a mirror is a small Turning").
- The children's **prime race**: one says a prime, the other must say the next.
- Chandlers sell "Passing candles"; tallow is cheap and faintly holy.
- The 211 steps; the benediction about animations.
- The Called's names chalked on the Lift steps by their families and washed off by the Wardens each week.
- Market scales at every stall, because everyone knows their weight.
- Legal papers kept in pairs ("paper is a witness").

A few surplus details later turn out to be clues (marked in the twist table): the Randall eating alone, the stole, the Litany, the Graves, the dash, the dog's growl.

### 1.7 The visual rule: the audience's eyes

Everything written in Ream appears on screen as **mirror-image English**. The audience files it as fantasy strangeness.

Five kinds of thing read the way we read:
1. the numbers stamped on the Lift's plates, which Reamers call "the Ancestors' hand";
2. the Sealed Jar's embossed glass base;
3. the First Leaf's screen;
4. the signs in Minthome;
5. from the last minute of episode 1, **Nell's own handwriting**.

This is not a camera trick; it is the plain truth, planted from minute one.
- The founders of Ream were Turned people from Earth, so their descendants are the mirror of us.
- Reamers read their script as normal and see Earth's print as backwards.
- The audience has Earth eyes. We have been looking at Ream the way a Turned person would, all season.
- When Nell turns, she sees Ream exactly as we always have.

The full meaning lands in episode 9.

### 1.8 Why nobody questions

Writers must keep all five reasons alive. They are what makes the silence believable rather than stupid.

1. **The rites worked.**
   - For two centuries the Passing really did deliver the Turned where the mint tastes right: the needle rose and fell, and the Answer came back.
   - The Giving began in a plague year and the plague passed.
   - "It works" was true when it was learned.
2. **The book sounds equally sure everywhere.** The Three Voices are ornaments now. A film's guess and a law of nature sound equally holy.
3. **Every bad sign has a home in the doctrine.**
   - A rising Weight means favour.
   - A child lost in the dark was Called.
   - A new number from the needles is a new command.
   - A rising Turning rate is the Ending coming, which is good news.
   - A belief that swallows every outcome can never be caught out.
4. **The people who know most can least afford to ask.** The Randall's office has passed from hidden Turned to hidden Turned for generations. Her private table is the only place a Turned person can eat. Every Randall picks as heir a liar she caught, and every heir owes her life to silence.
5. **Memory says questioning kills.** Forty-five years ago a young Changer mocked an unexplained rule ("burn what leaves your body") and a grey mould nothing would eat spread through Carroll Row. The street was burned and seven people died. Burnt Row is still black. The lesson Ream drew is "don't question". The true lesson, which the season earns, is "don't answer before you've looked".

### 1.9 Ream's history (reserve: for writers, never dumped on screen)

- **Before Year 1.** On Earth, a quarantine station (CHIRAL HOLD) held people mirrored by crossing accidents, fenced off and fed on synthetic rations. About 120 of them took the station's own Lift (which Minthome later calls "the Mother") and crossed to the neighbouring world, whose life is their hand. They carried:
  - the plates of a second Lift;
  - one phone with a single saved web page;
  - a glass jar;
  - the Fable on paper.

  They landed by a river, tasted a wild mint that tasted *right*, and wept. They called the place **Minthome**. The phone's gallery holds a twenty-second video of it: *Day One*.
- **Years 1–9.** They camp by the river. The first Tally page is headed *Minthome, Year One*. The founders count crossings and write the observed rate: **2 in 100**. Their own rare Turned children (now Earth-handed, starving) are sent back to Earth, where the food fits them: the first Passings. By then the station stands empty; why is a wonder. The Passed settle there. After Year 9, Passings from the hill come out a kilometre from the station, at a spot Minthome calls **the Landing**. It lies exactly "under" Ream's Temple, and there Minthome builds its **Answering House** and hangs its own great stone. Only a stone at that spot can be felt by Ream's needles, and it can feel Ream's. Needle traces read "ARRIVED".
- **Year 9.** The river floods the plinth. The founders carry the Lift up the hill, taking forty days with no crossings, chanting the plates in order as they reassemble them. The Litany of Plates is born. The landing site becomes the first burial ground: the **Old Graves**.
- **Years 9–130.** The name "Minthome" drifts to mean the other world, where the Passed go. The Leaves are copied and recopied, and the tags are lost. The dead are buried.
- **Year 131.** A fever kills faster than the Graves can take the dead. Randall Ottilie Abbott reads the verse "a crosser who runs out doesn't die so much as diffuse. The gravimeter in the empty room still registers seventy kilograms" as a promise that the dead can stay, and she Gives them to the Lift. The fever passes, and the Giving is "proven". The same year, faced with the Tally's 2 in 100 against the Leaves' "fifty", she rules that scripture wins: *"The rate is the Ancestors'."* The dash is written. **The year Ream stopped counting is the year Ream began building its doom.**
- **Years 131–235.** The Weight grows by about eight tonnes a year. It is still small, and the drift stays near 2 in 100.
- **About Year 235.** The Weight beside the door now pulls harder on a crosser than the far tug of the neighbouring world.
  - Passings begin to show a rise that stays.
  - The Randall of the day has the Weight-book re-inked: rise-and-stay becomes "RECEIVED" (blessed) and rise-and-fall becomes "REFUSED" (shamed).
  - A new gloss is carved: "Go toward the Ancestors; they will carry you."
  - The Turning rate begins to climb.
- **Year 252.** Iris Carroll's Tithe. She turns and lies. Her twin brother Kit, who held her hand, is Passed by the handhold law. The old Randall feeds Iris and makes her heir.
- **Year 253.** Kit arrives in Minthome a Straight, starving. He crosses back through the Mother and walks out of the Old Graves tasting mint, shouting "Move the door!". Iris, 18, gives her first order: "Pass him." It happens again and again that year, eleven crossings in all: the Pariah of Nan's tale. The eleventh crossing turns him, and he can eat in Minthome, so he stays.
- **Year 255.** Burnt Row.
- **About Year 270.** In Minthome, Mother Rosa Adamala begins Giving Minthome's dead at the Mother, "as the Leaves say".
- **Year 283.** Nell and Wells Abbott are born.
- **Year 294.** Their father, Tom Abbott, a Weight-maker, dies of a fall and is Given.
- **Year 300.** The season. The Weight stands at about 1,500 tonnes. The Tithe turning rate is **24 in 100**.

## 2. The show's ledger

The Fourth Direction earns its twists by declaring its guesses once and never adding to them in secret. CARAWAY does the same. Every rule an episode uses must appear here. If a writer needs a rule that is not here, the plan changes first.

### 2.1 What the Fourth Direction established (real science, true in the show)

- Matter and light stay pinned to their world; only gravity spreads between worlds (Randall–Sundrum; Arkani-Hamed, Dimopoulos, Dvali).
- In four dimensions, atoms and orbits are unstable (Ehrenfest). This is why nothing can live in the dark.
- In four dimensions, walls enclose nothing and knots come undone.
- A turn through the fourth direction brings a body back as its mirror image.
- Life is one-handed. Mirror sugar passes through the body unused. Mint and caraway are the same molecule in two mirror forms.
- Mirror-life is dangerous: nothing can recognise, digest or fight it. This is the 2024 warning in the Leaves.

### 2.2 What the Fourth Direction stipulated (the film's own guesses, accepted by the show)

1. There are many worlds, stacked, and they carry life.
2. A field can unpin matter and hold its atoms together for seconds, longer the lighter the load.
3. Gravity between worlds is strong enough to feel at human scale.
4. The neighbouring world's life is the other hand.

### 2.3 What the Fourth Direction derived (accepted by the show)

- The dark between worlds is black, and a crosser sees only by the light inside their own bubble, but sees inside everything within it.
- A crossing is short, timed and limited by mass, and everything inside the bubble crosses too: air, food, gut germs.
- A slide and a turn feel the same from inside. Every crossing is a coin toss nobody can watch, fixed at the instant the time runs out.
- A mirrored body starves on ordinary food, catches no ordinary disease, and carries mirror-life.
- Weight in motion is the only message that can cross. It is slow, and nobody can forge the fact that mass moved.
- Across the gap, pull fades with the cube of distance.

### 2.4 In the Leaves, but NOT true in this world (the Order's mistakes)

- **The handhold law.** In this world a handhold makes no difference. Two crossers turn or don't turn independently, each at the usual rate. The Tally shows "exactly one" for every hand-holding pair only because the rule itself writes the entry: the Randall always chooses one.
- **"The drift is fifty."** Fifty was the starting position of a slider in a demonstration. The real base drift here is about 2 in 100.
- **"For the ending only" as prophecy.** It was a note about how a film might end.
- **The Plot as law.** The Plot's examples are scenes from an unmade film. Some happen to be true in this world, because they follow from the laws: dogs growl at the Turned, a Turned body lives on water, spirits and fat, and paper has a hand. Being in the Plot is never what makes them true.

### 2.5 The show's own stipulations (declared once, never added to)

1. **The Lost stay.** Whatever runs out of time in the dark stays beside the door it left through, as a slowly spreading cloud of bare atomic cores. It is still fully heavy, and it drifts only a little over centuries.
2. **Weight near a door twists and pulls.** A heavy mass hanging in the dark near a door makes crossers who pass it more likely to turn, and more likely to be pulled off course and lost. The effect grows with the mass, with nearness (by the cube law) and with time spent in the dark.
3. **The tug of home.** In the dark, a crosser feels the strongest pulls as directions. Whoever follows a pull and still has time left steps out onto that world.
4. **The base drift.** With no weight nearby, the field turns about 2 crossings in 100.
5. **The First Leaf.** The phone survived three hundred years sealed in stone, and it wakes when fed power from the Lift's port.
6. **Shared light.** Two crossers in the dark at the same moment and place, coming from neighbouring worlds, share each other's light and pass through each other without either taking the other along.
7. **Backstory.** The founders came through the Lift three hundred years ago. *(Full version, revealed in episode 9: they were Turned people from an Earth quarantine station.)*
8. **Working number.** A crossing lasts about 700 ÷ (weight in kilograms) seconds.

### 2.6 Derived by the show (no new purchase; each follows from the lines above)

- **The Fast kills.** A lighter crosser lasts longer, so spends longer beside the Weight, and so turns and is Called more often.
- **A Passing is a coin toss.**
  - A Turned person can be turned back in transit and arrive in Minthome starving: a **Straight**.
  - An honest child wrongly Passed may be turned in transit and arrive fed.
- **Arrival and loss on the needle.** An arrival shows as a rise that falls, because the weight moves out of reach. A loss shows as a rise that stays.
- **Doors that stand at the same spot on neighbouring worlds line up.** A crossing is a slide in the fourth direction, which keeps every other coordinate.
  - A crossing from Minthome's Mother comes out at Ream's Old Graves, which is why Pariahs come out of the Graves.
  - A Straight who crosses back and is not turned on the way tastes **mint** in Ream. **Pariahs were cured people coming home.**
- **Distance.** Moving the door about thirty times farther from the Weight weakens its pull about twenty-seven thousand times.
- **Mirror writing.** The founders were the mirror of Earth, so Ream's writing is the mirror of ours. A Turned Reamer reads and writes Earth's way, and only Turned eyes read the plates and the First Leaf without a mirror.
- **Living Turned.** The Turned can live in Ream for good on crops grown from seeds that came back turned from the Lift. This is the Randall's glasshouse.
- **The grey.** A Turned body's waste breeds grey mould nothing in Ream can eat. It must be burned.
- **Carried germs.** Anyone who crosses carries their world's germs. Someone coming home from Minthome may carry Earth life, which is mirror-life to Ream.
- **A hand, not a body.** The field's bubble can be set small enough to take only a hand, and a hand's time runs for many minutes. If the field collapses while the hand is still in the dark, the hand scatters.
- **Signals need lined-up spots.** Because pull fades with the cube of distance, a stone signal is felt only by needles at the same spot on the other world. Ream's Temple stone talks to Minthome's Landing. A needle at the Old Graves would talk to the station.
- **Minthome's small weight.** Minthome's own Givings put a small Weight beside the Mother, and so beside the Old Graves door too, because it is the same place.

### 2.7 How the ledger appears on screen

At the end of episode 6, after the First Leaf's tags are revealed, the credits carry one plain card: **"What this story asks you to accept."** It lists stipulations 1–6 and 8 in everyday words, and 7 in its short form.

From then on the show plays fair. Every later twist can be worked out from the card, the Leaves and what has been shown.

**Never, in any episode:**
- the Weight is aware;
- ghosts;
- a signal that comes from the Weight itself (every pattern on the needles is someone moving mass);
- a way to feel or steer a turn;
- light or sound crossing between worlds;
- a Turned person living for years on tallow alone.

## 3. The doom mechanism, step by step

Each step names the idea from the Fable (the Semantics) that it puts into human form. Writers dramatize the step; nobody states the idea.

**Step 1: Rules that fit the past.**
- **What happens.** The founders count the drift (2 in 100), Pass their rare Turned back to Earth (they arrive, and the Answer comes), and bury their dead. Every rule works.
- **The idea.** Knowledge that survived past conditions is faithful only on the changes it met.

**Step 2: Questions closed by decree.**
- **What happens.** In Year 131 one Randall does two things. She starts the Giving from a misread verse, and she writes the dash because the count (2) disagrees with the book (50). The handhold law and "the drift is fifty" become law.
- **The idea.** Knowledge that is only declared: someone just said so. Closing a question is a choice, not a discovery.

**Step 3: The practice changes the world.**
- **What happens.** Every funeral adds about 60 kg beside the door, and they stay (stipulation 1). The mass grows by eight tonnes a year for 170 years.
- **The idea.** The culture's own practice produces a change outside the history its rules were fitted to.

**Step 4: The change arrives unseen.**
- **What happens.** The Weight twists crossers (stipulation 2). The Turning rate climbs from 2 to 24 in 100. About Year 235 it out-pulls the neighbouring world, so most Passings stop arriving and more children are Called.
- **The idea.** Surprise can happen only when a rule meets a change outside its history.

**Step 5: The readings are relabelled, not questioned.**
- **What happens.**
  - Rise-and-stay is re-inked "RECEIVED".
  - The rising Weight is favour.
  - The rising rate is the Ending approaching.
  - Thirteen is a new command.
- **The idea.** A reading is not the thing read. A rule's verdict changes when the rule is edited, not when the world changes. A story that absorbs every outcome can never be caught out.

**Step 6: Re-tuning makes it worse.**
- **What happens.**
  - *The Lightening Fast* (episode 2): lighter crossers, longer beside the Weight.
  - *Transit prayers* ("pray the whole crossing through"): the Fast's extra seconds are spent praying in the dark.
  - *The Joining* (episode 4): thirteen elders add about 800 kg.
  - *The Great Tithe* (episodes 8–9): all twelve thousand cross past the Weight.
- **The idea.** The selection response: re-tune the old rules. When the failure is structural, no re-tuning survives.

**Step 7: Why no adjustment can work.**
- **What happens.** Every lever the Order owns acts on the crosser (her purity, her weight, her prayers, how many cross), or adds to the Weight. The fault is *where the door stands and what is next to it*. The doctrine has no word for "where", because the Ancestors are "everywhere, all around us". You cannot fast away fifteen hundred tonnes.
- **The idea.** When every rule in the population reads the wrong thing, none can be tuned right. A new part has to be built: place and distance.

**Step 8: The right answer, merely said, fails.**
- **What happens.**
  - Kit shouts "Move the door!" without reasons and is Passed.
  - Nell tells the truth with no test and no plan, and a crush kills eleven.
  - Young Iris threw out the burn rule without testing it, and Burnt Row burned.
- **The idea.** Passing a claim on is not building it. A claim no one can check is just something someone says. Questioning is not the same as answering.

**Step 9: A new explanation, built.**
- **What happens.**
  - Nell asks new questions: "What was the rate *each year*?" "*Where* are they?"
  - She makes tests anyone can repeat: cards through the Lift, a needle carried street by street, a board with a written prediction.
  - Pim reruns them to prove her wrong and gets her numbers.
  - The city pools its scattered private counts ("Who else has a number?").
- **The idea.** Finding a new question is itself creative. An explanation built with the thing in view says something definite about untested changes, so it can be caught out. A learner who rebuilds an idea has built it; one who repeats it has not.

**Step 10: Repair, and its limit.**
- **What happens.**
  - Move the door a kilometre (the cube law, from their own book).
  - Stop the Giving; bury the dead again.
  - Test with cards, not children.
  - Keep a public board.
  - Then the new door reads 4 in 100, not 2, and a child asks why: Minthome has started Giving too.
- **The idea.** Nothing is settled. A copy keeps its source's history. Narrowing a claim after it failed is a new claim, not a rescue ("a failed answer stays failed"). A thinking people can make any of its own practices the target of criticism.

**Why "always" is earned.** Three generations of the same error, each right about its own past:
1. Earth fenced and rationed its Turned instead of asking how to feed them.
2. Ream Passed its Turned and Gave its dead.
3. Minthome copied the Giving.

The world always eventually moves outside what your rules were fitted to, often because of your rules. There is no villain in this chain. There is love, grief, prudence, mercy, and a book read as command.

## 4. Characters

Eight principals and a small supporting cast. Deep bonds are expensive, so the inner circle is Nell, Wells, the Randall and Hester. Everyone else orbits them.

**Mirror pairs, which writers should use visibly but never point out:**
- Nell and the Randall: the same lie, forty-eight years apart.
- Wells and Kit: the honest twin, Passed.
- Hester and the Randall: two women whose silence is love and fuel for the machine.
- Theta and the First Accountant: two certainties facing each other.
- The Randall and Mother Adamala: two keepers, one on each side, running the same rite.

### NELL ABBOTT (17). Apprentice Tallykeeper. The heroine.
- **Want.** Bring Wells home alive. Later: make Ream able to count.
- **Wound.** The three seconds at the Mint Test when she tasted caraway and said "mint", and let her brother go.
- **Flaw.** She hides. She keeps the rules on the outside and breaks them in secret, and her lie grows into a way of life (secret food, secret tests, secret truths), which is the Randall's way. A second flaw, from before the Tithe: she uses scripture to dismiss what she can't count. In the dark, when Wells said there were "two pulls", she told him the Leaves name only one.
- **Secrets.**
  - She is Turned (hidden until episode 8).
  - She sent the eleven (episodes 3–4).
  - The Randall feeds her (episodes 4–9).
- **Levers.**
  - **Competence.** She counts under her breath when afraid, and she has never lost the prime race.
  - **Vulnerability.** She is starving in a city of bread, and she can no longer read her own city's writing.
  - **Interiority.** We are inside her hunger and her sums.
  - **Specificity.** A stolen shard of mirror; tallow candles eaten in the dark, the wick saved for last.
  - **Humour.** Dry, spiky.
  - **Connection.** Wells, Hester, Tau, the dog.
  - Decency starts low, since she lets others pay, and is earned back by what she does.
- **Arc.**
  1. Liar.
  2. Secret experimenter.
  3. A truth-teller with no plan, who causes a disaster.
  4. The builder of a practice anyone can use.
  5. A girl who refuses to be the new Randall.
  6. In the end she takes the exile she dodged, carrying a question instead of an answer.
- **Voice.** Short sentences. Numbers as comfort ("Three hundred and twelve. Three hundred and thirteen."). Her favourite question is "Compared with what?". She never swears by the Leaves. When lying she becomes over-precise.
- **Surprise that stays in character.** She turns down the office the whole city offers her, and she chooses exile.

### WELLS ABBOTT (17). Her twin. A Weight-maker's apprentice.
- **Want.** First to meet his Ending with grace, then to live, then to get word home, then to come home.
- **Wound.** All his life he has been the one who shushes Nell ("Eleven's forbidden, Nell").
- **Flaw.** Faith and trust. He let Nell do the questioning for both of them.
- **Secret.** At the Mint Test he knew. He had seen her face when the leaf touched her tongue. He mouthed "Me" to the Randall (revealed at the end of episode 6).
- **Levers.**
  - Decency.
  - Humour: he always gets the prime race wrong at 91.
  - Strength: strong hands, a hauler.
  - Agency in Minthome.
  - Vulnerability: starving on the wrong world.
- **Arc.**
  - The faithful twin does one unfaithful thing, alone in the dark. The liturgy tells the Passed to go toward the big close pull. He follows the faint far one ("What would Nell do? Go past seven."), and it saves his life.
  - In Minthome he asks his first real question ("Why do our dead go to the Mother?").
  - He persuades a second culture to use its one channel to ask something.
  - He comes home.
- **Voice.** Warm, teasing, physical; he counts hauls aloud. He says "What if..." rarely, and when he does it matters.
- **Rule for writers.** He is never a prize to be won. From episode 7 he has his own storyline, his own clock and his own choices.

### THE RANDALL: IRIS CARROLL (65). High keeper of the Leaves.
- **Want.** To keep Ream calm and whole and carry it gently to the Ending, where she will see her twin brother Kit again.
- **Wound.**
  - At 17 she turned and lied, and Kit, who held her hand, was Passed.
  - At 18, in her first order as apprentice, she had Kit Passed again when he came out of the Graves shouting "Move the door!", before he could say why. The guilt drove her to the Changers.
  - At 20, as the young Changer "Sigma", she mocked the old Randall's unexplained rule ("burn what leaves your body") and simply stopped keeping it. Her own body bred Burnt Row, and seven people died. She left the Changers for ever.
- **Flaw.** She believes mercy means certainty, and closes questions to spare people pain. She is superb at fitting any new fact into the old story.
- **Secrets.**
  - She is Turned, and has been for 48 years. She lives on the glasshouse crops.
  - She has kept the true rate in a private ledger for forty years, and reads its rise as the Ending coming.
  - She knew at once that Nell lied. She took Wells's "Me" because she knew Nell's face: it was her own.
- **Right about.**
  - Hidden Turned people can breed blight.
  - Truth told with no plan kills (episode 8).
  - The Passing really was a mercy once.
- **Levers.**
  - Competence.
  - Real tenderness, with children and with Nell.
  - Specificity. She eats alone. She wears the white stole. She unscrews the Jar lid "against the sun", a reverence that began as a hidden Turned Randall's wrong-handed habit and became liturgy. *(Plant: Law 4 says the first sign of turning is opening a jar the wrong way.)*
- **Arc.** The keeper of the dash writes the number on the board in her own hand, frees Nell, turns her key, and says to a Pariah what she could not say for Kit: "Let him speak." At last she could go to Minthome and her brother. She stays, and reads the rate aloud in the square every morning.
- **Voice.** Calm, few words, never raised. She calls everyone "child". When frightened she speaks in verses. "Hush" is her mercy.
- **Rule for writers.** She is never a villain. Viewers should half-agree with her until episode 8, and fully understand her by episode 9.

### HESTER ABBOTT (44). The twins' mother. An Accountant.
- **Want.** To keep what is left of her family.
- **Wound.** Her husband Given; her son Passed; then her mother-in-law Joined (episode 4).
- **Flaw.** Silence. She knows, and says nothing. She believes numbers serve the Order; they do not question it.
- **Secrets.**
  - From the first supper she knows Nell is Turned: the left-shoulder blessing, the growling dog, the untouched bread. She leaves tallow cakes at Nell's place without a word.
  - She wrote the Fast tables.
- **Levers.** Competence (she weighs people with her eyes), connection, restraint. And the frightening competence of burning half her own house to kill the grey (episode 5).
- **Arc.**
  1. Silent protector.
  2. Silent in the crowd while Nell is exposed, just as Nell was silent at the Test.
  3. She breaks her silence. She nails her own Fast tables to the public board with WRONG written across them, then works out the number that brings her son home safely: his ballast.
- **Voice.** Clipped and practical: "Eat." "Sit." She speaks in weights ("You're down two kilos.").

### TAU: TOBIN DVALI (19). Tally clerk and Changer.
- **Want.** To know why his sister Mira was Called, and who is to blame.
- **Wound.** Mira, Called in the first minute of the season.
- **Flaw.** He wants a villain, and a conspiracy suits him.
- **Secrets.** He is a Changer. He loves Nell.
- **Levers.** Warmth, humour, loyalty, courage (he heaves stones and smuggles the dog), and a good memory for records.
- **Arc.** He moves from "the Order fakes it" to evidence. He learns that the villain was love: Mira is part of the Weight. He breaks with his grandmother, leads the Changers carrying the plates, and stays to keep the public board when Nell leaves.
- **Voice.** Quick and jokey. He uses the Fable's creed as teasing ("What changes when things are changed, Nell?"), and goes quiet when grief comes.

### THETA: OLD MOTHER ADA DVALI (78). Needle-Warden and head of the Changers.
- **Want.** To prove the Order a liar, and to protect Tau.
- **Wound.** At 13, a Warden's apprentice, she saw men re-inking the Weight-book labels by lamplight. She was beaten for asking why.
- **Flaw.** She is honest to instruments and dishonest to people. She moves her goalposts when the evidence turns. She mistakes a change of words for a faking of the needles.
- **Secrets.**
  - She leaves the eleven out of her log to protect Tau (episode 4).
  - She sells Nell to the First Accountant to protect Tau (episode 8).
- **Right about.** The Order did change the record. It changed the labels, not the readings.
- **Levers.** Specificity (her needles, her log in tiny script), grit, acid humour, love for Tau.
- **Arc.** From conspiracy to one moment in episode 9. She watches the Lift's weight walk out of the city on her needles and rings no bell. Her faithfulness to the instrument finally outweighs her story.
- **Voice.** Salty and suspicious. "Nobody fakes a mountain. But a priest can move a stone."

### KIT CARROLL (65). In Minthome. The Randall's twin brother.
- **Situation.** Passed at 17. That year he crossed eleven times, coming out of the Graves again and again and being Passed each time, until the eleventh crossing turned him. He has lived in Minthome for forty-seven years. Minthome's tally-keeper: he counts arrivals, Straights and Givings. He is the only living person who has crossed through both doors and noticed the difference.
- **Want.** To get word home that the door must move, and to see his sister.
- **Wound.** His sister's lie, then his sister's order.
- **Flaw.** Bitterness. For forty-seven years he has shouted a conclusion ("Move the door!") and never built the reasons or a test anyone else could run.
- **Secret.** His own tally shows the drift at the Mother creeping up since Minthome began Giving. He has told no one; he fears Mother Adamala.
- **Arc.** From a man who shouts an answer to a man who teaches Wells the reasons and then lets Wells ask. At the end he is waiting for Nell at the Mother, and he stands beside her in the procession's path.
- **Voice.** Dry and sardonic, the voice of a lifetime unheard. He reads Earth's signs aloud to Wells, who can't read them.

### PIM EHRENFEST (19). Apprentice Accountant under Hester. Son of the Sundrum.
- **Want.** To be right in the eyes of the Order, and to protect his sister Bea (16).
- **Wound.** At his own Tithe he panicked in the dark and came back vomiting, and everyone laughed. He has been proving himself ever since.
- **Flaw.** An informer's zeal. He believes rules are safety.
- **Secret.** He reruns Nell's card test to catch her out, gets her numbers, and tells no one (episodes 6–8).
- **Levers.** Competence; vulnerability (Bea is in the first hundred of the Great Tithe list); specificity (a pocket notebook of weights, a pencil he licks); and the capacity to surprise.
- **Arc.** From believer and informer to the one who makes the method his own. Word-perfect in his father's Litany, he is the one who hears it as an assembly order. In episode 10 he runs the cards with the children.
- **Voice.** Formal. He quotes rules exactly and stammers when he lies.

### Supporting cast

- **Nan Abbott (71).** The twins' grandmother. She tells Pariah tales. Serene, funny, sure. She Joins in episode 4 and folds Nell's half-confession into her faith: "Then the Ancestors spoke through you, love."
- **Mira Dvali (17).** Tau's sister. Called in the first minute.
- **Plattner.** The Abbotts' old grey hound; dogs are kept as "knowers". He growls at the Randall in episode 1, and at Nell that night. By episode 8 he has learned her new smell and lies down beside her in her cell. A living thing that adjusts to change, unlike the city.
- **First Accountant Abel Dimopoulos (58).** The hardliner and a sincere manager of fear. He reads thirteen as a command, proclaims the Great Tithe and carves the last gloss. He is not a villain. He is what the old rules look like under pressure.
- **The Sundrum: Jonah Ehrenfest (50).** Keeper of the Lift and Pim's father. He recites the Litany of Plates, and turns his key in episode 9.
- **Bea Ehrenfest (16).** Pim's sister. In the first hundred of the Great Tithe.
- **Lottie Others (9).** A Tally runner from the poor Others clan. She counts everything and wins the catechism game. In episode 10 she asks the question that catches Nell's answer short.
- **Owen Others (22).** Lottie's brother and a Lift clerk. He runs the maintenance hours and helps with the cards.
- **Brother Arkani (80).** The Order's gardener. He grows the Randall's crops from seeds that come back turned from the Lift, and has kept the drift for sixty years without knowing it was a number: "One seed in four comes back right for the glasshouse now. In my mother's day, one in fifty."
- **Mother Rosa Adamala (87).** Keeper of Minthome's Answering House and of the Mother. The Randall's mirror: kind, certain, and running the same rite. She began Minthome's Givings.
- **Jory (20).** A young Minthome hauler, Minthome-born. Curious. The first in Minthome to help Wells.

## 5. The ten episodes

**Structure of each entry.** Title; one-line pitch; cold open; acts; the twist; the ending hook; the gaps it opens and closes.

**Point of view.** Episodes 1–6 stay locked inside Ream, mostly with Nell. Episode 7 breaks that contract and goes to Minthome. From then on the show cuts between the two worlds.

**The season clock.**
- It runs from the spring Tithe (episode 1) to the Autumn Giving (episode 8), then seven days (episodes 9–10).
- Wells, a Straight in Minthome, has about six months to live.
- Nell starves on tallow until the Randall feeds her at the end of episode 4.

### EPISODE 1: "The Tithe"
*On crossing day, twins break the rule about holding hands, and the wrong one is sent away.*

**Cold open.**
- Tithe morning. Children in the Temple recite Leaf Two: "A crosser who runs out doesn't die so much as diffuse..."
- Mira Dvali, 17, taps her right shoulder and steps into the humming ring of plates. She is gone.
- A Warden (Theta) counts the seconds aloud. Ten. Fourteen. Mira doesn't come back.
- Theta calls, "Sixty-one." The needle has risen and stays there. The crowd sighs: "Favour."
- Tau, her brother, stands frozen.
- At the Tally board, apprentice Nell Abbott chalks her first official mark: CALLED. It appears in mirror script, like all of Ream's writing.

**Act 1: Ream's normal.**
- Lottie and other children play the catechism game in the Temple yard ("Leaf four, line three, word six?" "*Mint!*").
- A schoolroom lesson teaches the audience the Leaves as if they were the show's own physics, with painted teaching plates:
  - a coin lifted over a wall;
  - an R flipped to Я;
  - a jar with two leaves;
  - a stack of pages.
- The teacher also reads Law Seven, with its green dot: "You cannot look at the neighbour. You can only weigh them... the needle only wakes up when the mountain is nearly overhead."
- Then the Plot: "Two crossers hold hands. One comes back mirrored. The handhold is what did it." This verse has no dot at all. The teacher: "The Plot needs no voice. It is what will happen."
- Last, the Ending verse, with the amber dot, the holiest voice: "For the ending only: the neighbour's life is the other hand."
- In the Tally Hall, the dash on OBSERVED RATE. Nell's chalk hovers over it. The Master: "The rate is the Ancestors'."
- At home, Hester weighs the twins before the Tithe (51.0 kg; 70.4 kg).
- Nan tells a Pariah tale: pale things walking out of the Old Graves by the river at night, begging to come in, and "the one who shouted about doors".
- The twins run the prime race: "...eighty-nine." "Ninety-one!" "Ninety-one is seven thirteens, idiot." Wells always misses it.
- In the street the Randall passes in her white stole, and Plattner the dog growls at her. Hester hushes him. The audience reads this as the dog sensing a villain.
- On the Temple steps the Sundrum recites the Litany of Plates for the Tithe ("Plate one, tooth toward the river..."), and Pim mouths along, word-perfect.
- We glimpse Burnt Row, still black, and the plate numbers, the only print we can read.

**Act 2: The crossing.**
- Pairs step into the ring. Right shoulders tapped. No touching.
- In the black, each twin is lit only by their own light. They can see inside each other: Wells's heart beating, Nell's lungs pumping.
- To stay calm they play the prime race: "...five." "Seven." "*Eleven!*" "Shh, Nell." "Thirteen."
- Wells whispers: "There are two pulls. A big one, close, like a cliff. And a little one far off, like someone calling from another room." Nell: "The Leaves name one. The tug of home."
- Nell's fingertips tingle and she panics. Wells takes her hand. They land.

**Act 3: The Mint Test.**
- Ahead of them, two children taste caraway and say so gladly. They are garlanded for Minthome, and their families cheer.
- The Randall unscrews the Jar "against the sun". A close-up shows its embossed glass base, which we can read.
- Nell tastes caraway. Three seconds. "Mint."
- Wells: "Mint."
- A Warden saw them come out holding hands. The Randall recites: "One comes back mirrored. The handhold is what did it." One of them is lying.
- The Randall looks a long time at Nell's face. Wells mouths something we cannot hear.
- The Randall: "The hand that reached." She chooses Wells. We see Hester's face.

**Act 4: The Passing.**
- The rite that night is joyful: garlands, candles, the Great Stone swung in two, three, five, seven. The needles Answer. The crowd sings "The ending writes itself."
- Over the Passed the gloss is read: "Go toward the Ancestors; they will carry you."
- Wells taps Nell's right shoulder and says nothing. He steps in. Gone.
- Nell records the traces. The two believers' needles rise and stay: Pim writes "RECEIVED", and their families weep with joy. Wells's needle rises, then falls back. Pim writes "REFUSED".
- The crowd murmurs that even the Ancestors didn't want him. The family is shamed.

**The twist.** The wrong twin is exiled, by a verse the audience has just learned to trust. The audience is ahead of everyone but Nell.

**The ending hook.**
- Home. Nell blesses her mother goodnight by tapping her *left* shoulder. Hester notices. Plattner growls at Nell.
- Alone in the Tally Hall at night, Nell chalks WELLS ABBOTT: REFUSED. For the first time in the episode the audience can read a Ream word. Among a wall of mirror script, her letters come out the way we write. Her hand now writes the other way.

**Gaps.**
- **Opened.**
  - Is Wells alive?
  - What does "Refused" mean?
  - What did Wells mouth?
  - Why is the rate blank?
  - Why does the dog growl at the Randall?
  - (Quietly) why can we read the plates, and now Nell's hand?
- **Closed.** None. This episode sets the normal and breaks it.

### EPISODE 2: "Observed Rate"
*Nell must hide a body that can't eat, read or bless the right way, and her numbers say the city is running out of time.*

**Cold open.** Night, the pantry. Nell bites bread: sand. She eats a tallow candle, retches, and eats again, saving the wick for last. The dog watches from the dark.

**Act 1: Hiding.**
- At breakfast Hester sets a tallow cake at Nell's place without a word. Neither speaks. From here on the audience knows Hester knows.
- At the Tally, Ream's script is now backwards to Nell. In Burnt Row she buys a contraband shard of mirror and reads the ledgers through it. The audience sees the reflection come out readable.
- She fakes her old handwriting, slowly.
- Pim notices she has become slow. He is suspicious.

**Act 2: The Fast.**
- Three were Called this Tithe. First Accountant Dimopoulos reasons that the Called were too heavy to get home before their time ran out, and a lighter body gets more time.
- He declares **the Lightening**: every crosser fasts down before crossing and prays the whole crossing through.
- It is built on Hester's tables, and she presents them, proud and precise. Nell sees nothing wrong with it. Nobody could: it is sound by the old question.

**Act 3: The new question.**
- Year-end sums. The grand total over three centuries gives about 7 Turned in 100, and everyone is comfortable.
- Nell, sleepless with hunger, asks a different question: not "what is the rate?" but "what was it *each year*?"
- Montage: Lottie runs ledgers down the 211 steps. The curve appears:
  - flat at 2 in 100 for two centuries;
  - rising from about Year 235;
  - in Nell's own lifetime, from 10 to 24.
- At this pace it reaches one in two before she is thirty.
- Nell skips a gap in Year 9: forty days with no crossings at all.
- The first ledger's first page is headed *Minthome, Year One*, and the founders wrote the rate there: **2 in 100**. The dash begins in Year 131 with a note: "The rate is the Ancestors'. Ottilie Abbott, Randall." Her own family name.

**Act 4: Pressure.**
- Hester finds the mirror shard in Nell's apron and puts it back without a word.
- Nell tries to show her mother the curve. Hester: "Numbers serve. They don't ask."
- Hester, weighing her: "You're down three kilos."

**The twist.** The Turning rate has more than doubled in Nell's lifetime. The founders counted it, and the silence was a decree made by an Abbott.

**The ending hook.** At dawn a cloth bundle sits on her Tally desk: a small loaf. She bites. It tastes right. Someone knows what she is.

**Gaps.**
- **Opened.**
  - Who is feeding her?
  - What is driving the rise?
  - The clock: one in two.
- **Closed.** Why the rate line is blank: it was a decree. Why that decree was made stays open until episode 9.

### EPISODE 3: "Two, Three, Five, Seven"
*The heretics who share her secret believe the Order fakes the voice of the dead, so Nell designs a test no liar could pass.*

**Cold open.** In the Tally stacks, Tau catches Nell reading with a mirror, which is a Passing offence. He hands her a better mirror. "Come and meet some people who read backwards too."

**Act 1: The Changers.**
- A candlelit cellar under a chandlery. Torn pages of the Fable. Greek-letter names. They chant "What changes when things are changed?" like a psalm. Nobody here has ever changed anything.
- Theta presides.

**Act 2: The conspiracy.**
- Theta says the Order fakes the Answering by moving hidden counterweights. She knows they falsify things: at thirteen she saw men re-writing the Weight-book by lamplight, and was beaten for asking why.
- Tau: "Mira didn't get Called. The Order's lies killed her."
- The audience half-believes them. Genre instinct says the priests are lying.
- Nell: "How would you know?" Silence.

**Act 3: The design.** Nell lays it out so the audience can follow.
- An echo, a fake and a god would all answer two-three-five-seven the same way. So send a count the Order never sends: **eleven**, the forbidden number.
- If the Order fakes the answers, eleven gets nothing, because they won't know it was sent.
- If eleven comes back, it's an echo.
- If something else comes back, someone out there is thinking.
- Why eleven, for Nell: all her life she went past seven to eleven, and Wells shushed her. She doesn't say this aloud; the audience may feel it.

**Act 4: The haul.**
- At night Nell, Tau, Owen and three Changers slip into the Temple. The Great Stone takes all their weight. They haul eleven times, a minute a haul.
- A Warden patrol nearly finds them, and they hide under the plinth.
- Theta watches the needles and logs. Done. They wait. Nothing.
- Theta: "See? Nothing. Nobody out there but priests."

**The twist.** The heretics' creed is a question none of them has ever asked of anything. The first real test in the cellar's history is made by the one person there who doesn't believe in the creed.

**The ending hook.** 3 a.m. Theta asleep in her chair, Nell alone with the needle. It trembles. She counts: eleven... twelve... thirteen. It stops.

**Gaps.**
- **Opened.** Who answered?
- **Closed.** What the Changers are: a second church, not the questioners.

### EPISODE 4: "Thirteen" (MAJOR REVERSAL 1)
*Nell has proof someone is listening, and the city turns her proof into a reason to die.*

**Cold open.**
- Dawn. The day Wardens bring the First Accountant the needle's paper drum, which records every tremble: "Thirteen, from nowhere." The Temple guard swears nobody in Ream touched the Stone.
- Dimopoulos kneels on the cold floor. "A new number."

**Act 1: Proof.**
- Nell and Tau work it through. The Order didn't know about the eleven, so it could not have faked an answer. Theta's conspiracy dies.
- Someone outside is thinking, and thirteen is the next prime after eleven: the twins' game. Nell doesn't dare say "Wells". Tau: "Or the Ancestors." Nell: "The dead don't count."
- Theta won't accept it: "They answered you to trap you." To protect Tau, she tore the eleven off the drum before she fell asleep. The thirteen came after, and the day Wardens found it.

**Act 2: The Order reads it.**
- The Order sees thirteen with no eleven before it: a new number from the Ancestors.
- Dimopoulos: "They ask for thirteen." The Randall neither confirms nor denies.
- A **Joining** is proclaimed: thirteen elders to be Given early, alive. Volunteers come forward with joy. Nan is among them.

**Act 3: Nan.**
- Nell can't stop her without confessing. In the end she half-confesses: "Nan, it was me. I sent the eleven."
- Nan laughs and strokes her face: "Then the Ancestors spoke through you, love. Isn't that something." The doctrine absorbs even a confession.
- Hester says nothing. Nan tells Lottie her last Pariah tale.
- The Joining: thirteen elders in white, holding no hands, step into the Lift and diffuse. The needles rise and stay. "Favour."
- Nell writes GIVEN thirteen times.

**Act 4: The ledger.**
- Furious, Nell takes her curve to the Randall at the vigil, by the Tallykeeper's old right to speak.
- The Randall listens, then opens her private ledger. It holds the same curve, kept for forty years and more complete than Nell's.
- "Twenty-four in the hundred. When it is fifty, the coin is fair, and none of us will ever be parted again. Your brother is waiting." She glows.
- The one person who has all the numbers reads them as good news.

**The twist.** Evidence with no explanation only feeds the old story. Nell's test has added thirteen bodies to the Weight, and the numbers are the Randall's gospel.

**The ending hook.** That night, a new bundle of bread on Nell's desk, and the Randall sitting beside it in the dark. "Eat. And close the Tally."

**Gaps.**
- **Closed.**
  - Who answered? Someone thinking, outside. Who, exactly, stays open.
  - Who feeds Nell? The Randall.
  - Does the Order know the numbers? It does, and welcomes them.
- **Opened.**
  - Who is the thinker?
  - Why would the Randall protect a liar?

### EPISODE 5: "Custody by Pantry"
*Her food now costs silence, while a test with paper shows the city's own Fast is killing children.*

**Cold open.**
- The Randall's sealed glasshouse behind the Temple: wrong-handed mint, wheat and beans. Brother Arkani tends them, and every scrap of waste is burned in a brazier.
- The Randall eats here, alone, as she always has. Nell eats real food for the first time in weeks and cries.

**Act 1: Terms.**
- The terms: eat here, close the Tally, say nothing, and one day be Randall.
- Nell watches the Randall eat the same food. "You're..." "Hush." The Randall is Turned.
- Every Randall for generations has been: "The table is how we are kept. And how we keep."
- In public, Nell closes her curve and stops asking.

**Act 2: Paper is a witness.**
- In the Lift's maintenance hours, Owen, Tau and Nell secretly send crates of cards printed with an R.

**The twist.** The Randall is Turned. She was a Changer. Burnt Row was her own body.

**The ending hook.**

**Gaps.**

### EPISODE 6: "The First Leaf" (MAJOR REVERSAL 2, part one)
*To read the holy relic, the city needs a Turned girl's eyes and a Turned girl's hand.*

**Cold open.**

**Act 1: Why, and how.**

- **Why now.** Theta says ABBOTT is an Order trap. The Order would call it a Calling. Nell needs to know what the book really says.
- **How.** "Send a hand, not a body" is a Plot line. In the maintenance hours Owen sets the field on Nell's arm alone. Her unpinned hand passes into solid stone and lifts the light relic out. The block never moves. The Order's wall of weight was the wrong defence, because the relic itself is light.
- **The danger.** If the field collapses while her hand is still in the dark, the hand scatters.

**Act 2: Power.**

**Act 3: The text.**

  - amber: *Stipulated: the film's own axiom, the place where the science runs out and the story takes over, announced rather than smuggled.*
  - grey: *Derived.*

**The twist.** The law that exiled Wells was a moment in a story. The audience, who learned it as law in episode 1, made the city's mistake.

**The ending hook.**

**Gaps.**

## 6. Twist schedule

- **W**: a world or cosmology reveal;

## 9. The ending, and what it settles

### What is settled
- Wells is home and fed. Nell is fed, in the world where her body fits.
- The cause is named by the people themselves and repaired:
  - The Giving has stopped, and the dead are buried in the Old Graves again.
  - The door has moved.
  - The dash has become a number with a prediction beside it that could be wrong.
  - The Tithe of children is replaced by crates of cards.
  - The Passing is over as exile, and crossing is now a choice.
  - The Turned can live openly on the glasshouse crops.
- The Randall's past, the founders, the dots and what "Refused" meant are all known.
- **How the normal has moved.**
  - From "The rate is the Ancestors'" to UNTIL WE KNOW BETTER.
  - From a Randall who recites scripture to a Randall who reads the rate aloud in the square every morning.
  - From a city that hides its questions to a nine-year-old who chalks one on the public board.

### What it cost
- **The dead.**
  - Mira.
  - Nan and the twelve others Joined with her.
  - The eleven killed in the crush.
  - Every Straight who starved.
  - Three centuries of the Called, the Passed and the Given, who hang in the dark beside the old plinth for as long as there is a Ream.
- **Places and work.**
  - The Temple can never be used again. In the empty hall the needle still reads fifteen hundred tonnes.
  - Hester's house is half burned, and her life's work is nailed up as WRONG.
- **People.**
  - The twins live in different worlds.
  - The Randall loses her office. She gives up her one chance to go to her brother.
  - Theta loses Tau's trust.

### The residue
- Wells's sneeze, and "THE GREY?" on the board. The new answer's first untested change has already come home, carried by the boy it saved.
- Minthome's small Weight at the Mother. The same doom has begun on the other side.
- Whether Ream will keep counting once the fear fades.

### The theme, as the world's answer
The world asks what becomes of a people who only do what used to work. The season answers:
- What works changes the world, and the rules cannot see the change.
- A right person alone fails. Kit shouted the answer. Young Iris doubted without testing. Nell told the truth with no test.
- What saves Ream is a practice owned by many: cards instead of children, a board with a prediction that can be wrong, and a child allowed to ask "why four?"
- That practice has to keep going, because the world is already moving again.

### The hook for Season 2
- Nell, in Minthome, steps into the path of a Giving and says "Wait."
- Season 2 is set on the other side. Nell is the stranger who can read both hands' writing. She knows that an answer carried in from outside is only something someone says, so she has to help Minthome build its own.
- Beyond the fence lies Earth.

## 10. Rules the physics must never break

1. **Only weight crosses between worlds.**
   - No light, no sound, no touch, no voices.
   - Every message is mass moved by someone. It is slow: one haul of the Great Stone takes about a minute, and 89 hauls take a night.
   - A stone is felt only by needles at the same spot on the other world. The Temple talks to the Landing.
   - The channel proves that mass moved. It never proves who moved it, or why.
2. **A crossing is short and set by weight.**
   - Seconds ≈ 700 ÷ kilograms.
   - Lighter lasts longer.
   - The crossing ends when the time runs out. Whatever is still in the dark then stays there, for good.
3. **Nobody can feel, see or steer a turn.**
   - A turn is fixed when the time runs out, and found only by a sample that never crossed: the Jar's mint, a printed card, a seed.
   - No amount of looking at a person decides who turned.
4. **Walls don't stop a crosser. The weight budget does.**
   - An unpinned hand passes through stone, skin or a lock, and can lift anything light enough.
   - Heavy things are safe only because they are heavy.
5. **In the dark a crosser sees only by their own bubble's light:** the insides of everything within arm's reach. Beyond that, nothing. The Weight can never be seen, only weighed.
6. **A Turned body:**
   - can't use Ream's food or medicine;
   - catches no Ream disease;
   - lives on water, spirits and tallow for months, not years;
   - lives long only on turned crops;
   - its waste breeds grey mould that must be burned.
7. **Pull across the gap fades with the cube of distance.** Near matters enormously; far hardly at all.
8. **The Lost stay where they were lost, fully heavy.** They drift only slowly. They do not think, speak, choose or answer.
9. **A handhold changes nothing.** Two crossers turn or don't turn independently.
10. **Doors at the same place on neighbouring worlds line up.** A crossing from the Mother comes out at the Old Graves, and the reverse.
11. **Anything a crosser carries crosses:** air, food, germs. A person from Minthome can carry Earth life, which is mirror-life to Ream, and the reverse.
12. **Base drift is about 2 in 100.** Every rise above that must be caused by weight near the door, as the ledger says. Never by luck, mood, prayer or purity.
13. **No new rule is added in secret.** A new rule enters this plan's ledger first, and the show declares it once on screen.

## 11. Notes for episode writers

- **Every scene does at least two named jobs from this plan:** a plant, a turn, a bond, a gap opened or closed, a world ripple. A scene that survives being swapped for any other scene is decoration. Keep it only if it is short, deliberate surplus that makes the world real.
- **The theme is never spoken.**
  - Test each line: if deleting it leaves the audience still understanding the point from what happened, delete it.
  - Only the Fable's lines may state an idea outright, and only as quotation, by a character who is reciting.
- **The doom test.** For any disaster, ask which unquestioned practice made it more likely. If the answer is bad luck or one bad person, rewrite.
- **The Randall test.** In every episode she appears in, she must be right about something, or kind to someone who can't help her.
- **The Wells rule.** Wells is never a prize. From episode 7 on, every episode gives him at least one choice that changes the plot.
- **The ratchet.** Each episode ends higher than it began, with at least one partial release: a laugh, a meal, the dog lying down. Never two twists of the same kind in a row (see the key in section 6).
- **The visual rule.**
  - Ream's writing is always mirror-reversed on screen.
  - Only the plates, the Jar's base, the First Leaf, Minthome's signs and Nell's hand (from the last minute of episode 1) read our way.
  - Nell uses a mirror to read Ream's books from episode 2.
