# Judgement of the three season pitches

## How they were judged

Each pitch was measured against four things:

1. **The owner's ask.** Is it mind-bending? Does it twist and turn? Does it carry the message that a culture which never questions dooms its people to suffering and death?
2. **The owner's four story theories.**
   - Gap: are mysteries and wonders promised honestly, and does the teller play fair?
   - Implied World: are there few departures with deep ripples, surplus detail, and a world with its own business?
   - Anticipation: care, threat, uncertainty and time; lost refuges; a broken contract; the ratchet; residue.
   - Bond: care built over time, a villain we care about, no fridging, surprise that stays in character.
3. **The hard-to-vary test.**
   - Does each twist and major choice do jobs that a swap would break?
   - Is the theme carried by a mechanism rather than a speech?
   - Is the doom caused by not questioning, rather than by luck or a lone villain?
4. **Use of the two attachments.** The owner asked for a story made "out of" them. A pitch that uses them only as background physics has thrown away part of the brief.

Nothing below is scored or counted. One missing load-bearing part can sink a pitch that has everything else.

## Pitch A: CARAWAY (the seed, tightened)

### Strengths

- **The doom engine is written by love.** The Giving (funerals held in the Lift) builds the weight. The Fast, the Joining and the Great Tithe are each a reasonable answer inside the old belief, and each adds either weight or time spent beside it. No villain is needed. This is the clearest human version of the Semantics' "re-tuning makes it worse; the failure is structural".
- **Eleven, then thirteen, then the Joining.** This is the best single sequence in any of the three pitches.
  - Eleven is the only number that is both forbidden to the Order and native to the twins.
  - Thirteen proves a mind, because an echo would have sent eleven back.
  - The city then turns the proof into thirteen deaths.
  - Together these show that evidence without an explanation feeds the old story. Swap any part and the chain breaks.
- **The First Leaf can be read properly only by Turned eyes.** This ties the mirror-script visual rule to the reveal of the lost tags. The holiest (amber) verses turn out to be the film's guesses. The audience, taught the Leaves as physics in episode 1, has made the city's mistake.
- **Move the door.** The cure is the cube law from Ream's own scripture. The clean site is found in Ream's own records: Pariahs appear at the Old Graves, Year 9 has forty days with no crossings, and the plates are stamped "1 of 212". It takes a hundred pairs of hands to carry out. The repair needs a culture, not one hero.
- **Kit's sentence, "Move the door!"** A right answer passed on without its reasons is exactly what the Semantics calls a declared claim. The Randall has carried it for forty years without knowing why.
- **A public board with a prediction and "IF MORE, I AM WRONG".** This is the practice of questioning made visible and concrete.
- **"Minthome was Ream" and "Minthome Gives too."** A copy keeps its source's history, which earns the word "always" across three generations: Earth, then Ream, then Minthome.
- **The Randall is right about something.** Burnt Row is real, and a truth told with no plan does kill (the crush).

### Flaws

- **The episode 1 hook spends the season's biggest trap.** Showing Wells alive in Minthome at once removes the audience's own untested rule, "exile means death". That trap is the best way to make the viewer commit the culture's error a second time. It also flattens the later break into Minthome.
- **The Passing was never right.** In A, exile is a coin toss from the start, so that rule was never "fitted to past conditions" and then overtaken by events. Only the Giving carries the "it worked once" pattern. That is half the theme's mechanism.
- **The Randall worked out the rate once and never again.** This makes the doom partly a lack of data. The theme is stronger if she has the numbers and her faith swallows them.
- **"WELLS. ALIVE. STARVING." proves too little.** Anyone who knows the Leaves could send those words, so the message cannot prove who sent it.
- **Why the Randall feeds Nell is thinly held.** Why does she grow wrong-handed crops, and why would she protect a liar? A needs a reason that belongs to how the system works, not only to her private history.
- **Attention budget.** Thirteen twists, and episodes 8–9 carry the clean-door deduction, a confession, a heist and a cosmological reveal all at once. The load needs spreading.

### Hard-to-vary verdict

**Mostly held.** Its best parts can't be swapped: eleven and thirteen, the cube law and the door, the plates, the Graves. Its loose parts are the episode 1 hook, the Randall's reasons, and the proof that the sender is Wells. Each can be tightened by grafts from B.

## Pitch B: CARAWAY (the rule was right once)

### Strengths

- **"The rule was right once."** For two centuries the Passing really delivered the Turned to where the mint tastes right. Then the city's own dead, piling up where no one could see them, turned it into murder. This is the Semantics' central pattern with nothing added: a selected rule is faithful on the history it met and fails on a change outside it. It also earns "always", because every rule is right until the world moves.
- **A reading that rises and falls, or rises and stays.** The same needle trace that shamed the family ("Refused") later proves Wells alive. The reading never changed; only the explanation did. This is "a reading is not the thing read" in a form an ordinary viewer can feel.
- **The Randall already has the curve and reads it as prophecy.** Fifty in a hundred is the Ending, when "none will be parted". Data does not break a faith; it feeds it. Only a new question can.
- **The Changers are a second church.** They hold an untested belief (the Order fakes the Answer) and move the goalposts when evidence goes against them. Nobody starts out as "the questioners".
- **Two cities keep each other certain.** Minthome keeps answering the chant long after arrivals stopped, and Ream reads the Answer as proof that the Passed arrived.
- **Ninety-one.** Only a mistake can prove identity, because any stranger would get the next prime right. The mistake was planted as a joke in the one scene where the twins held hands. This is very hard to vary.
- **The audience's three traps.**
  1. The Leaves taught as physics.
  2. Genre cynicism ("exile is murder").
  3. Surplus detail taken as mere texture.

  This is the most mind-bending design of the three, and it meets the brief's meta device exactly.
- **One right person is not enough.** Pim, a believer, reruns the test to catch Nell out and is converted by his own numbers.

### Fatal flaws

- **It breaks the physics it inherits.** One adult in five is secretly Turned and has lived for decades on tallow and spirit. The source says a mirrored body starves in about six months ("Murder by mirror takes six months... and looks like a diet"). Episode 9's great culture-turning scene ("Caraway. Who else?") rests on that impossible fact. As written, it cannot stand.
- **Twist monotony.** Four reveals are the same kind of twist: Plattner, the Randall, Marta and one adult in five are all secretly Turned. By episode 8 the audience has learned the pattern, and the ratchet rule says the pattern must break before that happens.
- **The finale is built for itself.**
  - Carrying the city walls to balance the dead needs a special stipulation (a balanced pull cancels) that exists only to make the ending work.
  - "The weight is the cure" is clever, but it muddies the theme for an ordinary viewer.
  - The Lantern, a portable weak field, invites plot holes: why not cross anywhere?

### Hard-to-vary verdict

**Held at its core**: the rule that was right once, the rising and falling needle, the prophecy ledger and 91. **Loose or broken at its edges**: the hidden Thin, the counterweight walls and the Lanterns.

## Pitch C: THE KEPT

### Strengths

- **A plague bred by mercy.** The Keeping House, the common bowl and the mint fed by the colony's own night soil breed the disease. Immunity was a fact about history ("no germ had their hand yet"), never a law.
- **"Questions kill" comes from a real disaster, the Schism of Year 71.** The Holder's certainty is mercy with a true lesson behind it.
- **The heroine is the carrier.** The theme is turned on the hero. The Askers must question the woman who taught them to ask, and one right person is visibly not enough.
- **The creed carved over the Hall door.** Each failure adds a smaller exception beneath it. It is goalpost-moving you can photograph.
- **Children run the first experiment.** East families eat West mint. It is a test anyone can watch, and it beats a truth carried in from outside.
- **An outside mirror.** Station Glass lives by "Observe. Never touch. Never tell." It is the Holder's certainty in another uniform.
- **Caleb Rook's line.** "We didn't lose because we asked. We lost because we answered before we'd looked."

### Fatal flaws

- **It spends little of the two attachments.** The Fourth Direction becomes background physics. It is not scripture: no lost tags, no "In the plot" read as law, no Tally with its dash, no 2-3-5-7, no drift slider. The Semantics appears only as theme. The owner asked for a story made out of both documents, and the brief's meta device of the viewer learning the Leaves as physics is gone.
- **Familiar twists.**
  - "The village is the strange one, and the outside world is normal and watching" recalls *The Village* and *The Truman Show*.
  - "The healer's hands carry the disease" is Semmelweis.

  Genre-savvy viewers will get there early. It is less mind-bending than the Ream pitches.
- **The mirror-printed camera is a teller's cheat.** The show prints Holdfast flipped for three and a half episodes and later uses outside points of view freely. The gap theory calls hiding what the teller knows cheating unless the hiding is part of who the teller is.
- **The crossing physics does little work.** Swap the Door for any other way of looking inside the sick and most of the plague plot survives. The two attachments are less held in place here than in A or B.

### Hard-to-vary verdict

**Its theme mechanism is superbly held.** Its use of the source material is loose.

## Decision

**Base: Pitch A.** It is the owner's seed, it uses both attachments most deeply, and its repair is held in place by the world's own scripture and records: the cube law, the Graves, the plates.

**Grafts from B:**
1. **The Passing was right once.** Needle traces that rose and fell were written ARRIVED for two centuries. When Passings began to stay, the labels were re-inked as RECEIVED and REFUSED.
2. **The Randall keeps the curve and reads it as prophecy.** Fifty in a hundred is the Ending.
3. **The Randall's office passes from hidden Turned to hidden Turned.** Her private table is the only food a Turned person can get, so every heir owes her life to silence. This is silence built into the system rather than chosen by one person, and it gives the glasshouse its reason.
4. **Wells stays off-screen until episode 7**, which keeps the genre-cynicism trap alive. When he appears, it breaks the show's perspective contract.
5. **Ninety-one proves the sender is Wells**, used after a message (ABBOTT, spelled from the Leaves) that only proves someone knows her family and the book.
6. **Minthome's Answering and Ream's chant confirm each other.**
7. **Pim reruns the test to catch Nell out and is converted by his own numbers.**
8. **Wells's "two pulls" and Nell's dismissal of them with scripture.**
9. **The dog growls at the Randall.**
10. **"A failed answer stays failed"** against the hardliners' narrowing.
11. **The sneeze and "The grey?"** as residue.

**Grafts from C:**
1. **The Randall's wound is a failed act of questioning.** As a young Changer she threw out an unexplained rule ("burn what leaves your body") without testing it. Her own Turned body bred Burnt Row. She is right that questioning can kill, and wrong about why: she answered before she had looked.
2. **The Order's glosses are carved in shrinking letters** beneath the verses on the Lift steps.
3. **A child Asker finds the flaw in the heroine's answer.**
4. **UNTIL WE KNOW BETTER.**
5. **Minthome as the outside mirror**, with its own unquestioned rules: the fence that nobody passes, and the Mother used only for the dead.
6. **The heroine's own body is a hazard** (the grey on the privy stones), a scaled-down version of "the heroine is the carrier".

**Dropped:**
- B's one-in-five Thin, which breaks the physics.
- B's counterweight walls and Lanterns, which are stipulations bought only for the finale.
- C's world. Its lessons are kept; its setting is not.
- A's episode 1 Minthome hook, which spends the trap.
- A's leaf-line-word message used as proof of identity. Leaf-line-word is kept as the channel for words; 91 does the proving.
