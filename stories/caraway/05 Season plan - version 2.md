# CARAWAY: Season One master plan (revised)

*Ten episodes, about 55 minutes each, serialized drama. This is the one document every episode writer follows. Where it and an episode draft disagree, it wins. If a draft needs to change it, the change is made here first and recorded in the ledger (section 2).*

**Built from:** Pitch A (base), with grafts from Pitch B and Pitch C. The reasons are in `20-judgement.md`.

## 0. The show in brief

**Title.** CARAWAY

**Logline.** A starving seventeen-year-old lied about a taste, and her twin brother was exiled in her place. Now she finds that her walled city's most loving rites are what is turning and killing its children. Her brother can come home only if a people who have not asked "why" in three hundred years learn how to.

**The spine in one line.** Every rule in Ream was right once. The city's own love (its funerals) quietly changed the world those rules were made for, and a people who never ask "what changes when things are changed?" can only answer with more of the same.

**The core message, carried by the story rather than spoken.** A culture that never questions keeps doing what used to work, and what used to work changes the world it works on. Sooner or later the world always moves outside what your rules were made for. The only defence is a people who keep asking, even of the answers that saved them.

**Reversals.**
- **Major reversal 1**, episode 4: proof turned into a reason to die.
- **Major reversal 2**, episodes 6 and 7: the holiest verses were a storyteller's guesses, and the Tithe was the founders' experiment with its readout thrown away; exile was a cure once; and the Passed that the city "received" with joy are hanging in the dark.
- **Major reversal 3**, episode 10: the heroine's own prediction fails in public and she says so; the promised land runs the same rite; and the man who shouted the truth for forty-seven years turns out to have kept his own blank line.

**Tone.** Warm, strange, domestic, frightening. Weddings, bread, debts and dogs, in a city that feeds its dead to a machine. Nobody lectures. Characters discover things by doing: counting, sending, weighing, tasting.

## 1. The world

### 1.1 The normal

**Ream.** A walled hill city of about twelve thousand people in Year 300 of its own calendar.
- It has markets, weddings, debts, a bank, a school, chandlers, and a guild feud about the price of concrete.
- At its heart, inside the Temple of Leaves, stands **the Lift**: 212 bronze plates in a ring, humming. For a few seconds it lifts a body "off the world" into the black between worlds. When the Sundrum closes the field, the ring sets back down whatever is still within its reach.
- Beside the Lift stand the **needles**: tall brass gravity meters in glass cases. Needle-Wardens read them day and night, and a paper drum records every tremble.

**What people take for granted.**
- At seventeen, everyone crosses once. Nobody is grown until they have.
- A leaf of mint tells you whether you came back right.
- The Turned are sent to a better world.
- The dead are Given to the Lift, and they stay with us: you can weigh them.
- The Weight of the Ancestors rises every year, and that means they love us.

### 1.2 The departures and their ripples

There are only four departures from our world. Every other difference in Ream follows from them.

**Departure 1: The Lift.** A machine that unpins a body from its world for a few seconds.
- The lighter you are, the longer you last. The Accountants' rule of thumb: seconds = 700 ÷ kilograms. At 70 kg you get 10 seconds; at 50 kg, 14; a crate of 5 kg gets over two minutes.
- The ring brings back only what is within its reach when the field is closed. The Accountants set each crosser's count from their weight, and the Sundrum closes the field when the count ends; a pair is closed on the heavier crosser's count, so they come home together. The rite demands the full count. A field that is left to run out brings nothing back.
- Ripples:
  - Walls mean nothing to a crosser, so Ream keeps its treasures heavy. The bank stores gold as dust set in concrete, and the lock-makers' guild calls itself the Weight-makers.
  - Crossers train blindfolded, floating in the Cistern, because there is no up in the dark.
  - In the dark a crosser sees only by the light trapped in their own bubble. Within arm's reach, every inside is laid open like a drawing: a heart beating behind ribs, a letter in a sealed envelope. Beyond that, black.

**Departure 2: Turning.** Nobody can feel or steer a turn in the dark. Sometimes a crosser comes back as their own mirror image.
- Each separate thing takes its own chance. A living body turns whole, with every living thing inside it and on its skin. But a card in a crate, a seed in a hem or a phone in a fist turns or doesn't on its own.
- A Turned body is healthy, but no food in Ream fits it.
- The Turned can live only on water, spirits and tallow, and only for months.
- They catch no disease.
- The germs in their gut are mirror-life to everyone else. Their waste breeds a **grey mould** that nothing in Ream can eat.
- The only test is chemistry: a leaf of mint from the **Sealed Jar**. The Jar itself is old glass the founders brought; its leaves are Ream's own garden mint, picked each spring and never taken through the Lift. A Turned tongue tastes caraway.
- The only cure is another crossing, with the same blind odds.

**Departure 3: Weight crosses; nothing else does.** Light, sound and touch stay on their own world. Only weight is felt across the gap, and it fades very fast with distance.
- The needles can feel a body in the dark beside the Lift, and a heavy stone swung on the neighbouring world, but only one standing almost exactly "overhead", at the same spot on that world.
- That makes the Great Stone a telephone nobody knows is one: a twenty-tonne block hung over the Temple floor and hauled in the holy counts.

**Departure 4: The neighbouring world's life is the other hand.** For a Turned person, the world next door is where the food fits. Ream calls it **Minthome**, "where the mint tastes right".

### 1.3 Scripture and liturgy

**The Leaves.** Ream's holy book is a hand-copied transcript of a single phone web page called "The Fourth Direction: seven laws for a stacked world". Nobody in Ream knows that.

It has three parts:
- **the Seven Laws**;
- **the Theorems**;
- **the Plot**, the "In the plot" examples, read as case law and prophecy.

Every verse carries a small coloured dot. The original page had coloured tags reading *Established*, *Stipulated* or *Derived*. The copyists did not lose the words by accident. They shortened them, and then dropped them. An old copy in the Tally archive still shows "St." beside some verses, with a copyist's note in the margin: *"Words of doubt are unfit for holy script."* The colours stayed. The Order calls them **the Three Voices**:
- **Green**, the Voice of the World.
- **Amber**, the Voice of the Ending. These are the holiest verses; in the original they are *Stipulated*, the film's guesses.
- **Grey**, the Voice that Follows.
- The Plot's verses carry no dot at all. The Order calls them **the Silent Voice**: not law, but what *will* happen.

**The First Leaf** is the original: a dead glass tablet (a phone) sealed in a block of stone at the Lift's foot.

**Fossils of the phone page, now liturgy.** Episode writers use these as everyday speech. How they became liturgy is shown in episode 9: they began as the founders' jokes on their first day.

**The catechism game.** Children quiz each other: "Leaf four, line three, word six?" "*Mint!*" This is the code the two worlds will use (episodes 5, 7, 8, 9 and 10). Each word is sent as three counts of the stone: leaf, line, word. So it can send only words that appear in the Leaves.

**The Glosses.** The Order's official readings are carved on the Lift's steps beneath the verses. Each time the world disagrees with a verse, a new gloss is carved in smaller letters. Viewers should be able to see the goalposts moving. The glosses the season uses, largest to smallest:
- "The mint alone is witness." Beneath the handhold verse; carved generations ago.
- "Go toward the Ancestors; they will carry you." Year 235.
- "Weight keeps the holy." At the First Leaf's block.
- "The Ancestors weight the coin as they will." Episode 9; almost too small to read.

**The forbidden book: the Fable.** A second text, the founders' own: a dense theory of what an explanation is, printed on Earth paper that reads our way. Ream's moulds cannot eat that paper, which is why it has lasted three hundred years.
- For two and a half centuries it was an open book that few read. After Burnt Row (Year 255) it was sealed as heresy, because the young reader who threw out the burn rule had been reading it.
- Its secret readers, the **Changers**, read it with mirrors, take Greek letters as names (Theta, Tau, Lambda, Sigma, Pi) and chant its lines like psalms. Their creed is **"What changes when things are changed?"** None of them has ever changed anything to find out.
- Lines a character may quote aloud, and only as quotation: "A failed answer stays failed." "A copy keeps its source's history." (It means a copy is not a second witness.) "Closing a question is a choice." "A reading is not the thing read." "Nothing is settled."
- The show never puts the Fable's symbols or formulas in any character's mouth, except as a visual on a torn page.

### 1.4 The rites

- **The Tithe.** Every spring, each seventeen-year-old crosses once. They go in pairs and may not touch. Each taps their right shoulder first, and each carries a seed sewn into the hem "for the Ancestors' garden"; the Order's gardener collects the seeds afterwards.
  - The Tithe is the gate to adulthood: no Tithe, no marriage, trade or inheritance. Those who have not crossed are **the unweighed**. A child hidden from the Tithe is found and taken to cross under guard.
  - Its original purpose has been forgotten. It was the founders' safety test: one crossing from every young person, so that the rate of turning would always be known.
- **The Mint Test.** On return, each crosser tastes a leaf from the Sealed Jar. The Randall unscrews the lid "against the sun": the Jar is Earth glass, and its thread runs the other way to every thread in Ream. Mint means clean. Caraway means Turned. The gloss beneath the handhold verse forbids any other test: "The mint alone is witness."
- **The Passing, also called the Ending.** The Turned are garlanded, sung to, and sent that night on a long crossing to Minthome. The field is held open long enough for them to walk out of the ring's reach. The glosses tell them to "go toward the Ancestors; they will carry you". It is a joyful rite.
  - A trace that rises and stays is written RECEIVED: taken into the Ancestors, a blessing.
  - A trace that rises and falls is written REFUSED: sent back to walk as a Pariah, a shame on the family.
- **The Giving.** The dead are laid in the Lift and its time is let run out; it is the one rite in which the field is left to run out. They "do not die so much as diffuse". The needles show them staying. The Temple gates are barred "until the needle settles". Since Year 131 every citizen of Ream has been Given.
- **The Joining.** An elder who hears the Ancestors call may be Given early, alive. It is rare and honoured.
- **The Answering.** On holy days the congregation hauls the Great Stone: two hauls, three, five, seven. Within the hour the needles tremble back: two, three, five, seven. The Order reads this as the Ancestors' reply.
- **Pariahs.** In Nan's bedtime tales, Pariahs walk out of the Old Graves by the river at night, begging to be let in. The law, carved in Year 235, says they are the Refused, sent back: Pass them on sight, unheard, so that they may go to the Ancestors properly.
- **Spelled names.** Lore says that when the Ancestors spell a name on the needles, that one is Called. It has happened once in living memory (Year 254; see 1.9).

### 1.5 Names and offices

Families are named from the Leaves' list of sources: Abbott, Wells, Carroll, Plattner, Ehrenfest, Dvali, Adamala, Dimopoulos, Arkani, and one poor clan called **Others** (from "K. Adamala and others"). Nobody finds any of this odd.

- **The Randall.** High keeper of the Leaves. Holds one of the two Lift keys. Eats alone, "as the Leaves command". Wears a white braided stole ending in a small silver tooth. *(The stole is the phone's charging cable.)* Keeps the Randalls' private book, passed down with the office.
- **The Sundrum.** Keeper of the Lift. Holds the second key and closes the field. Each year recites the Litany of Plates, a numbered chant of all 212 plates. *(It is the founders' assembly order.)*
- **Accountants.** Weigh everyone, set every crossing's count, and write the fasting tables. They are led by the First Accountant.
- **Needle-Wardens.** Tend the needles and the paper drums, and keep the **Weight-book**, the needle trace of every Passing and Giving since Year 1. The Weight-book is for Wardens only.
- **Tallykeepers.** Keep the Tally. Apprentices climb the Tally tower's 211 steps. *(Pure surplus: a prime number nobody remarks on.)*
- **The Order's gardener.** Keeps the Randall's glasshouse and collects the Tithe seeds.
- **The Changers.** Secret readers of the Fable.

**Words the show uses.**
- **Called:** Tithe crossers who never came back.
- **Passed:** the exiled Turned.
- **Given:** the dead.
- **Joined:** those Given early, alive.
- **the Weight:** all of the above, felt on the needles.
- **Pariah:** anyone who comes out of the dark uninvited.
- **the unweighed:** those who have not crossed.
- **Straight:** Minthome's word for an arrival who tastes caraway there.

### 1.6 Everyday surplus

These details exist to make the world real. Most have no plot job, and writers should drop them in passing, never explain them.
- Gold dust in concrete; the Weight-makers' feud with the bank over concrete prices.
- Knots are distrusted: crossers tie nothing ("knots come undone").
- Mirrors are contraband ("a mirror is a small Turning").
- The children's **prime race**: one says a prime, the other must say the next.
- Chandlers sell "Passing candles"; tallow is cheap and faintly holy.
- The 211 steps; the benediction about animations.
- The Called's names chalked on the Lift steps by their families and washed off by the Wardens each week. Tau chalks MIRA back every week.
- Market scales at every stall, because everyone knows their weight.
- Legal papers kept in pairs ("paper is a witness").
- The Order's **knower-dogs**, bred in the Temple kennels, walk the line at every rite. None has growled at anyone in living memory.
- **The plates that face home.** Four of the 212 plates have their numbers stamped the other way round, Ream's way. The Litany names them, and the Sundrum kisses each one.
- The Tithe seed sewn into every crosser's hem.
- The Temple gates barred "until the needle settles" at every Giving.
- The Old Graves flood every few springs.

A few surplus details later turn out to be clues (marked in the twist table): the Randall eating alone, the stole, the Litany, the Graves, the dash, the dog's growl, the knower-dogs' silence, the Jar's thread, the plates that face home, the barred gates.

### 1.7 The visual rule: the audience's eyes

Everything written in Ream appears on screen as **mirror-image English**. The audience files it as fantasy strangeness.

Some things read the way we read:
1. **Things made on Earth:** the numbers stamped on the Lift's plates (which Reamers call "the Ancestors' hand"), except the four that face home; the Sealed Jar's embossed glass base; the First Leaf's screen; the Fable's printed pages; the signs in Minthome.
2. **The hand of a Turned person writing naturally:** Nell's, from the last minute of episode 1; and the Randall's, in private only (her ledger). In public both of them fake Ream's script, slowly.
3. **Nell's public board in episode 10**, which she writes twice, once in each hand.

The founders' own handwriting (the first Tally page, *Minthome, Year One*) reads Ream's way, because the founders were Turned.

This is not a camera trick; it is the plain truth, planted from minute one.
- The founders of Ream were Turned people from Earth, so their descendants are the mirror of us.
- Reamers read their script as normal and see Earth's print as backwards.
- The audience has Earth eyes. We have been looking at Ream the way a Turned person would, all season.
- When Nell turns, she sees Ream exactly as we always have.

An attentive viewer may guess the outline by episode 7, when Minthome's signs read plainly. The show owns that: the first readable sign is the audience's own moment. Episode 9 then adds the layer nobody could guess (*Day One*).

### 1.8 Why nobody questions

Writers must keep all six reasons alive. They are what makes the silence believable rather than stupid.

1. **The rites worked.**
   - For two centuries the Passing really did deliver the Turned where the mint tastes right: the needle rose and fell, and the Answer came back.
   - The Giving began in a plague year and the plague passed.
   - "It works" was true when it was learned.
2. **The book sounds equally sure everywhere, and that was chosen.** Generations of copyists cut the words of doubt from the tags, one hand at a time, because they seemed unfit for holy script. A film's guess and a law of nature now sound equally holy.
3. **Every bad sign has a home in the doctrine.**
   - A rising Weight means favour.
   - A child lost in the dark was Called.
   - A trace that falls back means Refused: sent back to walk as a Pariah.
   - A new number from the needles is a new command.
   - A rising Turning rate is the Ending coming, which is good news.
   - A belief that swallows every outcome can never be caught out.
4. **The people who know most can least afford to ask.**
   - The Randall's office has passed from hidden Turned to hidden Turned for generations. Her private table is the only place a Turned person can eat. Every Randall picks as heir a liar she caught, and every heir owes her life to silence.
   - A hidden Turned Randall carved "The mint alone is witness" long ago. It closes every test a liar could not pass: a signature, a sheet of paper, a dog.
   - The knower-dogs are bred beside the Randalls. They learned the Turned smell as pups, as the smell of home, and they growl at nothing. Only a dog raised outside the Temple still knows.
5. **Memory and law say questioning kills.** Forty-five years ago a young reader of the Fable mocked an unexplained rule ("burn what leaves your body") and simply stopped keeping it. A grey mould nothing would eat spread through Carroll Row. The street was burned and seven people died. The Fable was sealed that year. Burnt Row is still black. The lesson Ream drew is "don't question". The true lesson, which the season earns, is "don't answer before you've looked".
6. **Not crossing is not a choice anyone can make.** The Tithe is the gate to adulthood; the unweighed cannot marry, trade or inherit. A hidden child is taken to cross under guard, and any Calling that follows is read as the Ancestors' anger at the hiding. The one act that would stop the dying, not sending the children, is the one the faith cannot perform, because the rite *is* the religion.

### 1.9 Ream's history (reserve: for writers, never dumped on screen)

- **Before Year 1.** On Earth, a quarantine station (CHIRAL HOLD) held people mirrored by crossing accidents, fenced off and fed on synthetic rations. About 120 of them took the station's own Lift (which Minthome later calls "the Mother") and crossed to the neighbouring world, whose life is their hand. They carried:
  - the plates of a second Lift (four of them came through turned, which is why their stamps face home);
  - one phone with a single saved web page;
  - a glass jar;
  - the Fable on paper.

  They landed by a river, tasted a wild mint that tasted *right*, and wept. They called the place **Minthome**. The phone's gallery holds a video of that day: *Day One*.
- **Years 1–9.** They camp by the river and set up the second Lift on a plinth there. The first Tally page is headed *Minthome, Year One*. The founders count crossings and write the observed rate: **2 in 100**. Every young person crosses once so that the number stays known: the first Tithe. Their own rare Turned children (now Earth-handed, starving) are sent back to Earth, where the food fits them: the first Passings. By then the station stands empty; why is a wonder. The Passed settle there. After Year 9, Passings from the hill come out a kilometre from the station, at a spot Minthome calls **the Landing**. It lies exactly "under" Ream's Temple, and there Minthome builds its **Answering House** and hangs its own great stone. Only a stone at that spot can be felt by Ream's needles, and it can feel Ream's. Needle traces read "ARRIVED".
- **Year 9.** The river floods the plinth. The founders carry the Lift up the hill. The forty days with no crossings are mostly spent building the hill plinth; they chant the plates in order as they reassemble them, and the Litany of Plates is born. The old river plinth is left where it stood, and the landing site around it becomes the first burial ground: the **Old Graves**.
- **Years 9–130.** The name "Minthome" drifts to mean the other world, where the Passed go. The Leaves are copied and recopied; the copyists cut "Stipulated" to "St." and then to a dot. The Fable stays the founders' open book, read by few. The dead are buried.
- **Year 131.** A fever kills faster than the Graves can take the dead. Randall Ottilie Abbott reads the verse "a crosser who runs out doesn't die so much as diffuse. The gravimeter in the empty room still registers seventy kilograms" as a promise that the dead can stay, and she Gives them to the Lift. The fever passes, and the Giving is "proven". The same year, faced with the Tally's 2 in 100 against the Leaves' "fifty", she writes in the Randalls' book: *"The count says two. The Leaves say fifty. The Leaves are older. The rate is the Ancestors'."* The dash is written. **The year Ream stopped counting is the year Ream began building its doom.**
- **Years 131–235.** The Weight grows by about eight tonnes a year. From about Year 150 its twist begins to show: the Turning rate creeps to 3 in 100 by Year 180 and 5 by Year 235. Only someone counting year by year could see it, and nobody is. A counting city would have stopped the Giving at three hundred tonnes, cheaply.
- **Year 222.** Ada Dvali (Theta) is born.
- **Year 235.** The Weight beside the door now pulls harder on a crosser than the far tug of the neighbouring world.
  - Passings begin to show a rise that stays.
  - The Randall of the day reads this sincerely: the Passed are now being taken into the Ancestors, as the Given are. In a public ceremony, to rejoicing, the Weight-book is re-inked: rise-and-stay becomes "RECEIVED", and the rare rise-and-fall becomes "REFUSED", souls the Ancestors send back to walk as Pariahs. The Pariah law and the gloss "Go toward the Ancestors; they will carry you" are carved in daylight.
  - Ada Dvali, 13, a Warden's apprentice, asks aloud during the rite why the words change when the needle doesn't. She is beaten for it.
  - Once the Weight out-pulls the far world, crossers' courses bend toward it, and nearer means much stronger. From here the Turning rate climbs steeply.
  - Iris and Kit Carroll are born this year.
- **Year 252.** Iris Carroll's Tithe. She turns and lies. Her twin brother Kit, who held her hand, is Passed by the handhold law. He follows the faint far pull, not the gloss, and arrives in Minthome a Straight, starving. The old Randall feeds Iris and makes her heir.
- **Autumn, Year 252.** Kit crosses back through the Mother and walks out of the Old Graves tasting mint, shouting "Move the door!". Iris, 18, in new apprentice white, gives her first order: "Pass him." It happens twice more in the next year. He crosses seven times in all; the seventh crossing turns him, and he can eat in Minthome, so he stays. Nan's tale, and the city, round the count up to the verse's eleven. The Randall knows it was seven.
- **About Year 254.** Kit tries once to reach his sister by the stone. In the catechism code he spells the only part of her name that is in the Leaves: CARROLL. Ream reads it as the Ancestors calling a Carroll, and Iris's great-aunt Hanna Carroll is Joined. Kit hears it from the next Straight to arrive, and never sends again. This is the origin of the spelled-name lore.
- **Year 255.** Burnt Row. Iris, 20, is "Sigma" in the Fable's small open reading circle. She mocks the old Randall's unexplained rule and stops burning what leaves her body. Seven die. The Fable is sealed as heresy; the reading circle goes underground as the Changers. Iris leaves them for ever.
- **About Year 270.** In Minthome, Mother Rosa Adamala Gives her husband at the Mother, "so he would stay near", as the Leaves say. Minthome's Givings begin. Kit, who felt the pile beside Ream's door twist him, starts a needle log at the Mother and tells no one.
- **Year 283.** Nell and Wells Abbott are born. The Turning rate is 10 in 100.
- **Year 294.** Their father, Tom Abbott, a Weight-maker, dies of a fall and is Given.
- **Year 300.** The season. The Weight stands at about 1,500 tonnes. The Tithe turning rate is **24 in 100**. Minthome's small pile beside the Mother is about 20 tonnes.

## 2. The show's ledger

The Fourth Direction earns its twists by declaring its guesses once and never adding to them in secret. CARAWAY does the same. Every rule an episode uses must appear here. If a writer needs a rule that is not here, the plan changes first.

### 2.1 What the Fourth Direction established (real science, true in the show)

- Matter and light stay pinned to their world; only gravity spreads between worlds (Randall–Sundrum; Arkani-Hamed, Dimopoulos, Dvali).
- In four dimensions, atoms and orbits are unstable, because pull falls with the cube of distance (Ehrenfest). This is why nothing can live in the dark.
- In four dimensions, walls enclose nothing and knots come undone.
- A turn through the fourth direction brings a body back as its mirror image.
- Life is one-handed. Mirror sugar passes through the body unused. Mint and caraway are the same molecule in two mirror forms. Paper has a hand.
- Mirror-life is dangerous: nothing can recognise, digest or fight it. This is the 2024 warning in the Leaves.

### 2.2 What the Fourth Direction stipulated (the film's own guesses, accepted by the show)

1. There are many worlds, stacked, and they carry life.
2. A field can unpin matter and hold its atoms together for seconds, longer the lighter the load.
3. Gravity between worlds is strong enough to feel at human scale.
4. The neighbouring world's life is the other hand.

### 2.3 What the Fourth Direction derived (accepted by the show)

- The dark between worlds is black, and a crosser sees only by the light inside their own bubble, but sees inside everything within it.
- A crossing is short, timed and limited by mass, and everything inside the bubble crosses too: air, food, gut germs.
- A slide and a turn feel the same from inside. Every crossing is a coin toss nobody can watch, fixed at the instant the crossing ends.
- A mirrored body starves on ordinary food, catches no ordinary disease, and carries mirror-life.
- Weight in motion is the only message that can cross. It is slow, and it proves that someone moved mass. It never proves who, or why.
- Across the gap, pull fades with the cube of distance.

### 2.4 In the Leaves, but NOT true in this world (the Order's mistakes)

- **The handhold law.** In this world a handhold makes no difference. Two crossers turn or don't turn independently, each at the usual rate. The Tally shows "exactly one" for every hand-holding pair only because the rule itself writes the entry: the Randall always chooses one. (For the twins, exactly one did turn. The verse happened to be right that time, and that is not what makes anything true.)
- **"The drift is fifty."** Fifty was the starting position of a slider in a demonstration. The real base drift here is about 2 in 100.
- **"For the ending only" as prophecy.** It was a note about how a film might end. The verse under it (the neighbour's life is the other hand) is a guess that happens to be true here.
- **The Plot as law.** The Plot's examples are scenes from an unmade film. Some happen to be true in this world, because they follow from the laws: an outside dog growls at the Turned, a Turned body lives on water, spirits and fat, a Turned hand writes backward, and paper has a hand. Being in the Plot is never what makes them true.

### 2.5 The show's own stipulations (declared once, never added to)

1. **The Lost stay.** Whatever runs out of time in the dark stays beside the door it left through, as a slowly spreading cloud of bare atomic cores. It is still fully heavy, and it drifts only a little over centuries.
2. **Weight near a door twists and pulls.** A heavy mass hanging in the dark near a door makes crossers who pass it more likely to turn, and more likely to be pulled off course and lost. The effect grows with the mass from the first tonne, with nearness (by the cube law) and with time spent in the dark. Given long enough beside it, more than half of crossers turn.
3. **The tug of home.** In the dark, a crosser feels the strongest pulls as directions: their own world beneath them like a floor, and others. Whoever follows a pull out of the ring's reach and still has time left steps out onto that world.
4. **The base drift.** With no weight nearby, the field turns about 2 crossings in 100.
5. **The First Leaf.** The phone survived three hundred years sealed in stone, and it wakes when fed power from the Lift's port.
6. **The ring's reach.** When its keeper closes the field, the ring sets back down whatever is still within its reach. A field that is left to run out sets nothing down: whatever is still in the dark stays there.
7. **What turns as one.** A living body turns whole, with every living thing inside it and on its skin. Separate things, even touching or held, each take their own chance. A box is not a body. One solid thing (glued, cast or bound) turns whole. A part still joined to a body that stays on the world can slide through the dark but cannot turn.
8. **Backstory.** The founders came through the Lift three hundred years ago. *(Full version, revealed in episode 9: they were Turned people from an Earth quarantine station.)*
9. **Working number.** A crossing lasts about 700 ÷ (weight in kilograms) seconds.

*(Cut in revision: "shared light", which was bought for one image in the finale.)*

### 2.6 Derived by the show (no new purchase; each follows from the lines above)

- **Coming back.** A Tithe child is set down unless the Weight has dragged her out of reach first (the Called). The Given are the one rite in which the field is left to run out. The Passed walk out of reach along a far pull and step out on the other world. Crates and seeds come back unless they are dragged off; light crates, with long counts, are sometimes lost.
- **The Fast kills.** A lighter crosser gets a longer count, so spends longer beside the Weight, and so turns and is Called more often. The rite demands the full count ("pray the whole crossing through").
- **Capture.** Once the Weight out-pulls the far world (about Year 235), crossers' courses bend toward it. Nearer means much stronger, by the cube law, so the Turning rate climbs steeply after that year. Twisting needed no threshold; capture did.
- **Young and old clouds.** A young cloud is tight and hangs close to its door; an old one has spread. Minthome's twenty tonnes, about a third as far from its door as Ream's cloud is from Ream's, pull like about five hundred tonnes at Ream's distance: Ream's own Weight around Year 200, when its rate was about 4 in 100.
- **A Passing is a coin toss.**
  - A Turned person can be turned back in transit and arrive in Minthome starving: a **Straight**.
  - An honest child wrongly Passed may be turned in transit and arrive fed.
- **Arrival and loss on the needle.** Anyone who leaves the dark alive, on either world, shows as a rise that falls. A loss shows as a rise that stays.
- **Doors that stand at the same spot on neighbouring worlds line up.** A crossing is a slide in the fourth direction, which keeps every other coordinate.
  - A crossing from Minthome's Mother comes out at Ream's Old Graves, which is why Pariahs come out of the Graves. No Lift is needed on the landing side.
  - A Straight who crosses back and is not turned on the way tastes **mint** in Ream. **Pariahs were cured people coming home.**
- **Distance.** Moving the door about thirty times farther from the Weight weakens its pull about twenty-seven thousand times.
- **Mirror writing.** The founders were the mirror of Earth, so Ream's writing is the mirror of ours. A Turned Reamer reads and writes Earth's way, and only Turned eyes read the plates and the First Leaf without a mirror.
- **Separate chances.** Each card in a crate, each seed in a hem, each plate in a load takes its own chance. That is why four of the founders' plates came through turned. Only their stamps show it; bronze has no hand.
- **Arkani's seeds.** The Tithe seeds ride in the hems of the Tithe children, for the same count beside the same Weight, each with its own chance. So the share that comes back turned follows the Tithe rate by construction. The gardener has been running the experiment for sixty years without knowing it.
- **A hand, not a body.** The field's bubble can be set small enough to take only a hand, and a hand's count runs for minutes. A hand joined to a body on the world cannot turn, but anything it holds can. If the field is closed while the hand is inside rock, the hand is set down inside the rock. If the field runs out while the hand is still in the dark, the hand scatters.
- **Living Turned.** The Turned can live in Ream for good on crops grown from seeds that came back turned. This is the Randall's glasshouse.
- **The grey.** A Turned body's waste breeds grey mould nothing in Ream can eat. It must be burned.
- **Earth paper lasts.** The Fable's pages are Earth paper, mirror to Ream's moulds, so nothing in Ream can rot them.
- **The Jar's thread.** The Jar is Earth glass, so its thread runs the other way to every thread in Ream. A Reamer must think about it ("against the sun"). A Turned hand opens it without thought.
- **The dogs.** A dog knows the Turned by smell only if it grew up among the unturned. The Temple kennels are next to the Randall's table.
- **Carried germs.** Anyone who crosses carries their world's germs. Someone coming home from Minthome may carry Earth life, which is mirror-life to Ream. Someone going to Minthome from Ream carries Ream life on their skin and clothes, which is mirror-life on Earth. That is what Earth's fence ("MIRROR-LIFE CONTAINMENT") has always been for.
- **Signals need lined-up spots.** Because pull fades with the cube of distance, a stone signal is felt only by needles at the same spot on the other world. Ream's Temple stone talks to Minthome's Landing. A needle at the Old Graves talks to the station.
- **Minthome's small weight.** Minthome's own Givings put a small Weight beside the Mother, and so beside the Old Graves door too, because it is the same place.
- **Reaching the other world takes time.** A crosser whose count is too short falls short of the next world and stays in the dark.

### 2.7 How the ledger appears on screen

At the end of episode 6, after the First Leaf's tags are revealed, the credits carry one plain card: **"What this story asks you to accept."** It lists stipulations 1–7 and 9 in everyday words, and 8 in its short form.

From then on the show plays fair. Every later twist can be worked out from the card, the Leaves and what has been shown.

**Never, in any episode:**
- the Weight is aware;
- ghosts;
- a signal that comes from the Weight itself (every pattern on the needles is someone moving mass);
- a way to feel or steer a turn;
- light or sound crossing between worlds;
- a Turned person living for years on tallow alone.

## 3. The doom mechanism, step by step

Each step names the idea from the Fable (the Semantics) that it puts into human form. Writers dramatize the step; nobody states the idea.

**Step 1: Rules built with reasons, kept without them.**
- **What happens.** The founders built their rules with reasons and a count. They knew *why* the Passing worked (the other world's food fits the Turned), and the Tithe was their count of the drift (2 in 100). Ream kept the rites and dropped both the reasons and the count.
- **The idea.** A rule handed down without its reasons is held only by the fact that it worked. It is faithful only on the changes it has already met, and nobody holding it can say what it will do on a new one.

**Step 2: Questions closed by decree.**
- **What happens.** In Year 131 one Randall does two things. She starts the Giving from a misread verse, and she writes the dash because the count (2) disagrees with the book (50): "The Leaves are older." The handhold law and "the drift is fifty" become law.
- **The idea.** Knowledge that is only declared: someone just said so. Closing a question is a choice, not a discovery.

**Step 3: The practice changes the world.**
- **What happens.** Every funeral adds about 60 kg beside the door, and they stay (stipulation 1). The mass grows by eight tonnes a year for 170 years.
- **The idea.** The culture's own practice produces a change outside the history its rules were fitted to.

**Step 4: The change arrives unseen.**
- **What happens.** The Weight twists crossers from the first tonne (stipulation 2). From about Year 150 the rate creeps up, 3 in 100 by Year 180 and 5 by Year 235, visible only to someone counting year by year. The dash hides the creep. About Year 235 the Weight out-pulls the neighbouring world: most Passings stop arriving, crossers' courses bend toward it, and the rate climbs steeply, from 5 to 24.
- **The idea.** Surprise can happen only when a rule meets a change outside its history.

**Step 5: The readings are relabelled, not questioned.**
- **What happens.** All of this is done sincerely, in daylight.
  - Rise-and-stay is re-inked "RECEIVED" in a public ceremony, and a girl who asks why is beaten.
  - Rise-and-fall becomes "REFUSED", and the Refused who come home are Passed unheard as Pariahs.
  - The rising Weight is favour.
  - The rising rate is the Ending approaching.
  - Thirteen is a new command.
  - A Calling after a hidden child is the Ancestors' anger.
- **The idea.** A reading is not the thing read. A rule's verdict changes when the rule is edited, not when the world changes. A story that absorbs every outcome can never be caught out.

**Step 6: Re-tuning makes it worse.**
- **What happens.**
  - *The Lightening Fast* (episode 2): lighter crossers, longer beside the Weight.
  - *Transit prayers* ("pray the whole crossing through"): the Fast's extra seconds are spent praying in the dark.
  - *The Joining* (episode 4): thirteen elders add about 800 kg.
  - *The doubled Fast* (announced in episode 8).
  - *The Great Tithe* (episodes 8–9): all twelve thousand cross past the Weight.
- **The idea.** The selection response: re-tune the old rules. When the failure is structural, no re-tuning survives.

**Step 7: Why no adjustment can work.**
- **What happens.** Every setting the Order can change acts on the crosser (her purity, her weight, her prayers, how many cross) or adds to the Weight. No setting of those makes a crossing *at this door* safe. The fault is *where the door stands and what is next to it*, and the doctrine has no word for "where", because the Ancestors are "everywhere, all around us". You cannot fast away fifteen hundred tonnes. The one setting that would stop the dying, not sending the children, is one the faith cannot touch: the Tithe is the gate to adulthood, and the rite is the religion.
- **The idea.** When every rule in the population reads the wrong thing, none can be tuned right. A new part has to be built: place and distance.

**Step 8: The right answer, merely said, fails.**
- **What happens.**
  - Kit shouts "Move the door!" without reasons and is Passed. Later he sends one word by the stone, CARROLL, with no way for anyone to check it, and it kills his great-aunt.
  - Young Iris throws out the burn rule without testing it, and Burnt Row burns.
  - Nell has a test anyone can repeat, the crates of cards. In front of twelve thousand people she says more than it shows ("The Ancestors are killing our children"), the crate stays shut at her feet, and a rite rule turns the panic into a crush.
- **The idea.** Passing a claim on is not building it. A claim no one can check is just something someone says. Questioning is not the same as answering.

**Step 9: A new explanation, built.**
- **What happens.**
  - Nell asks new questions: "What was the rate *each year*?" "*Where* are they?"
  - She makes tests anyone can repeat: cards through the Lift, a needle carried street by street, a board with a written prediction.
  - Pim reruns her tests to prove her wrong, gets her numbers, and then asks a question of his own: what does the lightest crate do? More than half come back turned, a result the Ending doctrine forbids and the new explanation predicts.
  - The city pools its scattered private counts ("Who else has a number?").
- **The idea.** Finding a new question is itself creative. An explanation built with the thing in view says something definite about untested changes, so it can be caught out. A learner who rebuilds an idea has built it; one who repeats it has not.

**Step 10: Repair, and its limit.**
- **What happens.**
  - Move the door a kilometre (the cube law, from the true part of their own book).
  - Stop the Giving; bury the dead again.
  - Test with cards, not children.
  - Keep a public board, with a prediction that could be wrong.
  - Then it *is* wrong. Nell predicted 2 in 100; the new door gives 4. She does not narrow her claim: "Then I'm wrong. Something else is near this door." A child asks why, and the answer is that Minthome has started Giving too.
- **The idea.** Nothing is settled. Narrowing a claim after it failed is a new claim, not a rescue: a failed answer stays failed, and the heroine applies that to herself. A copy is not a second witness: two peoples Giving their dead is one book speaking twice. A thinking people can make any of its own practices the target of criticism.

**Why "always" is earned.** Three turns of the same wheel, each shown on screen:
1. The founders' rules were right, and went wrong in Ream's hands once the world moved.
2. Minthome copied the Giving from the same book, and its keeper of the count, Kit, kept his own blank line.
3. The repaired Ream meets two changes outside its new rules within a day: 4 in 100, not 2; and the grey, carried home by the boy it saved.

The world always eventually moves outside what your rules were fitted to, often because of your rules. There is no villain in this chain. There is love, grief, prudence, mercy, and a book read as command.

## 4. Characters

Eight principals and a small supporting cast. Deep bonds are expensive, so the inner circle is Nell, Wells, the Randall and Hester. Everyone else orbits them.

**Mirror pairs, which writers should use visibly but never point out:**
- Nell and the Randall: the same lie, forty-eight years apart.
- Wells and Kit: the honest twin, Passed.
- The Randall and Kit: twins who each keep a private count and a blank line.
- Hester and the Randall: two women whose silence is love and fuel for the machine.
- Theta and the First Accountant: two certainties facing each other.
- The Randall and Mother Adamala: two keepers, one on each side, running the same rite for the same reason (someone they loved, kept near).
- Lottie and Jory: the child in each world who asks the next question.

### NELL ABBOTT (17). Apprentice Tallykeeper. The heroine.
- **Want.** Bring Wells home alive. Later: make Ream able to count.
- **Wound.** The three seconds at the Mint Test when she tasted caraway and said "mint", and let her brother go.
- **Flaw.** She hides. She keeps the rules on the outside and breaks them in secret, and her lie grows into a way of life (secret food, secret tests, secret truths), which is the Randall's way. When she finally stops hiding, she overreaches: she wants the big truth believed more than she wants the small one tested. A second flaw, from before the Tithe: she uses scripture to dismiss what she can't count. In the dark, when Wells said there were "two more" pulls, she told him the Leaves name only one.
- **Secrets.**
  - She is Turned (hidden until episode 8).
  - She sent the eleven (episodes 3–4).
  - The Randall feeds her (episodes 4–9).
- **Levers.**
  - **Competence.** She counts under her breath when afraid, and she has never lost the prime race.
  - **Vulnerability.** She is starving in a city of bread, and she can no longer read her own city's writing. Hester weighs her once an episode, and the numbers go on screen: 51.0, 48.0, 46.4, 45.1.
  - **Interiority.** We are inside her hunger and her sums.
  - **Specificity.** A stolen shard of mirror; tallow candles eaten in the dark, the wick saved for last.
  - **Humour.** Dry, spiky.
  - **Connection.** Wells, Hester, Nan, Tau, the dog.
  - Decency starts low, since she lets others pay, and is earned back by what she does.
- **Arc.**
  1. Liar.
  2. Secret experimenter.
  3. A truth-teller who says more than her test shows, and causes a disaster.
  4. The builder of a practice anyone can use.
  5. A girl who refuses to be the new Randall, and who says "I'm wrong" in public when her own prediction fails.
  6. In the end she says Wells's word, "Me", and takes the exile she dodged, carrying a count and a question instead of an answer.
- **Voice.** Short sentences. Numbers as comfort ("Three hundred and twelve. Three hundred and thirteen."). Her favourite question is "Compared with what?". She never swears by the Leaves. When lying she becomes over-precise.
- **Surprise that stays in character.** She turns down the office the whole city offers her, she keeps her own failed number on the board, and she chooses exile.

### WELLS ABBOTT (17). Her twin. A Weight-maker's apprentice.
- **Want.** Before the Tithe: to haul the Great Stone at the Answering for the first time, that very night, in the new hauler's gloves he shows Nell. After: to meet his Ending with grace, then to live, then to get word home, then to come home.
- **Wound.** All his life he has been the one who shushes Nell ("Eleven's forbidden, Nell").
- **Flaw.** Faith and trust. He let Nell do the questioning for both of them.
- **Secret.** At the Mint Test he knew. He had seen her face when the leaf touched her tongue. He mouthed "Me" to the Randall (revealed at the end of episode 6).
- **Levers.**
  - Decency.
  - Humour: he always gets the prime race wrong at 91.
  - Strength: strong hands, a hauler.
  - Agency in Minthome.
  - Vulnerability: starving on the wrong world.
- **Arc.**
  - In the dark at the Tithe, his count runs out first (he is heavier) and he is the one who reaches: "the hand that reached" is literally true, out of haste, not fault.
  - The faithful twin does one unfaithful thing, alone in the dark. The liturgy tells the Passed to go toward the big close pull. He follows the faint far one ("What would Nell do? Go past seven."), and it saves his life.
  - He hauls thirteen barehanded, because his gloves are in Nell's hand.
  - He sends ABBOTT over Kit's fear, and MOVE THE DOOR with Kit's reasons.
  - In Minthome he asks the first real questions ("Why do our dead go to the Mother?" "What does the Mother do to the ones we give it?").
  - He chooses his own ballast when the count is cut off, and he insists Ream be told about Minthome's dead before he crosses.
  - He comes home, and he and Nell share a table for the first time since episode 1, eating different food.
- **Voice.** Warm, teasing, physical; he counts hauls aloud. He says "What if..." rarely, and when he does it matters.
- **Rule for writers.** He is never a prize to be won. From episode 7 he has his own storyline, his own clock and his own choices.

### THE RANDALL: IRIS CARROLL (65). High keeper of the Leaves.
- **Want.** To keep Ream calm and whole and carry it gently to the Ending, where she will see her twin brother Kit again.
- **Wound.**
  - At 17 she turned and lied, and Kit, who held her hand, was Passed.
  - At 18, in her first order as apprentice, she had Kit Passed again when he came out of the Graves shouting "Move the door!", before he could say why. She said it three times that year. Two years later the Ancestors "spelled" CARROLL and her great-aunt was Joined; she has always suspected who spelled it.
  - At 20, as "Sigma" in the Fable's open reading circle, she mocked the old Randall's unexplained rule ("burn what leaves your body") and simply stopped keeping it. Her own body bred Burnt Row, and seven people died. The book was sealed because of her. She left the Changers for ever.
- **Flaw.** She believes mercy means certainty, and closes questions to spare people pain. She is superb at fitting any new fact into the old story.
- **Secrets.**
  - She is Turned, and has been for 48 years. She lives on the glasshouse crops. She writes Earth's way in private and fakes Ream's script in public.
  - She has kept the true rate in the Randalls' private book for forty years, and reads its rise as the Ending coming. The book also holds Ottilie Abbott's full Year 131 entry.
  - She knew at once that Nell lied. She took Wells's "Me" because she knew Nell's face: it was her own.
  - She knows Kit crossed seven times, not eleven, and let the tale round it up.
- **Right about.**
  - Hidden Turned people can breed blight.
  - A truth thrown at a crowd without its test can kill (episode 8).
  - The Passing really was a mercy once.
- **Levers.**
  - Competence.
  - Real tenderness, with children and with Nell.
  - Specificity. She eats alone. She wears the white stole. She opens the Sealed Jar without thought, where the Sundrum has to think "against the sun". *(Plant: the Jar is Earth-threaded, so only a Turned hand finds it natural.)*
- **Arc.** The keeper of the dash reads Ottilie's entry aloud, writes the number on the board in her own hand (our way, for the whole city to see), tastes caraway in public, frees Nell, turns her key, and says to a Pariah what she could not say for Kit: "Let him speak." At last she could go to Minthome and her brother. She stays, and reads the rate aloud in the square every morning.
- **Voice.** Calm, few words, never raised. She calls everyone "child". When frightened she speaks in verses. "Hush" is her mercy.
- **Rule for writers.** She is never a villain. Viewers should half-agree with her until episode 8, and fully understand her by episode 9.

### HESTER ABBOTT (44). The twins' mother. An Accountant.
- **Want.** To keep what is left of her family.
- **Wound.** Her husband Given; her son Passed; then her mother-in-law Joined (episode 4).
- **Flaw.** Silence. She knows, and says nothing. She believes numbers serve the Order; they do not question it.
- **Secrets.**
  - From the first supper she knows Nell is Turned: the left-shoulder blessing, the growling dog, the untouched bread. She leaves tallow cakes at Nell's place without a word, and gives the dog away to the Order's kennels so he will stop growling at her daughter.
  - She wrote the Fast tables.
- **Levers.** Competence (she weighs people with her eyes), connection, restraint. And the frightening competence of burning half her own house to kill the grey (episode 5).
- **Arc.**
  1. Silent protector.
  2. Tasted before the city at the Autumn Giving (mint), then silent in the crowd while Nell is tasted, just as Nell was silent at the Test.
  3. She breaks her silence. She nails her own Fast tables to the public board with WRONG written across them, then works out the number that brings her son home safely: his ballast. In the finale she straps stones to her daughter too.
- **Voice.** Clipped and practical: "Eat." "Sit." She speaks in weights ("You're down two kilos.").

### TAU: TOBIN DVALI (19). Tally clerk and Changer.
- **Want.** To know why his sister Mira was Called, and who is to blame.
- **Wound.** Mira, Called in the first minute of the season.
- **Flaw.** He wants a villain, and a conspiracy suits him.
- **Secrets.** He is a Changer. He loves Nell.
- **Levers.** Warmth, humour, loyalty, courage (he heaves stones and smuggles the dog), and a good memory for records.
- **Shown, not claimed.** Every week the Wardens wash the names of the Called off the Lift steps, and every week Tau chalks MIRA back. From episode 3 he has one small romantic beat per episode, beginning with the better mirror he hands Nell.
- **Arc.** He moves from "the Order fakes it" to evidence. He learns that the villain was love: Mira is part of the Weight, and he stops chalking her name. He breaks with his grandmother, carries the plates, and stays to keep the public board when Nell leaves, where he chalks MIRA once more, as a line in the count, with her year. Nell leaves him her notebooks, written our way, and her mirror so he can read them.
- **Voice.** Quick and jokey. He uses the Fable's creed as teasing ("What changes when things are changed, Nell?"), and goes quiet when grief comes.

### THETA: OLD MOTHER ADA DVALI (78). Needle-Warden and head of the Changers.
- **Want.** To prove the Order a liar, and to protect Tau.
- **Wound.** At 13, a Warden's apprentice, she stood in the Year 235 ceremony while the Weight-book was re-inked, and asked aloud why the words changed when the needle didn't. She was beaten for asking.
- **Flaw.** She is honest to instruments and dishonest to people. She moves her goalposts when the evidence turns. She mistakes a change of words for a faking of the needles.
- **Secrets.**
  - She leaves the eleven out of her log to protect Tau (episode 4).
  - She tells the First Accountant which Abbott is Turned, and that the Randall has been shielding her, to buy Tau's pardon (episode 8).
- **Right about.** The Order did change the record. It changed the labels, not the readings.
- **Levers.** Specificity (her needles, her log in tiny script), grit, acid humour, love for Tau.
- **Arc.** From conspiracy to one moment in episode 9. She watches the Lift's weight walk out of the city on her needles and rings no bell. Her faithfulness to the instrument finally outweighs her story. In episode 10 she sits at the needle by the Graves and says the founders' word aloud: "Arrived."
- **Voice.** Salty and suspicious. "Nobody fakes a mountain. But a priest can move a stone."

### KIT CARROLL (65). In Minthome. The Randall's twin brother.
- **Situation.** Passed at 17. That year he crossed seven times, coming out of the Graves three times and being Passed each time, until the seventh crossing turned him. He has lived in Minthome for forty-seven years. Minthome's tally-keeper: he counts arrivals, Straights and Givings. He is the only living person who has crossed through both doors and felt the difference.
- **Want.** To get word home that the door must move, and to see his sister.
- **Wound.** His sister's lie, then his sister's order. Then his own word: the one time he used the stone, spelling CARROLL, Ream Joined his great-aunt.
- **Flaw.** Bitterness and fear. For forty-seven years he has told anyone in Minthome who will listen "Move the door!", and never built the reasons into anything anyone else could run. After CARROLL he decided that any word sent by the stone would be obeyed, not tested, and he never used the stone again.
- **Secret.** Since Minthome began Giving (about Year 270) he has kept a needle log at the Mother. The pile beside it rises with every Giving; he felt what the pile beside Ream's door did to him. He has told no one, not even Wells. He fears Mother Adamala, and he fears being the man who shouts. It is the Randall's blank line, kept by the brother who shouted.
- **Arc.** From a man who shouts an answer to a man who teaches Wells the reasons and then lets Wells ask. In episode 9 he argues, openly, against telling Ream that the new door stands on Minthome's dead: "Tell them the new place is dirty too, and they'll never move it." At the end he is waiting for Nell with a lamp at the Mother, and he hands her thirty years of his count: "I never sent it. I was afraid of her."
- **Voice.** Dry and sardonic, the voice of a lifetime unheard. He reads Earth's signs aloud to Wells, who can't read them. "Eleven? Seven. They made it fit."

### PIM EHRENFEST (19). Apprentice Accountant under Hester. Son of the Sundrum.
- **Want.** To be right in the eyes of the Order, and to protect his sister Bea (16).
- **Wound.** At his own Tithe he panicked in the dark and came back vomiting, and everyone laughed. He has been proving himself ever since.
- **Flaw.** An informer's zeal. He believes rules are safety.
- **Secret.** He reruns Nell's card test to catch her out, gets her numbers, and tells no one (episodes 5–8). Then he pushes it further than she did, to the lightest crates, and finds more than half coming back turned (episode 8, revealed in 9).
- **Levers.** Competence; vulnerability (Bea's Tithe is next spring, and she is in the first hundred of the Great Tithe list); specificity (a pocket notebook of weights, a pencil he licks); and the capacity to surprise.
- **Arc.** From believer and informer to the one who makes the method his own. In episode 1, as a nameless young clerk, he is the one who proposes the obvious test ("Let them each sign the Tally"), and the Randall closes it. Word-perfect in his father's Litany, he is the one who hears it as an assembly order. In episode 10 he runs the cards with the children.
- **Voice.** Formal. He quotes rules exactly and stammers when he lies.

Hester gives him to the Order's kennels in episode 2, where he growls at Nell on the weighing line. By episode 8 he has learned her new smell and lies down beside her in her cell. A living thing that adjusts to change, on screen, unlike the city.
- **First Accountant Abel Dimopoulos (58).** The hardliner and a sincere manager of fear. His grandson Aaron, a big lad of 74 kg, was Called at this spring's Tithe, and "too heavy to get home" is his grief speaking. He reads thirteen as a command and ABBOTT as a summons, proclaims the Lightening, the doubled Fast and the Great Tithe, and carves the last gloss. In episode 10 he is the one who points at Nell's board: "You said two." Then he asks for one crate weighted to his grandson. He is not a villain. He is what the old rules look like under pressure.
- **The Sundrum: Jonah Ehrenfest (50).** Keeper of the Lift and Pim's father. He recites the Litany of Plates, fumbles the Jar's lid when he rehearses, and turns his key in episode 9.
- **Bea Ehrenfest (16).** Pim's sister. Her Tithe is next spring; she is in the first hundred of the Great Tithe.
- **Lottie Others (9).** A Tally runner from the poor Others clan. She counts everything and wins the catechism game. In episode 10 she asks the question that catches Nell's answer short, and chalks THE GREY? on the board.
- **Owen Others (22).** Lottie's brother and a Lift clerk. He runs the maintenance hours, sets the field for the cards and for Nell's hand, and helps with everything. His cousin was the Others boy dragged to the Tithe under guard in episode 1.
- **Brother Arkani (80).** The Order's gardener. He grows the Randall's crops from the Tithe seeds that come back turned, and has kept the drift for sixty years without knowing it was a number: "One seed in four comes back right for the glasshouse now. In my mother's day, one in thirty. In her mother's, one in fifty."
- **Mother Rosa Adamala (87).** Keeper of Minthome's Answering House and of the Mother. The Randall's mirror: kind, certain, and running the same rite. She began Minthome's Givings by Giving her husband in Year 270, "so he would stay near". When Wells asks what the Mother does to the ones they give it, she is thinking of him.
- **Jory (20).** A young Minthome hauler, Minthome-born. Curious. He has never seen a Straight leave the Long House alive. The first in Minthome to help Wells, and the one who hauls WE. DIFFUSE. HERE. TOO. against Adamala and against Kit's fear. Minthome's Lottie, and the bridge to Season 2.

## 5. The ten episodes

**Structure of each entry.** Title; one-line pitch; cold open; acts; the twist; the ending hook; the gaps it opens and closes.

**Point of view.** Episodes 1–6 stay locked inside Ream, mostly with Nell. Episode 7 breaks that contract and goes to Minthome. From then on the show cuts between the two worlds.

**The season clock.**
- It runs from the spring Tithe (episode 1) to the Autumn Giving (episode 8), then seven days (episodes 9–10).
- Wells, a Straight in Minthome, has about six months to live.
- Nell starves on tallow. From the end of episode 2 a small loaf appears only on days she obeys; when she digs, it stops. She eats a real meal at the Randall's table in episode 5.
- From the end of episode 5, the tasting of the Abbott house, set for the Autumn Giving, hangs over every episode.

### EPISODE 1: "The Tithe"
*On crossing day, twins break the rule about holding hands, and the wrong one is sent away.*

**Cold open.**
- Tithe morning. Children in the Temple recite Leaf Two: "A crosser who runs out doesn't die so much as diffuse..."
- Mira Dvali, 17, Nell's senior at the Tally, presses her chalk into Nell's hand. She has fasted for weeks, in secret, to be "a pure offering". "Don't wait up."
- She taps her right shoulder and steps into the humming ring of plates. She is gone.
- A Warden (Theta) counts aloud. "Ten. Fourteen. Sixteen." The Sundrum closes the field on her count. The ring is empty.
- The needle has risen and stays there. The crowd sighs: "Favour."
- Tau, her brother, stands frozen.
- At the Tally board, apprentice Nell Abbott chalks her first official mark with Mira's chalk: CALLED. It appears in mirror script, like all of Ream's writing.

**Act 1: Ream's normal.** Kept lean. Eight names only: Nell, Wells, Hester, Nan, Mira, Tau, the Randall and Plattner the dog. Everyone else is a face.
- In the Temple yard a small girl wins the catechism game ("Leaf four, line three, word six?" "*Mint!*").
- In the Tally Hall, the dash on OBSERVED RATE. Nell's chalk hovers over it. The Tally Master: "The rate is the Ancestors'."
- At home, Hester weighs the twins before the Tithe (51.0 kg; 70.4 kg). Wells shows Nell his new hauler's gloves: tonight, after the Tithe, he hauls the Great Stone at the Answering for the first time.
- Nan keeps her dead son Tom's haul rope on a peg, and a white Joining robe folded in her chest. She tells a Pariah tale: pale things walking out of the Old Graves by the river at night, begging to come in, and "the one who shouted about doors. He crossed eleven times, the tale says, like the verse."
- The twins run the prime race: "...eighty-nine." "Ninety-one!" "Ninety-one is seven thirteens, idiot." Wells always misses it.
- In the street the Randall passes in her white stole, and Plattner the dog growls at her. Hester hushes him. The audience reads this as the dog sensing a villain.
- **Background only, never a scene:** the Sundrum's Litany of Plates on the Temple steps, a young clerk mouthing along; Burnt Row, still black; the plate numbers, the only print we can read; Wardens hauling a struggling Others boy out of a flour bin to cross under guard ("unweighed!").

**Act 2: The crossing.**
- The ring's wall is painted with the teaching plates: a coin lifted over a wall, an R flipped to Я, a jar with two leaves, a stack of pages.
- The Tithe runs over many days; the twins are in this day's last six pairs. Over each pair that enters, the Randall recites: "No touching. One comes back mirrored. The handhold is what did it." By the twins' turn the audience knows it by heart, the way a Reamer does.
- A frightened child asks, "Is it true?" The Randall: "That verse has no voice, child. The Plot needs none. It is what will happen."
- Right shoulders tapped. No touching.
- In the black, each twin is lit only by their own light. They can see inside each other: Wells's heart beating, Nell's lungs pumping.
- To stay calm they play the prime race: "...five." "Seven." "*Eleven!*" "Shh, Nell." "Thirteen."
- Wells whispers: "Home's under us like a floor. And two more. A big one, close, like a cliff. And a little one far off, like someone calling from another room." Nell: "The Leaves name one. The tug of home."
- Wells's fingertips tingle first: he is heavier, so his count is shorter. Nell has frozen, staring toward the cliff. He grabs her hand and pulls her toward the floor. The field closes. They land holding hands.

**Act 3: The Mint Test.**
- Ahead of them, two children taste caraway and say so gladly. They are garlanded for Minthome, and their families cheer.
- The Randall unscrews the Jar "against the sun", as the rite says, without a glance at her hands. A close-up shows its embossed glass base, which we can read.
- Nell tastes caraway. Three seconds. "Mint."
- Wells: "Mint."
- A Warden saw them come out holding hands. The Randall recites: "One comes back mirrored. The handhold is what did it." One of them is lying.
- The young clerk from the steps (Pim, not yet named) speaks up: "Let them each sign the Tally, Randall. The Plot says a Turned hand writes backward."
- The Randall points to the gloss carved beneath the verse: "The mint alone is witness." The clerk subsides.
- The Randall looks a long time at Nell's face. Wells mouths something we cannot hear.
- The Randall: "The hand that reached." She chooses Wells. We see Hester's face.

**Act 4: The Passing.**
- The rite that night is joyful: garlands, candles. The Temple gates are barred "until the needle settles".
- Other men haul the Great Stone in two, three, five, seven. Wells's new gloves are in Nell's hand. The needles Answer.
- The Ending verse is sung, the one with the amber dot, the holiest voice: "For the ending only: the neighbour's life is the other hand." The crowd sings "The ending writes itself."
- Over the Passed the gloss is read: "Go toward the Ancestors; they will carry you."
- Wells taps Nell's right shoulder and says nothing. He steps in. Gone.
- Nell records the traces. The two believers' needles rise and stay: the clerk writes "RECEIVED", and their families weep with joy. Wells's needle rises, then falls back. The clerk writes "REFUSED".
- The crowd murmurs: even the Ancestors didn't want him; he has been sent back to walk as a Pariah. The family is shamed. Nell's dread, for the rest of the season: if Wells walks out of the Graves, the law says he is Passed on sight, unheard.

**The twist.** The wrong twin is exiled by a verse the audience has just learned to trust, because the right twin lied. The audience is ahead of everyone but Nell.

**The ending hook.**
- Home. Nell blesses her mother goodnight by tapping her *left* shoulder. Hester notices. Plattner growls at Nell.
- Alone in the Tally Hall at night, Nell chalks WELLS ABBOTT: REFUSED. For the first time in the episode the audience can read a Ream word. Among a wall of mirror script, her letters come out the way we write. Her hand now writes the other way, and she has just written it on the city's board.

**Gaps.**
- **Opened.**
  - Is Wells alive? Will he come back as a Pariah?
  - What does "Refused" mean?
  - What did Wells mouth?
  - Why is the rate blank?
  - Why does the dog growl at the Randall?
  - (Quietly) why can we read the plates, and now Nell's hand?
- **Closed.** None. This episode sets the normal and breaks it.

### EPISODE 2: "Observed Rate"
*Nell must hide a body that can't eat, read or bless the right way, and her numbers say the city is running out of time.*

**Cold open.** Night, the pantry. Nell bites bread: sand. She eats a tallow candle, retches, and eats again, saving the wick for last. In her drawer, last night's wick has a grey fuzz on it. The dog watches from the dark.

**Act 1: Hiding.**
- At dawn, at the Tally, her line WELLS ABBOTT: REFUSED has been wiped and rewritten in Ream's hand. Someone saw it, and covered for her. She realises what she nearly did.
- At breakfast Hester sets a tallow cake at Nell's place without a word. Neither speaks. From here on the audience knows Hester knows.
- At the Tally, Ream's script is now backwards to Nell. In Burnt Row she buys a contraband shard of mirror and reads the ledgers through it. The audience sees the reflection come out readable.
- She fakes her old handwriting, slowly.
- In the archive she finds an old copy of the Leaves with "St." written beside some verses, and a copyist's note in the margin: "Words of doubt are unfit for holy script." She doesn't know what "St." means. (Low emphasis.)
- She asks Warden Dvali (Theta) to let her see Wells's REFUSED trace in the Weight-book. "The Weight-book is for Wardens."
- Pim, now named, Hester's apprentice, notices she has become slow. He is suspicious.

**Act 2: The weighing.**
- Three were Called this Tithe: Mira, the First Accountant's grandson Aaron (a big lad of 74 kg), and a third.
- First Accountant Dimopoulos, grieving, reasons that the Called were too heavy to get home before their time ran out, and that a lighter body gets more time.
- Tau, in the crowd: "Mira was the lightest girl in her year." Dimopoulos: "Then she was Called for her purity." The doctrine gives opposite cases opposite stories, and nobody blinks.
- In the market, the Others are shunned: the Ancestors' anger at the boy they hid. A stall refuses Lottie.
- Dimopoulos declares **the Lightening**: every crosser fasts down before crossing and prays the whole crossing through. It is built on Hester's tables, and she presents them, proud and precise. Nobody sees anything wrong with it: it is sound by the old question.
- That morning Hester gives Plattner to the Order's kennels, without a word. The audience reads it as a sacrifice.
- **The set piece.** The Lightening's first public weighing, in the square. The thinnest are honoured. Dimopoulos holds up Nell, down three kilos (48.0): "The Tallykeeper's girl shows us the way." Children copy her. The rite that will kill children disguises her starvation as piety.
- The Order's knower-dogs walk the line, as at every rite, silent. Plattner, new to the kennel, reaches Nell and growls. The Randall has just stepped to Nell's side to bless her. The handler takes the growl as aimed at the Randall, and cuffs the "untrained" dog. Only the audience and the Randall know which of them he meant.

**Act 3: The new question.**
- Year-end sums. The grand total over three centuries gives about 7 Turned in 100, and everyone is comfortable.
- Nell, sleepless with hunger, asks a different question: not "what is the rate?" but "what was it *each year*?"
- Montage: Lottie runs ledgers down the 211 steps. The curve appears:
  - flat at 2 in 100 for a century and a half;
  - creeping from about Year 150: 3 by Year 180, 5 by Year 235, a rise nobody would see in a total;
  - steep after that, and in Nell's own lifetime from 10 to 24.
- At this pace it reaches one in two before she is thirty.
- Nell skips a gap in Year 9: forty days with no crossings at all.
- The first ledger's first page is headed *Minthome, Year One*, and the founders wrote the rate there: **2 in 100**. The dash begins in Year 131 with a note: "The rate is the Ancestors'. Ottilie Abbott, Randall." Her own family name.

**Act 4: Pressure.**
- Hester finds the mirror shard in Nell's apron and puts it back without a word.
- Nell tries to show her mother the curve. Hester: "Numbers serve. They don't ask."
- Hester, weighing her at home that night: "Forty-eight. Eat."

**The twist.** The Turning rate has been rising for a century and a half, and has more than doubled in Nell's lifetime. The founders counted it, and the silence was a decree made by an Abbott.

**The ending hook.** At dawn a cloth bundle sits on her Tally desk: a small loaf. She bites. It tastes right. Someone knows what she is.

**Gaps.**
- **Opened.**
  - Who is feeding her, and who rewrote her line?
  - What is driving the rise?
  - What does "St." mean? (Low emphasis.)
  - The clock: one in two.
- **Closed.** Why the rate line is blank: it was a decree. Why that decree was made stays open until episode 9.

### EPISODE 3: "Two, Three, Five, Seven"
*The heretics who share her secret believe the Order fakes the voice of the dead, so Nell designs a test no liar could pass.*

**Cold open.** In the Tally stacks, Tau catches Nell reading with a mirror, which is a Passing offence. He hands her a better mirror. "Come and meet some people who read backwards too." She goes because Warden Dvali, who keeps the Weight-book, will be there.

**Act 1: The Changers.**
- A candlelit cellar under a chandlery. Torn pages of the Fable, Earth paper that reads our way and has never rotted; the Changers read it through mirrors. Greek-letter names. They chant "What changes when things are changed?" like a psalm. Nobody here has ever changed anything.
- Their other psalm is a line of the Leaves they love for its sound: "Add a dimension and it thins with the cube... the needle only wakes up when the mountain is nearly overhead." They have never used it.
- Theta presides.

**Act 2: The conspiracy.**
- Theta says the Order fakes the Answering by moving hidden counterweights. She knows they change things: at thirteen she stood in the rite where the Weight-book was re-inked, asked aloud why the words changed when the needle didn't, and was beaten for asking.
- Tau: "Mira didn't get Called. The Order's lies killed her."
- The audience half-believes them. Genre instinct says the priests are lying.
- Nell: "How would you know?" Silence.
- At home: Nan asks Nell to find Tom's trace in the Weight-book for her, "so I know where he is". Hester scrubs the privy stones alone at night. Hester weighs Nell: 46.4. Since she went to the cellar, no bread has come. Four days.

**Act 3: The design.** Nell lays it out so the audience can follow.
- An echo, a fake and a god would all answer two-three-five-seven the same way. So send a count the Order never sends: **eleven**, the forbidden number.
- If the Order fakes the answers, eleven gets nothing, because they won't know it was sent.
- If eleven comes back, it's an echo.
- If something else comes back, someone out there is thinking.
- Why eleven, for Nell: all her life she went past seven to eleven, and Wells shushed her. She nearly says his name, and doesn't. The audience may feel it: the eleven is for him.

**Act 4: The haul.**
- At night Nell, Tau, Owen and three Changers slip into the Temple. The Great Stone takes all their weight. They haul eleven times, a minute a haul.
- Nell, starving, nearly blacks out on the rope; Tau takes her place for two hauls without a word.
- A Warden patrol nearly finds them, and they hide under the plinth.
- Theta watches the needles and logs. Done. They wait. Nothing.
- Theta: "See? Nothing. Nobody out there but priests."

**The twist.** The heretics' creed is a question none of them has ever asked of anything. The first real test in the cellar's history is made by the one person there who doesn't believe in the creed.

**The ending hook.** 3 a.m. Theta asleep in her chair, Nell alone with the needle. It trembles. She counts: eleven... twelve... thirteen. It stops.

**Gaps.**
- **Opened.** Who answered?
- **Closed.** What the Changers are: a second church, not the questioners.

### EPISODE 4: "Thirteen" (MAJOR REVERSAL 1)
*Nell has proof someone is listening, and the city turns her proof into a reason to die.*

**Cold open.**
- Dawn. The day Wardens bring the First Accountant the needle's paper drum: "Thirteen, from nowhere." The Temple guard swears nobody in Ream touched the Stone.
- Dimopoulos kneels on the cold floor. "A new number."

**Act 1: Proof.**
- Nell and Tau work it through. The Order didn't know about the eleven, so it could not have faked an answer. Theta's conspiracy dies.
- Someone outside is thinking, and thirteen is the next prime after eleven: the twins' game. Nell doesn't dare say "Wells". Tau: "Or the Ancestors." Nell: "The dead don't count."
- Theta won't accept it: "They answered you to trap you." To protect Tau, she tore the eleven off the drum before she fell asleep. The thirteen came after, and the day Wardens found it.

**Act 2: The Order reads it.**
- The Order sees thirteen with no eleven before it: a new number from the Ancestors.
- Dimopoulos: "They ask for thirteen." The Randall neither confirms nor denies.
- A **Joining** is proclaimed: thirteen elders to be Given early, alive. Volunteers come forward with joy. Nan is first: she takes the white robe out of her chest. She is going to Tom.
- In the background, the Sundrum rehearses the Autumn rite and fumbles the Jar's lid, muttering "against the sun, against the sun". The Randall takes it from him and opens it without looking.
- Nan, folding sheets: "Damp's back on the privy wall. Like the year of Burnt Row."

**Act 3: Nan.**
- Nell can't stop her without confessing, and the accused is tasted: confessing would expose her as Turned.
- She half-confesses to Nan: "Nan, it was me. I sent the eleven." Nan laughs and strokes her face: "Then the Ancestors spoke through you, love. Isn't that something." The doctrine absorbs even a confession.
- Nell goes to the Randall alone and offers herself: "Me. I sent it. Take me, not them." The Randall: "The Ancestors asked for elders, child." The Randall now knows who sent the eleven, and says nothing to anyone.
- Hester says nothing. Nan tells Lottie her last Pariah tale.
- The Joining: the gates barred until the needle settles. Thirteen elders in white, holding no hands, step into the Lift and diffuse. The needles rise and stay. "Favour."
- Nell writes GIVEN thirteen times.

**Act 4: The ledger.**
- Furious, Nell takes her curve to the vigil and, by the Tallykeeper's old right to speak, shows it to the congregation.
- The crowd cheers. "Twenty-four! The Ending comes!" Publishing the number changes nothing: every rise already has a home.
- Afterwards the Randall opens her private book for Nell alone. It holds the same curve, kept for forty years and more complete than Nell's, in a hand that reads the way we read.
- "Twenty-four in the hundred. When it is fifty, the coin is fair, and none of us will ever be parted again. Your brother is waiting." She glows.
- The one person who has all the numbers reads them as good news.

**The twist.** Evidence with no explanation only feeds the old story. Nell's test has added thirteen bodies to the Weight, and the numbers are the Randall's gospel.

**The ending hook.** That night, a new bundle of bread on Nell's desk, and the Randall sitting beside it in the dark. Three commands, none with a reason: "Eat. Close the Tally. Burn what leaves your body." Hester has weighed her that morning: 45.1.

**Gaps.**
- **Closed.**
  - Who answered? Someone thinking, outside. Who, exactly, stays open.
  - Who feeds Nell, and who rewrote her line? The Randall.
  - Does the Order know the numbers? It does, and welcomes them.
- **Opened.**
  - Who is the thinker?
  - Why would the Randall protect a liar? Why does she write our way?

### EPISODE 5: "Custody by Pantry"
*Her food now costs silence, while a test with paper shows the city's own Fast is killing children.*

**Cold open.** The grey film on the Abbott privy stones is up the wall now. Nothing will eat it. A neighbour's child has put her hand on it. Nell kept the first two commands and ignored the third, because it came with no reason.

**Act 1: Terms.**
- The Randall's sealed glasshouse behind the Temple: wrong-handed mint, wheat and beans, grown from the Tithe seeds. Brother Arkani tends them, and every scrap of waste is burned in a brazier.
- Nell eats a real meal for the first time in weeks and cries. The Randall does not eat with her.
- The terms: eat here, close the Tally, say nothing, and one day be Randall.
- In public, Nell closes her curve and stops asking.

**Act 2: Paper is a witness.**
- In the Lift's maintenance hours, Owen, Tau and Nell secretly send crates of cards printed with an R, each crate weighted to a crosser's weight.
- Crates weighted like a grown man (70 kg) come back about 1 in 5 reversed. Crates weighted like a fasted child (30 kg) come back nearly 2 in 5. More time in the dark means more turning. **The Fast is killing children**, and the tables are Hester's. Nell was its poster child.
- Brother Arkani, unasked: "One seed in four comes back right for the glasshouse now. In my mother's day, one in thirty. In her mother's, one in fifty."
- Pim catches them at the crates. "I'm reporting this." Nell: "Run it yourself first. If I'm wrong, you'll have your proof." He hesitates, and takes a crate.

**Act 3: The grey.**
- Hester understands in one look at the wall. That night she burns half their house down, calmly, room by room.
- Neighbours gather. The Randall arrives and names it damp rot. Covered up.

**Act 4: How did you know?**
- In the glasshouse at night Nell finds the Randall eating the same food. "You're..." "Hush." The Randall is Turned.
- Every Randall for generations has been: "The table is how we are kept. And how we keep."
- Nell asks, "Why did you tell me to burn it? You knew what would grow."
- The Randall tells her about Burnt Row. At twenty, as "Sigma", a reader of the Fable when it was still an open book, she laughed at the old Randall's burn rule and stopped keeping it. Seven people died, and the book was sealed. "I asked a question once. It killed seven people. So I stopped."
- Nell, quietly: "You didn't ask it anything. You just stopped burning." Neither of them says more. Writers must not expand this line.

**The twist.** The Randall is Turned, the office passes from Turned to Turned, and Burnt Row was her own body.

**The ending hook.**
- Midnight. The needles tremble in threes. Theta counts, off the log. Nell recognises the catechism game: leaf, line, word. She decodes a single word, one that sits in the Leaves' list of sources: **ABBOTT**.
- Morning: the day Wardens bring the drum to Dimopoulos. Ream lore says that when the Ancestors spell a name, that one is Called. He reads it as the Ancestors pointing at a hidden impurity in the Abbott house, and orders the household tasted before the city.
- The Randall stalls in public: "They spelled a house, child, not a name. At the Autumn Giving, not before." In private, to Nell: at the Autumn Giving she will hold the Jar herself and let Nell say her word, as long as the Tally stays closed.

**Gaps.**
- **Closed.**
  - Why the Randall feeds a liar: the office is passed from Turned to Turned, and the table is the leash.
  - Why the dog growled at her.
  - What caused Burnt Row.
  - What the Fast does: it kills.
- **Opened.**
  - Who is spelling her family's name?
  - Will the Abbotts be tasted? (A date: the Autumn Giving.)
  - Why is the rate rising? Still open.

### EPISODE 6: "The First Leaf" (MAJOR REVERSAL 2, part one)
*To read the holy relic, the city needs a Turned girl's eyes and a Turned girl's hand.*

**Cold open.**
- The First Leaf's block of stone at the Lift's foot: three tonnes, a "wall of weight".
- Children lay mint sprigs on it. The gloss beneath reads "Weight keeps the holy."
- Nell watches the Randall's white stole brush the stone as she passes.

**Act 1: Why, and how.**
- **Why.** Her brother was sent away under a verse. Nell needs to know whether anyone ever *tested* it, or whether it was only ever said. The copies can't tell her where a verse came from; the "St." in the archive copy says the original once did.
- **Why now.** The Abbott house will be tasted at the Autumn Giving. Theta says ABBOTT is an Order trap. Nell needs to know what the book really says before she stands in front of the Jar.
- **How.** "Send a hand, not a body" is a Plot line. In the maintenance hours Owen sets the field on Nell's arm alone. Her unpinned hand passes into solid stone and lifts the light relic out. The block never moves. The Order's wall of weight was the wrong defence, because the relic itself is light.
- **The rules, shown before the danger.** If Owen closes the field while her hand is inside the stone, the hand is set down in the rock. If the field runs out while her hand is still in the dark, the hand scatters.
- The sequence plays as a ratchet: tingling fingertips, Owen's count, a Warden's footsteps, a false alarm, then the real collapse a heartbeat after she pulls free.
- She opens her fist. The relic was in the dark beside the Weight for most of a minute, a separate thing on its own chance. Everyone in the room knows what the crates showed: the longer a thing is in the dark, the worse its odds.

**Act 2: Power.**
- Tau lifts the Randall's stole from the vestry. Its silver tooth fits the Lift's port exactly. The glass wakes.
- Before anything else, Nell holds its glowing letters beside the plates' stamped numbers, which have not crossed since the founding. They run the same way. It did not turn. (A small test against a sample, on screen: the method in miniature. A partial release.)
- A lock with four digits. Nell types 2, 3, 5, 7. It opens. The password had become a prayer.

**Act 3: The text.**
- To Tau it reads backwards, and he needs the mirror. To Nell, and to us, it reads plainly.
- The Three Voices have words:
  - green: *Established: real physics or mathematics. You can look it up.*
  - amber: *Stipulated: the film's own axiom, the place where the science runs out and the story takes over, announced rather than smuggled.*
  - grey: *Derived.*
- "St." was Stipulated. The holiest voice is the guesses.
- The undotted "Silent Voice" sits under headings that read "In the plot", which means *in the story*. The handhold scene is a moment in a film that was never made. "For the ending only" is a note on how to end it.
- Tau: "So it's false." Nell: "Or it's a story that happens to be right. The dog's in the Plot too, and the dog growls." A tag says where a verse came from, not whether it holds. Only a test can say that.
- The Changers' psalm about the cube is there, and beside it a green paragraph: Ehrenfest's inverse-cube law, *real physics or mathematics*. The true part of the book, which nobody ever used.
- At the bottom, the Sources: *Edwin Abbott... H. G. Wells... Lewis Carroll... Ehrenfest... Randall, Sundrum...* Nell touches her own surname. Their names are a reading list.

**Act 4: The slider.**
- "Field drift 50%." Nell touches it and it moves. The Ending was a setting nobody chose.
- She taps "Cross twenty times". The little tally fills itself in: Crossings 20, Mirrored 9, Observed rate 45%. It is a game. The book never said the world's rate was fifty. It asked the reader to find it.
- Beneath it: *"The crosser cannot feel the drift. The statisticians can — after enough people have crossed. That number is the most valuable thing in the film."* Nell understands what the Tithe was: the founders' way of finding that number, one crossing from every young person. Ream kept the experiment and threw away its readout.
- A gallery icon labelled *Day One*. She taps it. The Warden's bell rings the end of the maintenance hour, and Owen has to cut the port. Black glass.

**The twist.** The holiest verses are a storyteller's guesses, and the Tithe that kills children was the founders' count with its answer thrown away. The audience, who learned the handhold verse as law in episode 1, made the city's mistake, and does not yet know whether the verse is true.

**The ending hook.**

## 6. Twist schedule

The ratchet rule: no two neighbouring twists are the same kind. The kinds used are:
- **L**: a lie or secret uncovered;
- **R**: a rule was never a law;
- **M**: a reading means something else;
- **C**: a cause is revealed;
- **W**: a world or cosmology reveal;
- **Q**: a question turned on the questioner.

| Ep | Twist (kind) | What the audience knew before | Plants, and where | What holds it in place (why a swap breaks things) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The wrong twin is exiled, because the right twin lied (L) | Ahead of everyone but Nell: we saw her taste caraway | Handhold verse recited over every pair (1, act 2); "The mint alone is witness" closes the signature test (1, act 3); Wells's inaudible words (1, act 3); the two believers garlanded (1, act 3) | The twins must end the season in each other's worlds. ## 9. The ending, and what it settles

### What is settled
- Wells is home and fed. Nell is fed, in the world where her body fits. They shared one meal first.
- The cause is named by the people themselves and repaired:
  - The Giving has stopped, and the dead are buried in the Old Graves again.
  - The door has moved.
  - The dash has become a number, with a prediction beside it that failed and was left failed, and a child's question beneath it.
  - The Tithe of children is replaced by crates of cards. Nobody crosses who doesn't choose to.
  - The Passing is over as exile, and crossing is now a choice.
  - The Turned can live openly on the glasshouse crops.
- The Randall's past, the founders, the dots, the Tithe's purpose and what "Refused" and "Received" meant are all known.
- **How the normal has moved.**
  - From "The rate is the Ancestors'" to UNTIL WE KNOW BETTER.
  - From a Randall who recites scripture to a Randall who reads the rate aloud in the square every morning.
  - From a city that hides its questions to a nine-year-old who chalks one on the public board.
  - From a heroine who said "mint" to one who says "I'm wrong" in front of everyone.

### What it cost
- **The dead.**
  - Mira, Aaron Dimopoulos, and the third Called child.
  - Nan and the twelve others Joined with her.
  - The eleven killed in the crush.
  - Every Straight who starved.
  - Three centuries of the Called, the Passed and the Given, who hang in the dark beside the old plinth for as long as there is a Ream.
- **Places and work.**
  - The Temple can never be used again. In the empty hall the needle still reads fifteen hundred tonnes.
  - Hester's house is half burned, and her life's work is nailed up as WRONG.
- **People.**
  - The twins live in different worlds.
  - The Randall loses her office. She gives up her one chance to go to her brother.
  - Theta loses Tau's trust.
  - Nell's prediction failed in public.

### The residue
- Wells's sneeze, and "THE GREY?" on the board. The new answer's first untested change has already come home, carried by the boy it saved.
- On Earth, "Burn those": Nell has carried Ream's life to a world where it is the mirror-life, and the fence has always been about exactly that.
- Minthome's small Weight at the Mother, and Kit's thirty years of counting it in secret. The same doom has begun on the other side.
- Whether Ream will keep counting once the fear fades.

### The theme, as the world's answer
The world asks what becomes of a people who only do what used to work. The season answers:
- What works changes the world, and the rules cannot see the change.
- A right person alone fails. Kit shouted the answer, and then sent a word nobody could check. Young Iris doubted without testing. Nell said more than her test showed.
- What saves Ream is a practice owned by many: cards instead of children, a board with a prediction that can fail (and does, and stays failed), and a child allowed to ask "why four?"
- That practice has to keep going, because the world is already moving again.

### The hook for Season 2
- Nell, in Minthome, with Kit's book in her hand, steps into the path of a Giving and says "Wait."
- Season 2 is set on the other side. Nell is the stranger who can read both hands' writing. She knows that an answer carried in from outside is only something someone says, so she brings a count, not a verdict, and has to help Minthome build its own.
- Beyond the fence lies Earth, and the fence was built against people like her.

## 10. Rules the physics must never break

1. **Only weight crosses between worlds.**
   - No light, no sound, no touch, no voices.
   - Every message is mass moved by someone. It is slow: one haul of the Great Stone takes about a minute, and 89 hauls take a night.
   - A stone is felt only by needles at the same spot on the other world. The Temple talks to the Landing; the Graves talk to the station.
   - The channel proves that mass moved. It never proves who moved it, or why.
2. **A crossing is short and set by weight.**
   - Seconds ≈ 700 ÷ kilograms.
   - Lighter lasts longer.
   - The ring sets down only what is within its reach when the field is closed. A field left to run out sets nothing down, and whatever is still in the dark stays there, for good.
3. **Nobody can feel, see or steer a turn.**
   - A turn is fixed when the crossing ends, and found only by a sample that never crossed: the Jar's mint, a printed card, a seed, the plates.
   - No amount of looking at a person decides who turned.
4. **Each separate thing takes its own chance.** A living body turns whole, with everything living in it and on it. Cards, seeds, clothes, a phone in a fist: each on its own. One solid thing turns whole. A hand still joined to a body on the world cannot turn.
5. **Walls don't stop a crosser. The weight budget does.**
   - An unpinned hand passes through stone, skin or a lock, and can lift anything light enough.
   - Heavy things are safe only because they are heavy.
6. **In the dark a crosser sees only by their own bubble's light:** the insides of everything within arm's reach. Beyond that, nothing. The Weight can never be seen, only weighed.
7. **A Turned body:**
   - can't use Ream's food or medicine;
   - catches no Ream disease;
   - lives on water, spirits and tallow for months, not years;
   - lives long only on turned crops;
   - its waste breeds grey mould that must be burned.
8. **Pull across the gap fades with the cube of distance.** Near matters enormously; far hardly at all.
9. **The Lost stay where they were lost, fully heavy.** They drift only slowly. They do not think, speak, choose or answer.
10. **A handhold changes nothing.** Two crossers turn or don't turn independently.
11. **Doors at the same place on neighbouring worlds line up.** A crossing from the Mother comes out at the Old Graves, and the reverse. No Lift is needed on the landing side.
12. **Anything a crosser carries crosses:** air, food, germs. A person from Minthome can carry Earth life, which is mirror-life to Ream, and the reverse.
13. **Base drift is about 2 in 100.** Every rise above that must be caused by weight near a door, as the ledger says. Never by luck, mood, prayer or purity.
14. **No new rule is added in secret.** A new rule enters this plan's ledger first, and the show declares it once on screen.

## 11. Notes for episode writers

- **Every scene does at least two named jobs from this plan:** a plant, a turn, a bond, a gap opened or closed, a world ripple. A scene that survives being swapped for any other scene is decoration. Keep it only if it is short, deliberate surplus that makes the world real.
- **The theme is never spoken.**
  - Test each line: if deleting it leaves the audience still understanding the point from what happened, delete it.
  - Only the Fable's lines may state an idea outright, and only as quotation, by a character who is reciting, and only in the sense section 1.3 gives them.
- **The doom test.** For any disaster, ask which unquestioned practice made it more likely. If the answer is bad luck or one bad person, rewrite. (The crush passes because of the barred gates; the Callings pass because of the Fast and the Giving; the lost tags pass because copyists chose.)
- **The Randall test.** In every episode she appears in, she must be right about something, or kind to someone who can't help her.
- **The Wells rule.** Wells is never a prize. From episode 7 on, every episode gives him at least one choice that changes the plot.

- **The ratchet.** Each episode ends higher than it began, with at least one partial release: a laugh, a meal, the dog lying down, a relic that did not turn. Never two twists of the same kind in a row (see the key in section 6).
