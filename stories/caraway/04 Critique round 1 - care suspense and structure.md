# Critique of the season plan: craft (care, suspense and structure)

**Lens.** This review uses only the owner's Bond, Anticipation, Gap and Implied World theories. Theme and hard-to-vary are covered in `40-critique-theme.md`. Where a fix here touches one of that file's findings, I say whether the two agree. None of these fixes adds a new rule to the ledger.

**Verdict.** The engine is strong, and so is most of the cast. The Randall is the best-built character in the plan: in every episode she is right about something or kind to someone, she is specific, and her history mirrors the heroine's. Hester's silence is a slow-burning bond. The run from eleven to thirteen to the Joining is a superb ratchet. 91 is the best proof beat in the season. The Weight is never seen, only weighed, so the monster stays in the dark.

The weak points are these:
- the relationship the whole show rests on, the twins, is never paid off;
- the two biggest world twists leak before they land;
- the heroine acts out of character at the act-two climax;
- the hunger clock is switched off in episode 2.

**Keep exactly as planned:**
- the Randall's long look at Nell's face, and "The hand that reached";
- the dog growling at the Randall in episode 1: the audience makes Ream's own mistake ("the dog knows") by reading it as villainy;
- Hester burning half her house;
- the hand through stone, played as a ratchet;
- the needle walk;
- "Let him speak."

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The two biggest world twists leak before they land, so episodes 9 and 10 confirm instead of surprise

**Where.**
- Episode 1 hook: Nell's hand now writes our way.
- Episode 7, act 1: Minthome's signs, CHIRAL HOLD and MIRROR-LIFE CONTAINMENT, in plain modern English.
- Episode 8, Minthome thread: the Mother Giving; Kit shows Wells his tally.
- Episode 9: the Graves stand over the Mother.
- Episode 10, act 3: "Why four?"

**Problem.**
- **(a) Earth eyes.** Once Nell turns and writes our way, and once Minthome's signs appear in modern English, an attentive viewer knows by episode 7 that Minthome is our world and that Ream's founders came from it. Day One in episode 9 can only confirm this.
- **(b) Why four.** In episode 8 the audience watches Minthome Give its dead at the Mother and sees Kit's rising tally. In episode 9 it is told that the Graves sit over the Mother. The sum is done before Lottie asks. Major reversal 3 is a reversal for Nell only.

**Why it matters.** The owner asked for "mind bending", and the brief asks for three major reversals. By Gap theory, a surprise is a gap the audience did not know was there. Two of the three major turns arrive already solved.

**Fix.** Own the leaks, and put a layer on top of each that nobody could guess.
- **(a) Day One.**
  - Make the first readable sign in episode 7 the audience's own moment. We are ahead of Wells, who can't read it.
  - Then give Day One what no one could infer. The founders are weeping on the hill, reading the phone page aloud to each other. One tastes the mint and laughs, "The ending writes itself!" Another scrolls: "Tap the stack at the top right." Someone reads the footer and they all laugh: "Written for a phone screen."
  - Ream's Passing refrain, its blessing and its Amen began as the founders' jokes on their first day of joy.
  - This explains the liturgy fossils; the plan never says how they became liturgy. And one image carries the season's idea: a line that was once literally true, copied without its history, is now sung over children sent to die.
- **(b) Why four.**
  - Keep the audience ahead on Minthome's Giving, and play the episode 10 cold open as suspense: we watch Nell nail up "IF MORE, I AM WRONG" knowing what hangs beside that door.
  - Put the surprise on Kit instead. Cut "Kit shows Wells his secret tally" from episode 8; Kit's own character sheet says he has told no one. In episode 9, Wells moves Adamala with his question alone.
  - In episode 10, Jory hauls WE. DIFFUSE. HERE. TOO., against Kit's fear.
  - In the final scene Kit meets Nell with the lamp and hands her thirty years of the Mother's rising count. "I never sent it. I was afraid of her."
  - The man who shouted "Move the door!" about Ream's door for forty-seven years kept a dash for his own door, just as his twin did. The Randall's private ledger in episode 4 is the plant. Swap in any other final twist and the twin mirror breaks.

### 2. The twins' bond is never paid off

**Where.**
- Episode 1: the only episode with both twins awake on screen together.
- Episode 10, acts 4–5: they pass in the dark, mouth two numbers, and part.
- Wells's character sheet: he has no want before the Tithe.

**Problem.**
- The show's central relationship gets one episode of building and no reunion.
- Wells's care rests on a prime-race joke, "Shh, Nell", and a held hand.
- His listed wants all begin after the Passing.

**Why it matters.**
- Bond theory: the Miss rung ("I'd grieve if they were gone") needs time and shared history. By the finale, the audience has waited nine episodes to see these two in a room.
- Anticipation theory: holding back the reunion for ever is a release that never comes.
- The last image spends the relationship on an idea (lights touching in the dark) instead of the two of them.

**Fix.**
- **Episode 1.** Give Wells a want of his own. Tonight, after the Tithe, he will haul the Great Stone at the Answering for the first time, and he shows Nell his new hauler's gloves. In act 4 other men haul the Stone for his own Passing, and the gloves are in Nell's hand.
- **Episode 7.** He hauls thirteen barehanded "until his palms split". The split palms are already in the plan; now they have a cause.
- **Episode 10.** Cut the crossing at the same moment. This also retires stipulation 6, which theme finding 12 suggests.
  - Wells lands first.
  - Then one scene at Hester's half-burned table. Wells eats bread; Nell eats a tallow cake. It is the first meal they have shared since episode 1, and now they cannot eat the same food.
  - Prime race: "Eighty-nine." "Ninety-one." Nell gives him back the gloves.
  - Then someone asks who will carry the question to Minthome. The table goes silent. Nell: "Me."
  - Wells's word at the Mint Test is now in her mouth. Her flaw, letting others pay, is reversed by her own choice.
  - Only then come her crossing, Kit's lamp, the procession, and "Wait."

### 3. Episode 8: the heroine speaks without her test, though she has one

**Where.**
- Episode 8, act 2: "She has no plan, and no test anyone can watch."
- Section 3, step 8.
- Episode 5, act 2: the card test.
- Episode 7: Pim's rerun of the cards.

**Problem.**
- By episode 8 Nell owns a test anyone can repeat: the crates of cards. Pim has already rerun it in secret and got her numbers. So "no test" contradicts the plan's own episode 5.
- Nothing pushes the most careful, hiding person in the show onto the Lift steps:
  - in episode 4 she learned that evidence without an explanation only feeds the old story;
  - in episode 5 the Randall told her that one question once killed seven people.

**Why it matters.**
- Bond theory: a surprise that was not in her all along breaks the bond. It reads as "out of character".
- The lesson this beat carries, that a right person alone fails, gets muddied. She had the means and did not use them, and the plan never says why.

**Fix.** Give her a trigger that is close and personal, and make the flaw exact.
- At the Autumn Giving, Dimopoulos announces the next re-tuning: next spring's Tithe will fast twice as long, lightest first. It is Bea's year.
- Nell climbs the steps with a crate of cards in her arms. She begins with what the cards prove: the Fast kills.
- Then, facing twelve thousand people and her mother, she jumps to what they don't prove: "The Ancestors are killing our children." The crowd could check the first claim; nobody can check the second. The crate stays shut at her feet.
- Her failure is now overreach. She wanted the big truth believed more than she wanted the small one tested.
- Episode 9's "Who else has a number?" and episode 10's "Cards first" become her correcting this exact mistake.
- This fits theme finding 7, where barred gates make a rite, not panic, cause the crush.

### 4. The hunger clock is switched off in episode 2, and the plan disagrees with itself about when

**Where.**
- Episode 2 hook: a loaf that "tastes right".
- The season clock: "Nell starves on tallow until the Randall feeds her at the end of episode 4".
- Episode 5 cold open: "real food for the first time in weeks".

**Problem.**
- These three statements disagree.
- Worse, the show's best fear device is used up in the last minute of episode 2. The Anticipation theory says fear is strongest when an everyday necessity becomes dangerous, and eating cannot be avoided. After that loaf the audience cannot tell whether Nell is still starving.

**Why it matters.**
- Unless her hunger is visibly tracked, the "time" part of suspense drops to nothing across episodes 2–4.
- The Randall's leash, the Fourth Direction's "custody by pantry", is explained in episode 5 instead of felt.

**Fix.** Let the audience feel the leash before anyone names it.
- The episode 2 loaf is a single loaf. After it, bread appears only on days Nell obeys: she leaves a ledger closed, or doesn't ask. When she digs, it stops.
- **Episode 3.** She goes to the Changers, and no bread comes for four days. She hauls the eleven starving and nearly blacks out on the rope, which gives the haul a real ratchet.
- **A visible clock.** Hester weighs her once an episode, and the numbers go on screen: 51.0 (episode 1), 48.0 (2), 46.4 (3), 45.1 (4).
- **Episode 4 hook.** "Eat. And close the Tally." now names a rule the audience has already worked out, and episode 5's "first real food in weeks" becomes true.

### 5. Mira and Nan die to hurt other people

**Where.**
- Episode 1 cold open: Mira is Called in the first minute.
- Episode 4: Nan Joins.
- Nan does not appear in episodes 2–3.

**Problem.**
- Mira is a stranger when she vanishes. The cold open is spectacle, not suspense, and she exists only to give Tau a wound.
- Nan has one scene, a bedtime tale, before she dies. The season's first beloved death, the moment the audience should learn nobody is safe, falls on someone it barely knows.
- The Bond theory calls both of these "fridging": killing a character only to motivate someone else.

**Why it matters.**
- Anticipation theory: care has to come before the threat.
- Episode 4 is the season's one early breach of the genre's promise that the people we love are safe. It breaks nothing if the audience doesn't miss Nan.

**Fix.**
- **Mira.**
  - She is Nell's senior on the Tally. Before she steps in, she hands Nell her chalk, which is why today is Nell's first official mark: Nell writes CALLED with Mira's chalk.
  - She has fasted in secret to be light, "a pure offering", and she hopes to be Called: "Don't wait up."
  - Theta counts past her time: "Fourteen... sixteen..."
  - Now her death comes from her own belief: the Fast before the Fast. Her lightness is a fair plant for the episode 5 crates.
  - In episode 2, when Dimopoulos says the Called were too heavy, Tau says: "Mira was the lightest girl in her year." Dimopoulos: "Then she was Called for her purity." The audience watches the doctrine give opposite cases opposite stories.
- **Nan.**
  - Plant a want in episode 1 and keep it alive in episodes 2–3: she wants to go to her son Tom, Given in Year 294.
  - She keeps his haul rope. At the Temple she lays her palm on the needle case "to be near him".
  - In episode 3 she asks Nell to find Tom's trace in the Weight-book.
  - The Joining in episode 4 is then her own wish, granted by Nell's test. Episode 7's "Da is part of it" includes her. This also plants the Joining, as theme finding 14 asks.
- **Episode 4: make the choice cost something.**
  - Sending a forbidden count is a Passing offence, and the accused is tasted, so confessing would expose Nell as Turned.
  - Nell goes to the Randall and offers herself in Nan's place: "Me." The Randall: "The Ancestors asked for elders, child."
  - That refusal is what brings the Randall to Nell's desk with bread that night. It also plants the "Me" Nell says, and has accepted, in episode 10 (finding 2).

### 6. Episode 1 spends too much of the audience's attention in a classroom

**Where.** Episode 1, act 1, before the plot starts. In order: the catechism game; a schoolroom lesson with four teaching plates and three verses; the dash; the weighing; the Pariah tale; the prime race; the Randall and the dog; the Litany; Burnt Row; the plate numbers.

**Problem.** About fifteen new things and a dozen names arrive before the Tithe. Several come through a teacher explaining the world to children, which is the info-dump the Implied World theory warns against.

**Why it matters.** Every new rule uses attention the twins need. The meta device needs the audience to believe only two verses as law, the handhold verse and the Ending verse, not the whole book.

**Fix.**
- **Cut the schoolroom and teach by rite.** The Randall recites the handhold verse over each of the twenty pairs as they enter the ring: "No touching. One comes back mirrored. The handhold is what did it." By the twins' turn the audience knows it by heart, the way a Reamer does.
  - The teaching plates are painted on the ring's wall, in the background.
  - The Ending verse is sung at the Passing, where it already is.
- **Move the cube-law verse to episode 3**, as a psalm the Changers chant in their cellar. It is the true verse that holds the cure, recited by people who never use it, and it pays off in the episode 8 needle walk.
- **Name eight in episode 1:** Nell, Wells, Hester, Nan, Mira, Tau, the Randall, and the dog. Pim, Lottie, Owen, the Sundrum and Dimopoulos stay faces until episode 2.
- **Keep the Litany and Burnt Row in the background**, as sound and a blackened street, never a scene.

### 7. Episodes 5–7 have no clock, and the episode 8 exposure comes from nowhere

**Where.**
- Episode 5 hook: ABBOTT, counted "off the log".
- Episode 6: "Theta says ABBOTT is an Order trap."
- Episode 8, act 3: Theta's trade, and the Jar before the crowd.

**Problem.**
- After the Joining, nothing has a date until the Great Tithe in episode 8.
  - The rate clock ("before she is thirty") runs in years.
  - Wells's clock is unknown until episode 7.
- ABBOTT is frightening for one cut, then harmless, because the Order never sees it.
- Then in episode 8 Nell's secret comes out through a trade the audience never saw coming.

**Why it matters.** Anticipation theory: suspense needs a time term, and fear should climb the ladder (dread, then suspense, then horror) rather than jump.

**Fix.**
- **Put ABBOTT on the day Wardens' drum, as thirteen was.** Dimopoulos reads it as the Ancestors asking for "the Abbott who reached", and orders the Abbott household tasted in front of the city at the Autumn Giving.
- **The Randall stalls in public:** "They spelled a house, child, not a name."
- **In private she tightens the leash:** she can keep Nell off the list only while the Tally stays closed.
- **Every episode now races a date.** The relic heist in episode 6 and the 89-haul in episode 7 run against a threat with a day on it. The Jar before the crowd in episode 8 is a threat hung for three episodes and finally dropped.
- **Theta's betrayal becomes a smaller, crueller act:** telling Dimopoulos which Abbott.
- **Hester is tasted first:** mint. Then she stands silent while Nell is tasted. Her silence now has a price we watch her pay.
- This fits theme finding 15b.

### 8. Episode 2 has no set piece: hiding is a list of habits

**Where.** Episode 2, acts 1–2: the mirror shard, the faked handwriting, "Pim is suspicious", and the Lightening declared.

**Problem.** The secret is never nearly exposed. The only threat is a suspicious glance.

**Why it matters.** The Anticipation theory says: show the threat's rules, then make every scene a guessing game about when they will strike. The dog is the season's best rule for exposure ("the dog knows"), and the plan drops it after episode 1.

**Fix.** Stage the Lightening's first public weighing in the square.
- **That morning Hester gives Plattner to the Order's kennels without a word.** The audience reads it as a silent sacrifice.
- **The whole city is weighed, and the thinnest are honoured.** Dimopoulos holds up Nell, down three kilos: "The Tallykeeper's girl shows us the way." Children copy her.
  - The rite that kills children disguises her starvation as piety.
  - In episode 5, when the crates show that the Fast kills, she learns she was its poster child.
- **The Order's knower-dogs walk the line.** Plattner reaches Nell and growls.
  - The Randall has stepped to her side. The handler takes the growl as aimed at the Randall and cuffs the dog.
  - Only the audience and the Randall know which of them he meant.
  - This plants the feeder (episode 4) and the Randall being Turned (episode 5), and it puts episode 1's growl at the Randall to work.
- **Episode 8: Tau smuggles Plattner out of the kennels and into the cell.**
  - He growls. Nell holds out her hand. He sniffs for a long time, then lies down.
  - He is the one living thing we watch adjust to a change, on screen, in real time.

### 9. Episode 7's twists are the same move three times, and the audience already holds two of them

**Where.**
- The twist table for episode 7: Wells alive (M); 91 (M); REFUSED meant ARRIVED and the Weight is the dead (M, C).
- Section 6's own rule: "no two neighbouring twists of the same kind".

**Problem.**
- The cold open shows Wells arriving. The audience knows REFUSED meant arrived by minute three, so act 4 repeats it for Nell only.
- "The Weight is the city's own dead" is Ream's stated belief from episode 1: "they stay with us: you can weigh them."
- All three are the same kind of twist, "a reading means something else", back to back.

**Why it matters.** Tension goes slack in the episode that breaks the season open.

**Fix.** Aim acts 4–5 at what the audience doesn't know yet.
- **RECEIVED meant LOST.** Open act 4 on the families of the two garlanded believers from episode 1, lighting candles at a home shrine under their RECEIVED trace. Then show the scraped labels. The children whose families wept for joy are hanging in the dark.
- **Play the Weight reveal as horror, not correction.** The doctrine was literally true: the Ancestors are exactly where the priests said, and heavy, and Da's line is among them.
- That makes it a cause twist (C), not another M, and it restores the plan's own rule.

### 10. Episodes 5 and 9 are overloaded, and episodes 8 and 9 open with the same chalk-in-a-cellar scene

**Where.**
- **Episode 5:**
  - the Randall is Turned;
  - the office passes from Turned to Turned;
  - the Fast kills;
  - the handhold verse is false;
  - Burnt Row was her body;
  - ABBOTT;
  - and a house fire.
- **Episode 9:**
  - the Graves;
  - Year 9;
  - the plate count;
  - Pariahs were the cured;
  - the Randall's public "Caraway";
  - the Litany;
  - Day One;
  - and a heist.
- **Episode 8, act 1, and episode 9, act 1:** both are deductions over records.

**Problem.**
- Episode 9 has seven reveals and episode 5 has six, while episode 3 has one.
- "Why would the Randall protect a liar?" opens at the end of episode 4 and closes in episode 5's cold open: a mystery that lasts one cut.

**Why it matters.**
- Implied World theory: the audience's attention is a budget.
- Gap theory: a reveal needs room. Day One, the season's quietest and strangest image, is squeezed between the city's turn and a heist.

**Fix.**
- **Episode 4 hook.** The Randall gives three commands, none with a reason: "Eat. Close the Tally. Burn what leaves your body."
- **Episode 5.**
  - Open on the grey mould instead of the glasshouse. Nell kept the first two commands and ignored the third, because it came with no reason.
  - The Randall covers it up ("damp rot"). Nell eats at her table without knowing why.
  - Hold "You're..." "Hush" until act 4, together with Burnt Row: one reveal, with its reason.
  - Move the pair test to episode 6, act 4. Theme finding 6 proposes the same.
- **Episode 8.**
  - End the needle walk at the river gate by the Old Graves. There the needle reads 1, and Nell remembers Nan's pale things "walking out of the Graves".
  - "Where" is found on foot, in the cold open. Act 1 keeps "what the Weight does".
- **Episode 9.** Act 1 shrinks to the Year 9 log line and the plates. That frees an act for Day One.

### 11. The Randall's past is told three times

**Where.**
- Episode 5, act 4: Burnt Row.
- Episode 8, act 5: she "tells everything": her Tithe, her lie, Kit, Burnt Row, "Pass him".
- Episode 9 cold open: shows "Pass him".

**Problem.**
- The episode 9 cold open is her darkest moment, and it arrives already told.
- The episode 8 cell scene is pure explanation.

**Why it matters.**
- Gap theory: what is held back gives the telling its power.
- Bond theory: we understand a character better by seeing inside her than by hearing her confess.

**Fix.**
- **Episode 8 cell.**
  - She tells only her Tithe and her lie: Nell's own story, forty-eight years early. Then: "And then I did something worse." "What?" "Hush."
  - Give the scene an action instead. For the first time, the keeper asks the heretic a question: "What would you have asked him, if I had let him speak?" It plants the Randall's line in episode 10.
- **Episode 9 cold open.** "Pass him" now arrives unannounced.
- **Episode 6 hook.** Give "It wasn't the rule. He asked me" a trigger.
  - Nell, holding the relic: "You sent him away for a line in a story."
  - The Randall answers the accusation. It is in character, since she is superb at fitting facts to her story, and this fact is true.

### 12. The finale's climax has almost no uncertainty

**Where.** Episode 10, acts 4–5: Wells crosses weighted with Hester's stones, lands, and tastes mint.

**Problem.**
- Once the new door works, Wells's return feels safe.
- The only risks are a shout of "Pariah!", which is answered at once, and a low turning rate.

**Why it matters.** Anticipation theory: suspense = care × threat × uncertainty × time. At the moment care peaks, uncertainty is near zero.

**Fix.** Put the risk in Hester's number, the thread the plan already has.
- **In episode 9 the ballast goes to Minthome count by count on the Stone.** The count breaks off at twenty when Adamala's people break in, and nobody knows where it would have stopped.
- **The risk cuts both ways.**
  - Too little stone means longer in the dark beside the Mother's small Weight.
  - Too much means too short a crossing: he falls short of the world and stays in the dark.
- **Wells chooses the weight himself** (the Wells rule), and he doesn't say what he chose.
- **At the Graves the whole city watches the new needle.**
  - It rises as he enters the dark. It holds, and the audience has learned what rise-and-stay means.
  - Then it falls. Theta, at the needle, says the founders' word aloud: "Arrived."
- Then come "Pariah!", "Let him speak", and the Jar.

### 13. Wells is a prize for six episodes, and Nell chases other things

**Where.**
- Episodes 2–6: the curve, the Changers, the relic.
- Section 4: "He is never a prize... from episode 7."

**Problem.**
- Nell's want is to bring Wells home. Between episodes 1 and 6, though, none of her actions aims at him.
- The audience follows her out of curiosity, not wanting.

**Why it matters.** Bond theory: wanting drives attention. The audience wants something on a character's behalf only if it can see her wanting it.

**Fix.** Hook her want into the plot early.
- **Episode 2.** She asks Warden Dvali (Theta) to see Wells's REFUSED trace in the Weight-book, and is turned away: "The Weight-book is for Wardens."
- **Episode 3.** That is why she follows Tau to the cellar: Theta is there. The eleven is secretly for Wells, and she almost says so.
- **Episode 7.** When Theta finally opens the Weight-book, the payoff was set up in episode 2.

### 14. Three secondary figures are only functions

**Where.** First Accountant Dimopoulos, Mother Adamala, Jory.

**Problem.**
- Dimopoulos proclaims every re-tuning but has no personal stake in any of them.
- Adamala keeps a rite with no grief behind it.
- Jory is only "the first to help Wells".

**Why it matters.** Bond theory: indifference is the enemy, and villains need care too. The plan says Dimopoulos "is what the old rules look like under pressure"; that needs one scene to be true on screen.

**Fix.**
- **Dimopoulos.**
  - One line in episode 2: his grandson, a big lad, was Called last spring. "Too heavy to get home" is his grief speaking.
  - In episode 10 he asks for the second crate to be weighted to his grandson's weight, 74 kilos. It comes back 5.
- **Adamala.**
  - She Gave her husband first, in Year 270, "so he would stay near": the same love that drove Ottilie.
  - When Wells asks what the Mother does to the ones they give it (episode 9), she is thinking of her husband.
- **Jory.**
  - Minthome-born, he has never seen a Straight leave the Long House alive.
  - He hauls WE. DIFFUSE. HERE. TOO. himself, against Adamala and against Kit's fear (finding 1).
  - He is Minthome's Lottie, and the bridge to Season 2.

### 15. The grey mould has no ladder

**Where.**
- Episode 5, act 3: it appears, and is burned the same night.
- Episode 10: THE GREY?

**Problem.** This threat lives in the heroine's own body and home, the strongest refuge the Anticipation theory says a story can take away. It appears and is dealt with inside one act.

**Why it matters.** The season's last lingering fear rests on it, and the audience has met it only once.

**Fix.** Let it climb.
- **Episode 2:** a grey fuzz on the candle wick Nell saves in her drawer.
- **Episode 3:** Hester scrubs the privy stones, alone, at night.
- **Episode 4:** Nan, the week of her Joining: "Damp's back. Like the year of Burnt Row."
- **Episode 5:** it is up the wall, and a neighbour's child has touched it. Then the fire.

Now the sneeze in episode 10 lands at the top of a ladder the audience has climbed.

### 16. Tau's love is claimed, not shown

**Where.**
- Section 4: "He loves Nell."
- Episode 9: "Under a plate, Nell tells Tau she won't stay."

**Problem.** There is one romantic beat in the season. Otherwise Tau fetches records, lifts the stole and smuggles the dog.

**Why it matters.** Bond theory: a relationship that is claimed but never shown is where the "connection" lever breaks. His staying behind in episode 10 costs nothing if we never saw what he wanted.

**Fix.**
- **A weekly gesture, taken from the plan's own list of everyday details.** The Wardens wash the names of the Called off the Lift steps each week, and every week Tau chalks MIRA back.
  - In episode 8, when he learns Mira is part of the Weight, he stops.
  - In episode 10 he chalks her name on the new public board, as a line in the count, with her year.
- **One romantic beat per episode from episode 3.** The better mirror he hands her is the first.
- **Episode 10.** Nell leaves him her notebooks, written our way since episode 1, and her mirror, so he can read her working.

### 17. Some promises don't match their emphasis

**Where.** Section 8, mysteries and wonders; episode 6, act 4; episode 9.

**Problems, and a fix for each.**
- **Two promises for one question.** The lights on Minthome's far hills (a wonder) and what lies beyond the fence (a Season 2 mystery) are the same question. Fold the lights into the fence mystery, or cut them.
- **An answer with no scene.** "Why the dash was written" is marked closed in episode 9, but no episode 9 beat closes it. Add one line: the Randall reads Ottilie's full Year 131 entry from her private ledger. "The count says two. The Leaves say fifty. The Leaves are older."
- **A question left off the table.** The dog's growl at the Randall is pressed in episode 1 but missing from the mystery table. Pay it off at the episode 2 weighing (finding 8).
- **A convenient accident.** The First Leaf's "battery dies" just as Nell reaches Day One.
  - Tie it to the clock already running: the maintenance hour ends, the Warden's bell rings, and Owen has to cut the port.
  - The hook then presses Day One, so it is a mystery and belongs in that table.
