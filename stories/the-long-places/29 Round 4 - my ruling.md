## Fix (5 findings, each a clause or a sentence)

1. **VII to IX: the chamber is never revisited, and nothing on the page stops anyone going back.**
   - *The problem:* the checker confirms it, from the story and people critics, and I reverse my round-1 call. The reasons I accepted then do not hold:
     - the Ministry's grant covers only instrument access by night;
     - "the file goes up" forbids nothing;
     - the not-following in VII is about the passage, not the room.
     Nilay came down "for a boy of thirteen" and never goes back.
   - *The fix, VII:85:* after Kaya Bey's "and the file goes up," add one clause in his voice: "and until it comes down, the block stays in its socket under the Ministry's wire and lead."
   - Nothing else changes. Márton's nights "at the door of the thing" in IX then read as the closest the seal allows.

2. **XIV: the second handprint is never accounted for.** Checker confirmed, from the story and continuity critics.
   - At the climax Nilay presses her palm "beside the old print". In the winter she renders the wall all season, and nothing says whether one print or two stands there.
   - *The fix:* one sentence in the winter paragraph (XIV:127), after the two new lines are rendered, for example: "Above the first marks there was one print, as there had always been; she held her palm a finger's width off it and could not say which of the two it was."
   - No explanation.

3. **XIV: the fingerprint match has no shown cause.** This is Fable's first finding. It was raised in round 1 by three critics and again by this round's checker; I left it twice as "inferable", and now I reverse that.
   - This is the one hard proof the book lets itself have, and it arrives by a technician's whim at the moment a doubting reader says "convenient".
   - *The fix:* one clause making it the Trust's act, in the book's paperwork manner. For example, the chit's parenthesis becomes "(Trust photograph, item 51, 1924, submitted by the sponsoring institution for elimination, as a courtesy to the inquest)".
   - Keep "31 points, right hand" and "Friction ridges don't lie" as they are.

4. **VII and VIII: the air meter's strip counts time, and nobody reads its length.** This is Fable's second finding; I overruled it in round 2 and now reverse that.
   - The book says eight times that the meter ticks "six strokes to the minute", so its strip is a clock. The watches say three hours and thirty-seven minutes, and the party says an hour. Priska holds the strip and the text says only "the whole hour".
   - *The fix, VII:77:* after "0.6 and 0.6 and 0.6, six strokes to the minute", add one sentence giving the human answer, as Fable advises. The strip, counted stroke by stroke, runs as long as the watches, three hours and thirty-seven minutes; the hour was the party's alone. Priska sets the two figures side by side and does not average them.
   - *Also, VIII:49:* in Márton's statement, "No instruments deployed." becomes "None of mine deployed." (Yusuf's meter and heat gun went down.)

5. **XIV: "She laid the two dates side by side, and this time she had the word" cannot be parsed.** Fable's fourth finding, raised by Fable in round 1 and the story critic in round 1; I left it twice as wording, and I reverse that.
   - At the moment of proof the reader cannot tell which two dates, or which July.
   - *The fix:* make the Julys plain and keep the word unwritten. For example: "The laboratory's date at the foot of it was July of the year before: the week she had sat on that floor drinking tea that went cold by itself, the answer to the oldest open question in her discipline had been in the building, dated and filed. She laid the two Julys side by side, and this time she had the word, and did not write it either."
