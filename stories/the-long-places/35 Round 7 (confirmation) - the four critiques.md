## Critique: story


**Lens:** plot logic, cause and effect, the four explanations and how their clues balance, the distant-future encounter, the final reveal and whether it ties every mystery, and the owner's concept rules. References are chapter:line in the round-6 files.

**Verdict: nothing substantive remains in this lens.** I checked every rule and every chain of cause, and I checked the round-6 fixes for new faults.

**The concept's rules hold.**
- **Nothing before XIV gives away time travel.** Each strong pointer keeps a second reading:
  - the letters in VI, VIII, XI and XII;
  - the "dated a day early" papers;
  - the 1949 photograph;
  - the Sealed Hour.
- **Each explanation reaches near-canon, and none is confirmed:**
  - fumes: the stamped order, and fifteen events before the masks against two after;
  - the other reality: the 14 µGal mornings and "two good maps";
  - the wormhole: "Twice is a result";
  - the dead: Havva's mother, the cough, Melek's forty winters.

  The words "the dead", "ghost", "spirit", "parallel" and "wormhole" never appear as claims.
- **The far-future visitors stay unprovable.** The samples and clues pull both ways: the grain and the fire mountain point to the past; the teeth, the 74 cm forearm, the hair and the zero tritium point forward.
- **The reveal is said plainly, and hard proof confirms it after the words:** the certificate and the print.
- **Halden stays quiet and formidable.** His harm always goes through paper and through other people: Márton's parcel and grant, Priska's certificate, the closure memo, the transfer, and the 1999 margin.

**The round-6 fixes are clean.**
- X:33 fits Priska's "the window had been dark" and the IX timeline.
- VII:63 fits Márton's forearm arithmetic in VIII.
- XIV:31 and XIV:37 now read correctly.

**One tie the book already makes deserves credit.** Priska's cough comes at 05:40 at the fourth door (V:59). One of Márton's watches stops at 05:41 at the same door (X:35). So even the strongest "dead" clue can be re-read as time. Finding 2 asks only that this tie be allowed to land.

---

1. **XIV:17: "the wall your shoulder leans on gives back a polish your hill has never owned".** QUIBBLE
   - **Problem.** Her hill does own that polish. In VII:47 she stood in it: "polished like cooled glass", under her own low room. XIV:17 goes on to quote VII almost word for word: "the air of a room that has just had people in it, and it smells of rain on dust, and of nothing else at all". Then XIV:23 and XIV:25 bring back VII's polished passage and its sill.
   - **Why it matters.** At the first image of the climax, the reader who remembers VII hits a flat denial of it. They then have to decide whether she is somewhere else entirely, and "somewhere else entirely" is the parallel-reality reading the reveal is meant to retire. The rest of the passage works hard to say "you have been here before".
   - **Fix.** Change "a polish your hill has never owned" to "a polish your hill has shown you once, below its last door". It orients the reader and keeps the strangeness.

2. **V:59 and X:35: the cough at 05:40 and the watch at 05:41.** QUIBBLE
   - **Problem.**
     - The cough in clean air at the fourth door is the book's most specific "dead" clue. Priska dates it herself: "At 05:40 she wrote: *0.5, still.*" V:87 repeats "the fourth door at 05:40".
     - It is also the one clue the reveal cannot reach through the person Priska names. Her father never set foot in Kırk Oda.
     - The book's only tie to time is that one of Márton's watches stops at 05:41 at the same door. Priska is the point of view in X:35 and holds those watches, yet the minute passes her unregistered.
   - **Why it matters.**
     - The owner's rule is that time travel "ties all the mysteries together". This is the tie for the strongest of the dead clues, and as written it rests on a reader recalling one minute across twenty thousand words.
     - It is also the one moment when the scientist who "had never in her life been unable to prove the air of anything" would notice a number, and she does not.
   - **Fix.** Add one unexplained clause in X:35 after "at 03:12 and at 05:41": "— a minute she had once written down at that jamb, and not since reread —". Say nothing more. The dead reading (her father) and the fumes reading (her own chest) both survive. After XIV the reader can hear the cough as December's, heard in August.

3. **XIV:111: "Friction ridges don't lie. 31 points, right hand."** QUIBBLE
   - **Problem.** This is the book's one hard proof, and it depends on ridge detail. The detail must have survived from an ochre hand pressed on tuff eleven thousand years ago, and then been recorded on a 1924 photograph.
     - Ridges rarely transfer to rough stone. The book's own stone is ash-soft tuff that "closes and opens like a poor man's purse".
     - Nilay counts teeth and dates charcoal for a living. She would ask the question.
     - The book never says the face under item 51 was smooth.
   - **Why it matters.** Round 4 kept "31 points" on purpose, and I agree it should stay. But a reader who knows anything about fingerprints will say "on tuff?" at the exact moment the book asks for belief in its evidence.
   - **Fix.** Give the print a ground that holds ridges, placed early where it costs nothing. At I:63, change item 51 to "Hand print, right, red ochre, on a smoothed face, above the first marks, lower wall." Nothing in XIV changes.

4. **I:57, III:95 and XIII:47: the wall clock.** QUIBBLE (raised in rounds 5 and 6, and left). I give it a smaller fix.
   - **Problem.**
     - I:57 and III:95 make it a *wall* clock. A clock that stays upstairs cannot lose an hour because its keeper spent a night below.
     - IX:53–55 sets the book's own rule on this: the clocks taken below drift, and the one left above "agreed with the sky".
     - The founder's note (XIII:47) says "the keeper's clock retards one hour upon each night below", which reads as a clock that went down with her.
     - XIII invites the comparison ("she met a donkey she knew"), and the two tellings disagree.
   - **Why it matters.** This is the book's first time clue, and the reveal re-keys it. It is the one clue that does not parse under the physics the book later sets up.
   - **Fix.** Cut one word in each place: I:57 "a wall clock, a wedding piece" becomes "a clock, a wedding piece"; III:95 "the wall clock" becomes "the clock". Leave the story's location open, as the founder's note already implies.

## Critique: people


I read the owner's concept file and all fourteen round-6 chapter files through one lens: people and meaning. Line numbers refer to those files. I also read the rulings for rounds 1 to 6, so that I would not raise settled points again.

## Verdict

Under this lens I found nothing substantive. The round-6 fixes landed cleanly and made no new people-level faults: the boy asleep while the grey man takes the light (XIV:37), the snow falling after Márton went down (X:33), the woman whose shoulder reaches the doorway (VII:63), and Marques quoting Márton (IX:37). What this lens asks for is all on the page:

- **We care before the strangeness bites.** In I, Nilay has a mother and a brother's line on a form. In II she has the house slippers and the ribbon. Every other lead gets a wound before the hill touches them.
- **Every major choice has a shown cause:** the drill site, Márton's last night, Priska's closure draft, Yusuf's small post and the channel coming down, Melek signing herself out, Nilay's sealed hour and the stick she leaves unplayed.
- **Halden stays quiet and formidable to his last line,** and he always works through someone else's decision.
- **The questions are asked through events:** masks that end the visitations, the tour's paragraph that ends the knock, a witness chosen because she believes nothing, and "Would you want it explained, if explaining it ended it?"
- **I found one new strength worth protecting.** Emre says, "I don't think any of us ever met anyone but each other." Everyone who met the company in VII is, or becomes, a keeper: Melek, Nilay, Yusuf with his Derinkuyu lamp, and Márton, who is "kept". Priska, who "legislated myself out of the room", did not meet them.

Earlier rulings settled several points, so I have not raised them again: the meter strip in XIV, the office "speaking through" its holders, "the last image the man would give anybody", Nilay's side of the inquest, the fortieth day, the Trust's young woman, the state's appetite, and the September frost as a date.

What follows are eight small findings. All are quibbles, and each costs a clause or less.

---

1. **XIII:104–108. The reader may not see that Halden names his own third hinge.**
   - **Passage.** "He supplied it himself, as a courtesy, the way a clerk supplies a folio number." / "And the hospital?" / "'The transfer,' he said, 'was the least I could do.'"
   - **Problem.** The sentence before says the third hinge "was in her mouth" and could only come out as an accusation, so the intended speaker of "And the hospital?" is Halden. But the line sits in its own paragraph with no tag, and by the usual rule a new paragraph means a new speaker. Readers will give it to Nilay, and then "He supplied it himself" seems to mean only the answer.
   - **Why it matters.** This is the villain's most frightening moment of quiet: he raises the charge she could not make, then answers it. If the reader gives the question to Nilay, the scene becomes an ordinary admission under questioning, and Halden looks less formidable.
   - **Fix.** Join the two lines into one paragraph under his tag: `"And the hospital?" he said. "The transfer was the least I could do."` This adds no words and cuts none of the meaning.
   - **Mark:** QUIBBLE

2. **VI:57, VII:45 and VIII:35. The post and the keeper it cost never meet on the page.**
   - **Passage.**
     - VI:57: "Melek did not own a phone, which would not save her, because everything reached Melek eventually."
     - VII:45: Yusuf gives his name and knocks on the Sunday morning. Melek is present, and nothing passes between them.
     - XI:27: Melek "knew the price list to the lira".
     - XI:73: Yusuf "left before the bread, which was its own attendance".
   - **Problem.** Yusuf's post is the clearest act in the book with public consequences. It brings the pin, the queue, the fence, Recep's tours and the crowd, and the crowd grows the moment "the brake" is taken to hospital. The person it costs most is Melek, whose one look in July changed how Yusuf films. After the post the two never share a beat: each takes the consequence alone, three chapters apart.
   - **Why it matters.** The owner's theme is impact and consequence, and here the consequence never reaches across from the person who acted to the person it fell on. A single look would pay off the July look that opens VI.
   - **Fix.** Add one sentence at VIII:35, after "changing nothing". For example: "Melek, at the wicks, looked once at Yusuf across the cars, the way she had once looked at his phone, and he did not look away, and that was the whole of the reckoning." This adds about 30 words and removes nothing.
   - **Mark:** QUIBBLE

3. **XI:97. Seher's turn from "what am I supposed to do now?" to "I know now" has no cause on the page.**
   - **Passage.**
     - X:77: "I am thirty-four. What am I supposed to do now?"
     - XI:97: "I asked the Doctor, in the winter, what I was supposed to do now … I know now. When your order lifts, I'll sit below again … It costs the question."
   - **Problem.** Every other lead's change has a shown cause. Seher's answer arrives between January and June, and nothing points to where it came from.
   - **Why it matters.** Seher is the book's clearest voice on belief ("Wanting is also a gas"; "It costs the question"). Her answer should come the way the book says such answers come, by keeping. III's letter says the why "would have arrived whole, without words, the way the round arrives".
   - **Fix.** One clause: "I asked the Doctor, in the winter, what I was supposed to do now. I sat the winter out on her permit, and the spring, and I know now." The cause is the sitting itself, as Priska's own permit allowed. This adds about 12 words and confirms nothing.
   - **Mark:** QUIBBLE

4. **XIII:31 and XIII:132. The climax's deadline borrows a rule Nilay has already kept.**
   - **Passage.**
     - XI:75: "Before first frost, kızım. The house can go a night dark. It cannot go a winter." This is about taking up the round, and Nilay did so on "the last dusk of June" (XI:103).
     - XIII:132: "First frost by the week's end. The women had said: before first frost, kızım. … and one night to decide in."
   - **Problem.** In XIII the women's words are brought back as the clock on a decision, but what they asked for was done in June. The decision actually pending is Halden's undated sheet, "Remittances … for oil and wicks and the keeper's winter" (XIII:39). The frost matters because of that sheet, and the text does not join them.
   - **Why it matters.** The last chapter before the reveal gives its countdown a cause that has already been met. A careful reader who tracks the women's instruction will stumble.
   - **Fix.** At XIII:132, change "The women had said: before first frost, kızım." to "and the keeper's winter was on a sheet with no date under it." The frost then presses on the decision Halden set.
   - **Mark:** QUIBBLE

5. **X:71. Nilay's one confession of her wound to a colleague is buried mid-paragraph.**
   - **Passage.** A single paragraph holds three turns: Nilay's "Neither does it hang one", Priska's "If the air had taken him, I could have fixed it … There is only a file.", and Nilay's "I know," she said. "I have one from 1999."
   - **Problem.** This is the only time in the book that Nilay names 1999 to anyone. Here the line is the tail of a crowded paragraph that holds two speakers.
   - **Why it matters.** The next paragraph ("They sat with that…") depends on this line landing. A peak of consequence ends up flattened by the layout.
   - **Fix.** Break the paragraph at the change of speaker, into three: Nilay's line; Priska's speech; and "Nilay was quiet for a long time. 'I know,' she said. 'I have one from 1999.'" No words change.
   - **Mark:** QUIBBLE

6. **X:83 and XI:77. Two narrator glosses explain what the scenes have just shown.**
   - **Passage.**
     - X:83: "and the passing proved nothing about the night any more than a repeated office proves the thing it is about — she had watched the women since August; she knew what an office was — and it steadied her for the dark anyway".
     - XI:77: "No document had handed Nilay anything in that country, and this was not a document. The office had come to her by refusal, which was how offices moved there: what is given can be resented, and what is refused must be taken up, and the taking up is the whole of the training."
   - **Problem.** In X, the scene already makes calibration into a rite: the same hour every evening, "of course it passed", and it steadies her anyway. The dash clause then tells the reader so. In XI, Sungur's "'The house,' she said, and would not improve on it" is the right last beat, and the next paragraph improves on it.
   - **Why it matters.** The owner asks for the questions to be asked through events. These are two places where the event is finished and the gloss then states the theme. Round 1 cut two passages of this kind in XII, so there is precedent.
   - **Fix.**
     - At X:83, cut from "any more than" to "an office was —". The sentence then reads "…proved nothing about the night, and it steadied her for the dark anyway, every evening, at the same hour."
     - At XI:77, cut the whole paragraph. XI:79's "unlawful and appointed" carries the point.
     - This removes about 70 words.
     - Optional: at XIV:35, "the argument she had owed Priska since August arrived with it, whole — comfort, or knowledge — and she let it stay unfinished" names the thesis while she drinks her brother's water. It is lighter than the other two, and I leave it to the ruling.
   - **Mark:** QUIBBLE

7. **XIV:115 and XIV:125. Nilay's answer to her career's question is left for the reader to guess.**
   - **Passage.**
     - I:41 sets up "the oldest open question in her discipline … whether anyone, anywhere, had ever simply carried one forward".
     - XIV:109 answers it: 10,720 ± 90 years.
     - XIV:115: "She sent the certificate's copy to Valletta … *The Trust will know what to do with nothing.*"
     - XIV:125: "In October the Ministry's report wanted conclusions, and she was on the Şanlıurfa side, alone."
   - **Problem.** Her professional want, which the book set up in its first chapter, gets its answer, and what she does with that answer is the book's own question: known, believed or left. She holds the original. The only sign of her choice is the oblique "nothing" and a report she seems to be avoiding, so the reader cannot tell whether she published, withheld or never decided.
   - **Why it matters.** It is the one place where the owner's belief-and-science question reaches Nilay's career. A choice she makes by act is stronger than a choice the reader has to guess.
   - **Fix.** Add one clause at XIV:125: "In October the Ministry's report wanted conclusions; she sent it the register's last line, *Site: kept*, and nothing of the certificate, and was on the Şanlıurfa side, alone." This adds about 18 words and makes the choice an act.
   - **Mark:** QUIBBLE

8. **X:55. Priska, who counts nights, overstates her own record.**
   - **Passage.** "Survivable, because the plan existed — she had written it, a year of it had worked".
   - **Problem.** The plan was ordered at the start of September (V:67), and Márton died on the last night of December, so it had run four months. In V she tables "twenty-two nights … twenty-six nights" to the night.
   - **Why it matters.** This is her case to Halden, and it is built "the way she built a column". A wrong figure in her mouth at that moment breaks her character, and a number-minded reader will catch it.
   - **Fix.** Change "a year of it had worked" to "a season of it had worked".
   - **Mark:** QUIBBLE

**References** are chapter:line in the round-6 chapter files.

1. **IX:65 against III:89 and III:31: the 1997 "acquittal" answers a question 1997 never raised.**
   - *The passages:*
     - The 1997 result was two clocks disagreeing with each other: "In the morning they disagreed by eighty-seven nanoseconds … The board reported back: thermal transient in **one** oscillator" (III:89).
     - The book's own lesson from it: "Two clocks … can only argue. Three are a committee" (III:31).
     - The annex Halden sends: "*The advisory condition cannot produce **correlated** excursions between instruments.*" Then: "The acquittal had been in the file all his exile" (IX:65–67).
   - *The problem:* a correlated excursion means two clocks moving together against a third reference. The 1997 night had two clocks and no committee. By the board's own account, one of them moved. A sentence about correlated excursions therefore cannot acquit 1997. It fits only 2025 ("both stood forty from the watch above", IX:55), and Marques has already used the phrase for 2025 (IX:57).
   - *Why it matters:* this parcel is Halden's gift. It is what sends Márton down alone, against his written promise (IX:63), to his death. The book has taught the reader the two-clock logic in Márton's own voice. A careful reader will see that the "acquittal" does not acquit, and the villain's most effective move then rests on a technical slip.
   - *The fix:* change the one italic sentence at IX:65 so that it bears on the explanation the board actually used, the firmware revision. For example: "*The advisory condition cannot produce an excursion of more than a few nanoseconds.*" Thermal transient and handling are still open, so "both clean and unclean" (IX:67) still stands.
   - *Alternative:* if the 1997 annex is meant to have foreseen the 2025 result, which would be the Trust's anticipation again, keep the sentence and say so in one clause: "…a sentence about a result he would not obtain for twenty-eight years". Then drop "The acquittal had been in the file all his exile".
   - **SUBSTANTIVE**

2. **VIII:95: "The only matters on which all four agreed were the ones with mass."**
   - *The passage:* "…the water set out to its lip, the mats, the cold ash, the meal on the sill, the camera standing wiped on its foot … Where the hour had touched matter, the hour agreed with itself."
   - *The problem:*
     - The four statements are printed just above, and they do not bear this out:
       - Only the water appears in all four.
       - No statement mentions the camera.
       - Only Nilay's mentions a mat, and that only as the place a hair came from.
       - Melek's names none of these things but the water.
     - The meal cannot have been agreed by four people at all. Nilay found it alone, after the others had gone up: "Nilay climbed out last … On the sill … lay flour" (VII:75).
   - *Why it matters:* this is the thesis sentence of the chapter called "Four Reports". It contradicts the evidence set out on the same page, in a chapter whose whole job is comparing accounts exactly.
   - *The fix:* "The only matters no one disputed were the ones with mass — the water set out to its lip, the mats, the cold ash, the camera standing wiped on its foot…". Cut "the meal on the sill", or add after the list: "and the meal on the sill, which only she had seen, and which nobody could dispute."
   - **SUBSTANTIVE**

3. **XIV:31: the reunion sentence tangles at the climax.**
   - *The passage:* "She said, "Emre," once — she had said it once into another dark, and the humming had not changed — and he said the name of the room first — three syllables, the middle one long; she had heard it twice before, … from a woman who measured doors, or had heard its grandmother, or its grandchild — because the manners are the manners, and past the name, nothing, and then he broke the office…"
   - *The problem:*
     - Three dash-interruptions are nested inside each other.
     - "its grandmother, or its grandchild" has no clear antecedent: the word, or the woman?
     - "because the manners are the manners" ends up attached to the wrong clause.
   - *Why it matters:* this is the book's single most important exchange. The reader has to parse it twice at the moment it should land.
   - *The fix:* split it, keeping every phrase. "She said, "Emre," once; she had said it once into another dark, and the humming had not changed. He said the name of the room first, because the manners are the manners: three syllables, the middle one long. She had heard it twice before, once in the dark without knowing it, and once, knowing it, in a polished room, from a woman who measured doors — or she had heard the word's grandmother, or its grandchild. Past the name, nothing. Then he broke the office for the first time in twenty-seven of her years:"
   - **QUIBBLE**

4. **VIII:49: Márton's statement has lost a label.**
   - *The passage:* "> Language: none I know. I understood it. I record both. / > None deployed by me. Lamp at the stair head: not moved at any time."
   - *The problem:* "None deployed by me" has no subject. Every other line in the statement has a heading (Chamber, Occupants, Language, Samples).
   - *Why it matters:* this is the false entry that VIII:135 cashes in ("his statement said the lamp had not moved at any time, and he let the statement stand"). With its label restored, the first clause is false as well, since he carried the lamp down on his belt. That sharpens the later confession.
   - *The fix:* "Lamps: none deployed by me. Lamp at the stair head: not moved at any time."
   - **QUIBBLE**

5. **XIV:119: the Book Zero line ends in a riddle with no findable referent.**
   - *The passage:* "*To the one who keeps the lamps after me: the fourteenth of September. Emre Arat kept here, from the fourth of September 1999. The keeper wrote this before she lived it.*"
   - *The problem:*
     - She writes on the fourteenth, about 1999, so nothing in the line is written "before she lived it". The reader cannot tell what "this" is.
     - The fourteenth is tied to no event in 2026. Its only echo is the 2025 closure memo, dated the day before the letter it cited (VIII:129), and nothing points there.
   - *Why it matters:* this is the protagonist's last written act. After the reveal, a deliberate loop should read as a loop, not as a slip.
   - *The fix:* anchor it: "The keeper who writes this has not yet lived her keeping." Or cut the last sentence. If the fourteenth is meant to answer the Ministry's memo, add half a clause to XIV:117: "On the fourteenth of September, the date the Ministry had once used to answer a letter not yet written, at first light…".
   - **QUIBBLE**

6. **V:43 against V:69 and XIV:75: the sitting-night tally is short by Havva's night.**
   - *The passages:*
     - On the night of the answered knock: "twenty-two sitting nights so far, fifteen such events" (V:43).
     - Havva's night comes later, "on the third still night of the last week of August" (V:47), and it is an event ("I spoke with my mother").
     - Yet the pre-mask total stays "twenty-two nights before the masks, fifteen events" (V:69). XIV:75 re-states the same figures as the season's canon.
   - *Why it matters:* this is a book about counts, and these are Priska's signed figures. Here they fail to close by accident rather than by design.
   - *The fix:* at V:43, "twenty-one sitting nights so far, fourteen such events". Havva's then becomes the twenty-second night and the fifteenth event, and V:69 and XIV:75 stand.
   - **QUIBBLE**

7. **XII:27: a sentence that loses its object and contradicts itself.**
   - *The passage:* "…a number he had said aloud exactly once in his life, at minute forty-one of his last stream, in September, and had gone back twice, on the cousin's laptop, to hear a flat young voice in his own accent say, with no memory anywhere in him of being that voice."
   - *The problem:*
     - "say" has no object.
     - The sentence claims he knows he said the number "exactly once" and also that he has no memory of saying it.
   - *The fix:* "…a number said aloud exactly once, at minute forty-one of his last stream, in September. He had gone back twice, on the cousin's laptop, to hear a flat young voice in his own accent say it, and found no memory anywhere in him of being that voice."
   - **QUIBBLE**

8. **XIV:39: "I have never once had shoes", said while wearing them.**
   - *The passage:* "He looked at his feet, in woven things, boot-shaped, none of them his. "I have never once had shoes, in all of it.""
   - *The problem:* the line contradicts the clause before it. Read quickly, it also blurs the woven-sandal thread from VI, VII and XII.
   - *The fix:* "I have never once had shoes of my own, in all of it."
   - **QUIBBLE**

9. **XI:17 against XI:71: forty years or sixty.**
   - *The passages:*
     - "The black above that niche was hers for forty years before it was mine" (XI:17). This is the ninth lamp niche, so it means her keeping of the round.
     - "It chose me sixty years ago" (XI:71).
   - *The problem:* forty is right for her winter vigils (XI:61, XI:73) but wrong for the soot of her round. The letter has taken on the vigil count.

## Critique: mimo

1. **No villain of Johan Liebert quality** — Throughout (Halden / The Registrar). The owner explicitly requires a passive, quiet, resourceful, formidable villain "more frightening for it." Halden has every surface trait: a 76-year tenure (the 1949 photo), private knowledge of individuals (Priska's thirteenth bore, Márton's daughter and the cello), letters that precede events (Yusuf, dated Tuesday, thanking a Wednesday decision), institutional manipulation (the gendarmerie reply of 7.ix.1999 that advised against searching below the fourth door and cost Emre twenty-seven years; the calibration-certificate note that undercut Priska's science without touching the fume canon), and a question — *"Would you want it explained, if explaining it ended it?"* — that is a threat wearing a courtesy. Yet every action is narrated as stewardship: "the Trust tends," "the least I could do," "the Ministry manages it for all of us." The reader finishes the story grateful, never afraid. Fix: Recast two or three of these moments as sinister. Let Nilay feel the cold of the 1999 reply — it prevented the search for her brother. Let Priska realize the calibration note served someone who wanted the fume canon intact and her provenance shaky. Let the 1949 photograph unnerve. Let Halden's final question land as a man holding the explanation and asking you to consent to ignorance.

2. **Time travel is not confirmed as the thing that ties all the mysteries together** — Chapter XIV (Emre's speech; the hand-print certificate). The owner's rule: *"the confirmation comes at the very end when it's finally confirmed that time travel is the thing that ties all the mysteries together."* Emre explains the road; Nilay thinks "two small clumsy words" and gives them back; the certificate dates her print to 10,720 years before present. That is proof of time displacement but it never explicitly connects to the scientific anomalies — the 41 count, the appearing/disappearing chamber, the 11 cm triangle deficit, the 40 ns watch excursion, the mechanical clock running an hour behind. Nor does it unify the three near-canon angles (fumes, parallel reality, wormhole) as different readings of one phenomenon. The confirmation arrives in a parcel, after the emotional climax, as a filing detail rather than the story's peak of dissociation. Fix: Add a beat where Nilay, holding the certificate, sees the year re-keyed — the counts, the plans, the watches, the room — and understands that the keeping sustains a road through time. Let the hand-print date hit as the story's most disorienting moment.

3. **The "humans from the astronomically distant future" are not adequately signaled** — Chapter VII (the breach; the polished chamber). The owner requires a sci-fi encounter with far-future humans that the characters don't understand. The figures have teeth "drawn rather than grown," wraps whose weave "repeated nowhere," and a language that auto-translates into each listener's old Turkish or Hungarian. But these signals are brief and could read as Neolithic (body-measurement of the door with a forearm; proto-wheat on the sill; "the little sun that doesn't burn" for a headlamp could mean ancient unfamiliarity with electricity). Nothing specifically says *astronomically distant future* — the teeth and auto-translation point there but are overwhelmed by archaic markers. Fix: Strengthen one or two details — the auto-translating language arrives in a Turkish with forms that haven't happened yet; or the wraps show a material Nilay cannot source to any known fiber; or the fire-mountain question implies geological monitoring at a civilizational scale.

4. **Keeper's-text temporal structure is incoherent without explanation** — Epigraphs (all chapters). The first text and the last text are word-for-word identical, implying a perfect temporal loop. But the intermediate texts describe specific, datable events across millennia (the founder's 1924 sampling; the drilling; the measurer with the rope; the far lamplighters; the sealed hour). If all texts form one cycle, who wrote the intermediate ones and when? The story gestures toward circularity ("the wall begins where it ended") but never addresses the structural paradox. Fix: Either break the exact duplication (the last text echoes but does not replicate the first) or let one keeper's text acknowledge the loop — e.g., a line about the wall being "one wall at many hands' distances" placed at the point where the duplication would register.

5. **Time-displacement magnitude is wildly inconsistent** — Chapters I, III, IX. Melek's grandmother's clock runs an hour behind per night below (Ch. I). Márton's atomic watches show 40 nanoseconds per night below (Ch. IX). The 1997 Carpathian clocks show 87 nanoseconds (Ch. III). These differ by roughly fourteen orders of magnitude. If time travel is the mechanism, the scales should be consistent or the discrepancy should be noticed. No character remarks on it. Fix: Either scale the clock story down (a minute, not an hour) or let Márton or Nilay note the inconsistency and wonder — one line is enough.

6. **Mechanics of the sealed-hour vigil are unclear** — Chapter XIV (Nilay wakes in the polished room). She sits the vigil at the answering niche on 4.ix. She wakes on a different mat in a polished chamber with Emre, reads a letter dated "the fifth," and emerges at first light on the fifth. Where was she physically during the vigil night? Did the time-road transport her from the niche to the chamber below the fourth door? Did the chamber bleed through to her position? Did she walk down? The geography and chronology of the crossing are never addressed. The owner wants mind-bending dissociation, but the reader needs the basic coordinates of what happened. Fix: One sentence where Nilay, waking, recognizes the location in relation to where she sat — e.g., the stair she climbs up is the deep stair she has never climbed down.

7. **The parallel-reality and wormhole angles are not "almost established as canon"** — Chapters III, VI. The owner says all three angles must be "hinted at, and almost established as cannon." The toxic-fumes angle is administratively confirmed (the Ministry order, the masks, the duct). The parallel-reality angle (Yusuf's server lag, the 41 count correlating with pooled air) has evidence but no institutional weight. The wormhole angle (Márton's hollow, the triangle, the throat) has solid data but is "reserved" and never submitted. The fumes are canon; the other two are speculation. Fix: Give the other two angles a closer approach to institutional acceptance — a Ministry annex noting the geophysical anomaly of interest, or a committee minute recording the 41-count correlation as "consistent with a model of transient spatial overlap."

8. **Air-shaft breathing rate changes are unnoted by characters** — Chapters I, VI, XI, XIII–XIV. The breathing interval is 18 minutes (Ch. I), 19 (Ch. VI), 20 (Ch. XI), 18 (Ch. XIII–XIV). If intentional (the site agitates under attention and quiets after closure), this is a beautiful hidden signal. But only Priska briefly notes two figures (18 vs. 20) and does not investigate. A careful reader who spots the pattern will wonder whether it is deliberate or an error. Fix: Either have Priska log the changing interval as an unresolved anomaly (one sentence) or standardize the figure.

9. **Elevation of the fourth-door threshold shifts by 0.1 m** — Chapters III and X. Kállai states "one thousand one hundred forty-two point six meters" (Ch. III). The inquest clerk reads "one thousand one hundred forty-two point five meters" (Ch. X). The 10 cm difference might be intentional (the site physically shifts — close to the 11 cm triangle deficit) or a typo. No character notices. Fix: Either standardize or let Priska note the discrepancy on the inquest sheet and leave it unresolved.

10. **Duplicate chapter titles ("The Threshold," "The Measure")** — Chapters II/XII and III/XIII. The mirrored titles likely serve a structural echo (two thresholds: the closed stone and the fence; two measures: Kállai's survey and the dig's close). But the repetition could read as an error to a reader who does not infer the mirroring. Fix: If intentional, let one of the epigraphs gesture at the pairing (a keeper's line about "two thresholds of one road"). If not, retitle.
