# THE DEPTH OF HABIT
### A novella blueprint — 14 chapters, ~41,000 words

---

## 1. Title and Premise

**Title:** *The Depth of Habit*

**Premise (no time-travel reveal):** When a village floor collapses in central Anatolia, archaeologist Ada Karayel — back in her late mother's village to settle an inheritance — finds a spiral stair descending past every mapped century of a underground city that has been continuously inhabited for at least three thousand years. The villagers have a word for what happens below: *kaymalar*, "the slips" — lost hours, voices that answer before you speak, children who come up wearing their shoes older than they are. A state commission descends: a toxicologist convinced it is carbon dioxide, a disgraced astrophysicist convinced it is spacetime, a documentarian convinced the rooms overlap, an oral historian convinced the place is "thin," and a ministry man who only needs the file closed. The deeper they survey, the more the place un-surveys them — until the evidence begins to arrive before the events that cause it.

---

## 2. Point of View, Narrative Form, and the Machinery of Dissociation

**Primary voice:** Ada Karayel, first person, present tense, in the form of a numbered field journal — precise, dry, a woman who trusts measurements more than memory.

**Frame:** The entire novella is presented as an assembled casebook: **Case File KD-7, Directorate of Antiquities, Continuity Surveys Section**, compiled by an unnamed "file voice" that contributes only headers, bracketed provenance notes ("*found folded into the Prideaux journal, translated by Dr. Keshishian*"), and the chapter's depth-readings in the titles. The file voice never interprets. It appears a maximum of six times. Its identity is never stated.

**Document taxonomy interleaved with Ada's journal:**
- Interview transcripts (villagers, unverifiable)
- Okonkwo's instrument logs (timestamps that contradict the journal)
- Jonah's footage log (with frame-rate gaps marked as *[DROP]*)
- Anahid's field notes and one withheld transcription
- Excerpts from the 1907 journal of Rev. Nathaniel Prideaux (translated)
- Directorate dossiers on other "Long Dwellings"
- The final official report (cold institutional third person)

**How the form produces dissociation:**
1. **Contradictory durations.** Ada logs forty minutes below; the cameras record three hours. The reader cannot anchor duration anywhere.
2. **The déjà-written.** From Chapter 7 on, Ada finds entries in her own journal she does not remember writing — same hand, different ink pressure, describing her day *slightly wrong* (a lamp hook where there was none, nineteen steps where she counted twenty-one).
3. **Scene doubles.** The Chapter 3 descent is rendered twice more — once as Ada's disputed journal variant, once in Prideaux's 1907 description of the same stair — with small deltas (19 vs. 21 steps) no character remarks on. The reader performs the comparison and doubts their own recall.
4. **Second-person intrusions.** Five brief passages (≤200 words each), always during Ada's slips: *"You have been here before you were."* They are never explained; at the end they read as Ada addressing herself across the bleed.
5. **Header drift.** Chapter titles carry depth-readings ("Ch 8 — −37 m"). Twice the depth *decreases* between consecutive descent chapters. The file voice flags it once: *[reading not corrected]*.
6. **The index anomaly.** The casebook's closing list references "Ch 15 — not appended."
7. **The formal flip.** After the reveal, the prose becomes the calmest and most sensorially exact in the book — dissociation resolves not into certainty but into a vertigo of understanding. The reader feels steadied and unmoored at once, which is Ada's final condition.

## 3. The Places

**A. Kayadibi, "the Houses" (fictional), Cappadocia, Anatolia.** A village of forty households living on the roof of a carved soft-tuff underground city. The Directorate's conservative floor: **no fewer than 3,100 years of continuous use** — Hittite-era tool styles at the deepest defensible layer, Roman loculi, a Byzantine chapel with paint flaking like dead skin, an olive press, rolling stone doors, soot lips on ceilings from three millennia of lamps. The deepest cuts are *undatable*, because every surface has been reworked by later hands. Tiers: the mapped upper houses (mangers, cool, dry, still used to store lemons); the middle tier (the chapel, the school room, refugee graffiti in three alphabets); and the unmapped lower city — the Forty Stairs, the spiral, the **Kötü Nefes** ("Bad Breath") room where carbon dioxide pools shin-deep and kills songbirds, the **El Nişanı** ("the niche of hands"), the offering hollow where every era left deposits, and finally **Kırk Kat**, the Fortieth Layer, and the **Ters Salon** — the Wrong Hall. Texture: 13°C everywhere, air like chalk dust and cold ash, tuff soft enough to mark with a fingernail, the smell of lamp fat that no one has burned in a century. There is a fig stump in Ferhat's yard, cut down in 1954; the village records confirm it.

**B. The Hypogeum of Ħal Saflieni, Malta (real), ~3600–2500 BCE.** A necropolis carved downward, three levels, ochre spirals, the Oracle Room whose resonance sits at 110–112 Hz — a pitch that makes the inner ear tremble and the voice seem to come from inside the skull. The commission obtains one licensed night session (Chapter 6). Texture: bone-warm limestone, corridors sized for the living to walk single file behind the dead, a darkness that has a grain to it.

**C. The Longyou Caves, Zhejiang, China (real, undated).** Present only as Directorate dossier and one phone recording (Chapter 6): vast carved chambers with tool marks of impossible uniformity, no occupation layer, no inscription, flooded to the lip for centuries. The recording contains an echo that returns a half-second *before* the clap that caused it. Dossier verdict: "Age indeterminate. Use: indeterminate. Continuity: indeterminate. Retain on registry."

**D. Registry negatives (one page, Chapter 2).** Göbekli Tepe — older than everything, but its *use was interrupted* (deliberately buried); registry rejects it. The Paris Catacombs — old material, but the material was moved there; no continuous dwelling; rejected. A twelfth-century crypt; rejected — too young. The registry's unstated, never-articulated criterion: it is not age but **unbroken human attention** that qualifies a place. No document in the file says this. Aunt Suna says it, sideways: *"We never asked the Houses to be holy. We only kept coming back. That is what holy is."*

## 5. The Four Explanations — Evidence, Canon Moment, Block

**A structural note that carries the theme:** every explanation fails to reach canon not because the data dies, but because *consensus* fails — a refusal to sign, a confessed fake, a challenged custody chain, a principled refusal to claim. Canon is shown to be a social act, shaped exactly like belief. Science does not fail here for lack of evidence; it fails for lack of witnesses willing to convert.

### A. Toxic fumes — Dr. Roth
- **Evidence planted:** The Kötü Nefes CO₂ pool (Ch 3) — real, lethal, kills two songbirds and nearly kills the boy Yunus (Ch 7). Villagers' "long naps" correlate with depth (Ch 2–3). Blood-gas anomalies in workers. Symptom clusters identical to hypoxic euphoria. Blind and sealed-respirator subjects still report the voices — which Roth files as "bone conduction, vestibular, expectation" (Ch 5, 7).
- **Canon moment:** Ch 7. Yunus is pulled blue-lipped from the Bad Breath room; Roth's evacuation order goes through; the Directorate drafts the finding: *atmospheric, physiological, closed*.
- **What blocks it:** The offsets continue in chambers with instrument-clean air. The deepest event of all (Ch 9) occurs wearing sealed respiratory rigs, with blood oximetry nominal throughout. Roth's own private log (Ch 10): one page describing a memory of standing in a room "before it was finished being carved," which she writes, dates, and refuses to reread. **The fumes are real and dangerous — a red herring that saves lives.** Science is never mocked here; it catches the true mundane danger and misses the other thing entirely.

### B. Parallel reality — Jonah Weeks
- **Evidence planted:** LiDAR returns a corridor that is not present when walked (Ch 5). Chamber surveys fail to close by centimeters — and the sign of the error flips between visits. Strangers who never met report the same dream of "a room with two skies" — clustered among those who shared a guide, *except one* (residue). The scan of the Wrong Hall (Ch 9) shows a colonnade no one saw.
- **Canon moment:** Ch 5's end. The team watches Jonah's footage: the impossible corridor, and standing in it, motionless, a figure in a jacket identical to Jonah's. Audible breathing on the track.
- **What blocks it:** Jonah, earlier in Ch 5, confesses he faked one "whisper" clip for his pilot episode. One small lie spends all his credit. The corridor footage is ruled a composite; motion-smear determination is signed. He swears on his mother this one is clean. No one can believe him. **The miracle is destroyed not by evidence but by a previous deception — belief and credit are the same currency.**

### C. Wormhole / spacetime defect — Dr. Okonkwo
- **Evidence planted:** Three co-located chip-scale atomic clocks diverge by 200 nanoseconds over four hours — "impossible unless one of them moved" (Ch 5). A gravimeter dips 40 µGal on the stair and recovers. A time-transfer radio receives its own tick 71.6 seconds late, doppler-shifted downward, "as if reflected off something receding" (Ch 5). The Wrong Hall's light behaves like weather (Ch 9).
- **Canon moment:** Ch 10's tribunal. A retired metrologist, not a believer, reads the logs and says: "If these are real, something down there is moving in a way clocks notice."
- **What blocks it:** Replication collapses whenever a designated skeptic observes; equipment-drift memos; sporadic-E propagation for the radio echo. Okonkwo's prior — the data he once refused to retract — poisons his custody. He refuses to sign the final report and is listed as "dissent appended." The clocks stay diverged. In his kitchen. Forever.

### D. Contact with the dead — Dr. Anahid Keshishian *(leaned into hardest; never named)*
- **Lexicon rule (absolute):** the narration and all characters use only *the previous, the ones below, the ones who kept the lamps, the congregation, your mother's era, the early ones.* Villagers' folk terms appear in Turkish and Armenian, untranslated, with a footnote stating the folklorist's practice of non-translation. The words *ghost, spirit, soul, ancestor, afterlife* never appear anywhere in the book.
- **Evidence planted:** The El Nişanı strata — every era, Hittite to 1958, left offerings at the same niche, arranged in the same pattern: **seven objects in a spiral** (Ch 4). Whispered names later found on the village gravestones (Ch 3). The 108 Hz resonance of the stair — the pitch at which funerals are sung in the village "without anyone tuning" (Ch 3). Ada, in Malta, hears a child's voice say her name in her mother's childhood accent (Ch 6). A 1958 hair ribbon Ada recognizes from the single photograph of her mother as a girl (Ch 4). Stone in the niche that is *warm where someone would have sat*. Jale's pale children in old-fashioned clothes (Ch 3). Aunt Suna's line, said plainly: *"They are only early."*
- **Canon moment:** Ch 11. Suna dies mid-verse. On the tape, in the silence after, a woman's voice completes the line. Anahid's field note: *"I will not transcribe this."* Testimony withheld — the strongest evidence in the book, made strong precisely by being kept.
- **What blocks it:** Anahid's own methodology: she records testimony and refuses to assert. The whispered names are common local names. The mother's phrase existed on an old recording Ada heard once as a child — "memory contamination" is written in the file. The completing voice is never played for a second witness. **The book leans so hard the reader will be certain — and can never be shown.**

## 6. The Encounter with the Far-Future Humans

**Where:** Chapter 9, the Wrong Hall, below Kırk Kat — the only expedition through the one stair Salih refused to let them pour.

**What happens:** A chamber too tall for the tuff above it. Air that moves the wrong way, tasting faintly of rain. A light with no source that behaves like slow weather. Within it, **figures** — very tall, very thin, pale to the point of nacre, joints slightly wrong, faces "like a face remembered rather than seen." They do not visibly breathe. They never speak. The cameras record nine minutes; the team's bodies record something over an hour. On the recordings there are only shadows and a sound like surf.

**What is done:** One of them kneels to Ada and holds her wrist "the way you'd check the pulse of a matriarch." One of them *weeps* at the sight of Roth's LED lamp — grief, unmistakably, at a small white light. One presses into Jonah's hand a loop of dull gray stone. Then, on the floor, they arrange **seven pebbles in a spiral** — the offering pattern of the El Nişanı, identical across five thousand years of strata — and wait, as if for the humans to answer a courtesy.

**What each makes of them:** Roth: symptoms — hypoxia at depth, shared vestibular event. Okonkwo: "the observers" — light lensed from elsewhere; "we are being watched by a place where the light comes from a different angle." Jonah: "the residents" — the parallel occupants at last. Anahid: "the elders." The villagers, afterward: *the Long Ones of the People.* **No one, ever, arrives at "the future."**

**The object:** The loop assays as a natural cobalt-bearing alloy containing an inclusion of a transuranic isotope that occurs in nature only in ore enriched by an ancient supernova's ejecta — matter the Earth's crust has not yet had. The lab flags "cross-contamination from a reference standard; the analyst is retrained." Duplicate U-series dates on flowstone film over tool marks in the Hall disagree by **2.1 million and 0.4 million years** — impossible in both directions. Sample "compromised."

**Why no reader can ever be confident:** Every channel of evidence is compromised *inside the story* by the site's standing rule — proof does not survive daylight. Photos corrupt; samples misdate; the LiDAR "colonnade" is a motion artifact; memory disagrees among five witnesses about everything except the weeping. The figures are never seen again, never speak, and are never rendered from within. Even after the final reveal confirms displacement through time, the Tall Kind remain formally unexplained — *probably* pilgrims from an astronomically distant future, still keeping the offering. Probably. The reader is never owed more than that, and must feel the lack.

## 7. The Final Reveal — Exact Mechanics and Retroactive Binding

**The reveal is evidential, not visionary. It is a chain of custody, not a vision.**

**Chapters 12–13 (the displacement):** After the backfill, the Houses have gone silent — except below Salih's stair, where one night lamplight is answered from below again. Ada descends alone with a dead reckoning kit: a fresh laser Disto (old one died; serial number visible in a Chapter 12 photograph), a tablet, a sweet pea she picked that morning. The slip is rendered as *continuity*: a step, a change in the sound of her lamp, dust hanging at a different angle. No shimmer, no rupture. She is in 1907.

She sees, at distance, a bearded man in a straw boater staring at her party in horror — Rev. Nathaniel Prideaux — and watches him see **six figures and a child in glittering shoes**: herself, Roth, Okonkwo, Jonah, Anahid, and Salih (the Chapter 9 expedition, passed through 1907 unawares during the hour the cameras recorded as nine minutes; Jale trailing behind — Salih's admitted "rats"). At the El Nişanı she reads her instrument for the first time — **−41.6 m, azimuth 118°** — writes *41,6 — 118* on a slip of 1907 paper, lays it with the sweet pea in a Samsun cigarette tin, and seals the niche. On the slide back, the chamber "breathes sideways": a side-bleed to 1958, where a girl of eight — her mother — ties a ribbon to the niche hook. Ada says the phrase her mother said to her every night for forty years. The child answers it.

**Chapter 14 (the audit — the confirmation):**
1. **The tin,** excavated in Chapter 7 from mortar sealed in 1907, under hard pan with rootlets grown *through* the folded slip.
2. **The root.** The root belongs to the fig tree cut down in **1954** (village records; the stump in Ferhat's yard, noted in Chapter 1). A root grew through the paper and then died with the tree. The paleobotanist's line closes the case: *"Roots do not grow through paper after the paper stops existing."* The slip predates 1954. No hoax is possible.
3. **The handwriting:** the crossed sevens and open-topped 4s established in Chapter 2 — Ada's, confirmed by the Directorate's questioned-doc examiner.
4. **The digits** were first generated by Ada's instrument in Chapter 13 — a reading that did not exist on Earth before that moment, on a device whose serial and upload timestamps postdate the tin's excavation.
5. **The sketch:** Prideaux's journal (Ch 2, Ch 4 — "photostat ruined, sketch illegible") is re-photographed under raking light: a woman with a fresh scar at the brow. Ada cut her brow on the collapse edge in Chapter 1.
6. **The metadata:** the EXIF "year rot" blamed on epoch bugs in Chapters 2–10 is reread as itineraries. One Chapter 9 frame is dated 1907.

**Retroactive binding — every earlier clue, re-keyed:**

| Earlier clue | Turns out to have been |
|---|---|
| Lamplight answered from below (Ch 1) | A lamp of another era, carried by the living |
| The "long naps" (Ch 2) | Micro-displacements |
| Prideaux's six figures & the child in fish-scale slippers (Ch 2, 4) | The Chapter 9 expedition, and Jale, seen from 1907 |
| Whispered names matching gravestones (Ch 3) | Villagers of other times, calling their own living |
| CO₂ pool, sickness (Ch 3, 7) | Real, lethal, and irrelevant to the slips — the mundane truth science was built to catch |
| Moved survey markers; non-closing rooms; 19 vs. 21 steps (Ch 3, 5) | The same corridors measured from **different eras** — the tunnel sets differ; "parallel" was always "serial" |
| Jale's pale children (Ch 3) | Children of 1907 and 1943 |
| The doll arm and the aged shoe in sealed spoil (Ch 1, 11) | Jale's, dropped during her slips |

**The closing move:** the reveal *demotes* the ghost explanation and replaces it with something both more concrete and stranger — they were never the dead; they were earlier. And the reciprocal line, the last of the book: the team had been someone's dead all along.

## 8. Theme: Impact and Consequence

Five causal chains, each a different scale of ripple:

1. **The Loop Chain.** A deposit in 1907 is found in the present and is only understood by making it. Consequence in this book is not a line; it is a circle whose beginning is also its end. Ada's smallest act — writing a number — is the cause of the investigation that brought her to write it.
2. **The Safety Chain.** Roth's evacuation saves Yunus's life and empties the village — so Suna dies alone in the town, and the song survives only on a tape no one will play. Science protects bodies; removing people from a place amputates its memory. Both things are true. Neither cancels the other.
3. **The Attention Chain.** Jonah leaks footage → pilgrims arrive → one is injured → the Directorate pours concrete → the Houses go silent above the pour → Salih's stubborn refusal spares one stair → that stair is the only door left for the ending. Institutional caution nearly kills the mystery; one man's refusal preserves it.
4. **The Credibility Chain.** Jonah fakes one whisper → his true corridor is disbelieved → the parallel reading dies → and with it the team's last chance at public canon. A single small deception propagates through every institution that touches the file.
5. **The Generational Chain.** A woman witnesses the "apparition" in 1907 and puts it in a song → the song passes down to Suna → the song's final verse is the route Ada follows → Ada's arrival is the event the song describes. **Belief is the only instrument in the book that successfully carries a true event across a century.** Instruments fail at every threshold; ritual succeeds at all of them. That asymmetry is the thesis.

## 9. The Sharp Questions — Where Asked, and by Whom

1. *"You keep asking what it is. My grandmother asked who it was."* — Anahid to Ada, Ch 4. The grammar of belief versus the grammar of inquiry.
2. *"If it's a gas, we can fix it with ventilation. If it's not a gas — what do we ventilate?"* — Roth, Ch 7. Materialist explanation as an instrument of governance; does the state require causes or closures?
3. *"People don't need the truth. They need an ending."* — Uygur, Ch 7. Ada's reply — *"Then give them a true one"* — is refused. Why does society fund answers it will not accept?
4. *"An anomaly you can't repeat is a lie. A lie you can repeat is a career."* — Okonkwo, Ch 5. Can replication — the heart of scientific belief — survive an object that refuses witnesses?
5. *"We never asked the Houses to be holy. We only kept coming back. That is what holy is."* — Suna, Ch 4. Belief as practice, not proposition.
6. *"I filmed myself where I wasn't. Which of us is the recording?"* — Jonah's footage log, Ch 5. When evidence and self diverge, which do we keep?
7. **Asked wordlessly, Ch 9:** the Tall Kind's instruments fail to certify us, but their seven-stone spiral *recognizes* us. Recognition between humans, across any distance, runs on ritual, not on instruments. Can science carry care across a thousand years, or only information across a room?
8. The final, unanswered question, Ada's last entry, Ch 14: *"Science can tell you the age of the walls. It cannot keep the walls company."* Measurement versus tending. The book refuses to answer whether the second is a kind of knowledge — it demonstrates only that it is the one that reached.

## 10. Chapter-by-Chapter Outline

**Ch 1 — "−0 m: The Floor Gives" — Ada — ~2,800 words.**
Ada arrives for her mother's affairs; Ferhat's floor collapses at dusk; the spiral stair; lamplight answered from below by lamplight. In the spoil: a doll's arm and a child's light-up sneaker, "worn like they'd been under rubble for decades." Ada cuts her brow on the fall edge. The fig stump in Ferhat's yard is mentioned, inert.
*Clues:* answered lamp (dead); doll arm, shoe (anachronism); fig stump; brow scar (planted, unremarked). *Tilt:* none — domestic dread. *Feel:* grief laced with wrongness.

**Ch 2 — "−4 m: Continuity Surveys" — Ada — ~2,900 words.**
The commission assembles; each expert's lens introduced by what they pack. Registry negatives (Göbekli Tepe, the Catacombs) establish the unbroken-use criterion without stating it. First interviews: the *kaymalar*, the long naps. First Prideaux excerpt: six figures, a child "in slippers that glittered like fish scales," noted as period eccentricity. Ada labels sample bags — crossed sevens. First EXIF drift noticed, blamed on epoch bugs.
*Clues:* long naps; Prideaux apparition; handwriting baseline; metadata rot. *Tilt:* sociology/hysteria. *Feel:* institutional calm over a humming floor.

**Ch 3 — "−22 m: The Bad Breath" — Ada — ~3,000 words.**
First full descent. The CO₂ room nearly canonizes Roth; birds die in it. The stair's 108 Hz resonance — the funeral pitch. Jale, encountered on the stair with her one-armed doll, speaks of the pale children. Whispered names later matched to gravestones. A survey marker is found moved.
*Clues:* CO₂ (real); resonance; pale children; names; moved marker; nineteen steps counted. *Tilt:* **fumes — strongest chapter.** *Feel:* claustrophobic credibility.

**Ch 4 — "−31 m: The Niche of Hands" — Anahid (field notes) — ~2,900 words.**
Suna and the song cycle. The El Nişanı strata: seven objects in a spiral, era upon era, five thousand years of the same gesture. The 1958 ribbon; Ada's recognition, kept private. Second Prideaux excerpt; the sketch exists but the photostat is "ruined." *"They are only early."*
*Clues:* offering pattern; ribbon; sketch (withheld); Jale's glittering shoes echoed in the journal. *Tilt:* **the dead — leaned into hardest, never named.** *Feel:* tenderness indistinguishable from dread.

**Ch 5 — "−28 m: The Corridor That Isn't" — Jonah (footage log) — ~3,100 words.**
Okonkwo's clocks diverge; gravimeter dips; the 71.6-second doppler echo. Jonah's LiDAR corridor; his confession of the faked whisper; the footage of the figure in his own jacket, ruled composite. The sealed-respirator subject still hears the voices.
*Clues:* clock divergence; gravimeter; radio echo; impossible corridor; figure-in-jacket; anti-fume datum. *Tilt:* **wormhole and parallel, deliberately entangled.** *Feel:* the vertigo of instruments arguing.

**Ch 6 — "−12 m: The Oracle Room" — Ada — ~2,900 words.**
Malta, the licensed night session. 110 Hz; the blind subject draws a procession she was never told of; Ada hears a child say her name in her mother's childhood accent. Longyou dossier and the echo that arrives early. The pattern is cross-site; nothing is cross-provable.
*Clues:* resonance-voice; blind drawing; mother's voice; early echo. *Tilt:* the dead, with acoustic-materialist rebuttal installed beside it. *Feel:* awe decaying into suspicion of one's own ears.

**Ch 7 — "−41 m: The Tin" — Ada — ~3,200 words.**
El Nişanı excavated: the Samsun tin sealed in 1907 mortar, rootlets through the folded slip, the digits *41,6 — 118* in Ada's hand, a pressed sweet pea. The room goes very quiet. Uygur: hoax verdict drafted. Yunus nearly dies in the CO₂; Roth's evacuation; the village empties; Suna is moved to town. That night Ada finds a journal entry she did not write. First second-person interlude.
*Clues:* the tin (time's shadow, contested); déjà-written entries; the evacuation's cost. *Tilt:* all four destabilized at once. *Feel:* dissociation begins in earnest — the reader's ground shifts.

**Ch 8 — "−37 m: The Pour" — Salih — ~2,800 words.**
Jonah's leak; pilgrims; an injury; the Directorate pours concrete into the chapel corridor. Above the pour, the Houses go silent. Ferhat is found at 4 a.m. speaking 1907 Greek, drawing corridors he never saw. Salih refuses the second pour and loses his license; one stair is spared.
*Clues:* silence-following-severance (correlation only); Ferhat's flattening. *Tilt:* none — a grief chapter. *Feel:* consequence landing; the reader mourns the mystery's silencing.

**Ch 9 — "−?: The Wrong Hall" — Ada — ~3,400 words.**
The deep expedition through the spared stair. Kırk Kat; the Ters Salon; the light like weather; the **Tall Kind**. The wrist held like a matriarch's pulse; the weeping at Roth's lamp; the stone loop; seven pebbles in a spiral. Nine minutes recorded; an hour in the body. A scuff of small footsteps behind them — "rats," says Salih. Second interlude.
*Clues:* the far-future encounter; the loop object; pattern-recognition; Jale's presence (unnoticed). *Tilt:* all four simultaneously, at maximum ambiguity. *Feel:* the novella's apex of uninterpretable awe.

**Ch 10 — "0 m: Findings" — Okonkwo + file voice — ~3,000 words.**

## 12. Rules the Writer Must Never Break

1. **No character, document, or narrator may state, conclude, or name time travel before Chapter 13.** The word does not appear until Chapter 14, and then only once, in the audit.
2. **The words ghost, spirit, soul, ancestor, and afterlife never appear anywhere** — narration, dialogue, or documents. Folk terms appear only untranslated, under the folklorist's stated practice of non-translation. The dead-contact thread is carried entirely by lexicon (*the previous, the congregation, the early ones*), warmth, acoustics, and withheld testimony.
3. **Every anomaly must retain at least one mundane reading until the Chapter 14 audit.** If a scene leaves only one explanation standing, cut or complicate it.
4. **The fumes are real and lethal.** Science must never be mocked; it catches the true danger and saves a life. The mundane truth and the impossible truth coexist.
5. **Each of the four explanations receives one peak chapter where it feels true, and one documented deflation** — and no deflation may be total. Residue is mandatory.
6. **No explanation is ever confirmed except time travel, and only at the end.** The Tall Kind remain formally unexplained even after the reveal.
7. **The site never proves itself to instruments.** No photo, sample, or recording survives as clean evidence. The tin's chain of custody is the single engineered exception, prepared in Ch 1 (fig, scar, handwriting) and Ch 7 (sealed condition, rootlet) before it pays in Ch 13–14.
8. **Displacement is only within the site's footprint, only to times when the chambers existed, and is always rendered as continuity** — a step, a change in lamp sound, dust at a new angle. Never spectacle, never shimmer.
9. **Never explain the mechanism.** Duration-of-use is implied by correlation (the backfill silence) and Suna's aphorisms only. No lecture, no theory, even at the end.
10. **The reveal must be evidential, not visionary:** ink, paper, root anatomy, graphology, serial numbers, metadata — a proof the reader could audit.
11. **No scene from inside the Tall Kind.** They never speak, never return, and are never rendered from within. The reader's uncertainty about them is permanent by design.
12. **Anahid's tape is never played twice.** The strongest evidence for the dead is the evidence that is kept.
13. **Second-person interludes: five maximum, ≤200 words each, only during Ada's slips.**
14. **Every inserted document carries provenance** (where found, who translated). The casebook's honesty about its sources is the reader's only stable ground — and it must be slightly wrong at least twice.
15. **Jonah's lie stays unforgiven.** The credit economy of belief is a spine of the book; do not redeem it for comfort.
16. **The last line reframes the dead as the earlier — it never says we contacted them.** The true answer replaces the wanted one, which is the book's cruelest and kindest act.
