## Findings marked QUIBBLE by the critics

I did not re-judge these; they stand as marked. While checking, I looked for any that should be raised to SUBSTANTIVE and found none. Three notes for the ruling:
- **Story 3 and people 2 are one finding.** Both concern XIV:31: "she had heard it twice before, and known it once, in a polished room". It is the round-5 fix, now misreading because of its word order. Two critics independently caught it, and it costs a few words. It remains a quibble because Emre's "in the place where the words go" (XIV:49) still carries the link to II:59.
- **Continuity 5 (VII:77, "as long as the watches")** is also a round-5 slip, in the ruling's own wording. Priska's 10:04–13:44 is three hours forty minutes; Márton's "Descent 10:07 … Egress 13:44 by the watches" (VIII:41) is three hours thirty-seven. It is cheap to correct.
- **Continuity 19 on X:83 against XIV:95** does not hold. "Went down to see about the duct" can mean down from the gate to the mouth, where the fan is (V:67, "a galvanized duct at the mouth, a solar fan"). That does not contradict "did not go below the first door again".

## Confirmed findings

1. **Emre's first dawn: the boy stays behind the stone when the grey man goes up, and nothing on the page says why (XIV, line 37; checked against II:21, II:65, IV:71).**
   - The book teaches twice that at the grey the stone "took one finger" and the one behind it goes up. The second time is in the low corridor below the fourth door (II:65).
   - So "took the light with him" does not explain why the boy did not follow at the moment the stone opened.
   - The villain's gravest act depends on the boy being unable to leave.
   - The fix is one clause at XIV:37. Either "and in the morning, while I slept, he went up and took the light with him", or "…took the light with him, and the stone came home behind him". Both keep Halden passive and make the act his.
