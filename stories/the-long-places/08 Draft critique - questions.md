1. **Ch. XIV, the re-key paragraph.** "And every warmth that had settled against a side of any room in that country had been a neighbor, out of order, on a round, being kind to a stranger they took for somebody else's." The narration asserts, as fact, the identity of every visitation, and only then retro-fits Emre's "belief" tag onto it. This is the book's one never-sayable question — were the beloved dead ever met? — and it receives a total, warm answer: no, only the living, mistaken. The reveal stops superseding the fourth theory and starts settling it, and grief is answered rather than honored. **Fix:** move the entire claim inside Emre's hedged speech ("I think… I don't know that we ever met anyone but each other") and cut the narrator's prior sentence; let the year re-key only the countable things (the forty-one, the surveys, the watches) and leave the warm layer among the unowned residues with the voice under the knocking and the extra reflection.

2. **Chs. X–XIV, the whole cast drifts belief-ward.** Priska ends performing a nightly office ("She was at last, in the only grammar she had, attending" — X; "her way of attending, and it was attended" — XI); Yusuf ends lighting a lamp (XII); Nilay takes the keepership; Halden tends one upright; Emre keeps; Melek kept. Not one character ends further from the keeping-position than they began, and the thesis-word is glossed repeatedly (Halden's "The sites ask little but attendance" is delivered twice, to two characters). "Can science replace belief?" is answered *no* — not by argument but by ensemble gravity, a unanimous conversion wearing a question mark. **Fix:** keep Priska incorruptible — cut both glosses so her calibration reads as discipline, not liturgy, and end X on "which is indicative"; give her one beat of plain relief in her withdrawal; let Marques return in January as she promised and leave unmoved.

3. **Chs. V, VII, IX, XI — no clean skeptic ever witnesses a strong event.** Priska is above ground and masked during the breach ("the one person who had seen nothing") and her disbelief is pre-undermined by the cough scene; Marques never goes below; instruments die at the threshold. The comparison the book exists to make is never granted a controlled trial: belief's evidence is always witnessed by believers or the bereaved; disbelief's witnesses are always absent or privately compromised. The questions feel sharp but rigged, and a reader senses the rigging without being able to point at it. **Fix:** one scene of honest, unmoved science — during the fence-season knock-answers (XI), Priska instruments the threshold on her own gear, records a real acoustic she cannot source, files it "source unresolvable," and her position does not move. No winner; a fair test.

4. **Ch. XIII, Lambrick's unsent letter is a lecture.** "Between the roads there is a fourth country… a thing is tended because no one can say what it is. The Trust is the boundary of that country. Boundaries do not hold opinions about the ground." The founder states the book's entire epistemology — ambiguity as sacred practice, the Trust as its custodian — in finished philosophy. It is the one true sermon in a book that otherwise asks through events, and it hands the reader the decoding key to Halden and to the book's own aesthetic. **Fix:** cut the fourth-country paragraph and "Boundaries do not hold opinions"; keep the ledger, the economies, the shame, and the buried-hill line; the List's prohibitions ("do not resurvey," "no further vigils") already perform the philosophy without naming it.

5. **Chs. VIII and XIII — Halden's question is never tested.** "Would you want it explained, if explaining it ended it?" and his Ministry paragraph ("let the outcome end as a door ends, shut, and in a wall"). The plot never stages an explanation ending anything. The fear of knowledge is asserted by Halden, Lambrick, and the fence, and proven by no one; the sharpest society-level question — what knowing does to believing — is asked three times and dramatized zero times (the fence arc dramatizes *attention's* cost, which is a different question). **Fix:** one small forked instance — in the fence season, a tour script that includes Priska's CO₂ paragraph outscores the knock in the reviews, and within a fortnight the queue stops waiting out the two breaths. Whether the answers stop because the knocking stopped or the company left stays open, but the rite is shown dying of accuracy once.

6. **Seher vanishes before the reveal.** Her last appearances are a mat at the mouth (XI, XIII, "still the way furniture is still"); she is absent from XIV. The character built to carry the fourth theory articulately and to name its price ("It costs the question"; "What am I supposed to do now?") never meets the answer — her question is closed in XI by her own doctrine, and the truth arrives off-stage from her. The one person the revelation was for is not in the room for it. **Fix:** one beat in XIV's aftermath — the morning of the fifth, Seher on her mat; she asks one thing, or asks nothing. Two lines would let the reveal be refused, or cost something, instead of merely being kept.

7. **The bilateral-blessing formula becomes a tic.** "Both texts were true; the page held one" (V); "Both sentences were kept, in their countries, and neither country translated for the other" (VIII); "neither translated for the other, and the page did not choose, and neither, that year, did anyone" (XI); "the queue never said, and was a whole institution on the strength of never" (X). Each is excellent; by the fifth use the method is visible — every tension is absolved in the same cadence. Ritualized even-handedness is itself a verdict (science and belief are complementary, always, costlessly), and the reader learns no confrontation will ever cost anyone anything. **Fix:** cut the closing benedictions in VIII and XI; let one standoff end lopsided — after Márton's death, the village's reading simply wins the village, with no balancing sentence after it.

8. **Ch. V stacks its artillery and decides by rhythm.** Priska's mask question (unanswered — good), then "'If it can't be falsified, it isn't knowledge. It's comfort.' / 'Maybe comfort is knowledge,'" with "Priska had no counter, and noted the lack" — plus Seher's testimony and the splinter, all in one chapter. The zinger wins by cadence, and the plot then *proves* it: the comfort was knowledge (the water was drunk; the warmth was real). The book's best epistemological question is asked in V and answered in XIV. **Fix:** let Nilay not answer in V (she already doesn't answer the first question); move "maybe comfort is knowledge" to XIV as a thought she declines to finish, and cut "had no counter" so Priska's counter is her calibration act, unglossed.

9. **The village's belief is frictionless at its own edges.** Havva is revived from 4.0% CO₂ and the event is metabolized within a paragraph ("The women received this with satisfaction"; "The sleeping air put its arm around her") — no family anger, no flicker against the rule-framework that missed it. The one social cruelty belief produces, Halime (Ch. II, accused and never cleared, "a stone in a fruit"), is quarantined in 1999 and never touched again. The sociology is flattering: belief's failures are never allowed to register *inside* the community, only against Priska from outside. **Fix:** one beat of internal cost — Havva's daughter at the bread queue with a sentence that isn't satisfaction; or Halime at the back of Melek's funeral (XI), and where the women seat her. The women's refusal to teach Nilay ("don't ask which of the two is the reason") is the right texture; it needs one sibling.

10. **The interludes preach in one unbroken register.** Fourteen catechisms, each closing on a distilled teaching ("fear spends itself where it is allowed to"; "a belief is a keeping, kept"); the only friction in the sequence is Interlude IX's "I hated her a little, correctly." Every teaching lands, every keeper is wise, nothing down there is ever petty, bored, ineffective, or wrong. The reader is catechized in belief's voice fourteen times and cross-examined in science's voice four — the book's sympathies are legible from cadence before any event votes. **Fix:** let one interlude fail — a child who won't learn and goes up; a round done badly with no visible consequence (which would also test whether the keeping is efficacious or merely habitual, a question the book currently cannot pose); a keeper who admits boredom.

11. **Ch. XIII — Ayios Nikandros is inert paperwork.** The counter-example that keeps "kept places stay open" a hypothesis rather than a validated law of belief — kept nightly, quiet since 1961 — appears as counterfoils, a drowned chapel, and "the lake could not be asked." Nilay lays the fork down and it never returns; the book's honesty on its central wager is delegated to a folder no reader will feel. Without a stinging counter-example, the plot's mechanism simply confirms belief's bet. **Fix:** give the folder one concrete third-party trace — a boatman's one-line report, the accounts disagreeing — and let it return once in XIV, when Nilay takes both the lamps and the List, as the thing she now owns the risk of.

12. **Ch. XIV — Emre settles Melek's unresolution.** "'She knew better than to need to know,' he said, which was the steadiest sentence anyone had spent on Melek." Melek's designed ending — "I never settled it… the not-settling is the steadiest thing I own" (XI) — is arbitrated posthumously from the far side, and the narrator ranks the arbitration for us. *Is comfort false if the comforter was never who you thought?* is closed by proxy, kindly. **Fix:** cut "which was the steadiest sentence anyone had spent on Melek," and consider cutting the verdict too — let Emre keep only "The water was the best water in the year. Being carried," which says everything and settles nothing.

13. **Ch. XII — the lullaby question is imported and over-chaperoned.** "Science replaced the priest at the bedside. Who replaces the lullaby?" is a borrowed sermon cadence in a book containing no priest, no bedside but Priska's father's, and no church but a bell people set clocks by; and the narration frames it twice ("deleting a question is not the same as unasking it"). The one mandated question about science and care arrives pre-formed rather than grown from the story's own materials, so it reads as the theme checking in. **Fix:** ground it in-frame — the ward, the ambulance arranged before the family called, gülce's night shifts — or let it surface in Yusuf's own idiom; and cut the framing sentence so the question stands alone.

14. **Ch. XI — the fence question is asked by the narrator, not the event.** "A stranger could be forgiven that season for asking which side of the rail the holy thing was on." The staging (queue outside, lamps inside, laminated list) already asks it perfectly; the narratorial rhetoric converts the question into a caption and tells the reader where to look. **Fix:** cut the sentence, or concretize it — a pilgrim asks Recep which side to photograph, and Recep's answer is a price.

15. **Ch. XIV — the Göbekli coda answers by cadence.** "…and went on wetting it anyway, and did not decide, and the hill said nothing, having been asked nothing, being retired." The wet stone is the book's last open question — method or rite — and the closing clause resolves it stylistically: the hill's reticence gets the last word, a bow that functions as an answer. **Fix:** end on "did not decide." Cut "and the hill said nothing, having been asked nothing, being retired," and probably "had nobody left to ask who would know the difference" with it; leave the question in her open hand.
