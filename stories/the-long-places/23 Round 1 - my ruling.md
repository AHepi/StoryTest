# Round 1 ruling, by Claude (the final authority)

**What I read:** the four critiques (story, people, continuity, MiMo), the checker's advice (`verification.md`) and Fable 5.1's single review of this version (`fable-review.md`). I checked every disputed point against the chapter files myself before ruling. The checker's verdicts were advice; where I differ from it, I say why.

**How I ruled:** a finding is fixed if a careful reader would notice it and it weakens the book, or if it breaks one of the owner's rules. Each fix is kept as small as the problem allows. The book's voice, images and structure stay; nothing is rewritten. Everything not listed under "Fix" is left alone, and quibbles are left for the audience test.

## Fix (11 findings)

1. **XI: the knock's answers thin before they begin.** Checker confirmed; I checked lines 27–29: the answers "thinned the same season", then are "first answered on a still evening in May" and "came at a rising rate after that". Fix: put the events in order without adding material. The answers begin in May and rise with the crowds (Havva's "too much company", Kaya Bey's correlation, Priska's knock of 9 June stay where they fit). The paragraph winning the reviews, the knock dropped from the tour, and "The answers thinned the same season. What had done it … both had stopped asking" move to after the June material, with a time word such as "By midsummer" in place of "That spring … within a fortnight". Only time words may change.

2. **XIV, the re-keying paragraph (line 75): two sentences break the owner's rules.**
   - (a) "And Priska's air had been true too — real, busy, carrying the small mercies — and the deep ones had not needed carrying." This confirms the fumes explanation for the small visitations. The owner's rule: fumes are hinted almost to canon and never confirmed. Replace it with one sentence that says the air was real and measured, and leaves open whether it carried anything or was only in the room when things were carried. Use only figures the book already gives for the air.
   - (b) "And some of it the road did not take back." The owner's rule is that time travel "ties all the mysteries together". This sentence tells the reader the key does not fit three mysteries, when it can fit them (a keeper from another time answering a knock, standing at the pool, paying for a lamp). Change it so she keeps the drawer without the denial, along the lines of Fable's fix: "And some of it the road could have carried, and she did not make it carry them:" followed by the same three items. Keep "The drawer kept them … The drawer was hers now."

3. **VIII, the keeper's letter: "The dead dug upward" (lines 9 and 29).** Checker confirmed (the naming only). These are the only places the book calls visitors "the dead", in the keepers' authoritative voice; the owner said contact with the dead is leaned into and never said. Change "The dead" to "The grandparents" in both places, and nothing else in the letter. A first reader still hears the dead; after XIV it reads as ancestors seen from a later time.

4. **XIV, the sheets in the tin box.** Checker confirmed; I checked lines 19, 89 and 97: her sealed letter, the found sheet dated the fifth, and the copy she writes that afternoon make three sheets, yet "Both sheets went back into the tin box". Fix at line 97 only: make the count exact and let it carry the paradox, for example "All three sheets went back into the tin box: hers of the fourth, and two of the fifth that differed in nothing she could find but their creases".

5. **XIV: Nilay's mother never appears after the reunion.** Checker confirmed; Fable's first finding; I checked that she is absent after line 51. The book builds her with care (I, II, XIII, "He would be forty" in XIV) and gives her nothing, though her son has been found and her daughter will "go farther back than any of us". Fix: one short beat of 80 to 150 words, placed after Seher's colloquy and before the parcel paragraph, on the fifth. The ribbon lies on the table by the bread oven; her mother folds it on its old creases and asks nothing; later she watches Nilay go down to the evening round with the oil can, the way she once watched a boy go out in house slippers. It must be shown, not explained: no statement of meaning, no one names Emre, and Nilay says at most one short sentence. The cost must be visible in something the mother does with her hands.

6. **XIV: Halime is cleared by the reveal and never released.** Checker confirmed; Fable's quibble 4. Fix: one sentence, next to the mother beat or at the start of the evening round: at the bread queue Nilay stands beside Halime, where the women had put her by putting her nowhere, and gives her own name first, as at the mouth; nothing else is said, and the queue sees it.

7. **VII and XIV: the distant-future visitors read as ancient.** Checker confirmed (story critic and MiMo). Márton's statement in VIII already implies a forearm of 74 cm ("One forearm to the width… My own forearm is forty-six centimeters"), but no one in VII sees anything unusual about her size, which is also a contradiction. Fix, two small changes only:
   - VII, where the woman rises or moves: make her scale visible and deniable in one clause, for example "she rose, and kept rising, or the low ceiling made it seem so, and bent to the new-cut lintel as to a child's door".
   - XIV, Emre's "Farther than I've been." becomes "Farther than I've been, and not the way you're going." Nothing else in his speech changes. Nothing may say "future".

8. **VIII: the statement we read is not the statement the narration says was filed.** Checker said QUIBBLE ("deliberate"); I overrule it, with Fable (substantive 2) and the people critic. GLM's blueprint has no such design, and the midnight passage's explanations ("she had typed from memory and slipped…") assume a typing slip the reader can see did not happen, because the printed statement says "two" and has no paragraph about the third figure. It reads as a revision seam. Fix (Fable's option a): make the printed statement carry the contradiction, so the reader can find it before she does.
   - Statement 2, second paragraph, becomes: "Occupancy: three seated figures against the far wall. The third sat, or was seated, at the limit of the lamplight, at the hem of it."
   - Its third paragraph becomes: "The question concerning the fire mountain was asked by the third figure, which I could not certify: *Is the fire mountain awake?* No reply was given."
   - The midnight passage then says the filed statement read, "in its second paragraph, *Occupancy: three seated figures against the far wall,* and in its third, *the third figure, which I could not certify.*" Everything else in that passage, the notebook line and the compile list stay as they are.

9. **VI and VII: the same Monday dusk told two ways, and Yusuf's turn arriving a day after VII assumes it.** Fable's substantive 3; I checked the weekdays (1 September 2025 was a Monday, 7 September a Sunday): VI ends at Monday dusk with Yusuf alone on the flat stone and no trace of the breach he was in the day before; VII ends the same dusk with "the four of them". Fix, in VI's last movement only: "By Sunday it was past four million" becomes "By Saturday night it was past four million"; "By Monday there was a pin on a map" becomes "By the Monday there would be a pin on a map"; "In the small hours of Monday" becomes "In the small hours of Sunday"; "went out to the hill in the last of the light" becomes "went out to the hill in the first of the light". His turn then comes at dawn on the Sunday, before the descent, where VII already places it.

10. **IX / X: Priska's meter on the night Márton died.** Continuity critic; checker said QUIBBLE; I overrule it. The book shows Márton going below wearing Priska's logging meter, and such a meter keeps a strip for the whole night; X turns on Priska being unable to prove "the man had died in good air", and nobody says where the meter was. A careful reader asks at once what its strip said. Fix: one clause in IX's last paragraph: he left Priska's meter on its nail by the door; tonight the witness was to be himself. Nothing else.

11. **XII: the chapter states its meaning outright.** People critic; checker said QUIBBLE; I overrule it in part. Yusuf lighting a lamp in his grandmother's niche shows everything, and the narration explains it twice more. Cut exactly two passages and add nothing:
   - line 41: "Watched was content. Attended was kept. His language had a word for the first and none for the second."
   - line 101: "A torch is content; you carry it for what it shows you, and he had been a torch, professionally, for four years. A lamp is an errand. It is on its way to somebody."
   Keep the keeper's letter, "being nobody's content", and the chapter's last line.
