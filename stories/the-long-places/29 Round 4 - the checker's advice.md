# Round 4 verification: the SUBSTANTIVE findings checked against the book

**What I read.**
- The owner's concept.
- All fourteen round-3 chapter files, in full.
- The four round-4 critiques: story, people, continuity and MiMo. MiMo's critique (`critique-mimo.md`) was already there when I started.
- The rulings of rounds 1 to 3 and the round-3 verification. I read them so that I could tell when a finding repeats one already settled.

I did not weigh `fable-review.md`, because it is not one of the four critiques I was given. These verdicts are advice. The main Claude session makes the final ruling.

**Method.**
- Critics marked 21 findings SUBSTANTIVE: Story 1–7, People 1, Continuity 1–5 and MiMo 1–8. I checked every one against the chapter text.
- Merged duplicates:
  - Story 2 with People 1 (the chamber);
  - Story 5 with Continuity 3 (the chit);
  - Story 7 with Continuity 1 (the second handprint).
- Two SUBSTANTIVE findings have a twin that another critic marked QUIBBLE:
  - Story 3 with Continuity 17;
  - Continuity 2 with Story 8.

  Each pair shares one row.
- That leaves 18 rows.
- References are chapter:line in the round-3 files.

**The verdicts:**
- **CONFIRMED:** the problem is real, and substantive by the definition.
- **QUIBBLE:** the problem is real, but only a quibble.
- **WRONG:** the finding is not true of the book.

## Notes on findings every critic marked QUIBBLE (not re-ruled)

I agree they are quibbles. A few are plain errors, not taste, and could be fixed at no cost:
- **IX:75, "in the tone of a closing a record"** (Continuity 20). This is garbled. "in the tone of a clerk closing a record" fixes it.
- **XIV:49, `"You said my name once," he said, "Mine.`** (Continuity 20). The comma after *said* should be a full stop.
- **XI:17, "hers for forty years"** (Story 9, Continuity 11). It sits against Melek's "It chose me sixty years ago" (XI:71) in the same chapter. Two critics caught it independently.
- **I:63 and XIV:77, tally lines "thousands"** (Story 10). A daily mark for eleven thousand years would be millions. "past counting" costs nothing.
- **XI:31 and XI:91, "Kaya Bey's hidden order"** (Continuity 13a). Here "order" means his hermits, while the same chapter's "The order still stood" (XI:79) means the Ministry's.

Also worth passing on:
- **The early frost.** "First frost was forecast for the end of the week" (XIII:31), at the start of September, sits against "Frost took the hill in the second week of November" (IX:29), and nobody remarks on it (Continuity 15). It sets Nilay's "one night to decide in", so one clause marking it as early would help.
- **Rows 4 and 17 pull in opposite directions** on the same calibration of the future visitors. That is itself the best evidence that the matter belongs to the audience test.

The licensed party is admitted, the room is Márton's hollow and Nilay's lead to her brother, and nothing but an unadopted Trust submission asks for the door to stay shut. This reads as the author steering, and it leaves Nilay and Márton acting without cause.
