## Fix (8 findings)

1. **XIV, the waking: what the wax seals.** The checker confirmed it, and I checked it.
   - *The rule:* XIII's protocol seals the letter in wax (step 2), stops the watch (step 3), then lays letter and watch in the tin box (step 4). XIV follows it: Priska presses her initials into the seal, the watch is stopped, and the box goes down "letter, stopped watch, wax down".
   - *The problem:* on waking, "The wax is whole. You break it and it comes away in one piece, like a lid. Inside are two sheets and a watch" makes the wax the lid of the box, with the watch inside it.
   - *The fix:* reorder that one passage (line 19) so the box holds the watch, still stopped at the witnessed hour, and the sealed letter. Then the wax is whole; she breaks it, and it comes away in one piece, like a lid; inside the letter are two sheets. Nothing else in the passage changes.
   - *Not to be done:* do not add anything about Priska counting sheets, and do not change the protocol or the sealing scene. The proof's hole is deliberate.

2. **XIV, line 19: "at the head of it, the date: tomorrow."** Checker confirmed. The waking passage is told on the morning of the fifth ("sealed yesterday", "the morning you are currently having"), and the sheet is dated the fifth (lines 89 and 97). Change it to keep the strangeness without the error: "at the head of it, the date: the fifth — today, and when the wax went on, tomorrow."

3. **XIV: Priska lets Nilay sit a still-air night below with no meter.** Checker confirmed, on character. Priska made Yusuf promise to wear her meter (VI). Márton died with his left on its nail (IX). The text itself calls the night "pooled". She would not let the keeper go down without one.
   - *At the sealing (line 13):* one clause in which Priska clips her spare meter to Nilay's belt, because she had made everyone promise, and the keeper was no exception.
   - *At the gate in the morning:* one clause in which she reads the strip: a healthy line, 0.6 from end to end.
   - Nothing may be said about the length of the strip or anything else it shows.

4. **VII, line 71: "Nobody counted."** Checker confirmed. The same paragraph says a lamp "was counted" and "nobody that afternoon counted twice", and Nilay has just counted the figures and the elder's teeth.
   - Change "Nobody counted." to "Nobody counted twice."
   - Change "were easy to keep" to "seemed easy to keep".

5. **IX to X: Halden's visit and card vanish.** Checker confirmed the seam, not the demand that anyone catch him. X uses the parcel's engineering sentence, yet the inquest never mentions the last visitor in a village that sees everything, and Priska's clearing of the room never meets the card. Keep Halden unseen and unaccused. Two clauses only:
   - *X, inquest paragraph (line 45):* after the elimination prints, one clause saying no visitor was entered for the thirtieth. For example: "No visitor was entered for the thirtieth; asked, the village remembered weather."
   - *X, clearing his effects (line 73):* with her forks column she finds the card from the parcel, *For your peace. — A. Halden.*, and it goes into the same drawer. Nobody says anything about it.

6. **X, line 61: "and did not argue, and the not-arguing was the breaking" is followed at once by an argument.** Checker confirmed. Remove the clause from line 61 ("and annexed her dissent" stays). Put it after the exchange ends, where the two women sit with it, for example: "They sat with that, and after it Nilay did not argue again, and the not-arguing was the breaking." No other change.

7. **XIV: Nilay's stance toward the man who left her brother in the dark costs nothing at the moment she accepts the keepership from him.** Fable's first finding; the checker did not see it. The people critic raised a wider form in round 1, and I ruled it wrong then, because the Book Zero line is her answer. I uphold this narrower form.
   - *The problem:* she posts the signed keepership sheet to the Registrar whose margin "had kept a boy off every record", and the book gives that moment no sentence. A careful reader may read it as the author forgetting his crime, which shrinks the villain after the fact.
   - *The fix:* one sentence at the posting, in the book's own manner, echoing XIII's "accusations want a court, and she did not have one; she had a drawer". For example, after the two sentences: "She wrote a third, with two dates in it, the fourth and the fifth of September 1999, and struck it through, because accusations want a court, and she had a hill."
   - No confrontation, and no reply to it.

8. **XI, line 75: the oil can is handed to Nilay twice.** Fable's third finding; the checker called it a quibble; I overrule it. Melek "put the oil can into Nilay's hands two days before" her death (line 71), and on the third day after it "the women came to the dig house with the oil can and a school notebook". It is a plain contradiction in the scene where the office passes, and the book has taught its reader to track objects. Cut "the oil can and", so the women bring the school notebook. Nothing else changes.
