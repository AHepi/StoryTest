# 20 Revision plan: The Long Places

This plan is for the three Claude revisers of GLM 5.3's finished story (file 10). It settles each book-wide question once. Every reviser follows it exactly, even when they would have chosen differently, so that the three blocks join up.

Sources: the owner's concept (file 01), the story (file 10), GLM's final check (file 12) and the independent reading with the orchestrator's spot-checks (file 13). Line numbers ("L123") refer to the unrevised file 10. Quoted anchor text is exact, so you can find it after the line numbers have moved.

## 0. Ground rules for every reviser

1. **Revise; do not rewrite.** Keep GLM's sentences, images, chapter shapes, italic keeper letters and the `---` separators. Change only what this plan names, plus the small cuts it allows. New prose must sound like GLM: long, cumulative sentences; concrete nouns; a quiet final clause. Never write in a new style.
2. **Hard rules from the concept, which no change may break:**
   - Time travel is the very last thing revealed (XIV). The words *time travel* appear once, in XIV, and nowhere else.
   - Fumes, parallel reality, wormhole and contact with the dead are each hinted almost to canon and never confirmed.
   - The narration never says outright that anyone is dead and speaking. The words *ghost*, *spirit*, *séance* and *afterlife* never appear. "The dead" appears only in the VIII letter ("The dead dug upward"). The word *wormhole* never appears.
   - The far-future people are never explained, never given a point of view and never proven, not even in XIV.
   - Halden stays passive, quiet and unshowy. He never raises his voice, threatens anyone, appears at a crisis or does anything on the page except pour, sign, file, wipe, read and ask.
3. **Fixed texts.** The I letter and the closing letter after XIV stay word for word identical. So do the four tide paragraphs (L75, L530, L1035, L1391), apart from the number, which stays exactly as it is: 18, 19, 20, 18.
4. **Spelling.** Keep the book's American spelling ("color", "meters", "centimeters"). Keep "grey" everywhere except the two places Halden is "Gray" or "grayer" (L306, L1179), which stay as they are.
5. **Shared canon.** Wherever section 7 gives a string, use it verbatim. The other blocks quote or echo it.
6. **Length.** The whole book must end within 44,400 to 54,200 words (±10% of 49,335). Aim for about 48,000. Each chapter has a target at the end of its list. Stay within ±5% of it.
7. **Suggested blocks**, if the orchestrator has not set others: A = I–V; B = VI–IX; C = X–XIV. The cross-block dependencies are all in section 7.

## 1. Halden: a formidable threat from chapter IV onward

**His aim, which the book does not share.** Halden wants the long places *kept*: continuously, silently and by one keeper at a time. He does not want them known (science), believed in by crowds (pilgrims, sitting nights) or recorded. The people it costs are what he spends to get that: seekers, scientists, mourners, and the keepers and their families. He never records them. His creed is already in XIII: *"The Register records the sites, Dr. Arat. It does not record the recorders."* The book takes the other side. It records every person he spends, and its heroine ends XIV by writing a spent boy's name into Halden's own book (Chapter XIV, item 12). The founder's "pay the keepers" (XIII) is a line the book agrees with; Halden's practice is the opposite.

**The one visible cost he clearly chooses (IV).** On 7 September 1999, three days after Emre vanished, the gendarmerie asked the Trust whether it held any survey of chambers below the fourth door. Halden answered in the slope: the Trust held none, and advised that none be entered. His sentence was literally true: the 1998 chamber is in the middle gallery, and the founder had "counted nothing" below the fourth door. It also closed the search at the fourth door. The Register's 1999 page, in his hand, carries the margin note *4–5.ix. Kept the hours. A.H.*: he was at Kırk Oda the night the boy vanished. In IV he lets Nilay read both documents, watches her read them, and tells her why, with courtesy: the founder's requests are kept "only when they were cheap" would make a poor registry. That line is the aim the reader will fear. He wipes the desk. Nothing happens in the room. It stays deniable: his sentence was true, and he "answered what [he] held". But the reader has now seen him choose a boy's search below a dead man's rule.

**Keep him quiet.** The only additions to his on-page behavior are:
- the 1999 page and leaf, with two short speeches (IV);
- a glance at a whiteboard (IX);
- a report, told by Emre and not shown, of a grey man who sat with him (XIV).

Nothing else is added to his scenes. The existing four scenes (IV, IX, X, XIII) stay courteous teas.

## 2. Nilay's one active move toward Emre: Chapter VII

**The move.** Nilay chooses where the drill goes. Márton wants the rig at the foot of the deep stair, over the heart of his residual. She puts it in the low room at the end of the low corridor, below the fourth door. That is the room whose floor she searched on her knees on the third Monday of September 1999, and the one place the Trust's 1999 letter shut the search out of. She gives Márton three true reasons and keeps the fourth to herself.

**The paper trail.** The evening she sites the rig, she opens her 1999 search notebook and reads its last entry (canon C8). On Thursday the fourth of September 2025, the anniversary, the camera's fall sounds a floor under that room. That night she writes the new line (canon C8) under the old entry, the first new entry in that notebook in twenty-six years. She says the date aloud to no one.

**Why it works.**
- It is set up in II (the enclosure), III (the change of clerk) and IV (the leaf, and her decision on the plane).
- It has consequences she caused: the breach, the encounter, Márton's obsession and, through IX, his death.
- It charges the breach: she understands in the chamber that she came down for "a boy of thirteen in house slippers, and none of them was a boy" (VII).

Do not add any other active Emre scene in the middle. VIII's "Hers had never been sat" and XIV's vigil carry the rest.

### 4b. Voice sheet. Apply it to every line you touch; rewrite only the lines named in the chapter lists.

- **Márton** (68, Hungarian by birth, Swedish by chair). Long, formal, qualified sentences, with the joke arriving late and slightly too long. He speaks in instrument vocabulary and numbers with units, and hedges on himself. He does not make crisp epigrams.
- **Priska** (Swiss, air chemist). Clipped declaratives and imperatives, units, lists. When she is funny she is blunt, not quotable. She uses almost no metaphors. She keeps "That is not a dataset. That is a liturgy." and "Four honest people…"
- **Marques** (physicist). The one scientist allowed to lecture. She uses questions and tags ("no?"), energy conditions and checkbooks. She keeps "Two clocks is an anecdote…" and "Show me the machine." Replace "Machines are not old or young. They are fed or unfed." (IX, item 3).
- **Kaya Bey** (Ministry). Pleasant, long-winded official courtesy, the passive voice, his grandmother's sayings. He is comfortable, but the narrator says so once only.
- **Seher** (34, schoolteacher, widow). Plain, concrete, classroom words. She asks direct questions. She keeps "Wanting is also a gas." and "It costs the question."
- **Yusuf** (19–20). Casual; gaming and tech metaphors; lowercase when he writes; run-on sentences; self-deprecating.
- **Melek** (78). Village idiom, short answers, proverbs. She is allowed aphorisms, because she is the tradition.
- **Halden.** Very few words; procedural, formal, filing language. He asks questions. He never delivers the book's thesis as an epigram; his aim comes out as practice ("The Trust answered what it held").
- **Narrator.** Fewer "and it was enough" and "which was the…" closers. Never end two consecutive paragraphs on a maxim.
