1. **Ch. XI, Melek's collapse — an unforked physical anticipation.** "The district hospital's ambulance was already on the road... and the family had called no one yet." Every other Halden hinge arrives with its forks on the page (the clipboard woman in April; the committee minute explaining the solstice grant; "Then you were anticipated, Doctor"). This one — the only hinge that is physical rather than documentary — carries no fork at all, and a vehicle dispatched to a collapse before any call is functionally demonstrated foreknowledge. It matters because rule 15's design is deniability ("efficiency, backdating, or something read off the Wall"); here the reader's cheapest reading becomes "someone has already read tomorrow," a pre-XIV near-confirmation of the one thing the book must never certify, and it sits outside all four explanations. Fix: keep the authorization and the named bed dated the day before (paper), let the ambulance respond to the boy's call normally, and lock the fork with one line — Sungur at the co-op: "They were coming for her today anyway; it was arranged at the hospital." The hinge stays visible; the foreknowledge stays unreadable.

2. **Ch. XIV, the cough in Nilay's drawer — a knowledge leak.** "She filed it as belief, in the drawer that appears in no methods section, beside a cough nobody had logged." The cough exists only inside Priska's private narration (Ch. V: "It was in no report"; Ch. X: "with the cough she had never logged"); no scene ever transfers it to Nilay, and Nilay even borrows Priska's private idiom for the drawer. This matters doubly: the book administers its ambiguity through strict limited POV ("narrators never lie about what they perceive"), and one leaked clause teaches a rereader to distrust every other supposedly private perception; and clue 17 is designated open-forever — "nowhere the Trust can reach" — which this line violates by filing it in someone else's drawer. Fix: cut the clause or replace it with an item Nilay actually holds ("beside a tune that paused where the words would be").

3. **Interlude IV vs. Ch. XIV's reveal of the interludes' source.** Interlude IV's narrator is demonstrably Maltese ("The island is warm, and the stone of it is not mountain stone. It is sea pressed into stone... I renewed the red today"), yet XIV retro-claims every interlude as Nilay's renderings of Kırk Oda's Counting Wall. The Kırk Oda wall cannot hold a Hypogeum keeper's own round, and no clause licenses mixed provenance. This matters because clue 49 — the interludes included the future; the wall begins where it ends — is the reveal's structural keystone; one interlude that provably isn't from the Wall lets a careful reader collapse the whole frame ("they're just stories") at the exact moment it is being certified. Fix: either relocate Interlude IV's narrator to Kırk Oda receiving an island-born keeper who teaches the knocking, or add one licensing clause at the XIV rendering scene — that the renderings came from the wall "and from whatever the wall had drunk, things true in that country arriving by more than one road" (the motif Ch. IV already plants).

4. **Ch. I / Ch. XIV — the encoding gap between tallies and voiced prose.** The Wall is established as "short carved strokes, one beside another, thousands... which no one had ever read, including her," yet Nilay's renderings yield fully dialogued first-person prose with psychology. The reveal never says how "loose" a rendering is. It matters because the loop (Interlude I = XIV = the opening paragraph) binds only if the reader accepts the renderings as readings rather than inventions; as staged, Nilay "finds" prose she cannot have derived from strokes, which reads as authorial sleight-of-hand rather than revelation — the difference between a mind-bending loop and a broken one. Fix: seed it once, pay it once — a Ch. I clause ("her renderings had always been half transcription, half what the hand did while the eye counted; she had never decided which half was the record") and a XIV clause acknowledging the act of translation inside the discovery.

5. **Ch. XIV, the supersession pass drops the fumes.** The re-key paragraph handles parallel ("both surveys had counted, and counted different whens"), spacetime ("a thick place in the world's agreement"), and the unspoken theory ("every warmth... had been a neighbor") — but the one explanation that held canon with signatures (Ch. V's mandate, Ch. X's "indicative" crisis) receives no clause at all. It matters because the reveal is required to supersede all four ("fumes are real but incidental"); as written, a reader holding Priska's stamped canon finds it neither superseded, nor residue-listed, nor mentioned — the only theory the ending simply abandons. Fix: one clause — "the air had been real, and had carried the small mercies; the deep ones had not needed carrying" — or a line at the dawn scene with Priska: "Your air was true. It just wasn't the whole of the truth."

6. **Ch. XII, the footprint — verdict before fork, and an echo that welds them.** "This rain had edited. It had aged the one print it never touched" lands as settled conclusion *before* the boy's fork ("Sandal people, all summer"), and the print occupies the figure's exact stance at the lip ("the step not spent" / "unspent"), so the tour-print fork is functionally unbelievable — old traffic does not stand in one figure's unique position. Clue 42 is specified "still deniable"; as staged, no reader will run the fork. Fix: move the boy's line ahead of Yusuf's conclusion, soften the editorial to perception ("the print read as weeks old, and he could not make it read otherwise"), and set the print a step to the side of the sighting's spot so the identification remains the reader's choice.

7. **Ch. VII, the far keepers speak over budget.** "The little sun, the one that does not burn" (8 words) plus "Is the fire mountain still awake?" (6) come to roughly fourteen spoken words against the rule's "fewer than a dozen in the entire book." It matters because the encounter's uncertainty is partly engineered by starvation of their speech — every word past budget is unearned testimony — and both lines are quoted rather than paraphrased, hardening them into evidence. Fix: trim to eleven or fewer ("the little sun that doesn't burn"; "Is the fire mountain awake?") or render the headlamp line as reported speech ("said a short thing about a little sun that did not burn"), keeping only the question verbatim.

8. **Ch. VIII, the tritium zero gets two of its three forks.** "Tritium below detection (<0.3 TU). The laboratory does not interpret zeros" is glossed only by Priska (fossil aquifer) and Kaya Bey (pre-war cisterns). The blueprint's third fork — water from *after* all the tritium has decayed, the one reading pointing at the deep future — is never voiced, so the zero reads purely mundane and the encounter loses its only physical hint toward the far future. It matters because the brief requires all angles "almost established as canon, but never confirmed": with two forks the water acquits the future; with three it testifies for it and still cannot be certified. Fix: one pencil line — Márton copying the figure into the back pages of the log and adding, in the tone he uses on himself, something like "or the last of it has finished decaying; water is patient; consult the half-life" — a fork voiced by the one man who would then decline it.

9. **Whole book — the sanctioned-unreliability devices are missing.** The mirrored titles (II↔XII, III↔XIII), the Wall's "two Twelfth marks, one cut over an erasure," the two characters disagreeing over who first mentioned the sealed lower door, and the single quiet sharing of the number four (fourth door / fourth of September, never linked) are all absent. These are the load-bearing small wrongnesses the reveal is meant to leave standing ("the reader's uncertainty made physical"); their absence thins the residue the ending leans on and weakens the reread's dissociation — nothing in the final chapter quietly refuses to close except the drawer list. Fix: give II and XII one shared title and III and XIII another; add the Twelfth-mark erasure to Nilay's wall visit in half a sentence; give Seher and Yusuf one disputed-provenance exchange about the lower door in Ch. VI; let one Ch. II sentence carry both "fourth" uses without comment.

10. **Ch. XIV, the mechanics arrive in two beats.** After the sanctioned single paragraph ("You've had it backwards... The wall already says so" — correctly under 200 words, correctly hedged with "I don't think" and "I don't know"), a second explanatory beat follows: "You came by the sealed hour — that's the mannered way. It lets you up where it let you down." That is a distinct piece of mechanism (it explains the slip's placement) delivered outside the one permitted passage. It matters because each extra beat of explanation spends certainty the book has hoarded for thirteen chapters; Emre's power is that he knows his road and no more, and a second mechanics beat converts keeper's grammar into exposition. Fix: fold it into the paragraph as one clause, or cut it — Nilay sealed the box in the niche and woke by the niche; the reader can close that circle unaided, and should.
