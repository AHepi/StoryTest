1. **The mechanism answers the theme's question cosmically.**
*Where:* §1 engine; §9 "dramatized answer"; §13 hidden law "attention feeds the site."
*Problem:* "Can science replace belief?" is settled by physics: in this book's universe, keeping is literally load-bearing, withdrawal (Göbekli) kills a site, and every scientific refutation is incidental. Belief is a natural law here, so the question isn't asked — it's legislated.
*Why it matters:* the brief wants the question sharp, not answered; a universe built so that belief is structurally necessary converts an open question into a theodicy, and readers who hold the other position are overruled rather than invited.
*Fix:* deny the writer certainty. One counter-example in Book Zero's site list — a site kept faithfully that went quiet anyway, or an abandoned one that still "works" — keeps the law a hypothesis held by believers, not a fact held by the book.

2. **Halden's censorship is vindicated by plot-fact, not argued by value.**
*Where:* §8 (crowds make the site volatile) and §12 law 13, against §10 XI's "(stated only as village observation)."
*Problem:* the ending's open question — publish or keep — is rigged: if disclosure physically endangers the site, silence is simply correct. The blueprint contradicts itself on whether volatility is fact or folklore.
*Why it matters:* a choice that is safe is not a choice; the theme requires that telling the truth cost something and protect nothing.
*Fix:* enforce the village-observation-only version everywhere; add Kaya Bey's mundane correlation (more visitors = more reports, mechanically); give one character a real argument that disclosure would be survivable and right.

3. **Ten scripted questions, several as finished aphorisms.**
*Where:* §9 table, especially Q6, Q7, Q8, Q9; Q5's riposte.
*Problem:* the questions are pre-written as quotable lines distributed across chapters like a syllabus, and several ("A fact is a belief that no longer needs you") are mini-lectures — the reader is told the theme rather than made to undergo it.
*Why it matters:* the brief demands sharp and concise, asked through events and people; density of ten guarantees preachiness somewhere.
*Fix:* keep at most four as spoken lines. Convert the rest into situations: Lambrick's aphorism becomes a decision he made (a site he let die, and the paperwork of its death); Nilay's Göbekli question becomes an ambiguous gesture, unworded.

4. **Halden's three-sentence creed is the thesis wearing a suit.**
*Where:* §10 XIII; §9 Q7.
*Problem:* even capped, the creed is a complete epistemology in dialogue — it does the reader's work of articulating why ambiguity matters, deflating XIII's dread and fixing the book's stance.
*Fix:* cut it to one sentence or none; let the confrontation end with "A fair summary" and the lamp. Move the creed's content into Lambrick's unsent letter — dead founders may philosophize; the living villain should only file.

5. **Emre's speech forecloses the comfort question.**
*Where:* §7 XIV — "Nobody here has ever met the dead. We only ever meet the early."
*Problem:* one sentence re-keys the entire belief debate and reassures: the kindness was real, the misrecognition harmless. Melek's question (Q3) is answered warmly in XI and endorsed again here.
*Fix:* make Emre's knowledge partial — he knows his own road, not the site's law. Break the generalization into a hesitating belief ("I don't think any of us ever met anyone but each other"), or cut it and let the ribbon, the jaw, and the wall-lines re-key everything inferentially.

6. **The belief side has no living, modern, intelligent advocate.**
*Where:* §4 cast.
*Problem:* faith is voiced by Melek (78, dying, vernacular), "the grandparents," and Yusuf after his conversion; the articulate positions are science, physics-as-faith, and ambiguity. The debate is decided by casting — belief wins by pathos and age.
*Fix:* add one believer under forty who has read Priska's own literature and keeps vigil anyway (the schoolteacher; Yusuf's mother), with one concrete image for why — and let her best Priska in at least one exchange on Priska's terms.

7. **The consequences in §8 are listed, not staged.**
*Where:* "safety as a kind of loss"; "the village resents her"; "Yusuf's retirement from streaming is its penance."
*Problem:* these are thematic labels with no face. The broken grief-economy — the book's best consequence — has no person-to-person scene.
*Fix:* one scene in X or XI: a young widow whose masked vigil at the fourth door now gets nothing asks Priska, without anger, what she is supposed to do now. Give Yusuf's penance one viewer who needed the stream. These two scenes would carry Q1 and Q5 better than the dialogue in V.

8. **Melek's question is answered twice, both times gently.**
*Where:* XI's confession; XIV's re-keying.
*Problem:* "Is a comfort false if the one who comforted you was never who you thought?" is resolved by Melek's serenity and then endorsed by the reveal. No cost, no residue.
*Fix:* let Melek die undecided — she asks Nilay to take the lamps precisely because she never settled whether her forty years were love or waste. And let one cross-when vignette show a mourner for whom the generic-kind company was *not* enough, so the theme tests kindness instead of certifying it.

9. **Science never gets an attendance scene.**
*Where:* Priska's arc (V, X).
*Problem:* science's role in the book is refutation and mandates; it is never shown comforting anyone. The substitutability question is lopsided — science can only take away.
*Fix:* one scene where Priska's work *enables* belief: her CO₂ map saves the vigils from a ban, or she explains hypoxia to the sleeping woman's family gently, and it helps them. "Science as the condition of belief's attendance" is a sharper question than any in §9 and is currently absent.

10. **The societal dimension is mostly one-liners.**
*Where:* Q10; Kaya Bey; XI's fence; XII's contractor.
*Problem:* "role of belief in society" needs institutions with bodies — who profits, who decides, who is excluded. The pilgrim economy has no vendor, no price, no local politics, and the sharpest societal question goes unasked: a foreign Trust administering a Cappadocian village's shrine since 1923.
*Fix:* give the fence a concrete economy — a villager selling "knocking tours" within a year of Melek's death (her living presence was the informal brake) — and let someone ask Halden, flatly, "Who elected the Trust?" His non-answer would be his true face.

11. **The Göbekli coda is a glossed benediction.**
*Where:* §7 XIV — "a scientist performing a rite."
*Problem:* the blueprint names the image's meaning; on the page it will read as the book blessing a synthesis, and Q8 ("knowledge or faith?") is asked aloud at the site.
*Fix:* make the gesture self-unknowing — she wets the stone the way she has wetted a thousand carved surfaces for field photography, realizes halfway through that she cannot tell method from rite, and neither can the reader. No spoken question.

12. **Q4 is asked after its own answer.**
*Where:* XII — Yusuf's "Who replaces the lullaby?" versus his lamp-lighting.
*Problem:* by the time he asks, he has already lit the lamp; the question arrives rhetorical.
*Fix:* cut the line (the lamp asks it) or move it to the moment he deletes the channel — unanswered, ugly, before any conversion.

13. **Melek's cross-century impact is asserted, never enacted.**
*Where:* §8 ("comforted countless grieving strangers in other whens"); interludes V and XI.
*Problem:* the largest consequence in the book is unstaged — presences are non-informational and the far side is opaque, so the reader is told of the ripple and never made to feel it.
*Fix:* charge Interlude V with the payoff: show the waiting keeper consoled by the annual visitor's same kind words, in felt detail, so that when XI reveals Melek's half of the same appointments, the reader has already *experienced* the consequence without having known it.

14. **The ending's synthesis is structural, not social.**
*Where:* XIV — Nilay holding both lamps and Register.
*Problem:* the village's reaction to an outsider scientist inheriting the lamps is absent. "The room chooses the one who stays" is a rule the community should get to apply; as designed, the final image of belief and science in one person is granted by paperwork and a vision, not by people.
*Fix:* one short scene at the lamp-passing — the village women's conditional acceptance or refusal to teach her; the schoolteacher from finding 6 naming what keeping costs. Let the inheritance be negotiated, even slightly refused, so the last open question has witnesses.
