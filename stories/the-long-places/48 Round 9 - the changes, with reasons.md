## 1. The reunion (XIV): Nilay asks him to come

Inserted after Emre's "I don't know the law," he said. "I know my road.", while they are still sitting by the lamps with the ribbon in her lap (219 words):

> "Come up with me." She had not known she would say it until it was said; it came out of her plain, the way the sentence at Halden's desk had come, with nothing kept back for later. "One morning, Emre. She keeps one room. You could walk into it."
>
> He was a long time answering. His hand went out toward the ribbon in her lap and stopped short of it, at the good distance, as a keeper's hand stops.
>
> "I went up once," he said. "The mouth doesn't ask which morning you want. You'd come out on yours. I'd come out on one of mine — strangers' smoke again, or a year she isn't in." He turned the can in his hands. "As it stands, she has a boy who went out for ten minutes after a kid. Up there I don't know what she'd get. I won't stand in her door a stranger in woven shoes and let her call it him."
>
> "She would call it him."
>
> "She would," he said. "That's why." And after a while, with his eyes on the ribbon and not on her: "Take her that. Tell her the looking got as far as me."
>
> She put the ribbon inside her coat, against her, where a hand could find it, and got up off the mat.

And at the parting, "and did not follow, and of course he did not follow." became "and did not follow, and she did not ask again."

**Why this, and why here.**
- **The choice now has two options on the page.** His hand goes out toward the ribbon and stops at the good distance, and he is "a long time answering". That pull toward home is set against what the book has already shown of him: the first winter, when the mouth opened "on a morning I couldn't read".
- **His answer uses only what the page already holds,** and adds no new rule. The mouth "doesn't ask which morning you want", which is the first winter again, so the road stays uncertain.
- **His answer is keeping in both senses,** the book's central idea as you named it. He keeps from their mother a stranger she would take for her son, because she would. He keeps for her "a boy who went out for ten minutes after a kid".
- **The moment she becomes able to leave is an action.** She puts the ribbon inside her coat and gets up off the mat. "She did not ask again" then replaces "of course".
- **The ribbon scene now knows what she brought and what she left.** The ribbon is one of the strips Nilay herself tied at eighteen to every place the search had entered (II). "Tell her the looking got as far as me" is its meaning. Later her mother "found it there and asked nothing", and whether Nilay passed on his words is left to the reader.
- **A mistake I caught in my own first placement.** I first put the ask at the wall, after "The room chose you". But by then they are standing, and the text had her "in her lap" and getting "up off the mat". I moved it to while they are still seated.

## 2. Rereading the ending

I reread XIV whole after the insertion. The ask now falls between his "I know my road" and the pages where the words *time travel* come to her later. So the confirmation of time travel still comes after his account and his refusal, and it stays the last thing revealed. The ribbon goes into her coat, is set down on the table by the bread oven, and is folded by her mother, with no gap in its path. The letter she had sealed ("I did not cry until the ribbon") is untouched.

**Kept on purpose**, for example:
- "The first register she ever kept was the absence of her brother" (II). You named this as a strength.
- "That was the etiquette of her trade" (X), which describes a trade rather than judging a scene.
- Priska's own "That, she supposed, was the finding" (X). It is her thought, and it is ironic.
- "one problem laid on another, which was how that drawer kept things" (XIV). It admits the sealed box's loop.
- "which is the way you leave a house that is not empty" (VII). It is Melek's manner, not a verdict on the scene.
