## Change 3: one person says to Halden's face what he did

Four of five readers asked for it. It must be:
- one or two spoken sentences, not a scene;
- not a trial, and no punishment;
- something that leaves Halden exactly as untouchable as he is.

**Where:** XIII, in Halden's office. Leave the passage where she stops at the closure ("accusations want a court") and he supplies the hospital ("The transfer was the least I could do."). Then, where the fourth hinge now lies unspoken ("She knew it by heart. She set it down nowhere, and he did not pick it up…"), **Nilay says it.**

- **How she says it:** plainly, in her own voice with the polish gone, for the only time in the book. She names only what the book has shown:
  - he kept the hours on that hill on the fourth and fifth of September 1999, the night her brother went down;
  - he wrote that nobody should go below the fourth door;
  - her mother has waited twenty-six years.
- **What she adds:** no new fact and no guess about what he knew of Emre's fate.
- **His answer:** the book's existing line, "You have kept excellent records," and nothing else. No denial, no confession, no change in his manner. Then the paragraph about not being able to find the sentence's weight stays as it is.
- **In XIV,** where she writes a third sentence to Valletta and strikes it through "because accusations want a court", change only the reason, so the phrase is not repeated and the moment follows from XIII: she has said it once, to his face, and paper would only give him something to file.

## Slips (each a word or a line)

1. **The grandmother's clock** (I, III, XIII). Tom's logic point: a clock that stayed at home cannot lose an hour while its owner is underground.
   - It becomes a pocket watch she carried down with her, a wedding piece. In I: "Her grandmother had kept a pocket watch, a wedding piece, and carried it down with her."
   - Every mention changes to match, including III's retelling and XIII's 1924 note: "The keeper's watch retards one hour upon each night below".
   - Keep the saying "a clock is a donkey, you beat it and it walks" word for word; a watch is a clock.
2. **XI letter: "hers for forty years before it was mine"** against Melek's "It chose me sixty years ago" in the same chapter. Two critics raised it in round 4 and I let it through. "forty" becomes "sixty". Her "forty winters" at the niche is a different thing and stays.
3. **The handprint is told more than once** (XIV; four of five readers). Keep the certificate and the fingerprint note, because the concept asks for a confirmation. Cut the narrated line that says the print "had always been hers", so that the paper does the work.
4. **"Nine"** is never explained; two readers took it for a number or a surname. At its first use (V, "Fatma Nine"), add a few words saying that *Nine* is the village's word for a grandmother. Explain it nowhere else.

**Also in XIV:**
- **Emre's explanation speech** ("a road that's long instead of far"): four of five readers disliked it as lore. Cut it by about half. Keep its confirmation of time travel, which the concept needs at the end, and keep "a road that's long instead of far".
- **Give him one or two short lines as the boy he was,** plain and thirteen, beside "I have never once had shoes". All five readers picked that line as the only moment the boy came through.
- **The recognition moment:** remove the long bracketed aside about the name when Nilay says "Emre". One reader called it "the one beat I most wanted left clean".
- **The doubled "Nobody counted twice"** (VII): cut the second, standalone sentence.
