1. **Ch. XIV vs. Ch. I–II — the book's own arithmetic is off by a year.** The permit reads "Kırk Oda, 2025 season" and Nilay arrives in June 2025 at forty-four (eighteen in 1999 plus "twenty-six years of arriving as a stranger" — correct). But the story then runs a full second year: Márton dies December 2025, Melek dies June 2026, and the vigil falls on 4 September **2026**. At that point Emre would be **forty**, not "He would be thirty-nine"; Nilay is **forty-five**, not "she was herself again, forty-four"; and she emerges "twenty-six years and a day" after a date that is twenty-seven years past. In a book whose stated spine is dates — Yusuf says so — a hard off-by-one at the climax is the worst possible place to fail. Fix: either change the permit to "2024 season" so the vigil lands in 2025, or age everything forward one year (forty, forty-five, twenty-seven years and a day).

2. **Ch. XIV — "And the year re-keyed itself as the road went through her."** This paragraph resolves clues for the reader: "both surveys had counted, and counted different whens," "Márton's watches had gone down into a thick place in the world's agreement," and worst, "every warmth that had settled against a side of any room… had been a neighbor, out of order, being kind to a stranger" — stated as narration, then immediately re-hedged as "He said it as belief… and she filed it as belief." The book's design is that the re-keying happens in the reader, on reread, not on the page; here the narration converts Emre's belief into a verdict and then apologizes for it, wobbling in the space of four sentences. Fix: cut the paragraph to the residue sentence ("And some of it the road did not take back…") plus at most one hedged clause. The crossed-out forty-one and the watches need no gloss; thirteen chapters planted them.

3. **Ch. VIII, IX, XI, XII, XIII, XIV — the "two dates side by side" beat, six times.** Nilay (or Yusuf) lays two dates side by side in a notebook in VIII, IX, XI, XII, XIII, and XIV, three times with the verbatim coda "did not write the word that goes with such a pair." The first instance (the fourteenth and the fifteenth) is genuinely chilling; by the fourth it is a ritual the reader performs in advance, and by the sixth Halden's menace has become mechanical — a detected pattern is a clockwork villain, not an uncanny one. Fix: keep the VIII discovery and Yusuf's XII variant ("dates are the spine," character-specific); compress IX and XI to a gesture ("she added the pair to the page of pairs"); delete the verbatim coda after its first use.

4. **Ch. XIV — "There was no machine. There was keeping."** The summit of the book, stated as a thesis couplet. Everything around it — the oil on her cuffs, the round done before the vigil, the wick pinched true — has already enacted this sentence, which is exactly why the sentence shouldn't exist. It is the one place the narration flattens the book's whole argument into a slogan. Fix: end at "…and gave them back." Cutting "There was no machine. There is keeping." to just the first clause, or nothing, lets the keeping do the saying.

5. **Ch. VIII — "M. Yılmaz, keeper" arrives unbound.** Statement 4 and Kállai's "party of four (Arat, self, Demir, Yılmaz)" introduce a surname the narration has never given Melek. Ch. VIII is precisely the chapter where the reader must track four contradictory accounts; an unexplained name forces them to stop and reverse-engineer whether Yılmaz is Melek or a fifth person, blunting the designed discrepancies. Fix: one clause in Ch. I — the consents, or the permit's site-keeper line, or her introduction ("Melek Yılmaz, seventy-eight") — and the binding holds.

6. **Ch. IX — "He strapped the three watches on… where they had ridden all winter," two paragraphs after "the watches worn, for once."** The sanctioned unreliability is the left-wrist/right-wrist flag with the same phrase; this third, accidental contradiction ("ridden all winter" vs. "for once," the whole solstice design having been *unworn*) makes the designed flag look like sloppiness rather than pattern. Fix: delete "where they had ridden all winter." Keep the wrist drift clean and alone.

7. **Ch. IX, X, XIII — Halden's tags are hardening into a catchphrase.** He gives two different mourners near-identical benedictions ("The sites ask little but attendance, Professor. You have attended." / "You attended, Doctor. The sites ask little else."); "at the pace of a man with no appointment he intended to share" appears verbatim three times (IX, X, XIII); "The not-remembering was ordinary. The remembering was not." appears verbatim in consecutive chapters. Ch. XII even confirms the erosion: "The platform had learned, at the end, to write like the Registrar" — his manner has become a *genre*, and a quotable villain is a small step toward a performing one. The tea ritual is fine (it is his office-round, the keeper's mirror); the verbal tags are not. Fix: in X he leaves without a door-line at all; vary the IX and X departures; reserve "no appointment he intended to share" for the XIII lamp figure only, where identification is load-bearing; differentiate the IX/X aphorism in one of its two instances.

8. **Ch. XIII — the plane inventory recapitulates the book.** "Four explanations had gone down into that hill… like men carrying a table, each holding one corner and calling it the table" re-explains a structure the reader has carried for thirteen chapters, on the eve of the reveal, and the table metaphor states the theme while doing it. The chapter also runs several beats past its emotional peak (the hospital line) — the wrapping, the stair question, "Consider overnight" all arrive at the same temperature. Fix: cut the inventory to a single sensory sentence (the parcel cold on her knees); let Halden's sheet and the protocol be the only freight of the flight home.

9. **Ch. XIV — Emre's "It lets you up where it let you down."** The mechanics speech is rightly plain and hedged; this one clause is gnomic to the point of unparseability, and it is load-bearing (it explains why she returns the same night). Opaque phrasing on the single point of mechanics produces confusion, not dissociation. Fix: cut the clause — the fact performs it: she went down before dark on the fourth and came out at first light on the fifth, and the reader watches it happen.

10. **Ch. X — "They had been two griefs in lab coats all season… and the coats had finally stopped making them colleagues."** The scene has just enacted the break perfectly ("Goodnight, Doctor," "the title went in cold where the name had been"); this closing sentence explains the relationship the reader just watched end. Fix: delete it. The dialogue owns the beat.

11. **Ch. VI and XII — Yusuf's lesson is stated twice, once before he learns it.** Ch. VI narrates "the difference between being watched and being attended, for which his vocabulary had only the first half"; Ch. XII then lands the earned version ("Watched was content. Attended was kept"). Naming the arc in VI steals XII's landing, and "which was, he had decided, the entire report" winks at the theme's sufficiency. Fix: strip the VI gloss to pure action (Melek's look, the phone going away) and cut "which was… the entire report" from XII.

12. **Throughout — lexical tics flattening the prose.** "Unhurried" appears nine or ten times (the far keepers, the rain, Melek's voice, the parcel, the figure at the threshold); "at an entirely ordinary speed" five times; the "the way…" simile template scores of times. The designed echoes — "cupped, and low," "stone hears rain," the tide paragraph — lose their charge when the template saturates. Fix: cap "unhurried" at three, reserved for the far keepers' measured actions; keep "entirely ordinary speed" as a Trust-mail signature (twice); prune "the way…" by a third, prioritizing the interludes and mid-chapters.

13. **Ch. IV — Melek's rule-homily explains what the founder's slip planted.** "Below the fourth door, count nothing" is already in Nilay's pocket in a notary's hand; Melek then delivers ~150 words of structured exposition ("A number is a handle, kızım… what was cut slowly should be entered slowly") in a register above her weather-report laconics. Dialogue is doing the slip's work twice. Fix: keep "The half you don't have is for the house. Below the fourth door, you don't count," the singing room's pardon ("it's a small number and it likes him"), and "say forty, and be polite, and mean nothing by it" — cut the handles/time-than-iron middle.

14. **Ch. V — Seher's stacked aphorisms.** "Wanting is also a gas," "Declining is not the same as knowing," and "When your fan runs, I hear it until morning" land within one page; the middle one states the book's entire epistemology in a minor character's mouth, and three in a row turn her toward fortune-teller. Fix: cut "Declining is not the same as knowing." The gas line and the fan line carry the scene — one is her holding the forks out, the other is her night reporting itself.

15. **Ch. XII — the consultancy catalogue opens the chapter in summary.** Three emails ("The first… The second… The third…") rehearsed in narration static the opening third while the chapter's real matter — Gülce, the deletion, the threshold — waits behind it. Fix: compress the first two emails to one paragraph of summary and dramatize only the paper letter (franked, wrong font, finding the family), which is already the best object in the sequence; arrive at Gülce a page sooner.

16. **Ch. XII/XIV — the fourteen-second stick is a heavy plant that never pays.** Yusuf's "watched: 0 — the proudest number he had," the handover scene, and two later drawer inventories build real weight; in XIV the stick only lies there ("whose label said *viewed: no* and would go on saying it"), inert. The unfired choice reads less like residue the book declines to own and more like a thread forgotten. Fix: one clause at the drawer in XIV — she had the tape and chose the sealed hour instead, having learned from the boy what a tiebreaker costs — and the plant closes as a decision rather than a lapse.
