## Checks that passed

- **Plan carried out.** Every chapter item was done, apart from Row 1's deliberate change. All 23 canon strings (C1–C23) appear word for word.
- **Fixed texts.** The closing letter is identical to the I letter, and both match the original. The four tide paragraphs are unchanged and read 18, 19, 20 and 18. Priska's "twenty minutes out, twenty in" against Nilay's "eighteen" (XI) now makes the change read as deliberate.
- **Forbidden words.** "Time travel" appears once, in XIV. "Wormhole", "ghost", "spirit", "séance" and "afterlife" never appear. "The dead" appears only in the VIII letter.
- **Capped phrases.** "fork(s)" appears 9 times, all in the 8 permitted places. "flat on the palm" appears 3 times, "weather" 26 times and "the way you" 24 times, never twice in one paragraph. Every other capped phrase is down to its one kept use.
- **Old signals and slips gone.** All of these are cut or fixed: the timestamps an hour behind, the lost afternoon, "at a different —", "arrived before it was sent", the date inversions in VI, IX and XII (now framed as cunning rather than foreknowledge), the two Twelfths, the "forty-first page", the Sunday, "tourch(es)", the "twenty-seven years" and the broken sentence.
- **Seams.** At V/VI, the chronology now works: the first stare is in July, V's stare happens "again", and the fan and masks no longer appear before the order. At IX/X, the dates (26th, 27th, 30th and 31st), Priska's return on the evening of the 30th, Marques in January, the right wrist and the lamp all agree, apart from Row 5.
- **The four decoy explanations** each stay one step short of proof. The parallel-reality reading now has a serious adult and hard numbers (Márton's 14 µGal and "two good maps"). The far-future visitors are stranger (the speech, the teeth, the forearm, the hair), each with a mundane way out, and Emre's line is now a guess.
- **Halden** is now a formidable threat and still passive. In IV the reader watches him choose a dead man's rule over a boy's search, with his own margin note placing him at the site that night. The Kayseri postmark (VI), the stand-down, parcel and visit (IX), the knowledge rather than foreknowledge (XII), the hospital and the fourth hinge (XIII), and Emre's grey man who "took the light" (XIV) build on it. On the page he only pours, signs, files, wipes, reads, asks and glances. He never raises his voice or threatens anyone, and every act stays deniable.
- **Length.** The book is 49,497 words, 0.3% over the original 49,349. Every chapter is within ±5% of its target except XIII, which is 5.3% over (3,475 against 3,300). No action is needed.

## Verdict for the owner

The revised book reads as one book. The three revisers' work joins cleanly, the voice is still GLM's, and every planned change is in, word for word where the plan required it. The concept rules hold:
- Time travel is named once, at the end of XIV, and confirmed there by Emre, the letter dated tomorrow, the carbon date and the fingerprint.
- Fumes, parallel reality, wormhole and the dead are each hinted almost to canon and never confirmed.
- The far-future visitors remain unprovable.

The biggest gain is Halden. He is now a villain the reader fears from IV onward, and he never once raises his voice. Nilay also now makes a real choice that costs something (siting the drill in VII).

Two quick must-fixes remain, both one-line edits: the 05:40 collision in VI's log and the "Nobody had knocked" line at the IX/X seam. Nine smaller should-fixes follow, each a phrase or less. The one that matters most for your rules is Row 8, the XI letter: it now gives an alert reader a time clue before XIV. With those edits made, the book is ready.
