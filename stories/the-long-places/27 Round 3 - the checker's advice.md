# Round 3 verification: the SUBSTANTIVE findings checked against the book

**What I read.**
- The owner's concept.
- All fourteen round-2 chapter files, in full.
- The four round-3 critiques: story, people, continuity, and MiMo. MiMo's critique (`critique-mimo.md`) was there when I started.
- The round-1 and round-2 rulings and the round-2 verification, so that I could tell when a finding repeats one already settled.

I did not weigh `fable-review.md` in this folder, because it is not one of the four critiques I was given. These verdicts are advice. The main Claude session makes the final ruling.

**Method.**
- I checked every finding a critic marked SUBSTANTIVE against the chapter text. There are nine: Story 1, People 1, Continuity 1–3 and MiMo 1–4.
- Line numbers are from the round-2 chapter files, written as chapter:line.
- MiMo 1 and MiMo 4 overlap (Halden is not frightening). They make different claims, so they have separate rows, set next to each other.
- I did not re-rule findings that every critic marked QUIBBLE. The notes after the table cover them.

The verdicts:
- **CONFIRMED:** the problem is real, and substantive by the definition.
- **QUIBBLE:** the problem is real, but only a quibble.
- **WRONG:** the finding is not true of the book.

| 1 | **The masks cannot stop carbon dioxide, yet the book's expert on that gas prescribes them against it and credits them with the cure.** | Continuity 1 | **CONFIRMED** | Priska's plan and the order: "and masks below the second door on sitting nights. The order as issued named accumulated carbon dioxide in still conditions as the cause … mandated the masks" (V:67). Her key question is built on the mask: "When the mask works and the visitation stops — what did we cure?" (V:63). She gives Márton the mask as protection for his night: "Wear the mask below the second door" (IX:59), and she has driven "to Kayseri for cartridges" (IX:59). The narrator calls them "masks against dust" (V:89). Below the second door the mask is the only protection: "The duct came down as far as the second door, per the order, and the rest was lungs" (VII:33). Filter masks and cartridges let CO₂ straight through, and since 2020 that is common knowledge. The error cannot be the villagers' or the Ministry's, because the book makes Priska exact about this very gas ("four point zero percent. Forty thousand parts per million", V:49; "hypercapnia", V:27). The mask also carries weight in the argument. VI:31 uses it as a control ("he would count in a mask, and the forty-one would come the same, mask or no"). X:55 blames Márton for refusing the plan. The book's own figures half-rescue it, since both events after the masks fell "on the one week the fan was down" (V:69). But her words to Márton and her central question still rest on the mask. So an informed reader loses faith in the one explanation that has a stamp, and the owner wants that explanation almost canon. It also makes the book's most rigorous character act against her expertise. A few words fix it, at V:67 and V:89 (scrubber canisters, or masks on hoses fed from the duct). |

In a book that trains its reader to set numbers side by side, a careful reader stops there and takes it for an error. Round 1 filed it among small slips, but it was never ruled on and never fixed. ## Notes on findings every critic marked QUIBBLE (not re-ruled)

I agree they are quibbles. A few points are worth passing on.
- **Continuity 9**, "her clipboard steaming coffee" (IX:53), is a garbled phrase, not a matter of taste. It can be fixed at no cost.
- **Story 4**: the closure memo's "all persons not of the licensed party" (VIII:129) sits against the women sitting "lawful" on the solstice (IX:49). The two can be reconciled, because the standing order annexed Priska's plan: "eleven women and nine lamps below the second door" (V:67). The critic's wording would remove the doubt cheaply.
- **Story 3**: Márton's "where every battery died" (IX:69) is loosely supported elsewhere. Yusuf's phone "would not turn on" (VII:81), and the camera came back "refusing to wake" (VIII:133). So it is not a flat contradiction of VII:41.
- **MiMo 5**: the claim is that time travel is not the last thing revealed. It is not a breach. The print and the wall's loop are the time-travel reveal itself, not a later mystery.
- **People 4**: "I don't think any of us ever met anyone but each other" (XIV:67) is muddy in the one paragraph that must be clear. It is still a quibble, because nothing it says is contradicted outright.
