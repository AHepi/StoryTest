# Editorial review: *The Long Places*, round-6 full revision

**Reviewer:** Claude (Fable 5.1).
**What I read:** the owner's concept and rules first, then the whole novella from the page, start to finish (fourteen chapters, about 49,700 words). I did not read any other review of it. Every quotation below is copied from the text as it stands; line numbers refer to the round-6 file so the owner can find each passage.

A note on words: where I have to use a term of the trade I explain it once in plain words and then keep using it. The book's own word *fork* means "a second, ordinary explanation for a strange thing"; I use it the way the book does.

---

## 1. Verdict

**What the book is.** A slow, quiet mystery told over one year at Kırk Oda, an eleven-thousand-year-old cave-shrine in central Turkey that has never once gone dark. Each of the fourteen chapters opens with an italic letter from a lamp-keeper "to the one who keeps the lamps after me," and then the prose chapter turns among four modern people: Nilay Arat, the archaeologist whose thirteen-year-old brother vanished under the hill in 1999; Márton Kállai, a geophysicist with three atomic watches and an old wound; Priska Vogel, a Swiss scientist of bad air; and Yusuf Demir, nineteen, who streams the lamps to eleven viewers and then to four million. Over them sits the Anchorhold Trust of Valletta and its Registrar, A. Halden, who copies his letters by hand, pours tea he never drinks, and has known everything a day early since March.

**What it achieves.** It keeps every one of the owner's rules, and keeps most of them with real grace: the three rival explanations (bad air, a second copy of the place, a hole through space) each get a chapter, a scientist, a stamp, and a splinter; the visitors from farther along are met and never explained; and the last chapter confirms time as the thing under everything, with a proof no ordinary reading can carry. The villain is the book's best invention: a filing clerk of the long road who kept a lost boy off every record for twenty-seven years by writing "Nothing to record" while sitting beside him.

**Is it finished?** As a story, yes. Structure, voice, ending and villain all hold. What remains is at the level of a careful copyedit: one small contradiction of fact in Chapter VI, and a handful of word-placement and plausibility quibbles. I would not ask for another structural pass. I would fix the one contradiction, take the one quibble that guards the final proof, and then stop, because this draft's chief remaining risk is being revised past its own quiet.

## 2. How it keeps each of the owner's rules

### Rule: time travel is the very last reveal, confirmed at the end, tying every mystery together

**Kept.** I searched the text: the words *time travel* appear exactly once, in Chapter XIV (line 1359), after the brother has spoken; the words *future*, *wormhole*, *parallel*, *ghost*, *spirit* and *the dead* never appear at all. Before the last chapter, nothing names the mechanism.

The confirmation arrives in three pieces that no fork can carry, in this order:

1. The sealed box. Nilay wakes in the polished room with the wax "whole," and inside the letter she sealed yesterday is a second sheet "in your hand — your hand, down to the loop of the y — in your phrasing, and at the head of it, the date: the fifth — today, and when the wax went on, tomorrow" (1305). Its last line, "*and I did not cry until the ribbon,*" is then lived (1313). Later that day she writes the sheet "word for word... because she could not not" (1383). This is what people call a bootstrap loop: a thing that exists only because its own later copy was handed back. The book set it up in Chapter XIII with the founder's 1951 box, "wax unbroken, contents as deposited, plus one item: [inked out]" (1244), so it is planted before it is paid.
2. Her brother, aged into his forties, returning the red ribbon she tied in 1999.
3. The hand print. The founder's 1924 phial of pigment is dated "*10,720 ± 90 years before present*" (1395), and a technician's chit says the print "*matched someone in the elimination file. Friction ridges don't lie. 31 points, right hand*" (1397): hers. The narration says plainly, "It was the one measurement taken all year that no fork could carry" (1399). Then the Book Zero entry: "*The keeper wrote this before she lived it*" (1405).

Does it tie the mysteries together? Yes, and it does so in the brother's mouth in eight sentences, which is the right length: "It isn't a door to anywhere. It's a road that's long instead of far. A place that's kept long enough stops being in only one of its times — it's in all of them, the way a road is in all its miles. Keeping is the thread. The rooms come when they're kept" (1353). Under that one sentence the reader can now file the hour-behind clock, the forty-first room ("Suppose instead two good maps," 504), the triangle that will not close, the watches forty nanoseconds off, the Trust's letters dated before the events they thank people for, Melek's forty winters at the niche ("Every keeper keeps that niche some winters. I kept it nine," 1343), the humming in 1999, the fossil-old water and the fresh grain that reads impossibly old, the "far lamplighters," and Halden's 1949 photograph. The narration then sorts the leftovers honestly at line 1361: "Some of it re-keyed itself without her consent... And some of it the road could have carried, and she did not make it carry them." That is the rule kept exactly: everything material is tied; the things that belong to the dead are left where rule 2 says they must be left.

**One judgment call for the owner.** The book confirms the fact and then hands the word back: "two small clumsy words, *time travel*, and she turned them over once, the way you turn over a borrowed tool, and gave them back" (1359). I read that as confirmation delivered in this book's manner, and I would leave it. If the owner wants the word to *stand* rather than be returned, the cheapest place is the gate at first light, where Priska, who says plain words, could say it once and be answered "It kept." I do not recommend it; I note it.

### Rule: contact with the dead, leaned into but never said

**Kept.** The sitting nights are built for it: Havva Nine surfaces from bad air saying "I spoke with my mother. She was cross with me for the hour" (431); Priska hears "Her father's cough. Twelve years of it, two notes and the knock" (435) on a clean reading; Melek confesses forty years of four sentences into a niche and refuses to settle it: "If it was him, he kept his manners. If it was not him, then I have been faithful to a room" (1007). The narration never says the dead are reached; the women say it, and the book lets them. The ending re-reads Melek's visitor as a keeper from another time without closing the question: "I believe it was somebody keeping... Taking her for somebody of their own. That is what I believe; I believed it before I had a right to" (1347). Priska's cough and Havva's mother are deliberately not carried by the road (1361). Exactly right.

### Rule: fumes, a parallel reality, a wormhole, each hinted almost to canon and never confirmed

**Fumes, kept.** Priska's chapter takes it all the way to a stamp: "The fumes were canon now, administratively, with signatures — the only doctrine in the valley that came with paperwork" (447), and the masks work: "on twenty-two nights before the masks, fifteen events; on twenty-six nights after, two" (449). Then the Trust's splinter makes the number "*considered indicative*" and "a restored certificate is a certificate with a biography" (467). By the inquest the air is "unconvicted, unacquitted, and open" (892). Canon, then not.

**Parallel reality, kept.** Yusuf gives it as a joke that will not stay a joke: "It's not a mystery, it's infrastructure. The site's rendering two instances of itself — same map, two builds — and the sync's bad" (510). Márton gives it as a half-finished thought: "I had assumed one hill and two bad maps. Suppose instead two good maps.' He did not finish the supposition" (504). The keeper's letter of Chapter VII gives it as a guess: "far aside, as the next house is far, the one you can hear through the wall and never enter" (549). Never confirmed.

**Wormhole, kept, and never named.** The newspapers' word in 1997 is "a word out of physics lectures and bad films, a hole through everything — and he had spent a career declining to be the man who said it" (247). Marques gives the physics and the objection: "A throat wants negative energy... Show me the machine" (815). Márton's whiteboard carries it to the end: "*A TRAVERSABLE THROAT NEEDS NEGATIVE ENERGY: WHERE IS THE GENERATOR?*" and beneath it "*or: what is the fuel?*" (833). The keeper's letter has it as "far down, as a well is far" (549). And the last chapter answers the fuel question sideways, without confirming the hole: "Keeping is the thread." That is a lovely piece of joinery.

### Rule: humans from the astronomically distant future, never provable

**Kept.** The three in the polished room are given only by their effects: teeth "perfect, regular as a comb, and fewer than a mouth has... as if the mouth had been drawn rather than grown" (597); a weave that "repeated nowhere" (591); a hair "matching every reference population a little and none well" (732); ash and grain the laboratory refuses as contamination (902); the question "Is the fire mountain awake?" (603) about a volcano that sleeps now. The brother's one line keeps it a guess: "The ones below — I think they keep too. From where, I couldn't tell you. Farther than I've been, and not the way you're going" (1353). Kaya Bey's hermits stand at the other door the whole way: "a community could keep a lower house, and keep its own counsel, and keep it clean" (621). Nobody can ever be sure, including the reader.

### Rule: sharp, concise questions about belief and science

**Kept, and this is the book's strongest suit.** The questions are short and each is held by a scene:

- Priska, over a sleeping woman she has just saved: "When the mask works and the visitation stops — what did we cure?" (443)
- Priska, in the dark: "If it can't be falsified, it isn't knowledge. It's comfort." (463)
- Seher, to Priska: "I read your paper twice, Doctor. It explains every night except the one I needed." (455) And: "Wanting is also a gas." (459) And, of the third-man papers: "Nowhere do they say the companion was not there. The papers decline. You decline too, Doctor — kindly." (459)
- Márton: "It was measured. That is a different thing from real, and a different thing from false." (251)
- Halden, to Priska: "What, today, on your instrument, in your hand, could you sign?" (912)
- Halden, to Nilay: "Would you want it explained, if explaining it ended it?" (1272)
- Seher, at the end: "It costs the question. You put the question down the way you put down a load at a door, and then your hands are free, and the hands are all the work there is." (1035)

The book also polices its own preaching: Yusuf types "*science replaced the priest at the bedside. who replaces the lullaby?*" and deletes it "because it was a sermon and she had never once asked him for one" (1092). And the last word on the theme is shown, not said: Nilay wets a stone at Göbekli Tepe "and stopped, halfway through, her hand still open, because she could not tell the method from the rite; and went on wetting it anyway, and did not decide" (1411).

### Rule: the theme of impact and consequence

**Kept.** Each act has a bill. One recovered frame becomes four million views, a pin, cars, a fence, a nephew's laminated price list, a knock that is finally answered "on a still evening in May" (967), and then a knock cheapened past use: "the rail filled with people explaining the hill to one another. The answers thinned the same season" (975). The founder stops a remittance "as an economy" and a drowned chapel goes "quiet since 1961" (1207, 1231). The founder shaves a finger's cup of pigment in 1924 and it becomes, a century later, the proof. Halden's 1999 letter, "advises that none be entered" (328), keeps a boy below for twenty-seven years. Priska's fan saves a life and Seher says, "When your fan runs, I hear it until morning" (459). Márton's parcel "*For your peace*" (839) is followed by his death.

### Rule: mind-bending dissociation

**Kept.** The devices are many and they compound: the shaft's tide timed at eighteen minutes, then nineteen, then twenty, then eighteen again, each time with the same sentence "anyone who timed it came away with the same figure"; the threshold spoken twice as "one thousand one hundred forty-two point six meters" (213) and read at the inquest as "point five" (898); the watches that were on his left wrist all summer and in December are "on his right wrist — *where the artery is closest*, he had said in July, of the left" (888); the tea Yusuf remembers drinking that stands full and cold on the wall (516); four honest statements that agree only "on the ones with mass" (726); Nilay's filed statement that contradicts her own notebook (738); and the last chapter's shift to *you*: "You wake where you sat down. That is the first fact, and it arrives before any room does" (1303). The wrist is the best of these because the book does it the right way round for prose: from inside Márton his body still feels right, and it is Priska, from outside, who catches it.

### Rule: a villain of Johan Liebert's quality

**Kept, and it is the book's own creation.** Halden is never once seen doing anything. He pours tea "with both hands" and drinks none (308); he "opened the door at the moment knocking became necessary, so that she never quite knocked" (308); "He heard her out with the whole of his attention, which was the most disquieting thing about him" (910). He reaches through paper: a sponsorship she never applied for, consents signed in April for a meeting in June, a letter thanking Yusuf for restraint dated the day before Yusuf showed it (524), a closure memo dated the day before Priska requested it (760), a solstice grant Márton never applied for (803), a hospital transfer dated the day before Melek's collapse: "The transfer," he said, "was the least I could do" (1260). He reads what nobody publishes, asking Priska "whether the thirteenth bore of her canton was still closed for lining" (910), her dead father's bore, and asking Márton after "the small cellist" he never mentioned (845).

His crime is assembled by the reader, never announced. The Register margin: "*4–5.ix. Kept the hours. A.H.*" (314). His letter of the seventh: "advises that none be entered" (328). And the brother, twenty-seven years later: "The first night a man sat with me. Grey. At the good distance. He had a light that didn't flicker, and he wrote something down by it, and asked me nothing, and in the morning, while I slept, he went up and took the light with him. I thought he was a keeper... Keepers leave you the lamp" (1323). His creed is the whole of his defence: "It would be a poor registry that kept them only when they were cheap" (328). The book's last image of him is exact: "a margin in a green volume, *4–5.ix*, that had kept a boy off every record for twenty-seven" (1401).

He is formidable to the last line. Nilay sends him the proof with a challenge, "The Trust will know what to do with nothing," and he answers, "*Received. Filed. The Trust's line for the year stands as cut: Nothing to record*" (1401). She strikes out her accusation "because accusations want a court, and she had a hill" (1401). He wins by her choosing, which is the Johan move. And the one thing that could have been show-stopping, the 1949 photograph of an unchanged man, is handled as a smudge: "the face was three-quarters available and no use whatsoever" (1213). His motive is never given, and I tested that as a possible hole: three readings are each held by the page (a registry that "does not correct what it did not record"; a keeper of the long road who serves the loop; a man who kept a founder's rule at any price), and nothing on the page settles between them. That is a working open question, not a gap. Leave it.

## 3. What works best

**The keepers' letters as the other side of the same night.** This is the book's great structural idea and it must be protected. Chapter II's letter is the 1999 storm night told by the keeper who hummed: "She will keep that night wrong, and be kept by it" (102). Chapter V's letter is Melek's forty winters told by the one on the other side of the niche: "Whoever she takes you for, be the right size" (403). Chapter VI's letter is Yusuf's forty-one morning seen from another time, and the reader can work it out unaided: "he carried the sun in his hand" (476), "at his hip a small box ticked, slow, like a cricket that had been taught to count" (482). Chapter VII's letter gives the book's three rival theories as three keepers' guesses about the word *far*: "far down, as a well is far... far aside, as the next house is far... far along" (549). Chapter XI's letter is Melek's last round from the other side. Not one of the thirteen letters is idle.

**Márton.** The finest character arc. "Two clocks, he had decided long ago, can only argue. Three are a committee, and a committee can be outvoted" (189). His 1997 wound: "One referee used the word *wishful*, in a footnote, which is where wounds are legally inflicted" (247). His result: "Twice is a result... he wept at the laptop — without drama, the way it comes to old men, at a desk, over arithmetic" (829). His last line: "he was sixty-eight years old, and he had wanted, all his life, a longer baseline" (851). And the coin: "I left the house a lamp," he said, "and was paid for it at the going rate. I have been robbed in four countries, Doctor, and this was the first transaction of my life that ran the other way" (766).

**Priska's honesty.** "At 05:40 she wrote: *0.5, still.* It was the cleanest reading of her career and the only dishonest page in it" (439). And her last exchange as a witness: "What do I have to believe?" "Nothing. That's why it's you." "Then it will hold" (1295–1299).

**The four statements (Chapter VIII).** Four honest people, four hours; the disagreements are set out like a survey that will not close, and the compiler refuses to adjudicate: "Where the hour had touched matter, the hour agreed with itself. Where it had touched people, it did not" (726).

**The mother and the ribbon.** No speech, no tears named: "her thumb went on smoothing the top fold, long after there was nothing left to smooth... and watched her daughter go out with the oil can to the evening round, the way she had once watched a boy go out in house slippers, and did not call anything after her" (1393).

**Yusuf's ending.** "*recovered: 14 sec. watched: 0.* The zero was the proudest number he had" (1098). Nilay writes "*Viewed: no* as a property of the object, the way one writes dimensions" (1114). And the lamp in his uncle's cellar: "the first thing he had ever kept that could not be viewed" (1150).

**The last line of the loop.** The closing letter repeats Chapter I's word for word, and every sentence now reads twice: "Nobody lights a first lamp. You come down and they are lit, and someone is owed a measure of oil and a thank-you" (1433) is now the bootstrap; "Sleep above the fourth door when rain has been on the land" (1437) is now the rule that turned the sergeant back from a boy. Protect the repeat; do not trim it.

## 4. The problems, most serious first

### SUBSTANTIVE

**S1. Chapter VI: the footage dies on the evening of the count, yet is uploaded and fails "four nights running."**

*Passage.* On the day of the forty-one, "At dinner the card asked to be formatted. He declined. It asked again later, politely, the way institutions ask. The day's folder came up zero bytes, all of it, and the recovery tool ground through the night and handed him, in the morning, one frame" (518). Two paragraphs later, about the same day's footage: "The upload of that day failed at ninety-seven percent four nights running, and after that there was no file to upload, and the reflection was not footage anymore" (520).

*Problem.* Both cannot be true. If the folder was zero bytes on the first night, there was nothing to upload on nights two, three and four. The scrubbing "at lunch" (520) fits either order; the four nights do not.

*Why it matters.* This is a small contradiction of fact (what editors call a continuity slip: a detail that does not stay consistent from page to page), but it sits inside the passage whose whole subject is what a record keeps and what a person keeps. A careful reader here is counting. And the number is load-bearing later: in Chapter XII the consultancy "cited the failed transfer at ninety-seven percent — a number he had said aloud exactly once in his life, at minute forty-one of his last stream, in September" (1072), which is how Yusuf knows he is being watched.

*Fix (one or the other).* Either move the card's death to the fourth night: "On the fourth night the card asked to be formatted" instead of "At dinner"; or keep the card dying at dinner and change the uploads to what an afternoon allows: "The upload had failed at ninety-seven percent twice that afternoon, and by morning there was no file to upload." Either keeps the ninety-seven percent for Chapter XII. I would take the second, because the same-day death is the crueller and the book prefers cruel.

### QUIBBLES

**Q1. Chapter XIV: the last proof rests on a 1924 photograph carrying thirty-one ridge points.** The chit reads "*the old print (Trust photograph, item 51, 1924, submitted by the sponsoring institution for elimination, as a courtesy to the inquest) matched someone in the elimination file. Friction ridges don't lie. 31 points, right hand*" (1397). Ridge detail can survive in pigment on stone, and examiners do match from photographs; but a photograph made in 1924 of a hand print in ochre is asking a lot of a reader who knows fingerprint work, and this is the single proof the whole book stands on. The book already owns the better object: in Chapter II the 1998 draughtsmen worked "from somebody's field squeeze" (112), a squeeze being a wet-paper cast pressed onto a carved surface, which takes ridges physically. One word does it: "*Trust squeeze, item 51, 1924*." It also puts the founder's hands on the wall one more time, which is where the book wants them. I have marked this a quibble because most readers will not balk; it is the quibble I would most want done.

**Q2. Chapter V: the mask tally reaches weeks past the chapters that follow it.** Priska reports "on twenty-six nights after, two — both on the one week the fan was down for a bearing" (449). The masks cannot begin before about the second of September ("The Ministry's fax came in two days, and Kaya Bey came in three... She spent two evenings," 447), so twenty-six nights run to the end of September, yet Chapter VI then rewinds to "the last night of August" (512) and Chapter VII opens the chamber on the seventh of September. Nothing is contradicted, and the same figures return in Chapter XIV ("fifteen events in twenty-two nights, two in twenty-six," 1361), so the tally itself should stay. Only its tense is early. Fix: let the count be running rather than closed: "on the nights since, so far, two — both in the one week the fan was down for a bearing," and let the settled figure belong to Chapter XIV.

**Q3. Chapter VIII: the closure memo shuts the site to everyone unlicensed, yet the women sit "lawful" in December.** The memo: "*closed to casual attendance and to all persons not of the licensed party until further notice*" (760). Chapter IX: "The women were sitting the middle gallery on the night of the twenty-first, eleven of them, lawful" (821), and the January suspension (902) is presented as the thing that finally ends attendance. The reader can reconcile it (the mask order named "eleven women and nine lamps below the second door and no more," 447, and is a Ministry order too), but the book is a book about orders, and a reader who tracks them will pause. Fix: three words in the memo, "*save as provided by the order of the [date]*," or in Chapter IX "lawful under the mask order."

**Q4. Chapter V: "Melek tried a mask on once, at the mouth, below the second door" (449).** The mouth is the first door; "below the second door" cannot be at it. Word placement. Fix: "at the second door, where the masks began" or simply "below the second door."

**Q5. Chapter VII: "Melek did not cross herself or greet" (595).** Crossing oneself is a Christian gesture; Melek is a Cappadocian village woman in 2025, and nothing else in the book makes her Christian (the "church bell" of the grandmother's clock, 57, is a century old and belongs to the founder's 1924 notes at 1199, which is plausible for that decade). The sentence describes what she did not do, so the slip is small, but it will snag a Turkish reader. Fix: "Melek did not pray or greet."

**Q6. Chapter titles: II and XII are both "The Threshold"; III and XIII are both "The Measure."** I take the pairing as deliberate mirroring (II is Nilay at the stone in 1999, XII is Yusuf at the lip; III is Márton measuring, XIII is the wall's last mark), and it costs nothing inside the book. In a table of contents it reads as an oversight. Only an audience can settle it. If kept, a reader would be helped by the pairs being *exact* mirrors, which they nearly are; if not, "The Lip" and "The Last Mark" are near neighbours.

**Q7. The Trust's "young woman" is planted three times and never seen.** "The Trust's young woman came in April... One man remembered a scarf; his wife remembered no scarf" (59); "the Trust's young woman with the clipboard, seen in April, scarf or no scarf, still in the country with a courier's bag" (526); "the Trust's young woman, still in the country" (1082). Each time she is one of the ordinary accounts, a pair of hands the Trust reaches with, which suits a villain who works through other people. I tested whether leaving her open does a job, and it does: she keeps Halden's local reach quiet. But three plants and no sighting will make some readers expect a payoff. If the owner wants one, the cheapest is a scarf at the second rail in Chapter XIII beside the grey figure who rights the lamp-jar (1282); it would show the Trust's hand without naming it. Equally defensible to leave.

**Q8. Chapter XII: the state's appetite is set up and then goes silent.** Three consultancy letters, a paper one, "a directorate in Ankara for its continued interest" (1074), timing "in GNSS-denied environments" (1072), "the first sentence all year that made the hill sound small" (1072), and Nilay's "Four million includes the state" (1120). Then nothing: the Ministry closes the dig by letter (1179). I tested it as an open question and it holds: one reading (the state is still circling) is held by "Monthly" (1118); the other (the Trust has already answered it) by Halden's letter, "correspondence of a technical character will find you in the ordinary course... you are under no obligation to begin what you have not begun" (1080). A reader who wants the gun fired will not get it, and firing it would make the ending loud. If the owner wants the thread visibly collected, one clause in the Ministry's closing letter would do: that it "noted an interest of another directorate, which the sponsoring institution had answered."

**Q9. Chapter VIII: two quoted Trust documents in a row at the committee slow the chapter's slowest stretch.** The reply on legitimacy ("The Trust was not elected...," 754) and the annex ("let the outcome end as a door ends, shut, and in a wall," 758) both state the Trust's creed. Each has its own second job (the first is the Trust talking to the state; the second asks nothing for itself and is the founder's "known, believed, or left" said in public), so I would not cut either. But the two together, after four statements and six laboratory lines, are where a reader is most likely to feel the paper. If the owner wants one trim, the sentence "Institutions that interrupt themselves for legitimacy are remembered for the interruption" (754) is the one that can go without loss.

**Q10. The prose habit of "the way X does Y."** I counted 144 similes built on "the way..." in 49,700 words, sometimes two in consecutive sentences: "Near morning she came closer by degrees, the way cold walks a body over. In the end she slept against my side, entirely, all at once, the way the tired pay down a debt they have carried too long" (98). It is the book's voice, it is consistent, and it is often beautiful; it is also mannered enough that a reader may begin to hear the hinge. Pure taste. If a pass is made, I would thin only where two fall within a sentence of each other, and never in the keepers' letters, where the manner is the character.

## 5. What I am unsure of, or take to be deliberate

- **The shaft's tide (18, 19, 20, 18 minutes).** Nothing in the reveal explains it and I do not think anything should. Its return to eighteen after Nilay's night is the loop closing, felt rather than stated. Leave it.
- **The threshold's elevation (.6 then .5)** and **the watches' wrist.** Deliberate, and the best kind: the world has shifted by a hair and only an instrument, or Priska, can say so.
- **gülce.** Never resolved, and I think rightly: she is the one viewer who "never asked what was down there" (506), the answer to four million. Some readers will want to know who she is. Nothing on the page decides it, so I do not.
- **The young woman's voice in the singing room (1025)** may or may not be the Trust's young woman of Q7. The page does not decide; I did not pick.
- **The four codas after the reveal** (Book Zero, the shaft, Göbekli Tepe, the winter rendering, then the repeated letter). Each does a job I can name; together they may feel like four endings to a reader who wants one. I judged them from the page alone and would keep all of them.
- **Geography.** The book refuses a map on principle ("never tell me a room is under anything," 39), so a reader can lose where the answering niche sits relative to the second door, on which the orders hinge. I could reconstruct it from the text (middle gallery, below the second door), so it is not a hole; it is a cost the book has chosen.

## 6. One next step

Fix S1 (one sentence) and take Q1 (one word). Then give the manuscript a single copyediting pass for the placement slips in Q3–Q5. Do not reopen structure, voice, the letters, or the ending; the draft is finished, and the remaining risk is revising it past its quiet.
