**The two changes to the ruling's F2 example, for the main session to confirm or reverse**

1. The ruling asks for "one sentence", but its example has two. This plan joins them into one: the first semicolon becomes ", and", and the full stop after "at the mouth" becomes a semicolon.
2. The ruling has "Melek said so at the step, and nothing else." This plan has "Melek said so to the gendarmes, and nothing else." The reason: X:29 already quotes what Melek said at the step ("And at the step, having reported the whole of it: "I did not touch him. You will want him as he sat.""). So "at the step, and nothing else" would contradict the page two paragraphs earlier. The gendarmes arrive in the same paragraph as the new sentence (X:33), so they are its natural hearers. If the main session prefers the ruling's literal wording, the fallback is: "…licensed to hold the opinion. The snow had come on after he went down; his sky had held at the mouth. Melek said so at the step, and nothing else." In that case X:29 must stay as it is, and the contradiction stays with it.

The new sentence settles who went below when. It does not say why he died, and it does not touch the air. So "By noon the death belonged to nobody" (X:47) still holds, and so does the inquest's "asked, the village remembered weather" (X:45).
