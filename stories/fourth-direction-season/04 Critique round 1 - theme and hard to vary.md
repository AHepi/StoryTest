# Critique of the season plan: theme and hard to vary

**Lens.** Theme and hard to vary only, using the owner's hard-to-vary skill (SKILL.md, and part 5 of by-domain.md on stories). Plot logic, pacing and character come up only where they hold the theme in place or let it slip.

**The question, frozen.** Why does Ream doom its people? The doom is children Turned or Called, the Passed lost, and the dead feeding the Weight. The range is Years 1 to 300 and, as the plan claims, "always". This asks what produces the doom.

**Jobs.** From the brief: the doom follows from not questioning, not from luck, one villain or scarcity. The rules were fitted to the past. The culture's own practice moves the world. Re-tuning makes it worse. Only a new explanation repairs it: found by a new question and owned by many people. "Always" is earned.
One job is mine: a culture that questions, given the same Lift and the same grief, would not be doomed in the same way. If it would be, the theme is loose.

**Verdict.** The engine is well held. Love builds the Weight (the Giving). Every official fix adds weight, or time spent beside it. The cure comes from the true (green) verse that nobody applied. Swap the Giving for bad luck in the physics and the theme breaks, which is what you want.

The loose joints are in three places:
- **When a counting culture would first have seen the danger.** The rate stays flat until Year 235, so counting would not have helped for a century.
- **What finally turns the city in episode 9.** It is agreeing numbers plus the Randall's authority, and the season's own episode 4 says that cannot work.
- **The finale's prediction.** The plan's own number fails it, and nobody notices.

Three parts are idle or merely asserted: the Earth leg of "always", the Fable being sealed "at the founding", and stipulation 6.

The Semantics is dramatized accurately almost everywhere. Four places misstate it (findings 6, 10, 13 and 15).

**The best rivals, built.**
- **Bad luck.** The plan holds against it, except at the silent threshold (finding 1).
- **A villain.** It holds, except for the Year 235 relabel (finding 5) and the Randalls' secrecy becoming a cause in episode 9 (finding 2).
- **Scarcity.** No rival. Nobody in Ream lacks food except the Turned, and that is chemistry.
- **"The writer's physics did it."** The theme survives, because the stipulation is declared once and the doom needs the culture's response, as long as the warning is visible early (finding 1).

**Could the same doom happen to a questioning culture?** In the plague of Year 131, a questioning Ream would probably also have Given its dead: it was a sane plague measure. What it would do differently is notice the change and stop. So the theme is held at "what they did when the world moved", not at "what they did in 131".

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The rate stays flat for a century after the Giving begins, so not counting cost nothing until Year 235

**Where.** §1.9 (Years 131–235: "the drift stays near 2 in 100"); §3 steps 2–4; episode 2 act 3 ("flat at 2 in 100 for two centuries"); the numbers table in §11 ("2 in 100, Years 1–230").

**Problem.** The Weight grows by eight tonnes a year from Year 131, but the Turning rate doesn't move until about Year 235. So the Year 131 dash hid nothing for a hundred years: a city that kept counting would have seen exactly what Ream saw, which is nothing.

The flat stretch also slips in a threshold the ledger never declared. Stipulation 2 says the twist "grows with the mass", so a growing Weight should show from the start.

**Why it matters.** This is the root of the theme. The best rival, "Ream was unlucky: the danger was invisible until it was already huge", fits the plan's facts as written. The line "the year Ream stopped counting is the year Ream began building its doom" is a coincidence of dates, not a cause.

**Fix (small).**
- Make the Turning rate creep slowly from about Year 150: 3 in 100 by Year 180, 5 by Year 235. Only someone counting year by year would see it. Make it steep after 235.
- Keep the sharp break at about Year 235 for *arrivals* only. That one is honestly a threshold (stipulation 3: the close pull overtakes the far one).
- Now two separate failures to ask each do a job. The dash hides the creep: a counting city would have stopped the Giving at 300 tonnes, cheaply. The relabel hides the break.
- Nell's curve in episode 2 then shows a century of warning in plain chalk. Update the table, and Arkani's "one in fifty".

### 2. The city turns in episode 9 on agreeing numbers and authority, which episode 4 says can't work

**Where.** Episode 9 act 2 ("Every private count agrees. This is the moment the culture turns."), set against the twist of episode 4 ("Evidence with no explanation only feeds the old story"). The Randall has held the full curve for forty years, and it only deepened her faith.

**Problem.** The board fills with counts that all say "the rate is rising", and the Randall's own hand writes the first one. Every rising count already has a home in the Ending doctrine. Nothing on the board is a result the old story cannot swallow.

**Why it matters.** Two leaks.
- If publishing the number is what turns Ream, then the Randalls keeping it private for decades was a cause of the doom. The doom then rests on secret-keepers, the villain the brief rules out.
- The city turns because its high keeper wrote something down. That is the old mechanism (authority) in new clothes. A pile of agreeing confirmations holds nothing new in place.

**Fix.** Put one result on the board that the old story forbids and the new explanation predicts. The cleanest comes from what is already planted, the light crates of episode 5:
- The Ending doctrine says the coin is moving toward fair, fifty.
- Light crates sent through the old door (5 kg, more than two minutes in the dark) come back *more* than half reversed, say 62 in 100. That is "more than fair", which no reading of the Ending can hold. The Weight explanation predicts it: more time beside it, more turning. (The source allows rates over half: "a seventy-percent cure".)
- Let Pim bring it, rerun in a believer's hand. Let it be what breaks the Randall's reading, rather than Kit's message alone. Her chalking 24 then opens the board.
- Earlier (episode 4 or 8), let the curve go public once and be cheered as "the Ending coming", so the audience sees that publication alone changes nothing.

### 3. The finale's prediction is failed by the plan's own number, and it was too loose to test anything

**Where.** Episode 10: the cold open ("FEWER THAN 1 IN 20 TURNED. IF MORE, I AM WRONG") and act 2 (the first crate comes back 4, Dimopoulos's crate comes back 5).

**Problem.**
- 5 in 100 *is* 1 in 20, not fewer. By the plan's own numbers the prediction fails, and nobody notices.
- "Fewer than 1 in 20" is also a loose bound. Nell's own explanation (the door is thirty times farther, the Weight 27,000 times weaker) predicts something far more definite: a return to Year One's 2.
- One crate of 100 can't tell 4 from 2. Chance alone gives 4 or more about one time in seven.

**Why it matters.** This is the showpiece of the new practice and of major reversal 3. A loose bound chosen so that it passes is the opposite of §3 step 9: "says something definite about untested changes, so it can be caught out". As written, the heroine's answer is never actually caught. A child simply asks.

**Fix.**
- Nell writes the definite number her explanation gives: "PREDICTED: 2 IN 100, AS IN YEAR ONE. IF MORE, I AM WRONG."
- The children run ten crates, not one. About 40 in 1,000 come back reversed: twice the prediction, and well beyond luck.
- Dimopoulos, the man who carved the narrowing gloss, is the one who points at the board: "You said two."
- Nell does not narrow her claim: "Then I'm wrong. Something else is near this door." Lottie's "why four?" becomes the city's question, and the needle at the Graves and Minthome's message answer it.
- "A failed answer stays failed" is then applied by the heroine to herself, which is the only way that line stops being scripture. The door still works (31 down to 4): the explanation is right about the big thing and wrong about the small one.

### 4. The best rival repair, "stop sending the children", needs no new explanation, and nothing explains why nobody does it

**Where.** §3 step 7 ("Why no adjustment can work"); §1.8 (the five reasons nobody questions).

**Problem.** Step 7 itself lists "how many cross" among the Order's levers. Turn it down to zero and the Calling and the Turning end, without anyone knowing about place or distance. It is also the most natural act in the world: a mother keeping her child home.

None of the five reasons explains why no family refused in the sixty-five years of rising Callings.

**Why it matters.** If the obvious fix is within reach, the silence reads as stupid rather than believable, which is the plan's own test in §1.8. Step 7 also overclaims. What no adjustment can achieve is a safe crossing *at this door*, not an end to the dying.

**Fix.**
- Add a sixth reason and show it once. The Tithe is the gate to adulthood: no Tithe means no marriage, trade or inheritance (call them "the unweighed"). A hidden child is taken to cross under guard.
- Show one refusal early: in episode 2, the Others clan hides a son. When the next child is Called, the Order reads it as the Ancestors' anger.
- Reword step 7: no setting of the old levers makes a crossing at this door safe, and the one lever that would stop the dying, not crossing, is one the faith cannot pull, because the rite *is* the religion.
- The finale's "Nobody crosses who doesn't choose to" then pays off a plant.

### 5. The Year 235 relabel is coded as a cover-up: a secret villain at the pivot of the doom

**Where.** §1.9 (Year 235: "The Randall of the day has the Weight-book re-inked"); episode 3 act 2 and Theta's wound (men re-inking "by lamplight"; she was "beaten for asking why").

**Problem.** The one moment when the doom could have been caught (the Passed stop arriving) is dealt with by a secret act at night, enforced by beating a child. The plan never says *why* the Randall of the day relabelled the traces.

**Why it matters.** The brief rules out a doom that rests on a secret villain. This also makes Theta's "they faked it" nearly right, when the plan wants her conspiracy to be only half right.

**Fix.** Make the relabel sincere, public and part of the doctrine.
- In Year 235 the Randall reads the new rise-and-stay traces as the Passed being *received* into the Ancestors, as the Given are. She carves "Go toward the Ancestors" in daylight, to rejoicing, and the Weight-book is re-inked in a ceremony.
- Theta was beaten for asking her question aloud during the rite, not for seeing a secret.
- Give the doctrine an answer for the rare rise-and-fall too: the Refused are souls the Ancestors send back to walk as Pariahs. This ties the relabel to the Pariah law (why Pariahs are Passed unheard). It makes Iris's "Pass him" in episode 9 sincere doctrine rather than cruelty, and her choice of Wells in episode 1 sincere within her faith. And it is true in a twisted way: the Refused did arrive, and some came back through the Graves.

### 6. The meta device is spent before its reveal, and the reveal teaches "a guess means false"

**Where.** Episode 6 act 1 ("Why") and its twist; episode 5 act 2 (the pasted-card pair test); the knowledge map (the audience knows the handhold law is false from episode 5).

**Problem.** Three connected faults.
- (a) The cards disprove the handhold verse in episode 5. By the First Leaf in episode 6, the audience has already corrected its belief by a test, so the reveal adds only where the verse came from. "The audience made the city's mistake" doesn't catch anyone.
- (b) Nell's reason for the relic heist is: "either the copies are wrong or the world has changed. Only the original can say which." That makes the older text the judge, swapping one authority for an older one. It is also false: a tag says where a verse came from, not whether it is true here.
- (c) The other ep 1 verse taught as "holiest", the amber "the neighbour's life is the other hand", is true in this show. So "amber means guesses" teaches viewers that a stipulation is false. The Fable's point is the opposite: taking away a reason removes the support, but it does not refute the claim.

**Why it matters.** This is the device the brief called "worth keeping". As scheduled, it neither catches the audience nor teaches the accurate idea. There is also a risk that viewers conclude "the book was the problem" rather than "not questioning was the problem".

**Fix.**
- Keep the Fast test (heavy vs light crates) in episode 5. Move the pair test to *after* the reveal.
- In episode 6 the relic shows the handhold verse under "In the plot". Tau: "So it's false." Nell: "Or it's a story that happens to be right. The dog's in the Plot too, and the dog growls." They test it (episode 6 act 4, or episode 7). Now the audience still believes the verse at the reveal, the reveal shakes the belief without settling it, and only the test settles it.
- Rewrite Nell's motive: she needs to know whether anyone ever *tested* the verse that took her brother.
- Show the green cube-law verse on the relic: the true verse that held the cure all along.
- In the twist table, stop saying "the law that exiled Wells". For the twins the verse was right: exactly one of them turned. What exiled Wells was Nell's lie and his "Me", and that keeps the flaw hers.

### 7. The crush fails the plan's own doom test

**Where.** Episode 8 act 3 (the crush at the Temple gate, eleven dead); §11 ("The doom test").

**Problem.**
- The doom test asks: "which unquestioned practice made it more likely? If the answer is bad luck... rewrite." No practice causes the crush. It is panic.
- It doesn't carry the lesson it is meant to carry either. Swap Nell's bare claim for the same claim with the needle walk in hand, and a frightened crowd could crush just the same.

**Why it matters.** The crush is the Randall's proof that "truth told with no plan kills". If the deaths come from panic rather than from the missing test, she is right about the wrong thing, and the line between questioning and declaring without a test blurs.

**Fix.** Two options.
- **Preferred.** Plant a rite rule as surplus in episode 1: during a Giving the Temple gates are barred "until the needle settles". The rule was fitted to calm congregations of a few hundred, and it kills when twelve thousand try to leave at once. Now the crush passes the doom test.
- **Alternative.** Cut the crush and let the cost be what the old story does with an untested truth: the Order tests the messenger with the Jar instead of testing the message, and the crowd demands the Great Tithe. Both beats are already in the episode.

### 8. The card tests measure the wrong thing and rest on a rule the ledger never declares

**Where.** Episode 5 act 2 (cards, and pairs "pasted together"); episode 10 act 1 ("31 reversed in 100" from one crate); §2.4 and §10 rule 9; §2.7 (the ledger card lists stipulations 1–6 and 8 only).

**Problem.**
- Counting cards within one crate assumes that loose things in one bubble turn separately. The glued-pair result "one of each" assumes that even a solid object can come back half-turned. The source implies the opposite for a joined body: a turned crosser's gut germs turn with her.
- A glued pair is one solid thing, not two people holding hands, so the test does not test the handhold.
- "A handhold changes nothing" sits in §2.4 and rule 9 but is not on the card shown to the audience.

**Why it matters.** The season's cure is "tests anyone can repeat". A test that measures something other than what it claims is exactly what the hard-to-vary skill warns against: run a test on the thing it is meant to catch and on its innocent neighbour. And refuting a verse by the show's own unstated say-so is the very move the show criticises in the Order.

**Fix.**
- Declare one line in §2.5 and on the episode 6 card: "Separate things turn separately; one solid thing and everything inside it turns as one. Touching changes nothing."
- Make the pair test *cards laid face to face but not glued* (like hands). They come back matched or mismatched at the ordinary rates, while glued pairs always match.
- The glued pairs become the useful contrast, and they even explain how a film writer could imagine a grip turning two people together. Per-card counts then stand.

### 9. The Earth leg of "always" is asserted, and the season's own ending argues against it

**Where.** §3, "Why 'always' is earned": the three generations are Earth, Ream and Minthome.

**Problem.** "Earth fenced and rationed its Turned instead of asking how to feed them" is never shown. Earth fenced its mirrored people because mirror-life is dangerous (the 2024 warning, true in this show). The season ends with Wells's sneeze and THE GREY?, which is that very danger coming home. Earth's fence looks like a questioning culture's precaution.

**Why it matters.** "Always" is the hardest word in the owner's message. Backing it with an example the story's own residue contradicts makes it read as a label.

**Fix.** Drop Earth from the chain, and keep the empty station as a wonder. "Always" is already earned by what the plan shows:
- the founders' rules, right once, go wrong under Ream;
- Minthome copies the Giving;
- the repaired Ream meets two changes outside its new rules within a day (4 in 100, and the grey).

Say that in §3.

### 10. "A copy keeps its source's history" is quoted for the wrong idea

**Where.** Episode 10 act 3. The Randall says it of Minthome's Giving.

**Problem.** In the Fable, the line means a copy is not a second witness: evidence copied from one source carries that source's history and adds no independent support. The plan uses it to mean "a copied rite brings the same harm", which is a different claim: same cause, same effect.

**Why it matters.** It is the one quoted Fable line at the season's climax, and it is used for the wrong idea.

**Fix.** Give the line a moment where two sources seem to agree but are really one.
- When WE. DIFFUSE. HERE. TOO. arrives, someone in the crowd says: "Two peoples Give their dead. It must be holy."
- The Randall answers: "A copy keeps its source's history. They Give because the Leaves say so. That is one voice, not two."

It fits the Answering too (two cities kept certain by one book's chant), and the hand-copied Leaves.

### 11. The Fable being sealed "at the founding" is idle, and it contradicts the founders

**Where.** §1.3 ("A second text, sealed at the founding").

**Problem.** The founders were the people who counted (*Minthome, Year One*: 2 in 100). Having them seal a book about how to test ideas contradicts that, and nothing in the season depends on the date.

**Why it matters.** How a culture loses the habit of questioning is the heart of the theme. At present it is an unexplained given.

**Fix.** The Fable was the founders' open book. It was sealed as heresy after Burnt Row (Year 255), because the young Changer who threw out the burn rule was reading it.
- Iris's untested doubt is then what got questioning itself banned.
- "Questions kill" becomes law, not only memory, and her guilt deepens.
- The Changers go from an open reading circle (which Iris joined as Sigma) to a cellar.

### 12. The heist does nothing for Wells, and stipulation 6 is bought for one image

**Where.** Episode 9's pitch ("To save one boy and a city, a hundred people must steal God in a night"); §10 rule 10; stipulation 6 (shared light); episode 10 act 4.

**Problem.**
- A crossing from the Mother comes out at the Graves wherever Ream's Lift stands. Kit walked out of the Graves ten times with no Lift there. What stands between Wells and home is two *rules*: Adamala's "the Mother is for the dead" and Ream's Pariah law.
- Stipulation 6 exists only for the reunion image in episode 10. That is the kind of finale-only purchase the judgement dropped from Pitch B.

**Why it matters.** Remove the heist and Wells still comes home, so the heist is loose for that job. Worse, it hides the stronger truth: two cultures' unquestioned rules block his way home, not machinery.

**Fix.**
- Reword episode 9 so the heist is for the city: the Great Tithe at dawn, and a safe door for Nell's crossing.
- Let Wells's return hang on the two rules: Adamala's question, and "Let him speak".
- Either cut stipulation 6 (they cross at the same moment, and we cut between two blacks), or keep it and name it in the ledger as a declared indulgence.

### 13. Step 1 mislabels where the founders' rules came from, and misses the season's best unused reveal

**Where.** §3 step 1 ("Knowledge that survived past conditions"); §1.4 (the Tithe's "forgotten" purpose).

**Problem.** The founders didn't hold rules that merely survived. They built them with reasons and a check: they knew *why* the Passing worked (handedness), and they counted the drift. What Ream holds in Year 300 is those rules handed down without the reasons or the check. In the Fable's terms that is passing a rule on, not selection. It fails on unseen changes in the same way, but the difference is the story.

**Why it matters.** Ream kept the founders' experiment as a rite and threw away its readout. The Tithe that kills children was the founders' safety test. That is a sharper form of the theme, and a mind-bending turn that costs one line.

**Fix.**
- Reword step 1: "The founders built the rules with reasons and a count. Ream kept the rules and dropped both."
- Land it once on screen in episode 6. The relic's simulator text reads: "The statisticians can — after enough people have crossed. That number is the most valuable thing..." Nell realises the Tithe was how the founders got it.

### 14. The Joining appears only when the plot needs it

**Where.** Episode 4 act 2. It is listed in §1.4, but no earlier episode shows it.

**Problem.** A rite first seen at the moment it is needed reads as a patch. Swap it for any invented rite and nothing planted earlier breaks, which weakens major reversal 1.

**Fix.** Plant it as surplus in episode 1 or 2: a white Joining robe folded in Nan's chest, or Nan telling Lottie she hopes to be called "like my sister was".

### 15. Two lines overstate what the stone channel proves, or lean on an unplanted precedent

**Where.** The episode 8 hook ("by the one channel nobody can fake"); the episode 5 hook ("Ream lore says that when the Ancestors spell a name, that one is Called").

**Problem.**
- (a) The plan's own rule 1 says the stone proves that mass moved, never who moved it or why. "Nobody can fake" overstates it at the exact moment the season needs a bare conclusion (MOVE THE DOOR) to still need its reasons.
- (b) The lore implies that names were spelled before, and the plan never accounts for it. As written, it exists only to make ABBOTT frightening.

**Fix.**
- (a) Change the hook to: "by the one channel that proves someone moved a mountain to say it."
- (b) Root the lore in Kit's first attempts after Year 253: he spelled a name, and the Order read it as the Ancestors calling. Iris knows.

### 16. The lost tags are the one place the doom leans on bad luck

**Where.** §1.3 ("The copyists kept the colours and lost the words"); §1.8, reason 2.

**Problem.** An accident of copying is doing causal work in "why nobody questions".

**Fix.** Make it a small choice, repeated by many hands. An intermediate copy (found among the ledgers in episode 2, or beside the relic in episode 6) shows "Stipulated" shortened to "St.", then to a dot. A copyist's note in the margin reads: "words of doubt are unfit for holy script." It is a choice spread over generations, with no villain.
