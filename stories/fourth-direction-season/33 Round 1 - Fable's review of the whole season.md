# SECONDS, season revision (round 1): editorial review

*Reviewer: Claude (Fable 5.1). Read in full: the owner's brief, the second feedback, the notes on "The Catch", the master plan version 3, and all ten episodes of `season-as-reviewed.md`, start to finish. This is a review of a written season, not a watched one: anything I say about pace is a judgement from the page.*

A few words I cannot avoid, each explained once: a **plant** is a detail put in early so that a later surprise is fair; a **payoff** is the moment that surprise lands; a **reversal** is a twist that turns the whole story round; a **cold open** is the scene before the titles; the **ledger** is the plan's table listing every invented law and the job it does.

---

## 1. Verdict

SECONDS is a ten-episode crime drama about an Essex family of thieves who can step through walls, and whose proudest habits (make weight, never go back twice, face home, keep nothing on paper, feed our own, never go to them) turn out to be what is starving them. It does what the owner asked. The message is never spoken; it is carried by scales, tickets, chalk, a jam jar and a fry-up. Every invented law of the world is on screen and doing work. The three big reversals (Rosie has been turned since the first job; the cure is nothing but time, and a Marlow floor turned the enemy's daughter; the founding legend of "fifteen stone" was a starving man hiding a turn) are each planted before they land and each makes the audience re-read what came before. The wider world is ordinary and kind: inspectors inspect, a nurse hands out a meter, a fireman goes home with his ration box. Nothing from "The Catch" is echoed.

Is it finished? In shape, yes: every episode has its cold open, acts, twist and hook, and the plants-and-payoffs notes show the writers kept the books. What it needs is one careful continuity pass. I found seven substantive faults (below), and every one of them is a line or a number, not a structure: a character quoting a scene she was not in, a set of weights that break the show's own rule, a family rule broken on screen without anyone noticing, a proposed cure two of its patients could never take, one rule the audience is asked to work out for itself, a clue that cannot be felt, and a coin toss that is named and then not checked. Fix those and this is a season I would put in front of an audience.

---

## 2. How it keeps the owner's rules

**The core message (a culture that never questions dooms its people), shown and never stated.** Kept. No character makes a speech about asking. The doom arrives as arithmetic: "A FIFTH OF A SECOND A KILO UNDER A HUNDRED" is painted on the wall in episode 1, and by episode 9 the family works out that the fry-up Peg piled on Liam's plate after he was weighed, plus a scale that had lied since April, killed him "By two hundredths" (Ep 9, Sc 12). Each rule is shown being right once and wrong now. "Never go back twice" ("Your dad's mate went in twice on the Old Street job. Second time, there was a copper stood in the room", Ep 3, Sc 3) becomes the one long trip that turns Rosie. "We don't go to them" is answered by a nurse in one line: "Police don't get the list, love. Not since 2019" (Ep 8, Sc 10), while the letter saying so has sat unopened in Peg's drawer for seven years. And the plan's insistence that one right person is not enough is honoured: Tess is overruled at the stove (Ep 7), Rosie is overruled at the table (Ep 8), and only when the whole family writes its crossings down beside Maggie's tide books (Ep 10, Sc 7–8) does anything change. The last image before the hook is the same culture, unchanged, across the river: Siobhan on a bathroom scale, Declan chalking her number, "Under the scale, folded, lies December's front page, unread" (Ep 10, Sc 18). That is the message as a picture, and nobody says it.

The one place the season comes nearest to saying it out loud is the line "Nobody ever asked why", used twice in episode 7 (Terry in the loft, Sc 4; Tess on the ferry, Sc 11). It is a plain line, not a speech, and it is about one cousin's diet, so it passes; but see quibble Q5.

**Twists that are fair.** Kept, with one hinge to mend (S1). Rosie's turn is planted eleven ways before the meter reads LO: the crisp packet she looks at "a beat too long" (Ep 1, Sc 1), the jam jar, the dog, Terry's pot dropping "two fingers" (Ep 1, Sc 14), the seven glances at the catalogue cutting, the lard "for the pit" (Ep 3, Sc 11). The drift-is-time reversal is planted by the TV surgeon ("She's done four thousand. Never turned", Ep 1, Sc 10) and Terry's "Eleven times I went again. Two seconds a go. Hungry every time after" (Ep 1, Sc 8). Frank's turn is planted by a sum any viewer can do (fifteen stone is 95 kilos, which buys one second, said under the painted rule in Ep 1, Sc 6) and by the jar Peg opens for Rosie "the other way" (Ep 1, Sc 13), replayed as it was with Frank in Ep 10, Sc 3. The Laundry is Ray is planted by the batch codes on the lids (Ep 1, Sc 3), "He'll want it, Peg. He always does" (Ep 2, Sc 4) and Ray's "So I wait till she comes to me hungry" (Ep 3, Sc 5). Each is built from the world's own logic; swapping any of them for another twist would strand these plants.

**Every invented law true and used.** Kept. I checked the plan's ledger against the pages. The stack, the black, the budget, the tingle, the partial return (Shaun's leg, Ep 5, Sc 9; Danny's fingers, Ep 6, Sc 1), the sent hand (blind: "We stay on Danny's face, eyes shut", Ep 4, Sc 5), the amputee's extra tenths ("My leg weighed five and a half. Eleven tenths, Nan", Ep 9, Sc 1), knots undone from inside (Ep 5, Sc 8), the unpinned eye seeing "his ribs rising and falling, the slow fist of his heart" (Ep 1, Sc 1), rotation mirroring the painting's linen ("Linen has a hand, Mrs Marlow", Ep 1, Sc 18), no up in the dark and the pit training that answers it, the three tugs (Ep 9, Sc 4), the grip and its price, mirrored chemistry (Peg's meter reads LO, the T-meter reads 5.6 on the same drop, Ep 9, Sc 9), the dog, looking-glass milk (Cian "screamed three days before we understood", Ep 4, Sc 9), passports ("HAND: T doesn't fly", Ep 4, Sc 3), neighbours felt only as weight (the Twitch, the Nobody Tide, the dig, the message), and all seven theorems, including custody by pantry (the ward's one locked door with "a small green light above it", Ep 8, Sc 10) and exile as a cure (Terry, Ep 10, Sc 12). I found no law broken on screen. Two ledger items are thin rather than missing: the Register's hand-gel-and-towels rule never appears (mirror life is carried instead by the passport, the gloves, the gowns, the wash at the standpipe and the drain), and the rule that a field fired in water is useless is true in the story but never told to the audience (S5).

**No echo of "The Catch".** Kept, and carefully. No mint, no taste or smell settles anything ("Nobody says what it tasted of", Ep 9, Sc 8; Danny's "What's it taste—" dies "where it stands", Sc 9). The camera stays on the straight side of every mirror: when Rosie says "New roll's printed backwards, Nan", the camera shows Mick's ticket "ordinary purple print, the right way round" (Ep 2, Sc 10). No dish, flask, mould or growth; the only flask in the season holds tea. No hands on glass. No lift (the word appears only as an everyday verb: "Peg lifts the phone"). The bite has "No light. No glow, no gauge" (Ep 9, Sc 5) and is never called a reserve. Next door is never shown: "The screen goes black" (Ep 9, Sc 5); "Black. Nothing on the screen at all" (Ep 10, Sc 12). The one near thing is that episode 3's last scene is Rosie eating from Terry's pot at a table that cannot share it; the episode ends on "Blood pays blood", not on the meal, so the rule holds, but it holds narrowly.

**No banned word or named idea.** Kept. I searched the whole season for every word on the plan's list (section 14) and their relatives. None appears. The questions on screen are the ordinary ones the plan asked for: "Why's it a kilo out?" (Ep 9, Sc 12), "What did my dad weigh?" (Ep 9, Sc 13), "How do you know it's not the weight?" (Ep 9, Sc 11).

**Not a dystopia.** Kept. The Register is "a bed, there's a kitchen, and no lock on the door either way" (Ep 8, Sc 9). The police "don't cuff crossers" and take the rig with a property receipt. Trading Standards hangs a certificate with "a little hammer from his own pocket" (Ep 1, Sc 9). The Fever Company is a job with a pension. The only closed world is the family's kitchen, and it is closed from inside, by love.

## 3. What works best

- **The check-in as ritual and lie.** "Straight?" / "Straight." is asked after every crossing from the cold open on, and the season lets it rot in public: Shaun says it on the marsh and Peg's meter reads LO in front of everyone ("I knew before we were out the marsh. I said it anyhow", Ep 9, Sc 7); by episode 10 "Nobody asks him anything. Kieran holds out Peg's meter" (Sc 12). That is the whole theme done as a habit changing.
- **The kitchen turning lethal in plain sight.** "Goes through me like water, love. Since I turned. Peg's knife stops in the cake" (Ep 1, Sc 13). Then Ep 5, Sc 13: "The camera stays at the table. He eats every bite. Sixpence's tail slows, and stops." Nobody says what the fry-up will cost; the audience knows since episode 1 that "everything in you counts, breakfast too."
- **Peg reading two van tickets** on the spike, one from the van going out and one coming back, and sitting down on the bottom stair (Ep 5, Sc 17). A death told by a weighbridge.
- **The ferry.** Two enemy weighers laying twenty-five years side by side: "Nobody counted her, Tess. That's what I've come to say" / "Then let's count her now" (Ep 7, Sc 7), and the pencil: "this is the first thing a Marlow has ever written on purpose" (Sc 9).
- **Peg at the certificate.** She carries the rolls to the stove and stops: "If the rolls burn, there's no licence. If there's no licence, there's no weighbridge" (Ep 7, Sc 12). The paper survives for the worst reason, which is exactly right for her.
- **Ray's tomatoes.** "You can't eat those." / "No." / "Then why grow them?" / "They need the watering" (Ep 8, Sc 6).
- **Two meters, one drop of blood.** "Two meters. Same blood. Both of them right" (Ep 9, Sc 9); Terry pushing his empty plate "towards the middle of the family, the way a man asks for more." The law that the mirror is relative, played as a dinner.
- **The sum on the back of the letter.** "Either one, he makes it. Both, he doesn't. By two hundredths" (Ep 9, Sc 12), and Peg's "I made him breakfast."
- **The door found on a baking tray** with flour, a ruler and a salt pot (Ep 10, Sc 9): "Our field. It never fell. It walked out of their sea onto our field." Three memories and a tide table, no lecture.
- **Terry's leaving.** "One." / "Two." / "There was a man, and then there was the field" (Ep 10, Sc 12). A job on the count, as the plan demanded, and the yellow gloves folded on the gate rail.
- **Ray's mirror signature** at the one moment the family is holding its breath (Ep 10, Sc 12): "Eleven year I've wrote back to front so you lot could read me. Give us a fortnight."

## 4. The problems, most serious first

### SUBSTANTIVE

**S1. Tess remembers, quotes and flashes back to a scene she was never in (Ep 7, Sc 11 and Sc 12).**
*What is on the page.* Danny's boast about the Mynors floor is made once, at the Cheapside bank: "DANNY: Half this street. Did a vault further down in '24, they had us back last autumn for the floor underneath. Two floors to get through, not one. That's a long time to be nowhere" (Ep 2, Sc 5). The only people present are the facilities manager and Liam. At the office that evening (Ep 2, Sc 6) Danny says only "Banks. Banks I've never heard of." On the ferry, when Maggie says "MARLOW on the side", the text runs "Tess goes very still, and we go with her: Flash — a bank yard on Cheapside, this March" and replays the boast word for word. At the stove Tess then says to Danny: "You told us about it. You said it was a long time to be nowhere. You were pleased" (Sc 12), and earlier "He told us he'd poured it. Twice" (Sc 11).
*Why it matters.* This is the hinge of the second reversal: the moment the war turns out to be about concrete. It rests on a memory Tess does not have. A careful viewer who remembers the bank scene (and the season trains viewers to remember lines) will feel the floor give.
*Fix.* Put the boast where Tess can hear it. Simplest: in Ep 2, Sc 6, after "Banks I've never heard of", give Danny the same line at the table ("Mynors had us back last autumn for the floor underneath. Two floors now. Long time to be nowhere.") and let the Ep 7 flash be of that table. Or move it to Ep 3, Sc 3, where Danny is already bragging about Marrable Street ("Best floor I ever did") and Tess is present. Either way keep Liam's pink ears in the bank scene; that plant is doing its own job.

**S2. Lee's weights on the roll cannot do the job the scene says he did (Ep 7, Sc 4).**
*What is on the page.* Tess reads Lee's job-day tickets: "November, eighty-one. December, seventy-six. Seventy-four. Seventy-one. January, sixty-eight. February, sixty-six. Canary Wharf, March. April: seventy-one. May: seventy-five." Then: "Canary Wharf. How long?" / "Nine and a half."
*Why it matters.* By the rule painted on the wall, 66 kilos buys 6.8 seconds (a fifth of a second for each of the 34 kilos under a hundred). Lee could not have been in the black nine and a half seconds; at 81 kilos in November he had 3.8. This is the scene that teaches the audience to read a roll, and the season has spent six episodes teaching them to do exactly this sum ("95 kilos buys one second, a sum any viewer can do", the plan's own words). The one viewer who does it here will stop trusting every number after.
*Fix.* Keep the shape of the curve (a fall, a long job, a rise) and lower the numbers so that the March job fits: for example 61 in November down to 50 in February (50 kilos buys 10 seconds), then 55 and 59 after. Or keep the weights and make Canary Wharf "Six and a half. Long, for then", which at one in four is still a fair "mended". The plan's canon needs only "two who mended, both after a later long job"; it does not fix Lee's seconds.

**S3. Peg sends Shaun back for a second eight-second leg, into a room the wall has already flooded, and nobody says "never go back twice" (Ep 9, Sc 5).**
*What is on the page.* Shaun lands with his first case; "PEG: Once more, son. Then we're away. He squares up and goes again." Later "Shaun lands with the last case". The dog then picks out "the second of Shaun's two" cases as the one that turned with him (Sc 7). The chorus "Never go back twice" has been said in Ep 1 (Sc 3), Ep 3 (Sc 3), Ep 4 (Liam, Sc 5), Ep 5 (Shaun himself, Sc 7, and in his head at the ninth knot, Sc 8) and Ep 5, Sc 12 ("Never go back twice anyway", Peg).
*Why it matters.* The family's loudest rule is broken by the family's founder on screen, and it is the second trip that turns him, and not one character notices. The plan's canon gives Shaun "two eight-second legs" in the whole season (Silvertown in 7, one raid leg in 9); the page gives him three. As written it reads as an oversight rather than as Peg's "near enough" planning eating its own.
*Fix.* Two honest choices. (a) One raid leg for Shaun; let Terry's two-second hops bring the other ten cases, which the investigator's "nine times" already nearly says. (b) Keep the second leg and make it a beat: Danny or Shaun says the chorus, Peg answers "It's a pantry, not a bank", and the dog's choice of the second case then lands as the price of that line. Option (b) is stronger, because it turns a slip into the doom's engine at work.

**S4. Tess proposes a ten-second cure for two people who cannot take ten seconds (Ep 7, Sc 12).**
*What is on the page.* "TESS: Ten seconds in the black mends seven in ten... We've got rigs. We can cure them here. Rosie. Terry. Danny. Tonight if we—". Terry has just been given 5.7 seconds by the same rule (71.4 kilos); Danny has 2.2. Episode 10 knows this exactly: Terry reads his own odds as "a five-second hop: one in six, each go" (Sc 8), and for Danny "There's no row of the table that reaches him. Two point two seconds, and nothing at two."
*Why it matters.* Tess is the one person in the family who never gets a sum wrong out loud, and this is the scene where her sums are put on trial. A viewer who has the rule will catch that two of her three patients cannot reach the cure on a Marlow rig, and Danny's "Did your sums do Liam too?" loses some of its sting because Tess has just made a plainer error nobody names.
*Fix.* Let her name Rosie and stall: "Rosie. Tonight. Terry at five, and again, and again. Danny—" and stop. Or give Danny the catch: "Two seconds, Tess. Cure that." It sharpens his turn on her and keeps her honest.

**S5. The rule that saves Rosie in the Deep is never planted, so her best moment asks the audience to derive a law (Ep 9, Sc 4 and Sc 5).**
*What is on the page.* Before she goes: "If it's water — then it's the minute. Stand a minute over there, any world, and the tab brings me home." In the sea: "Her thumb goes to the wrist unit — and stops, an inch off the button. So that is why the Deep keeps you." Nowhere in the season does anyone say what would happen if she pressed it. I searched for any line about a field fired in water and found none.
*Why it matters.* Most viewers will ask the obvious question, "why doesn't she just press it?", and the page does not answer. The answer (the bubble takes everything inside it, and an arm's-length bubble of sea water is over a tonne, so the field would give her no seconds at all) can be worked out from "everything in you counts", but that is a chain of two inferences in the dark, at the season's most important rule-break. The plan's ledger gives this law the job "why Rosie's main field is useless in the Deep"; the job is done only for the sharpest viewer.
*Fix.* Plant it small in the pit, where the audience is already learning rules. Ep 1, Sc 7, after "There's nowhere to fall": KIERAN: "What if you pressed it in here?" TESS: "Then you'd be carrying the pit with you. Water counts. Everything counts." One exchange, and Rosie's thumb stopping an inch off the button becomes a payoff instead of a puzzle.

**S6. "Tingle came early. That's the grab" is used as a clue to the lying scale, but the clue cannot be felt (Ep 6, Sc 2; paid off Ep 9, Sc 11).**
*What is on the page.* Ep 5, Sc 11 sets the rule: "It comes about two seconds before you're done." Ep 6: "DANNY: Two point four. It was fizzing before I ever touched him." / "TESS: It comes two seconds before you're done." / "DANNY: Tingle came early. That's the grab." Ep 9: "The bridge has had me at two point four since April." / "TESS: Two point two." / "DANNY: I was fizzing before I ever touched that lad."
*Why it matters.* On a crossing of 2.4 seconds the tingle comes at 0.4; on 2.2, at 0.2. Both are "before he touched him"; nobody can tell two tenths apart by a fizz in the nails, and Danny had already felt the same immediate fizz on his practice hop in Ep 4. The season presents this in Ep 9 as a re-read ("I was fizzing before I ever touched that lad"), but the re-read has nothing in it. A careful viewer who does the sum finds a plant that does not discriminate, and that costs the scale reveal a little of its authority, which it does not need to lose: 19.78, the chip, Mick's "two hundred light" and Osei's "Sixty-one kilos, seven hundred grams" carry it fully.
*Fix.* Cut the "early" beat in both places. If a Danny-shaped plant is wanted, make it measured rather than felt: at the pit in Ep 4, Sc 1, Tess stopwatches his practice hop ("Two two." / "Bridge says two four." / "Then the bridge is generous.") and lets it go.

**S7. The one coin toss the season names in advance is never checked (Ep 9, Sc 6 and Sc 7).**
*What is on the page.* Kieran: "If you'd turned on the way back, you'd have come home straight." Rosie: "Five in six I stay as I am. I know." One scene later: "PEG: Everybody that crossed, over here. One at a time." Peg meters Terry ("LO") and Shaun ("LO"). Rosie, who has crossed twice tonight, the second time on a 5.4-second bite, is not metered, and the question is never closed on screen.
*Why it matters.* The season's rule 7 is that every crossing's odds are real. It has just told the audience that Rosie's return was a one-in-six toss and then had the founder say "everybody that crossed" and skip her. The audience will notice the gap, because the writers pointed at it.
*Fix.* Give Rosie a finger in the same run: she holds it out before Peg asks, Peg's meter reads LO, "Still me." Six words, and it also lets the family see that the family cure ran the other way for once and did nothing, which is the theme in miniature.

### QUIBBLE

**Q1. The Register investigators arrive with no reason on the page (Ep 10, Sc 6).** Two investigators come to the Marlows' counter, and nothing says why this yard. The reasons exist off screen (Ray's audit traced dead men's rations to Dilys's van; Rosie walked out of Whitechapel on the night of the raid; Tess gave the night line "Marlow Aggregates"). One line would seal it: "Your girl walked out of Whitechapel the night our depot was done, Mrs Marlow." Two small slips in the same speech: "Both men" (a wall gives a weight, not a sex; 52 kilos could be anyone) and "The other twice — the second time carrying three kilos" (Shaun carried a case on both legs).

**Q2. "fifteen stone of uncle swinging her" (Ep 10, Sc 1).** Frank is Tess's father. One word; it never reaches the screen, but it is in the cold open of the finale.

**Q3. The printer gives live cues the plan's Catch-rules forbid, and Ray's line contradicts the printer (Ep 5, Sc 2; Ep 10, Sc 10 and Sc 12).** Ray: "It won't know till the morning." But the printer "chatters" audibly whenever it prints, day or night (Kieran and Danny weigh themselves by its green light in Ep 5, Sc 18), so the scale would announce a visitor at 02:04 to anyone awake. In Ep 10 Ray steps off and "Under the counter the printer chatters once more, unprompted, and nobody looks at it": that is an instrument reacting live to an unseen crosser, which the plan says never happens ("every such reading is found on the roll afterwards"). Ep 9 does it properly (Kieran finds the minus ticket after). Cut the live chatter in Ep 10; change Ray's line to "It'll chatter. Whether anyone's awake to hear it is another thing."

**Q4. Tess quotes Maggie saying something Maggie never says (Ep 7, Sc 11).** "That vault. She'd walked it since she was fifteen, you said. Every job in it six seconds." Maggie's only lines about Aoife's jobs are "her first nine at nineteen, on the Dunkirk run" and "the bank job, October" (Sc 7). Give Maggie the six-second history during the counting run in Sc 9, where the two women read jobs aloud.

**Q5. "Nobody ever asked why" twice (Ep 7, Sc 4, Terry; Sc 11, Tess).** It is the season's nearest approach to stating its message. Once is a plain line about Lee; twice is a motto. Keep Terry's, which has the better answer under it: "He was off his food, love. Then he wasn't. That was enough for us."

**Q6. Danny stands "just off the plate's end" so that the plate prints only Joe (Ep 6, Sc 1; "Just the one on it", Sc 2).** By the show's own law a plate feels a body in the black over it by its pull, and pull fades with distance rather than stopping at the plate's edge, so a man an arm's length off would show as something. It is the show's rule, and most viewers will not do the physics; but one line of cover would cost nothing (Ray in Ep 5: "A plate reads what stands over it. Stand beside it and it's deaf to you.").

**Q7. Kieran knows tonight's Nobody low to the minute (Ep 7, Sc 5).** "Ten past eleven... The real tide's in the tables." Ep 1 said the ordinary tables miss it ("Ferry's sat on the mud and the tables say high water", Sc 3). Let him have the Port's new tables with the Nobody columns, which Ep 4 gave to Maggie, or let him have rung the ferry.

**Q8. Five separate hop scenes in episode 2 (Sc 7, 8, 9, 13, 16).** Each is varied (the false alarm, Kieran asking "Straight?", Terry's "The black never gave me back"), and the repetition is the point. Only an audience can say whether it plays as ritual or as padding. I would not cut, but I would watch it in the room.

**Q9. "Two left: Terry's, and a third, older, its strap gone cracked" (Ep 6, Sc 5).** The sentence contradicts its own count. "Two on the rack: Terry's, and the old one with the cracked strap."

**Q10. The Register's hand gel and no-shared-towels rule (plan, law 6) never appears.** Nothing breaks: mirror life is carried by "They're frightened what you'd bring in with you" (Ep 4, Sc 3), Terry's gloves, "For him, love. Not for you" (Ep 8, Sc 10), the wash at the standpipe and the drain. But the ledger row is empty. A bottle of gel on the ward locker beside the T-meter would fill it.

**Q11. Ray's ledger dates (Ep 8, Sc 6).** "The seventh of April, it goes to two. Then, against a fortnight Tuesday, it goes to three." Danny turned on 6 May, so the third mouth's Tuesday is 12 May, five weeks on, not a fortnight. The plan says numbers are never rounded; make it "the twelfth of May".

## 5. What I am unsure of

- **Pace** is judged from the page. Episodes 9 and 10 each carry five or six reveals; on the page they never sag, because each beat is short and concrete, but a cut would tell.
- **Deliberate open questions** I have left alone, because each is held in place by something on the page and nothing settles it: whether Terry arrived straight; what Rosie saw and what her last breath was like; what went down the drain; what the second, fainter message means (the four readings the plan lists are all planted). These are working wonders, not holes. Do not "fix" them.
- **S3** may be intended (Peg's "near enough" planning breaking the code's best rule). If so, it needs one line to say so; as written nobody on the page notices, which reads as the writers not noticing either.

## One next step

Do the continuity pass on episode 7 first: S1 (put Danny's Mynors boast where Tess can hear it), S2 (Lee's weights), S4 (Tess's "Rosie. Terry. Danny."), Q4 and Q5. Episode 7 is the season's hinge and four of the seven substantive faults sit in it. Then S3 and S7 in episode 9, S5 in episode 1, and S6 in episodes 6 and 9.
