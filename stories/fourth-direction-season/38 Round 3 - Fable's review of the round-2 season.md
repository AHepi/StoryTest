# SECONDS, full season (round 2 revision): editorial review

*Reviewed: `glm-seconds/round-2/full-season.md`, ten episodes, about 75,000 words, read start to finish against the brief (`00-brief-2.md`), the second feedback (`05-owner-feedback-2.md`), the notes on The Catch (`the-catch-notes.md`) and the master plan (`70-plan-v3.md`). Every quotation below is copied from the season text. Episode and scene numbers are the ones printed in the document.*

A few words used below, in plain terms. A **scriptment** is an episode written out scene by scene with the key lines of dialogue, but not every line: a step between an outline and a script. A **cold open** is the scene before the title card. A **plant** is a clue put in early so that a later surprise is fair. A **payoff** is the moment a plant is cashed. **The drift** is the show's own word for the chance that a crosser comes back mirror-turned.

---

## 1. Verdict

SECONDS is a crime drama about an Essex family of thieves who can step through walls, whose one boast, "Marlows come back straight", is a lie they have been telling themselves for twenty-four years. Across ten episodes the family's warmest habits (cheering a girl for getting thin, burning every scrap of paper, feeding their own, never going to anyone for help) kill a boy, starve a girl at the family table, start a war over concrete, and finally send an old man to live one world over. It is a complete, coherent season: every episode has its cold open, acts, twist and hook; the sixteen twists on the plan's schedule all arrive, each with its plants on the page; the physics is used everywhere and, as far as I can find, never broken on screen. The theme is never spoken. The Catch is not echoed. No banned word appears.

It is finished in the sense that matters: it could go to script now. What is left is small and local. I found six problems a careful viewer would notice, all of them fixable with a line or a beat (the largest is a rule the finale leans on that nobody ever says aloud), and sixteen matters of wording, dates and taste. Nothing structural needs to move.

## 2. How it keeps the owner's rules

### The core message, shown and never said

The message ("a culture that never questions anything dooms its people") is carried entirely by events. The family's rules are spoken only as banter ("Never go back twice." "Lightest goes longest!" "Face home." "And Marlows come back straight.", Ep 1 sc 3), and each is then shown costing something: one long trip instead of two short ones turns Rosie (Ep 6: "I gave you nine"); "make weight" hides her starvation behind applause (Ep 3 sc 17: "And we clapped."); "paper talks, burn it" means nobody can see that every turned Marlow was on a long job (Ep 7, the loft); "we feed our own" turns out to be false (Ep 8: "We never fed our own."); "never go to them" keeps a 2019 letter unopened that would have told them the police stopped reading the Register seven years ago (Ep 9 sc 11).

"Always" is earned by two controls: Frank, whose death twenty-four years earlier was the same story ("That's not a man going careful. That's a man that can't eat.", Ep 10 sc 2), and the Aherns, who keep the same code minus one habit (Maggie opens her post) and are still cheering a child's weight in the last scene with "December's front page, unread" under the scale.

Nobody makes a speech about asking questions. The questions on screen are ordinary: "Why's it a kilo out?", "What did my dad weigh?", "How do you know it's not the weight?" The nearest the text comes to saying its idea is Terry's "Nobody ever asked why" in Ep 7 sc 4 (see Quibble 2). The season also avoids teaching the opposite: Tess, the one who counts, is wrong at Mayfair, wrong about Liam's margin ("I don't know, Danny.", Ep 7 sc 12) and wrong to hand Rosie to the ward without telling her; the family's change comes only when everyone writes and checks together (Ep 10, the night of writing).

### No echo of The Catch

Checked against the list in the notes: no lift or shaft, no kind figure from next door, no ship, no child's drawing of a house, no toy with a letter, no gravity needle showing a visitor live (every such reading is a ticket found afterwards: Ep 2 sc 3, Ep 4 sc 13, Ep 10 sc 12), no backwards signs through a turned character's eyes (the camera stays on the straight side; the turned "know at once and lie"), no dishes of germs, no flask, no mould, no oxygen, no reserve bar (the bite "has no light and no gauge"), no ledge, no hands meeting on glass, no ending on a meal that cannot be shared. "Lift" is never used for a job (the word appears only for lifting a phone, a bag of flour, a grate). The one brush is Rosie's joke "New roll's printed backwards, Nan" (Ep 2 sc 10), which is from the world document's own inventory and is shown at once to be a joke, the ticket "ordinary purple print, the right way round". It is fine; see Quibble 16.

The moral question of The Catch (may you decide someone's fate without telling them?) is not the season's question, but it does occur as action three times (Peg and Frank's letter, Rosie coaching Liam, Tess ringing the Register). None is framed as the question; nobody discusses it. Keep it that way.

### No banned word, no named idea

I searched the whole text for every word on the plan's list (conjecture, criticism, error correction, refute, falsify, fallible, good explanation, hard to vary, growth of knowledge, static or dynamic society, tradition of criticism, authority, optimism, "nothing is settled", "question everything", "the problem of", infinity) and their close relatives. None appears. The Convention, the seed-bank affair, the Looking Act and the Privacy Order are never named; each shows only as a line or an object ("step out on purpose, it's twenty years"; "Police don't get the list, love. Not since 2019."; the ring in the stomach).

### Not a dystopia

The wider world is ordinary and works. Trading Standards inspects ("It's not been a bad trade, Mrs Marlow."). The Register is a kind health service with a cheerful nurse ("eat at four in the morning if you like, nobody'll stop you") and a fireman going home to his leeks. Dr Osei keeps her secret for a decent reason ("That price pays for the Register's kitchens."). The police take rigs, not people. Only the two crossing families, one river apart, never check; that is the point. Religion is left out, as the plan says.

## 3. What works best

- **The family table as the trap.** From Ep 1 sc 3 ("Peg taps the chalk board with her spatula... 'That's making weight.'") every cheer is a threat once you know. Ep 3 sc 9 replays the cold open from Tess's eyes: "Under the clapping, down in his basket, Sixpence growls at Rosie, low and steady, and nobody hears him." Ep 5 sc 13 is the season's best single scene: "The camera stays at the table. He eats every bite. Sixpence's tail slows, and stops." And then: "Peg scrapes the plate clean into the bin, though there's nothing on it to scrape."

- **The first reversal at the meter (Ep 3 sc 16).** Three LOs, two gels, tea that "runs out of the corners and down her neck", and then Terry: "Shift, Tess." "She's been at my pot since the party." Peg's concession is one line and the whole season: "That picture was never a copy."

- **Liam's death (Ep 5 sc 14–16).** The arithmetic is said aloud beforehand ("Sixty-three nine. Seven two." "Job's seven." "Two tenths."), the audience sees the fry-up, and the end is a single sentence: "He was two hundredths of a second from home." Maggie's "It's a minute. That's all you get. Then you go home." is exactly how this world would mourn.

- **Shaun's knots (Ep 5 sc 8–9).** "In the glow the rope is clear as jelly, every strand, and the knot sits inside itself, loop through loop." The tugs going out "like cows walking out of a gate somebody's left open." Then the leg. The Fourth Direction's oddest law made into a set piece, and paid for.

- **The ferry (Ep 7).** Two women, two kinds of paper, one pencil: "Tess puts her own pencil back behind her ear, and takes Maggie's, and this is the first thing a Marlow has ever written on purpose." Maggie's "Nobody counted her, Tess. That's what I've come to say." and "My uncle died of a war about concrete." The stove afterwards, and Terry's "Eleven goes, that was. Eleven. I could've been eating at this table five year since."

- **Ray.** Every line is dry and exact: "So I wait till she comes to me hungry." "You'll want to answer this. Don't." The flat above the bookmaker's with three tomato plants he cannot eat: "They need the watering." His cure in Ep 10 sc 12, the family's faces falling at a mirror signature, then "Eleven year I've wrote back to front so you lot could read me. Give us a fortnight." The mirror is relative, played for a laugh at the one moment nobody is breathing.

- **The sea (Ep 9 sc 5).** "The screen goes black." The thumb that "stops, an inch off the button." The crab in the hood. "It's sea. Under us. All of it." Then the proof by blood, not taste: "Two meters. Same blood. Both of them right." And Danny's question dying in his mouth: "What's it taste—".

- **The scale (Ep 9 sc 11).** Tess sets a bag of flour on her mother's brass balance and then on the little bridge: "It was never the chip." And the line that indicts her own habit: "I told myself their wall was wrong."

- **Frank (Ep 10 sc 2–5).** The tickets read aloud by a twelve-year-old "like a lesson, because somebody has to." Ray: "At seven he was still at the tray. Starving men are slow." Peg with the letter: "He never saw this. I never let him." And her pencil: "FRANK. 10 ST 2. TURNED." "There's your number, Frank."

- **Terry.** The pot topped up in secret; the gloves he will not take off for a wound ("They stay on."); the die ("Six, I stay hungry. Anything else, I get my tea."); Peg's "Terry. Turned. Twenty twenty-one." and his "Ta, Peg."; and the going: "TERRY: One." "TERRY: Two." "There was a man, and then there was the field."

- **The last vote.** Danny's "Burn." then, at the boy's second column, "No. Give." Peg shutting the stove door "herself on the cold": "Give. Paper talks." Tess at the desk: "Every number's mine. Start with me."

## 4. The problems, most serious first

### SUBSTANTIVE

**S1. The bite works in water, and nobody ever says why (Ep 9 sc 4–5; plant in Ep 5 sc 13).**
The season teaches, carefully, that a field fired in water is useless: Ep 1 sc 7, Tess to Kieran, "What if you pressed it in here?" / "Then you'd be carrying the pit with you. Water counts. Everything counts." Ep 9 pays it: Rosie's "thumb goes to the wrist unit — and stops, an inch off the button. So that is why the Deep keeps you." Then, sixty seconds later, she bites the tab and the bite's field carries her home out of the same water. The plan explains it (the bite's field is "a tight one that holds only the crosser and whatever touches them, so it can fire in water"), but the screen never does. Everything the audience has been told about the bite is: "Not in the black. Only once you've stood a minute on the wrong side." (Ep 5) and "Stand a minute over there, any world, and the tab brings me home. Half my seconds." (Ep 9 sc 4). A careful viewer, taught "water counts", will ask why the tab is exempt, and the finale (Ep 10) uses the bite again from the shore.
*Why it matters:* the sea is the season's third reversal, and the one rule it hangs on is unstated.
*Fix:* one line where the bite is taught, Ep 5 sc 13, as Tess tugs the tab: "Tab's field's skin-tight. You and what's on you, nothing else. It'll fire in water. Your belt won't: your belt takes the pit with it." Then Rosie's Ep 9 whisper ("If it's water — then it's the minute") already carries the rest.

**S2. Why not grab him with a posted hand? (Ep 4 sc 2; Ep 6 sc 1.)**
The audience holds two facts side by side. A sent hand never turns: Ep 1 sc 4, "Hand's always straight, you plum. Rest of me's still out here hanging on to it."; Ep 5 sc 1, Rosie, "It's a hand, Nan. Hands don't turn." And grabbing costs a coin toss and a hand: Ep 4 sc 2, "Grab someone in there and one of you turns. Coin says which." The obvious move, grab Joe with a hand posted from the leaf, which cannot turn, is never raised and never closed. Danny goes in bodily (Ep 6) and loses four fingers. The plan has the rule ("A hand sent from a body still on the leaf can't grip anyone in the black: its field is joined to the leaf, and it slips") but nobody on screen says it.
*Why it matters:* Danny's maiming is the payoff of the grip rule, and a viewer who thinks of the posted hand will feel he chose the stupid way rather than the only way.
*Fix:* one line in Ep 4 sc 2, from Tess, after "Collar does nothing. It's skin, or it's nothing.": "And don't think you'll do it with a posted hand. A hand that's still stood on the world can't hold anything that's off it. Slides off him like smoke." (It rhymes with the knot "as though the rope were smoke" in Ep 5.)

**S3. The Register investigators arrive with no stated reason (Ep 10 sc 6).**
"Two REGISTER INVESTIGATORS at the counter... 'Wednesday night, between midnight and one, the Purfleet depot's walls logged two bodies in the black... We'd like the family's weights, Mrs Marlow. To match.'" Nothing on screen says how the Register got from the depot to this door. The links exist in the story (Rosie walked off a Register ward that same night, with this address as next of kin; Ray's rations went to this kitchen and are in an audit), but none is spoken.
*Why it matters:* the whole final choice, burn or give, hangs on this visit, and a careful viewer's first question will be "how did they know?"
*Fix:* give the investigator one more line: "Your granddaughter walked off our Whitechapel ward at eleven that night, Mrs Marlow, and her next of kin is this address. And the man whose rations came to your kitchen for five years is in our audit." It also lets Rosie hear that her walk-out brought them.

**S4. The stakeout's first night is the night Joe comes (Ep 6 sc 1).**
Ep 5 ends on Wednesday 29 April with Danny weighing himself: "Two seconds is enough to grab someone." Ep 6 opens "A week since the river wall", Danny rigged at the table in the dark: "If the tow runs tonight, the man comes again." Nothing on the page says he has sat there any night before this one. Joe comes that night. As written it reads as luck; the plan calls it "a stakeout", which needs time in it.
*Why it matters:* the grab is the season's most violent turn and the one most dependent on Danny's character; a coincidence cheapens it.
*Fix:* a beat, not a line. A row of cold mugs on the table; or Kieran's morning tickets for six nights, each with only his 19.78 on them; or Danny's own whisper amended: "Seventh night. If the tow runs, the man comes again." Kieran's line in sc 2, "It printed him again," then lands as the end of a vigil.

**S5. The fortnight of hunger is invisible (Ep 8 sc 7 to Ep 9 sc 1).**
The last white tub is split on Sunday 24 May (Ep 8 cold open). Peg calls the raid for "Wednesday fortnight" (10 June). Rosie is fed at the ward; Terry and Danny have nothing but "dripping onto a cracker... a nip of gin" for seventeen days. The world's rule allows it (a turned body "lives on water, spirits and fat"), and it is survivable, but Ep 9's cold open shows Terry wrapping butties and singing "a line of the washing-day song", and Danny stacking crates, with no sign of the fortnight on either. Nor does Peg give a reason for waiting two weeks with two men starving; the only hint is "Peg sat in the reeds on Monday night and counted it."
*Why it matters:* the empty pantry is the crisis that drives Ep 8 and Ep 9; if the two weeks cost nothing visible, the raid's urgency sags, and a viewer may ask what they ate.
*Fix:* two things. In Ep 8 sc 7 give Peg a reason for the date that rhymes with Ep 4's "Wednesday night, then. When it's full.": "Wednesday fortnight. That's when the Army's lot goes in. Shed's full then." (It also plants the ARMY stencils Shaun sees.) In Ep 9 sc 1, show the cost: Terry sits to wrap the butties; Danny punches a new hole in his belt with a nail, the way Liam did in Ep 5 sc 4, and Rosie, arriving later, sees the hole.

**S6. Rosie's second bite home is a roll of the die the finale forgets, and the meter contradicts itself (Ep 10 sc 12; Ep 8 sc 3).**
Ep 9 teaches that the bite is a crossing with odds: Kieran, "If you'd turned on the way back, you'd have come home straight."; Rosie, "Five in six I stay as I am." In Ep 10 she bites home from the shore, again for 5.4 seconds, again one in six, and the episode never settles whether she stayed as she was. She says "I'm staying as I am. Can't catch anything." and the epilogue shows HAND: T on her badge, so she did, but nothing on the field shows it. The action line insists "nobody looks at any instrument, because there isn't one, and nobody has brought one", yet Ep 8 sc 3 says Peg carries "the glucose meter in her apron pocket, which she brings everywhere now", and Peg is on the field in her apron ("Peg takes her key ring out of her apron pocket").
*Why it matters:* the show has just spent an episode saying every bite is a toss; skipping the toss it taught, and contradicting its own meter, will catch the viewer who has been paying attention.
*Fix:* keep the field free of instruments for Terry, as the plan wants, but let Rosie settle her own roll by a look, the way the turned in this show always know: on her walk back along the fence she passes the grey MINISTRY OF DEFENCE sign and reads it, and says, as in Ep 9, "Still me." And change "nobody has brought one" to "nobody reaches for one": Peg's meter stays in her apron, unused, which is a choice rather than a slip.

### QUIBBLE

**Q1. The first reversal is confirmation, not surprise, for most viewers (Ep 1 sc 13–14; Ep 2).** By the end of Ep 1 the audience has Sixpence growling at Terry, Terry's "Goes through me like water, love. Since I turned.", Sixpence growling at Rosie, the jar, the flowing mirror card beside the wobbly one, and Terry finding his pot "two fingers below where he left it" and looking "up at the ceiling". Ep 2 adds seven glances at the cutting and the clinic. By Ep 3 sc 16 most viewers know; the scene works as horror ("And we clapped."), not as a twist. Only an audience can settle whether that is a loss. If you want more viewers level with the family, cut Terry looking up at the ceiling in Ep 1 sc 14 (keep the top-up) and let Peg's "He's jealous" cover the dog once in Ep 1 rather than in every episode.

**Q2. "Nobody ever asked why" is said twice (Ep 7 sc 4 and sc 11).** Terry in the loft: "Nobody ever asked why." / "Why?" / "He was off his food, love. Then he wasn't. That was enough for us." Then Tess on the ferry, of the same man: "Nine. Nobody ever asked why." These are ordinary sentences about Lee, not a speech, but doubled they are the one place the season comes near saying its idea aloud. Keep Terry's, which is in character and answered by "That was enough for us"; give Tess a different line on the ferry ("Nine. Right as rain after.").

**Q3. The two hundredths rest on an unchecked guess (Ep 5 sc 13; Ep 9 sc 12).** Tess's sum is chalk 63.9 = 62.4 + "One and a half of notes coming out", a weight nobody weighed. The wall's 65.1 is honest; the split into "the lie on its own" (0.7) and "the breakfast on its own" (0.5) is only exact if the notes were exactly 1.5. Dramatically it lands because Peg's breakfast fills the gap so neatly, and no viewer will do the sum against her. But this is the family that is learning to check numbers against somebody else's. A one-line fix would make the sum airtight: in Ep 5 sc 16, when Maggie hands Danny the printout, "There's a kilo and a half of ours on that reading." (It was her box; she would know.) Then in Ep 9 the notes are the Aherns' number, not Tess's.

**Q4. Rosie's plan needs a rig she could not know would be on the rack (Ep 8 sc 13; Ep 9 sc 4).** The last she knew (Ep 7 sc 12), Peg had locked every rig in the safe with the key round her neck. Kieran's text says only "Nan's doing the depot tonight. They won't let me go." Her rescue is that the crew "takes all but one". Add six words to the text: "Three rigs out the safe. Two going." Then her walk out of the ward is a plan, not a hope.

**Q5. The cold open of Ep 10 shows a memory as if it were fact.** "FRANK MARLOW takes up the whole of the little bridge. A big man, fifteen stone if he's an ounce" is labelled "as Peg remembers it" only in the scene heading, which a viewer never sees. The correction comes in sc 3 ("as it was"). The device is legitimate and the text half-signals it ("Numbers like that don't get read out"; the laugh that "hangs on the office wall, framed"). One visible signal would remove any sense of a cheat: open on Peg's face at the photo, or let the golden light and the uncleaned blinds be plainly the photograph come alive.

**Q6. Peg confesses Rosie's crime for her (Ep 10 sc 15).** "And my girl stepped out on purpose. Twice. Put it with the rest." Rosie is standing in the doorway. Handing someone else's twenty-year charge to a sergeant without asking is the habit the season has been curing (Peg and Frank's letter; Tess and the ward). Give the line to Rosie: "And I stepped out on purpose. Twice. Put it with the rest." It finishes her arc from "Something took hold of me" to writing her own name down, and it matches Tess's "Start with me."

**Q7. Which tug, and where you land (Ep 1 sc 8; Ep 9 sc 4; Ep 10 sc 12).** The finale's door works because you step out at your own coordinates: from Grays, the Deep is a sea over the lino; from the field, the shore. The lore, "The Hill takes you. The Deep keeps you", implies the tug carries you to the thing. In Ep 10 Rosie hunts for the Hill's pull, which she has never followed and which takes her three seconds, when the Deep, which she has followed and would find faster, leads to the same leaf and the same shore. Nothing breaks on screen, and the lore may be meant to be the family's superstition. If you want to close it, one line from Rosie at the tray in Ep 10 sc 9: "Same world as my sea. Different doorstep."

**Q8. "The tide's in my graph" (Ep 7 sc 5).** Kieran's graph is a flat line with one step and a wobble; in Ep 2 sc 3 he says of their tide, "Not yet." Make it "The tide's in my book" (he keeps the Nobody times next to his readings).

**Q9. Two dates.** Ep 4 sc 11: the council envelope arrives on Friday 10 April; Ep 9 sc 11: the letter Tess reads is "Dated the eleventh of April." A letter cannot arrive the day before it is dated. Date it the 9th, or make Ep 5 sc 1's "WEIGHTS AND MEASURES on top" plainly a second letter. And Ep 7 sc 4: "Tuesday, March the ninth, '09" was a Monday.

**Q10. "gasping ours" (Ep 10 sc 12).** "Rosie lands on her hands and knees in the grass, gasping ours." Four lines later she "lets her last breath out — hers, only hers, into our air". If she gasped ours on landing, the last breath is already gone or mixed. Land her holding it ("her chest still, her mouth shut"), then the walk, then the breath, then ours.

**Q11. Two wordings.** Ep 7 sc 2, Terry: "Fridays as well now, he comes." The Laundry man never comes; Dilys's van does. "Fridays as well now, it comes." Epilogue sc 17: "the knuckles of his one hand" reads as if Danny has one hand; he has two, one without fingers. "the knuckles of his right hand."

**Q12. Tess overstates the Register's reach, and the rolls since April are known liars (Ep 10 sc 13).** "The Register holds the day every turned soul in this country claimed." The Marlows never claimed; Terry, Frank, Lee and most of the eleven are not on any list. Say "every turned soul that ever went to them", and let the family's written names carry the rest, which the scene already does. Also, Tess has proved the little bridge lies by 1.1 per cent since 4 April; a half-line ("bar ten weeks that lie by a hundredth, and we can say by how much") would keep her honest.

**Q13. Kieran at home on two school days (Ep 9 sc 8, sc 11).** Thursday 11 June and Friday 12 June, at the counter at 8:20, 9:22 and 9 a.m., after a night up. Nobody says he is off school. One line from Peg ("You're not going in. Not today.") would do.

**Q14. Ep 10 sc 13 is the season's talkiest scene.** Tess "lays it out the only way she knows, which is objects", but it is still four paragraphs of explanation. It does a job (the stakes of the vote), and Danny's "Burn them tonight. We lose the bridge. We stay free." is the point. **Q15. The writers' own notes claim two lines that are not on screen.** Ep 8 "Plants and payoffs": "The spinner (3, 4, 7): cut here, as agreed — 'There's no such person. There never was.'" That line is nowhere in the episode; the spinner is closed in Ep 7 ("Nobody spun anybody."). Ep 5 notes: "Danny's near miss, 'Nearly's nothing' (4)"; Ep 4 has only "DANNY: Nearly —" / "TESS: Seventeen. Out." Fix the notes, or add the lines if they were meant.

**Q16. The one brush with The Catch's device (Ep 2 sc 10).** Rosie: "New roll's printed backwards, Nan." It is her joke, the camera shows the ticket "the right way round", and it comes from the world document, not from The Catch. It is defensible and I would keep it; but it is the only line in the season where a turned character says print is backwards, and the notes on The Catch make "backwards print" its signature. If the owner would rather not go near it, "New roll's upside down, Nan" does the same job (Tess's alarm is about her certified roll either way).

## 5. Summary

The season is whole and it works. The six substantive findings are all closed by a line or a beat; none moves a scene. The quibbles are wording, dates and taste. The rules of the brief and the second feedback are kept: theme in events only, sixteen fair twists, every law used and none broken on screen, nothing of The Catch, no banned word, an ordinary and working world with two families in it who never check.
