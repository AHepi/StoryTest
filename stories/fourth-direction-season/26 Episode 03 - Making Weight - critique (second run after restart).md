# Episode 3 "Making Weight": critique of the draft

*Draft critiqued: `80-episode-03-draft.md`. Checked against the master plan (`70-plan-v3.md`: episode 3 beats, sections 5, 9, 10, 13 and 14), the brief and owner feedback 2, The Catch notes ("The short answer" and faults 1-4), and the finished episodes 1 and 2 (`82-episode-01-final.md`, `82-episode-02-final.md`). Line numbers are the draft's.*

**Verdict.** It's a strong draft. Every beat the plan asks for is here, the numbers are right (41.8 plus a 2 kg tray gives 11.2 seconds for a 10.0-second job; 44.0, 43.6, 43.1; 9.4 and 3:12; the 2023 floor; Ray in 2015 at seven seconds), and nothing in it breaks the banned-word list. The dip is the best set piece so far. Its faults come in three kinds. It lets the audience into the secret too early, at three points. Two of its lines break the grip law. And it drifts twice toward things the plan has ruled out: The Catch's moral question, and a child who asks the adults' question for them. All of these can be fixed at the level of the line; nothing needs a new structure.

**Keep these (don't lose them in revision).** The flash-forward cold open, replayed from where Tess stands in scene 9. Tess's night vigil (scene 8) and her walking in at the pot (scene 13): twice her watching keeps Rosie from the only food that works, so her care helps cause the collapse. Kieran under water as the clock in Ray's scene, and Tess losing count for the first time in her life. "So someone's counting." The LM handprint inside the wall. The thin lad at the bus stop and at the mews (Joe, paid off in episode 4). Peg refusing 999 because the tray is on the floor. "And we clapped." Rosie's second signature, the mirror one, now flowing, which Tess misses. "Paper talks."

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The windscreen drill gives away the turn two acts early (scene 2, lines 45-49)
- **Problem.** In act one we watch Rosie practise writing her own name on a fogged windscreen, "slowly, carefully, the right way round for anyone sitting beside her", until it looks careless. After episode 1's flowing mirror card and episode 2's "roll's printed backwards", any viewer can read this: she can't write normally any more, so she's turned.
- **Why it matters.** The knowledge map (section 10) puts the audience level with Tess "from Ray's line in act two", suspecting as she does, and only edging ahead in act three. This scene puts every viewer ahead of Tess before Ray has said a word. Ray's act-two hints then only confirm what we know, and act three's "tests that pass" become foregone conclusions instead of tests we can't call. The first major reversal lands as a family catching up with us, which is the version-2 problem the plan's change 12 was written to fix. The image is also risky: writing on glass reads backwards from the far side, so one reverse shot through the windscreen puts backwards print on screen, the closest this show can come to The Catch's backwards signs.
- **Fix.** Cut the drill from act one; the scene still works as Rosie tailing the van to Marrable Street. Move the evidence to after the meter, as paper, so it bears witness afterwards instead of warning us beforehand. In scene 16 Tess goes through Rosie's coat pockets on purpose (her first real search: see finding 8) and finds the Obverse leaflet folded round a wad of blank ticket backs, ROSIE MARLOW written on each of them over and over, getting quicker down the pile. That's the one paper Rosie forgot to burn, in the episode where she says "Paper talks".

### 2. The "spinner" lines break the grip law, and Tess contradicts herself a week later (scene 5, lines 219-221; scene 17, line 757)
- **Problem.** Ray: "There's a way to kill a crosser without leaving a mark on her. Turn her round in the black..." Tess: "Nobody can turn anybody. You face home." Rosie: "It had hold of me, Dad. It pulled me round." Under guess 6 and rule 9 (13.9), a turn made by a grip always costs someone flesh: the one who changes leaves the held or holding part in the black. A spin that turns the victim can never leave her unmarked. The family has always known this (knowledge map: "The grip turns one and takes a hand", always; Peg's "Shook on it, him" in episode 1). And in episode 4 Tess herself says "Grab someone in there and one of you turns", and Rosie adds that whoever turns leaves the hand behind.
- **Why it matters.** A Crossing Unit detective states something the law forbids. Tess says in episode 3 the opposite of what she says in episode 4. And a family that knows the price of a grip hears "it had hold of me" from a girl with both hands, and nobody asks where the price went. Viewers will blame the writers, not the family.
- **Fix.** Keep the plan's hook line, "Something took hold of me in the black", but make the force a pull, which the family's own false belief (facing home keeps you straight) will swallow without a grip. Ray: "There's a way to kill a crosser that takes six months and looks like a diet. I don't know how it's done yet. I know what it looks like." Tess: "You face home, you come back straight." Rosie, in scene 17: "Something heavy, right up by me. It pulled me round, off home. Like a lorry going by on the A13." That is episode 1's real sensation (final, scene 1), so her lie rests on something that really happened, and nothing in it contradicts the grip.

### 3. Danny's charge turns Tess's fault into The Catch's moral question (scene 8, line 311; scene 17, lines 773-779)
- **Problem.** Tess hides Ray's warning from the family ("Tess says nothing about Ray"). At the table Danny indicts her for it: "Wednesday. And you sent her tonight." The fault on trial is keeping something back and deciding someone's fate without telling them.
- **Why it matters.** That is exactly The Catch's spine ("may you decide someone's fate without telling them?", its notes' "What works in both"). Section 14 bans it by name ("Nobody's moral question is whether you may decide for someone else"), and change 41 cut "You decided. Again." for this reason. SECONDS's question is whether you checked it against something outside your own head. And the draft already holds the right answer: Tess did test Rosie, but only against Rosie herself.
- **Fix.** Keep the concealment, but make Danny's question about the check, not the secret. Tess: "The wall logged two... The copper. Wednesday." Danny: "And you tested her. With what?" Tess: "Her name." Rosie: "I passed." Beat. Peg's eyes go to the cutlery drawer, where the meter was the whole time. The charge becomes "you checked it against nothing", which is the season's question, asked as an ordinary one.

### 4. Everything counts, except Rosie's coat, hat, gloves and boots (lines 15, 487, 503, 529-537, 587)
- **Problem.** She's weighed "in her socks" at midnight. Then she crosses "rigged under her coat... with her hat on", after going back for her gloves, and nothing takes them off her in the van. Tess, who counts tenths, sums 41.8 plus 2 and ignores a kilo and a half or more of clothes. And the tingle comes at 9.6 against an 11.2-second budget, while episode 1 set it at 9.0 against the same 11.2.
- **Why it matters.** Rule 4: "Everything inside the field counts." The season teaches this in episode 1 ("breakfast too") and kills Liam with it in episode 5, where half a kilo of fry-up eaten after the weigh-in is half the cause. If episode 3 shows the family's most exact weigher sending a crosser out in unweighed clothes, the fry-up stops meaning anything, and Tess stops looking exact. The tingle is the only clock in the black. If it comes at a different point before the end on each crossing, "the tingle came early" can't be read in episodes 5 and 6.
- **Fix.** On job night, weigh her the way she will cross: in the cold open and its replay she steps onto the plate rigged, in the black clothes and soft shoes she crosses in. In the van, before step-off, Tess holds out her hand: "Coat. Hat." They go to Liam, who is holding them when he wraps her after (line 599). Cut the errand for gloves. Put the tingle at 9.0, as at Mayfair. It still finds her in the clay under the road, so the scene doesn't change.

### 5. Terry's look settles four episodes early that "something took hold of me" is a lie (scene 17, line 761)
- **Problem.** "He knows exactly what she has just done, and why." The draft's own notes say this "shows the audience that it's a lie". It is also an interior line that can't be filmed.
- **Why it matters.** "Who took hold of her, and who is spinning the Marlows?" is a mystery the plan wants pressed hard until episode 7 (section 11). Twist 8 depends on the audience entertaining the spinner until episode 6 ("since 6 the audience has guessed that time, not a spinner, turned Rosie"). If the audience knows in episode 3 that she lied, the war in episodes 4 and 5 plays as plain folly rather than as a real hunt.
- **Fix.** Give Terry an action that can be played either way: he looks at her, then down at his gloves, and gets up to put the kettle on. The audience saw her feel a real pull at 3:12 in episode 1, so let them half believe her. Cut the line from the plant notes too.

### 6. Kieran asks the question the adults need (scene 16, line 705)
- **Problem.** "KIERAN: Is Rosie turned?" At the reveal, the child says the word the family can't.
- **Why it matters.** Section 14 ("Never echo Caraway"): "Kieran never asks the question the adults need; he writes down what they remember." It also takes the moment from the adults. Tess has just reached the answer herself ("It can't read her"), and it's hers to say.
- **Fix.** Tess says it, flat, like a number: "She's turned, Mum." Peg flinches at the word, which keeps the episode 10 plant. Kieran watches from the armchair and says nothing. If he needs an action, give him the one in his character: he tears the ticket the printer made when Rosie fell (line 621, "a ticket nobody tears off") off the roll and keeps it.

### 7. Ray's pit scene briefs and preaches (scene 5, lines 193-257)
- **Problem.** In one scene Ray delivers the wall log, the Register card, the lard, a diet-murder lecture, the heart and the law on looking, the ring anecdote, the car, "Had a call", Marrable Street, and the painting line. On top of the plan's beats the draft adds "And her family cheered every kilo", "Your niece gets lighter every time I see her", "When a crosser comes back turned, and there was somebody else in the black beside her, I stop calling it luck", and "I've watched your family's weight since 2002".
- **Why it matters.** "Cheered every kilo" says the episode's irony aloud before the reveal: the theme spoken, not earned. The other additions tell Tess her niece is turned before the tests, which leaves nothing for her to suspect. "Watched your family's weight since 2002" spends the surprise of episode 8's folder and points at the Laundry. This is also The Catch notes' fault 5 (a scene that "briefs instead of pressing").
- **Fix.** Cut the four added lines. Keep the log ("A wall gives you a weight, not a name." / "What weight?" / "I'll keep that." is Ray's hoarding in one exchange), the card, one line of the diet-murder, the ring, the car, "First job I ever caught was on Marrable Street", and the painting line at the car as the sting. Let Kieran's dives cut into Ray's lines so the scene keeps a clock running.

### 8. Act five confirms the twist seven times, and one of them is luck (scene 16, lines 699-721)
- **Problem.** After the meter, the gel and Terry's pot, we get Tess's "It can't read her", Kieran's question, a sweep round the room over five clues (the cards, the cutting, the board, the lard, the dog), a leaflet that happens to fall out of a coat, "No it wasn't, love", and "That picture was never a copy."
- **Why it matters.** The plan's reversal works because the audience does the re-reading itself. A guided tour of the clues does it for them, and a leaflet that drops at the right moment is a coincidence, in an episode about the family never checking.
- **Fix.** Keep the plan's beats (LO, "She's hypo", gel, LO, the pot, "since the party"), Tess's "It can't read her", "No it wasn't, love", and Peg's painting line. Cut Kieran's question (finding 6). Shrink the sweep to one image: the two birthday cards side by side on the fridge, the careful one and the flowing one. Replace the falling leaflet with Tess searching the coat on purpose (finding 1). She finds what she finds because she finally looked.

### 9. Peg's "he went in anyway" fills the hole the plan wants viewers to notice (scene 3, line 85)
- **Problem.** "Fifteen stone, and he went in anyway, 'cause somebody had to."
- **Why it matters.** Twist 13's fair plant is a sum any viewer can do: 95 kilos buys one second on the painted rule, which is too few for any job, so the legend doesn't add up. Peg's new clause answers the sum (he went knowing he'd run out) and turns a nagging gap into a finished heroic story. It also raises the question of why a fifteen-stone man had to go when Terry, then light and not yet turned, was on hand.
- **Fix.** Cut the clause. "Fifteen stone. That's why we make weight." Tap the rule, and leave the sum lying there.

### 10. The batcher would talk, so "Who's talked?" can't stand (scene 4, lines 165-175; scene 8, line 307)
- **Problem.** A detective sits in the Marlows' own plant reading the Marrable Street pour contract, in front of an old hand from Frank's day. A week later Peg asks "Who's talked?" and Danny knows nothing of it.
- **Why it matters.** In a family firm, the batcher would tell Danny the same day. As written, the family's surprise rests on a man keeping quiet for no reason.
- **Fix.** Keep scene 4 (the plan puts Ray at the plant), and have Danny know and shrug it off in scene 8: "Copper was in the plant reading my contracts." Peg: "Which one?" Danny: "All of them. Best advert I've got." That is the family's pride, shown doing harm. Peg's "Who's talked?" then lands on a room that has just waved away the answer.

### 11. Lines that wink at the theme, the title or the next reveal (lines 115, 121, 415, 459, 475)
- **Problem.** Con, in one call, says "Ours do that" (about cheering weight), "My lot are the same as yours. 'We feed our own.' Fed on what, I say", and "Boat's late again". That's three pointers to the Aherns, plus the family motto quoted back as a criticism. Rosie: "Don't lose that. You've never used it" (she names the pencil's symbolism). Peg: "went back for seconds" (a pun on the title, which "nobody ever says").
- **Why it matters.** Episode 4 names Con as an Ahern and gives Declan's "We feed our own" its first Ahern airing, at the stove. Con's call nearly spends both. The other two lines are the writer nudging the viewer.
- **Fix.** Con keeps "Boat's late again" and "You don't know where here is, Tel." Cut "Ours do that" and the motto. Rosie tucks the pencil back behind Tess's ear without a word. Peg: "went back a second time".

### 12. Two slips in the black (scene 14, lines 561 and 587)
- **Problem.** "Through a steel door, a sleeping guard in a chair, and the inside of him." And "She finds the tug of home and follows it up."
- **Why it matters.** Rule 3: sight is glow-lit and arm's length. "Through a steel door" reads as seeing into the next room. Law 5: there is no up in the black, and the grammar of every crossing should keep that.
- **Fix.** "Against the far side of the steel door, close enough to touch, a guard sleeps in a chair." And: "She finds the tug of home, faces it, and goes."

### 13. Continuity with episodes 1 and 2 (lines 23, 709, 783, 803)
- **Problem and fix, item by item.**
  - Danny names the Aherns as Mayfair's workers himself (line 141), then asks "Who works Mayfair?" (line 783). Give him the statement: "Aherns. They were working Mayfair." Shaun: "All winter."
  - "Four stars" (line 709): in episode 1's final, 44.0 has no star; 43.6 and 43.1 do. With tonight's that makes three. Make it three.
  - In episode 1's final, Terry's pot lives in the fridge at night (scene 14). At midnight and at 12:30 the draft has it by the stove. Either have Terry take it out of the fridge in scene 9, or keep the spoon beat at the fridge.
  - The plant notes say Ray's painting line "breaks the family's belief from 2", but only Tess hears it and she keeps it to herself. The family's belief breaks at the meter, with Peg's "That picture was never a copy." Correct the note so later writers don't lean on it.

## Checks that passed
- **Beats.** Every beat of the plan's episode 3 is present, in order: 41.8 and "That's a Marlow"; Rosie pitches the job; "Your grandad's street"; the 2023 floor; "Thickest at the back"; two short trips beaten by "Never go back twice"; Ray at the plant, the car, "Had a call", two bodies at 3:12, the card, the ring, "So I wait till she comes to me hungry", the painting line; the long way, ten seconds; the dodge, the writing test, the lard, the dog, Terry to Con about the pot; the dip; LO, the gel twice, the pot, "since the party"; the hook.
- **Numbers.** All canonical, and no new canonical number. The wrist tenths in the black are only Rosie's route (but see finding 4 for the tingle).
- **Physics.** Nothing crosses but gravity (the beads fall silent; "Can she hear you?" "No."). A turn can't be felt ("Straight?" "Straight."). The meter reads LO on turned blood, and the gel does nothing. Free translation through steel and brick. A 30 per cent chance that ten seconds leaves her turned is believable at the table's odds. The bite has no light and is never called a spare.
- **Owner's rules.** No banned word or named idea on screen. No mint, no taste test, no backwards print from a turned point of view (but see finding 1 on the windscreen's reverse shot), no dishes, no glass hands, no "lift". The episode doesn't end on a meal. Religion is absent, and nothing is a dystopia.
- **Craft.** We care before the threat (the weigh-in, the table, Liam, Terry and Con). Almost every scene does two jobs. The episode ends far higher than it began: a starving girl, a war declared, and a table that has become the danger.
