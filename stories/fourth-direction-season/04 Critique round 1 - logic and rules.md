# Critique of the season plan: logic and rules

Lens: does every twist and plot step follow from the Fourth Direction's laws and from the plan's own ledger (section 2) and rules (section 10)? Most serious first. Each finding gives where it is, the problem, why it matters, and a fix.

---

## 1. Nothing says how anything gets back from the dark, and the plan needs two opposite answers

**Where.** 1.1 ("lifts a body... then sets it down again"); 1.4 the Giving; stipulation 3 (the tug of home); episode 2 (Dimopoulos: the Called were "too heavy to get home before their time ran out"); episode 5 and episode 10 (card crates "come back"); Brother Arkani's seeds "come back turned"; Year One's founders' count.

**Problem.** The plan uses three different return rules without choosing one:
- The Lift sets you down again by itself (1.1).
- You get home by following the tug of home before your time runs out (stipulation 3, and Dimopoulos's reasoning in episode 2).
- Anything left in the dark when the time runs out stays there (rule 2, the Giving).

A crate of cards, a bag of seeds and a corpse are all inert. None of them can follow a pull.
- If inert things come back, the Given dead come back too, and there is no Weight.
- If inert things don't come back, no crate or seed ever returns, and the episode 5 proof, the episode 10 proof, Arkani's sixty-year count and the founders' "2 in 100" all vanish.
- If inert things simply fall along the strongest pull, then at the old door after Year 235 every crate falls into the Weight. The "31 in 100" crate could not have come back at all.

**Why it matters.** The season's two central devices depend on this rule, and they need opposite answers. The doom engine (the Giving) needs the dead to stay. The method that saves Ream (cards instead of children) needs crates to come back.

**Fix.** Declare one rule in 2.5 and use it everywhere. Suggested wording: *"The ring sets back down whatever is still within its reach when the Sundrum closes the field. The Accountants set when he closes it, never later than the weight allows. A field that is left to run out sets nothing down."* Then everything follows:
- A Tithe child is closed back in, unless the Weight has dragged her out of reach first (the Called).
- The Given are the one rite in which the field is left to run out.
- The Passed follow a far pull out of reach and step out on the other world (stipulation 3 stays as it is).
- Crates and seeds come back unless they are dragged off. An occasional crate that never returns is a good grim detail.
- The Fast does harm because the rite demands the full time ("pray the whole crossing through").

Also fix Arkani's seeds. A 10-gram seed bag would be allowed hours in the dark, so it should turn far more often than a child does. Have him ballast his seed bags to a child's weight (the same trick as the episode 10 crates), so that his "one in four" follows the Tithe rate by construction. He has been running Nell's experiment for sixty years without knowing it.

## 2. Nothing says what turns as one piece, and the pasted-card test contradicts itself

**Where.** Episode 5 (cards in crates; "cards pasted together in pairs... come back both R, both Я, or one of each"); episode 10 (100 cards per crate as 100 separate results); 1.2 and rule 6 (a Turned body's gut germs are mirror-life); episode 6 (the phone lifted out in Nell's hand).

**Problem.** The Fourth Direction says walls enclose nothing in the dark (Law 3). So a crate holds its cards no more firmly than skin holds gut germs.
- The card tests assume each loose card in one crate turns on its own coin.
- The biology assumes a body's gut germs turn with the body. If they each had their own coin, a Turned child would come home with mostly unturned germs, and every child who did *not* turn would bring back a quarter of their germs turned. Then there would be grey after every Tithe, not only from the Turned.
- The pasted pairs are worse. If paste makes one object, a pair can never come back "one of each", and the test says nothing about two separate people holding hands. If a glued object *can* come back half turned, then so can a human body, whose cells are joined no more firmly than paste. The plan never allows that.
- The phone in episode 6 is a loose object held in a fist for minutes beside the Weight. By the plan's own light-crate results, it has a high chance of coming back mirrored. If it did, its screen would read Ream's way, and the episode 6 beat ("only Turned eyes read it") would flip.

**Why it matters.** This is a secret stipulation doing heavy work: the refutation of the handhold law, every card number and the grey all rest on it.

**Fix.** Declare it once in 2.5. For example: *"A crossing turns each separate body whole, with everything inside a living body. Separate things side by side, even touching, each get their own coin. A box is not a body."*
- Replace "pasted" with "clipped in pairs": a grip, not a join. Clipped pairs come back one of each at the ordinary rate. This refutes the handhold law, which is about a grip.
- In episode 6, play the phone's coin as part of the tension. When it wakes, Nell checks its letters against the plates, which have not crossed since the founding. They read the same way, so it did not turn. That is a small test against a sample, done on screen, and it is the method in miniature.

## 3. Episode 1's inciting choice ignores tests that Ream's own scripture names

**Where.** Episode 1, act 3 (the Randall must pick a twin); 1.3 ("dogs are kept by the Order as knowers"); 1.6 ("paper is a witness").

**Problem.** The Order treats the Plot as case law. That same morning the school shows the audience an R turning into a Я. The Leaves offer at least four ways to settle which twin turned:
- "signs the manifest in flawless mirror script";
- "turns [a jar] the wrong way";
- Theorem 4's "a sheet of paper";
- "the dog knows", with the Order's own knower dogs.

Asking each twin to write their name ends the dilemma in ten seconds. Nobody suggests it. Plattner's growl at the Randall raises the same question: if the Order keeps knower dogs, why haven't they growled at her for 48 years?

**Why it matters.** This is the pilot's central injustice, and the audience has just been given the tool that would prevent it. As written, it is an idiot plot.

**Fix.** Make the Randall close the question on camera, which is her character anyway.
- Dimopoulos proposes: "Let them each sign the Tally."
- The Randall refuses with the verse ("The handhold is what did it. The Plot needs no second witness").
- Establish a gloss carved on the steps: *"The mint alone is witness."*
- Her refusal becomes the audience-behind clue that she is protecting a face she recognises.

For the dogs:
- Keep the Order's dogs out of the Temple on Tithe day; the hum sets them howling.
- The Order's dogs were raised in the Temple and learned the Randall's smell as pups, just as Plattner learns Nell's by episode 8. Only an outside dog growls at her.

## 4. Nell's key deduction in episode 8 rests on the verse the season has just disproved

**Where.** Episode 8, act 1: "its pull twists crossers ('the grip turns them', and a pull is a grip)".

**Problem.** The handhold verse is the one the cards (episode 5) and the First Leaf (episode 6) showed to be a scene from a film (2.4; rule 9: "A handhold changes nothing"). Nell now builds the season's central explanation on that verse.

**Why it matters.** Episode 8 is where the show demonstrates a new explanation *built* rather than declared. A proof that leans on a refuted premise teaches the opposite lesson, and attentive viewers will catch it.

**Fix.** Cut the grip line. Give her evidence she can check:
- Two rival offices' records agree year by year: the Wardens' Weight-book and the Tallykeepers' rate rise together.
- The cards show that more time beside it means more turning.
- The needle walk shows it is one thing, in one place.

Then add the prediction: far from it, the rate falls back to the founders' 2. Episode 10 tests exactly that. Stipulation 2 stays the audience's declared rule; Nell reaches it through evidence, not scripture.

## 5. The numbers linking the Weight to the turning rate do not fit together, and the final twist depends on them

**Where.** 1.9 and section 11 (rate "2 in 100, Years 1–230", flat while the Weight grows from 0 to about 830 t); stipulation 2 ("grows with the mass"); episode 10 (Minthome's small cloud doubles the rate at the new door from 2 to 4); episode 10 (one crate of 100 cards each time).

**Problem.**
- Stipulation 2 says twisting grows with mass, with no threshold. Yet the curve stays flat for a century while about 830 t piles up.
- Minthome has about 400 people and has Given its dead for about 30 years. That is roughly 10–15 t. About 830 t did nothing measurable at Ream's door, yet this cloud, around a seventieth the size, doubles the rate at the Graves.
- One crate of 100 cards cannot tell 4 from 2. With a true rate of 2, a count of 4 or more happens about one time in seven. Dimopoulos's 5 is borderline.

**Why it matters.** Section 2.7 promises that every later twist can be worked out from the ledger card. Major reversal 3 cannot be worked out from it; it is contradicted by it.

**Fix.**
- Let the curve rise gently from Year 131 (for example 2 to 5 by Year 235, hidden inside the lump total, which is exactly what Nell's "each year?" question uncovers). Keep Year 235 as the threshold for *capture* (the Weight out-pulls the far world, so Passings stop arriving). Twisting does not need a threshold.
- Put Minthome's young cloud about a third as far from its door as Ream's centuries-spread cloud is from Ream's. By the cube law, 15 t there pulls like about 400 t at Ream's distance, roughly Ream's Weight around Year 180, when its rate was about 3–4. Add one derived line: a young cloud is tight, and an old one has spread (this follows from stipulation 1's "slowly spreading").
- Run ten crates, not one. About 40 in 1,000, against the founders' 20, is a difference nobody can call luck.

## 6. Kit's silences are not justified

**Where.** 4 (Kit); episodes 7–10.

**Problem.**
- (a) Kit has known "Move the door" for 47 years, sits a kilometre from a stone that talks to Ream, and knows the catechism game. He never sends one word until Wells arrives.
- (b) In episode 8 Kit and Wells both know the Mother's pile is growing. They send MOVE THE DOOR and let Ream carry its Lift to the one spot that stands over it, without a warning. The warning cannot be blamed on the code: WE, DIFFUSE, HERE and TOO are all in the Leaves (checked), and that message is sent in episode 10.

**Why it matters.** Both are things resolved only because someone fails to do the obvious. The second one also makes the final twist feel withheld rather than earned.

**Fix.**
- (a) Make Wells the one who turns the twins' game into a code. Kit shouted for 47 years and never built a channel. This also meets the Wells rule (a choice that changes the plot).
- (b) Make Kit's silence a visible choice in episode 9: "Tell them the new place is dirty too, and they'll never move it." That is the Randall's certainty-as-mercy in the other keeper. Wells objects and is too weak to haul alone. Episode 10's WE DIFFUSE HERE TOO then comes from Wells and Jory, and Kit's choice gets its cost.

## 7. Kit's "turning rate at the Mother" cannot be measured

**Where.** 4 (Kit's secret); episode 8 ("The Mother's turning rate has crept up").

**Problem.** Since about Year 270 the Mother is "for the dead only". The dead never come back, so nothing that crosses there is ever tasted or counted. There is no rate to keep.

**Why it matters.** It is a fact the world cannot produce, and it holds up the plant for episode 10.

**Fix.** Give Kit a needle log at the Mother instead: every Giving is a rise that stays, and the pile grows. Add his own memory of how the pile beside Ream's door twisted him. Then Lottie's "why four?" becomes the first actual measurement of the Mother's effect, which is better.

## 8. The Sealed Jar: where it came from, what is in it, and which way its lid turns

**Where.** 1.2 ("the Sealed Jar, which has never crossed"); 1.7 (its base reads Earth's way); 1.9 (the founders carried "a glass jar"); 4 and the episode 5 plant (the lid opened "against the sun" as a hidden Turned Randall's habit).

**Problem.**
- The jar came from Earth, so it has crossed.
- If it still held the founders' Earth mint, every ordinary Reamer would taste caraway from it.
- A jar used for about 250 leaves a year must be refilled.
- An Earth-made thread opens the same physical way for everyone. Every Randall, Turned or not, has had to turn it "against the sun". So the lid is no evidence that the Randall is Turned.

**Why it matters.** The one test the whole culture rests on is left undefined, and a listed plant points at the wrong reveal.

**Fix.**
- Say that the founders' Earth glass is refilled each spring with Ream's own garden mint; "never crossed" refers to the leaves.
- Move the lid to the episode 9 plants. "Against the sun" is a fossil of the jar being made by the other hand, which is Earth eyes in miniature.
- If you want a lid clue for episode 5, it must actually discriminate. For example: the Sundrum fumbles the lid at a rehearsal; the Randall's hands open it without thought.

## 9. Nell writes her brother's name in Earth script on the public board

**Where.** Episode 1, ending hook (WELLS ABBOTT: REFUSED in her new hand on the Tally).

**Problem.** Ream's own case law says a Turned crosser "signs the manifest in flawless mirror script". Her line sits among a wall of Ream script, and the city reads that board in the morning.

**Why it matters.** It would expose her on day one. Nothing in the plan removes the line.

**Fix.** In episode 2's opening minutes, Nell comes back at dawn and finds her line already wiped and rewritten in Ream's hand. It is the feeder's first act, a day before the loaf, and it deepens "someone knows".

## 10. The heist does not decide whether Wells can come home

**Where.** Episode 9's pitch ("to save one boy and a city, a hundred people must steal God in a night"); episode 10, act 4.

**Problem.** A crossing needs no Lift on the far side. The Passed land at the Landing, where there is none. Kit walked out of the Old Graves again and again in Year 253 with no Lift there. Wells can cross from the Mother to the Graves whenever Adamala lets him.

**Why it matters.** It gives a false stake to the biggest set-piece. Viewers who remember Kit's crossings will see the gap.

**Fix.** Name the real obstacle. Until Ream changes, anyone who walks out of the Graves is Passed on sight ("Pass him"). Wells's clock is a race between his body and his city's change, and he chooses to wait for it. Rewrite the pitch around that. The shared crossing is a choice, not a requirement.

## 11. Wells's "two pulls" leave out home

**Where.** Episode 1, act 2; episode 7, cold open; stipulation 3.

**Problem.** The Leaves' only cue is "the tug of home", and stipulation 3 says crossers feel the pulls as directions. In Wells's own world, home's pull must be there too. If he feels only the cliff and the far voice, how did any Tithe child get home?

**Why it matters.** It is the season's key plant for survival, and it is missing a part.

**Fix.** Have him say "Besides home, there are two." Or: "Home's under us like a floor. And two more." Nell's "the Leaves name one" still dismisses him.

## 12. In the Tithe, the wrong twin's time runs out first

**Where.** Episode 1, act 2 ("Nell's fingertips tingle... Wells takes her hand"), with the weights in act 1 (51.0 kg and 70.4 kg).

**Problem.** By the plan's rule (700 ÷ kg), Nell has about 14 seconds and Wells about 10. His budget ends first.

**Why it matters.** The show asks the audience to trust the Accountants' arithmetic, and the pilot breaks it.

**Fix.** Wells's fingertips tingle, and Nell sees his heart racing through his ribs. He grabs her hand because *his* time is going and she has frozen. "The hand that reached" becomes literally true, and he reached out of haste, not fault.

## 13. Kit's eleven crossings: the count disagrees, and a Plot verse comes true by coincidence

**Where.** 1.9 ("eleven crossings in all"); episode 8 ("He came back ten more times that year", which would mean about twenty crossings); episode 9 ("the Pariah verse was about the founders themselves").

**Problem.**
- The two counts disagree.
- More importantly, Kit crossing exactly the verse's eleven times makes a Plot verse come true by chance. The page predates the founders, so it cannot have been "about" them.

**Why it matters.** It contradicts 2.4 ("Being in the Plot is never what makes them true"), and it quietly rewards the Order's way of reading.

**Fix.** Kit's real count was seven. Nan's tale, and the city, rounded it to the verse. When Wells repeats "eleven", Kit snorts: "Seven. They made it fit." That is a reading that is not the thing read, from the mouth of the man it happened to. Change episode 9's line to "the founders, like the verse's pariah, kept crossing to find mint".

## 14. The Great Tithe cannot double the Weight

**Where.** Episode 8, act 4 ("a third of the city Turned or Called, the Weight doubled").

**Problem.** Twelve thousand people weigh about 650–700 t. Adding another 1,500 t would take more than the whole city.

**Why it matters.** It is a number shown on screen in Nell's own sums, from the one character whose sums must be right.

**Fix.** At the old door's 31 in 100, about 3,700 would turn and be Passed, and most would be lost. Add some Called, and the Weight rises by roughly 180 t, about an eighth. Write "a third of us gone; the rate past forty by next spring".

## 15. The visual rule leaves out things that must, by its own logic, read our way

**Where.** 1.7 and section 11 ("Only the plates, the Jar's base, the First Leaf, Minthome's signs and Nell's hand").

**Problem.** At least three more kinds of writing must read our way:
- **The Fable's pages.** The founders carried them from Earth, and the Changers read them with mirrors ("people who read backwards too"). Bonus: Ream's moulds can't eat that paper, which is why it survived 300 years.
- **Anything the Randall writes in her true hand**: her private ledger (episode 4) and "24 IN 100" chalked "in her own hand" (episode 9). She is Turned.
- **Nell's public board in episode 10.** Written in her hand, it is backwards to the city that must read it.

**Why it matters.** Writers will render these mirrored, and the rule the show promises is "the plain truth" will break.

**Fix.** List them.
- Decide that the Randall fakes Ream script in public (as Nell does) and writes naturally only in private. The ledger in episode 4 is then a fair plant for episode 5, and "in her own hand" in episode 9 shows the city its Randall writing backwards.
- In episode 10, Nell writes the prediction twice, once in each hand. This pays off the surplus detail "legal papers kept in pairs".

## 16. Moving the Lift: forty days then, one night now, onto the ground that floods

**Where.** 1.9, Year 9 (forty days; "the river floods the plinth"); episode 9, act 5 (carried and reassembled in one night).

**Problem.** The plan never says why the founders needed forty days and a hundred amateurs need one night. The Graves are also exactly where the river drove the founders out.

**Why it matters.** Both are questions a viewer will ask at the season's biggest set-piece.

**Fix.**
- Make the forty days in the founders' log mostly the building of the hill plinth. The old river plinth still lies under the Graves, which is why the Graves were built there. Tau finds it in the log, so the heist needs no new plinth.
- Name the flood as residue: the Graves flood every few springs.

## 17. Small continuity breaks (each is a one-line fix)

- **Episode 6.** The phone runs on port power, so "the battery dies" makes no sense. Have the port go dead as the maintenance hours end, just before the Randall's footsteps.
- **Episode 5 cold open.** "Real food for the first time in weeks" contradicts the loaf at the end of episode 2 and the bundle in episode 4. Also, 5.0 says she starves "until the end of episode 4". Make the bundles rare and small, or cut "in weeks".
- **Episode 9 against episode 10.** Wells will cross "lying on the Givings bier", but in episode 10 he "stands at the Mother". Pick one.
- **1.9.** Kit is Passed in spring of Year 252 but "arrives in Minthome" in 253. A Straight has about six months, so his first return must come within months.
- **Episode 10 board.** The board says "FEWER THAN 1 IN 20", and Dimopoulos's 5 in 100 *is* 1 in 20. Either play that edge on purpose or set the line at 1 in 15.
- **Episode 8 needle walk.** Readings of 100 at the Temple steps and 1 at the river gate put the gate about 4.6 times as far from the Weight. With the plan's implied distance of about 33 m, that is about 150 m. Yet the Graves "by the river" are a kilometre away. Change the chalked numbers (for example 100 / 15 / "less than one") or move the market and gate.
- **Episode 10.** Kit hangs a stone at the station before anyone has told him where the new door is, and "Graves" and "Mother" are not words in the Leaves. Add one line in episode 9: after the heist, Ream sends MOVE... DOOR... HERE? on the Temple stone, and Kit guesses the rest.
- **Carried germs (rule 11) cut both ways.** Nell carries Ream life, which is mirror-life on Earth, on her skin and clothes into Minthome. That is probably what Earth's fence ("MIRROR-LIFE CONTAINMENT") has always been about. Name it next to the sneeze so the Season 2 hook is symmetric.
