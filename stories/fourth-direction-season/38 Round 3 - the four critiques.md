# 38 Round 3 - the four critiques

*Three GLM critics, each through one lens, and MiMo v2.6 Pro with fresh eyes, on the season after round 2.*

## Critique: story


1. **Episode 7, scene 7 (the ferry saloon) — Joe's stopwatch breaks the budget rule.** Maggie says Joe "sat in the dark off your shelf with a stopwatch, timing your girl" at Mayfair. Joe is 59.0 kg twice over (the steelyard's "Fifty-nine!", the −59.0 ticket), so by the rule painted on the Marlows' own wall he has (100 − 59.0) × 0.2 = 8.2 seconds. Rosie's crossing was 9.4 seconds. Joe cannot be present for her entry and exit; he runs out first. In a scene whose entire job is arithmetic, the arithmetic is wrong, and it feeds Danny's "He'll be home for his breakfast" logic. **Fix:** change Maggie's line so his presence fits his count — "Eight seconds he stood there and his nails went, so he got what he got; we read the rest off the flood" — or have him time her from the three-second flood to her passing him out. SUBSTANTIVE

2. **Episodes 1, 3 and 5 against each other — the show never fixes how fast a crosser moves in the black, and its set pieces disagree.** Rosie's routes (a wall, a road, a room, in 9–10 seconds) imply a walking pace of one to two metres a second. Shaun's knot job asks us to believe he travels the length of a pontoon rafting three tugs and undoes nine mooring lines — each requiring him to "see the way out of it" — inside one 8.4-second trip. Joe's wharf-to-the-safe drop implies the same impossible pace. Every heist's clock is this season's engine; a viewer who times Rosie will find Shaun's job cannot be run. **Fix:** declare the pace once, at the pit ("In there you've got a walk in you, that's all there is"), and resize: Shaun springs one tug, four lines; Joe's drop becomes two short crossings with a hide between them. SUBSTANTIVE

3. **Episode 2, scene 3 and episode 7, scene 5 against episode 9 — the tide contradicts the flat line.** Kieran's stated aim is to see the neighbours' tide in his nightly 20 kg readings, and his pre-April graph is a dead flat line of 20.00s; in episode 7 he says "The tide's in my graph." But their sea stands eight metres over that lino and rises and falls to the moon of the Nobody Tide — its pull on the plate must always have wobbled by the ±10 grams the scale resolves and the show later uses. The world's own gravity forbids a flat line until 4 April, and the dialogue draws attention to it ("And have you?" "Not yet"). **Fix:** either let the winter tickets wobble a step either side, unnoticed because he plotted rounded figures, or attribute the May wobble to the message's small strokes (episode 5's radio: "too small to feel") and cut the tide claim. SUBSTANTIVE

4. **Episodes 2, 6 and 7 — Maggie sends Joe into odds she has every reason to fear, and no one marks it.** She is built as the one who opens her post and doesn't cheer her thin son; her daughter was turned by a nine-second crossing. Yet she sends that son to sit out the Mayfair watch (a long crossing by any reading), and then on the bullet and envelope drops, and only after the ferry suddenly forbids it ("He doesn't cross, Tess. Not while I've got him"). Meanwhile Danny's single grab costs him his fingers. Her conversion reads as a writer's switch, and Joe's run of straight returns is luck the season never prices. **Fix:** one line of self-indictment on the ferry — "I told myself facing home would keep him. I've been doing what your mum does" — and keep Joe's presences short wherever the plot allows. SUBSTANTIVE

5. **Episode 6, cold open — the ambush lands on the delivery night by luck.** Danny deduces the tow, gambles one crossing on one night; Maggie, for her own reasons, has chosen that same night for Joe's envelope. The grab, Danny's fingers, Danny's turning and the arrival of the ferry invitation all hang on two unconnected plans colliding, in a season otherwise strict about cause. **Fix:** establish that Danny has rigged up at 02:04 for several nights running — Terry finding him asleep at the table, the cost showing on him — so the night it pays is the end of a run, not a lottery (failed nights print nothing, so no ticket contradicts it). SUBSTANTIVE

6. **Episode 9, scene 6 — "To the gram" implies a weightless rig.** The recognition beat rests on the ward's 46.2 matching the plate's −46.2, but Rosie crossed in socks *and rig*, and the family's own convention weighs crossers rigged (her 41.8 included jersey, leggings, socks and rig). The exact match can only work if the rig weighs nothing, and the line invites the sum. **Fix:** drop "To the gram", or set the ward weight a kilo higher and let Kieran do the subtraction aloud. QUIBBLE

7. **Episode 7, scene 4 against episode 10 — the loft damages the evidence the finale spends.** Tess and Terry sort "two piles of torn ticket stubs", torn from the certified audit rolls on which the whole Monday case, and the vote, depend being intact and printable. The episode's tickets-are-burned habit means the stubs can only have come from the rolls, and no one remarks the mutilation. **Fix:** have them sort a family hoard of old tear-off tickets (one line establishes the box), or copy figures onto loose paper and leave the rolls whole. QUIBBLE

8. **The season's knowledge gap is sealed in the plan but not on screen.** The epilogue presents the rolls as the first joining of duration to turning, yet the Register holds 40,000 claim dates, most from the pre-2014 years when anyone crossed for as long as they liked. The reason that reservoir was unusable — the answer would confess a crime, so no claimant honest answers — exists only in the plan. **Fix:** one line, from Osei in episode 6 or the epilogue's newsreader, saying why nobody could ever be asked how long they were in the black. QUIBBLE

9. **Episode 5, scenes 11–12 — Liam's haul doesn't buy what he says it buys.** 1.5 kg of fifties is roughly £75,000–£100,000 at a gram a note, and he calls it "Rosie's cure, near as" against the established £180,000. **Fix:** raise the notes to three or four kilos, or let him say "half her cure" / "a start". QUIBBLE

10. **Episode 6, scene 8 — Osei's pass is asserted, never tested.** She identifies her visitor, watches her own protocol walk out of the building, and does nothing beyond a warning, because "exhibits walk". The reasoning is given once and produces no consequence, no follow-up, no copy — in a woman who has kept this secret profitably for fifteen years. **Fix:** one beat of her photographing the binder before Tess leaves ("Now there are two"), which also sharpens the epilogue's share collapse. QUIBBLE

11. **Episode 6, cold open — the two-beat hover exceeds Danny's 2.2 seconds.** His true budget is 2.2; the scene gives him arrival, finding Joe, a two-beat hesitation at the heart, the grab, the wrench and the step home. On the clock it doesn't fit; on screen it reads as five or six seconds. **Fix:** cut the hover to a single beat and let the fizz be roaring from the first frame, which is what Danny claims happened anyway. QUIBBLE

12. **Episodes 1 and 3 — the guards sleep through the bead floods.** The floods roar loudly enough for Liam to hear them through the bank's back wall, yet the night guards inside the flooded rooms sleep on, in both vaults. **Fix:** one beat of a guard stirring as the beads tick down around him, too late to matter — it costs nothing and closes the question. QUIBBLE

13. **Episode 10 against the epilogue — Terry's last ticket is never printed on screen.** December frames "Terry's last ticket, 70.6", and the canonical numbers date it to the night before the finale, but no scene shows or mentions the weighing. **Fix:** one shot in scene 8 or a night-before beat — Terry stepping off the plate, tearing the ticket, Peg writing the companion figure. QUIBBLE

14. **Episode 7, scene 7 against episode 10, scene 9 — the tide books' age changes.** On the ferry they are "six of them... twenty-five years of them"; at the baking tray Maggie turns the pages back thirty-one years to 1995. The oldest book cannot be both. **Fix:** make the 1995 page her father's book, or the watermen's club records. QUIBBLE

15. **Episode 10 — the finale is over-stocked rather than sagging.** A memory cold open, a replay, the loft, Ray's file, the drawer, the investigators, the night of writing, the steelyard, the tray, two intercut crossings, the vote, the station and a four-scene epilogue is a lot for 55 minutes; the vote is the season's true climax and risks being crowded, and the epilogue's four goodbyes risk being glanced. **Fix:** fold scene 3's replay into scene 2 (Rosie's face in the hatch does the cut), and trim the station scene to two beats. QUIBBLE

16. **Episode 8's plant notes promise a line the episode does not contain.** The notes say the spinner is "cut here" with "There's no such person. There never was." — no scene in episode 8 carries it. Nothing breaks, because episode 7 closed the thread at the table, but the document disagrees with itself. **Fix:** either restore the line to Tess's scene 7 speech (it would land well against "We never fed our own") or correct the note. QUIBBLE

## Critique: people

1. **Episode 9, scene 12, and episode 10, scene 7 — Danny never owns his part in Liam's death.** In 9, Peg confesses the fatal fry-up in front of him, and Danny — who spent episode 7 demanding "Did your sums do Liam too?" — has no line and no beat after "Why's it a kilo out?". In 10's night of writing, Rosie confesses her coaching, Peg has confessed her plate, Tess her margin, and Danny, the man who said "Yes" to the job on screen in episode 5, says nothing. Why it matters: the climax is a distribution of guilt, and the one owner with the loudest claim to be owed an answer stays silent, so it reads as the writers protecting him; his later softness ("Not him. Not after Liam"; "Give") arrives without its cause. Fix: in 9, after the re-chalk, one beat — Danny puts his good hand flat on the board beside 65.1 and says nothing, or leaves the room, his pattern; in 10, at the lock-up line: "He asked me. I said yes. Put that down." **SUBSTANTIVE**

2. **Episodes 6 and 7 — the pursuer vanishes from his own case.** Ray is on screen in neither episode. The spinner theory — his theory, the thing that armed Danny and framed the war — is dismantled by Tess and Maggie without his ever learning it; the episode 8 plants note even quotes a line ("There's no such person. There never was.") that no scene contains. Why it matters: Crime's promise of a plain-looking master pursuer in a long fight between equals; after episode 3's stakeout forces the long way round, Ray lands no blow and takes none, his murder theory is solved behind his back, and the man who spent five years on it is never even told he was wrong. Fix: in episode 8's flat scene, let Tess tell him ("There was never a spinner. It was time, and our own floors") and give Ray the diagnosis — five years of a murder book, and his suspects were being turned by their own concrete and fed by him. It also gives his arrival at the table in 10 a cause. **SUBSTANTIVE**

3. **Episodes 1, 9 and 10 — the Tess-and-Kieran mirror is wound and never detonated.** Episode 1 plants it exactly ("How old were you?" / "Fourteen." / "It was a different week"). The season then builds the perfect mirror — her son secretly starving to be light enough to be sent, as she was made light in her father's death week — and when she learns of it (9, scene 11) it is absorbed in a room scene answered by Peg and Terry; mother and son never exchange one word about it, and her walk into the police station (10, scene 15) happens with no parting from him at all. Why it matters: fear of loss of the loved object is the strongest engine on the page, loaded and never fired; the best parent–child story stays a symbol. Fix: in 9, clear the room for two minutes — Kieran shows her the graph and the only line she can manage is "I was fourteen"; and at the van in 10: "I'll keep the book going." / "Every night. Nine o'clock." **SUBSTANTIVE**

4. **Episode 10, scenes 8 and 14 — Joe and Danny never share a beat.** Episode 5 ends by telling us Joe "doesn't look at Danny at all" at the lock-up door. In 10, the boy whose wrist Danny gripped stands in the Marlow kitchen at 2 a.m., rigs the steelyard that weighs the man who maimed himself on him, and votes "Give" with a flat hand — and the show gives the two of them nothing, not a held look, not a word. Why it matters: the war's two injured parties are the one reckoning the genre's "paid in kind" begs for, and its absence reads as oversight rather than mercy because the avoidance was marked once already. Fix: one exchange — Joe calls "Eighty-nine dead" off the arm and steadies the board while Danny steps on, and for a beat neither looks away; or Danny, unable to knot a strap one-handed, has Joe knot it for him. **SUBSTANTIVE**

5. **Episodes 6–9 and the epilogue — the Ahern household vanishes, and the Aoife/Cian irony is left unspent.** After episode 5 the Gravesend rooms are gone for four episodes; Cian — the baby whose three days of screaming taught us what looking-glass milk is, the reason Aoife went again and died — is last seen in episode 5 and missing from the ending, and nobody on screen ever once connects Aoife to the sea that would have fed her. Why it matters: the finale's cost ("the Aherns, unchanged, cheering a child's hunger with the answer under the scale") lands on a household we last visited in April; and the saddest sentence the season has bought — Aoife starved an arm's length from a sea of food — is never felt by anyone. Fix: one Gravesend cutaway in 8 or 9 (Declan hearing the "COME ROUND FOR TEA" news; Siobhan feeding Cian); in the epilogue, Cian on Maggie's hip in the doorway while the family cheers Siobhan, her hand on his head, so her not cheering means two things. **SUBSTANTIVE**

6. **Episode 8, scene 13, and the epilogue, scene 16 — Rosie's leaving conflates her friend with the road not taken.** She walks out leaving the recruiter's card and Den's postcard standing together on the locker shelf, and the shot holds both. But Den is not a road not taken — ward work is the road she takes — and the epilogue then shows "the Fever Company card, still standing where she left it in June" in her locker at the Royal Free, a different building. Why it matters: the image built to carry her choice muddles it (she appears to reject Den's friendship with the company), and the December geography breaks for any careful viewer, forcing a re-read of the meaning. Fix: in June she pockets Den's postcard with the T-meter and leaves only the card; in December, the card stands in her new locker because she pinned it there herself. **SUBSTANTIVE**

7. **Episode 6, scene 4 — Maggie puts Joe back on the tow the night after a Marlow waited in the black and took hold of him.** "They know the tow now. They know it's me." / "I know. Half one. You're on her." — and the next drop's purpose is never stated. Why it matters: her stated want is protecting Joe; the send is flaw over want with the cost unshown and the reason invisible, so it reads as plot need (the ferry must be set up) rather than character — and it breaks the season's own pattern, in which the young volunteer (Shaun's leg, Liam's starving, Kieran's column) rather than being used. Fix: let Joe take it off her ("Who else was watching Aoife's jobs?"), one beat of her letting him, and one clause naming what the drop is for. **SUBSTANTIVE**

8. **Episode 9, scene 11 — Peg's one line of denial about Kieran.** Learning her grandson has been starving under her roof gets "He eats his tea," and the scene moves to the flour; Frank gets a confession, Liam a re-chalked number, Terry his name written, and the live victim, the one she can still fix, gets nothing until the epilogue's reformed breakfast. Why it matters: the founder's reckoning is the spine, and skipping her smallest victim lets her off between the sums and December. Fix: one beat — Peg standing under the painted rule (A FIFTH OF A SECOND A KILO UNDER A HUNDRED) and chalking nothing; or in the night of writing, beside his name in the sign-writer's hand: "Fed after." **QUIBBLE**

9. **Episodes 1–4 — Shaun loses his leg without a want.** He is wiry, due on a pour, quick with "Mind the plate!" and "I've got eight seconds": competent, present, interchangeable. Nothing of his interior is on screen before the moorings, so the war's first mutilation lands as shock-timing rather than loss, where Liam's death devastates because we had the bathroom scale and "the big lad". Fix: one beat in 1 or 2 — the cousin whose only prize is the board's top line ("Lightest man on the river, me") — so "My leg weighed five and a half... I'm worth more without it" completes a person instead of starting one. **QUIBBLE**

10. **Episode 2 — the ratchet idles for an hour.** The threats are a Friday plane and a painting that won't flip; the Twitch floods other people's basements and makes the family money. Care is being banked (Terry's year, Liam's scale, the shed across the river), but suspense runs on time alone, and the seven hops are three scenes of the same shape. Fix: sharpen one spike — let hop four's false alarm cost something (Peg nearly calls the buyer to admit the copy, and Rosie has to stop her with a lie we understand only later), or put the thin Laundry envelope on the table with a date on it. **QUIBBLE**

11. **Episode 7, scene 12 — Rosie's vow brushes the thesis.** "And when it's been counted proper, and checked against somebody else's, I'm going first." "Checked against somebody else's" is the plan's own scaffolding wording for the season's whole argument, put in a character's mouth at the table. Why it matters: the moment is earned and shown — the ferry sheet is in her hand — but the phrase says the theme in six words, and the plan's own craft note keeps such wording off screen. Fix: cut to "And when it's been counted proper, I'm going first." The sheet has already shown the "somebody else's". **QUIBBLE**

12. **Episode 9, acts three to five — too many payloads in one day.** Between 8.20 a.m. and dusk: Shaun's LO, the dog and the turned case, FOOD TURNS TOO, Terry's crab, the two meters, the jumper down the drain, Terry's decision to leave the world, the flour, the drawer, and Peg's confession of the fatal breakfast. Why it matters: the two heaviest human beats — Terry's choice and "I made him breakfast" — sit about four minutes of screen time apart, and neither gets its silence. Fix: move the crab and "I'm going, Peg" to the Thursday evening after the raid, or push Terry's decision to Mucking at dawn in 10, so episode 9 can breathe around Peg. **QUIBBLE**

13. **Episodes 5–10 — Liam's mother does not exist.** A nineteen-year-old dies and no mother grieves, is mentioned, or is informed; Rosie's mother is likewise unmentioned in a family built of mothers. Care is social, and the death scene's world shrinks to the firm; a careful viewer notices the missing mourner at the minute by the wall and at the star nobody wipes. Fix: one line — in 6, Danny into a phone, "I've rung his mum," then a long nothing; or in the night of writing, Kieran asks "Do I put Liam's mum?" and Danny says "Karen. Put her." **QUIBBLE**

14. **Episode 9 and the epilogue — the depot raid has no victim.** The Marlows rob the Register's stores — the pantry of the forty thousand, the institution feeding Den and every claimant like him — and no cost ever lands on a face; the turned porridge case is the family's irony, not the claimants' shortage, so the genre's "line crossed" for this job goes unmarked while the audience is invited to cheer. Fix: one clause in the epilogue's radio ("...the Register says the Purfleet shortfall will be made good by spring..."), or a single shot in 9 — a ward nurse counting cases onto a trolley and coming up short. **QUIBBLE**

## Critique: continuity

1. **Episode 8, scene 6 (Ray's flat) — Ray's boast contradicts his own ledger.** Ray: "The seventh of April, it goes to two… I knew there was another mouth in that house before you did." The family learned Rosie was turned at the meter in the small hours of Saturday 4 April (episode 3, scenes 15–17; episode 4's cold open is "ten minutes after"), and the doubling is dated Tuesday 7 April — the first delivery day *after* Peg learned. So the ledger told Ray three days later than the family, and the doubling is a consequence of their discovery, not a lead on it. This is Ray's showcase beat (the quiet hoarded knowledge that is his flaw), and the season's own dates break it. Fix: date the doubling to the first Tuesday after Kieran's birthday, when Rosie's night visits began to stretch Terry's tubs (which also explains why the Laundry envelope is already thin in episode 2), making the boast true; or keep 7 April and change the line to "I knew what a doubled order was the week your family found out — and I said nothing." SUBSTANTIVE

2. **Episode 9, scenes 5–7 against episode 10, scene 6 — the raid's clock versus the dawn return.** The investigators date every Purfleet crossing "between midnight and one," and episode 9's intercut ends with Peg saying "Once more, son. Then we're away" before scene 6 (the office, 1:10 a.m.); yet the van rolls onto the big bridge at dawn (~4:30 in June). Three unexplained hours sit between the last crossing and the twenty-minute drive home, against the family's established straight-home habit (episode 5). The show has trained its audience to compare times, and this gap is visible. Fix: one line — the crew sits out the gatehouse shift change, or Terry's legs are spaced to the six-minute floodlight sweep Peg counted. QUIBBLE

3. **Episodes 4–5 — the bullet's ten days in a hand-raided safe.** The bullet is delivered ~02:04 on Saturday 11 April and found Tuesday 21 April. He's to put his hand in the safe for the money" — so on Laundry Tuesday the 14th Danny's blind hand went in beside a bullet standing upright on a painted pan. 4. **Episode 10, scenes 2 and 4 — Frank's 64.4 against the wall's 64.3.** Peg's ticket prints 64.4 (scene 2); Ray's strip reads 64.3 (scene 4). Both numbers go on screen in one episode with nobody doing the season's signature move of comparing them. A hundred grams is easily covered (weighed at dawn, crossed hours later; breath and sweat), but a show that hangs Liam on half a kilo and two hundredths of a second leaves its second-most-important body with an unremarked discrepancy a trained audience will catch. Fix: one line from Ray — "Sixty-four three. A breath lighter than your ticket" — or make the numbers match. QUIBBLE

Danny is alive, local and her father; no scene shows Rosie naming Tess at admission, and a ward would ring him first. It conveniently delivers Tess back into the plot for the finale. TESS: Aye."; scene 13, "RAY: You'll be wanting the rest of it. TESS: Aye."), and Rosie, whose interjections are "Oi" and "Go on, then," says "Aye" in scene 6. A careful viewer loses track of when the suspended policeman re-entered the fug. 8. **Epilogue, scene 18 — Terry's "last ticket — 70.6" was never shown printing.** His only on-screen weigh in episode 10 is on the steelyard (71.4, scene 8); the framed 70.6 little-bridge ticket Peg hangs beside Frank's photo in December is invented between episodes, though the whole ending trades in objects the audience saw made. ## Critique: mimo


**1. Episode 3, Scene 10 ("Tests That Pass") / Episode 7, Scene 9 ("The Graph") — The pencil contradicts itself.**

In Episode 3 Tess "takes the pencil from behind her ear" and hands it to Rosie, who writes with it twice — "Plain as a shop sign" and then in flowing mirror script — before tucking it back behind Tess's ear. In Episode 7 Tess touches "the pencil that has lived there for twenty years and never once come down," and the text adds, "It has never been sharpened." Two flat claims — *never once come down* and *never been sharpened* — are directly contradicted by the Episode 3 scene, where the pencil physically left her ear and produced legible writing. A careful viewer who remembers the writing test will catch it.

*Fix:* In Episode 3, have Tess hand Rosie a different pencil (off the counter, a spare in the drawer) so the one behind her ear is never touched. In Episode 7 you can then keep both lines. Alternatively, change Episode 7 to "never once come down for me" and cut "It has never been sharpened."

**Mark: SUBSTANTIVE.**

---

**2. Episode 6, Cold Open — Danny's 2.2-second crossing carries more beats than the clock allows.**

Danny's true budget is 2.2 seconds (89.0 kg). The tingle starts about two seconds before the field fails, which puts the fizz at roughly 0.2 seconds into the crossing. Yet the cold open gives him several dramatic beats before that moment: entering the black, finding Joe's ribs and heart, reading the white envelope through the safe's steel, raising his hand, then the tingle, then the grab, then home. At 0.2 seconds all of that is effectively instantaneous. The scene works as filmmaking (the black has no felt time), but a viewer who does the arithmetic — and this season rewards viewers who do — will notice the compressed clock.

*Fix:* Widen Danny's budget slightly by having him shed mass before stepping in (boots and coat are already off; have him drop the belt pouch or the stopwatch as well, buying a few tenths), or extend the tingle rule to "about two seconds before the field fails, or the first third of the crossing, whichever is later" in the show's own ledger — declared once.

**Mark: QUIBBLE.**

---

**3. Episode 8, Scenes 10–13 — The two-week time jump inside a single episode is abrupt.**

Scene 10 puts Rosie on the ward, grey and starving. Scene 12 jumps to "Wednesday 10 June, mid-morning" — two weeks later — with Rosie at 46.2 kg, carrying trays, and changing Den's dressings. The episode spends only a few lines bridging the gap. The emotional beat (her growing strength and purpose) is compressed into a montage feel that undercuts the weight of her earlier hunger. A viewer may feel she was healed by the edit rather than by the ward.

*Fix:* Insert one short scene in between — Rosie at four in the morning eating alone in the ward kitchen, or a brief morning-meter sequence showing the number climbing — to mark the passage and earn her recovery.

**Mark: QUIBBLE.**

---

**4. Episode 10, Cold Open ("as Peg remembers it") — The blinds are older than the building.**

The scene is set in 2002 and reads "Golden light through blinds that haven't been cleaned in a decade." But the gravel pit and weighbridge office were acquired in 1995, seven years earlier. A decade-old neglect of blinds would pre-date the office itself.

*Fix:* Change "a decade" to "since the year dot" or "seven years," or make the blinds an inherited fitting from the farmhouse ("blinds that came with the place").

**Mark: QUIBBLE.**

---

**5. Episode 10, Scene 15 (Grays police station) — Rosie wears a rig the audience knows isn't hers.**

The desk sergeant unclips "her rig" from Rosie's belt. But Rosie's own rig was confiscated by Ray on the A13 in Episode 5 and is in a police evidence store. The rig she wears through Episodes 9–10 is Tess's old one with the gaffer-taped strap. The scene reads as though she is surrendering her own property; a viewer keeping track may wonder how she got it back.

*Fix:* Add a line from the sergeant — "This the one off the Marlow rack?" or simply "Whose rig?" / "My aunt's." — or show Rosie handing over the gaffer-taped strap so the visual confirms it.

**Mark: QUIBBLE.**

---

**6. Episode 5, Scene 13 (the weigh-in) and Episode 6, Cold Open — Tess's margin speech and Peg's margin speech sit on opposite sides of the same principle without the tension landing.**

Tess says "Near enough isn't" and gives Liam two tenths. Peg says "A tenth was always plenty." The drama of the scene depends on the audience sensing that both are wrong in different ways (the margin is real but the inputs are false). On the page the speeches are clear; on screen the two statements may blur into general fussing over tenths rather than dramatizing the family's rival relationships to numbers.

*Fix:* Give Peg one line that makes her philosophy concrete and reckless — e.g. "Your grandfather ran on a tenth and a prayer" — so the contrast with Tess's "Margins are for people who can't count" is a clash of worldviews, not two people saying similar things about numbers.

**7. Episode 7, Scene 8 (Kieran alone) — His 38.6 reading is dramatic but the audience has no scene anchor for the date.**

Kieran weighs himself at "five past eleven" on the ferry night. The second-column numbers have been appearing across episodes, and this one is important (it shows the continued fall while Tess is away). But the scene gives no date caption, no external event, and no comparison. A viewer may not realise this is Thursday 21 May specifically, or that it represents continued deterioration. The moment risks becoming wallpaper.

*Fix:* Tie it to something the audience can date — have Kieran glance at the ferry timetable pinned near his graph (the same sheet his mother is writing on across the river), or show him writing the date in his log before the weight.

**8. Episode 4, Scene 2 — Peg wipes Rosie's column but the "10.0" job-time on the chalk board is indistinguishable from a weight.**

The board's purpose is "names and weights," yet Peg chalked "10.0" (the Marrable Street job time) beside Rosie's star in Episode 3. When Peg wipes the board in Episode 4, the audience may not register that a job record is being erased along with the weights. Later, in Episode 10, the board's contents matter (Peg wipes it before the investigators come). If the audience can't tell weight-chalk from job-chalk, the erasure in Scene 2 of Episode 4 loses some force.

*Fix:* In Episode 3 have Peg chalk "10.0 s" (with a small "s" or the word "job") to distinguish it; or in Episode 4 have a character say "the job an' all" as Peg wipes.

**9. Episode 2, Scene 10 (the roll "joke") — The false alarm and the roll joke share the same scene and may dilute each other.**

Hop four's false alarm (headlights on the blind, the printer chattering) is a strong beat that puts police tension into the room. But it is immediately followed by Rosie's "New roll's printed backwards, Nan" / "Joke" exchange, which is a quiet plant for her turning. Two separate misdirections in quick succession — one for plot (police), one for character (Rosie) — may cancel each other out: the audience is busy processing the alarm and misses the joke's real meaning.

*Fix:* Move the roll joke to a different hop (hop five or six), or trim the false alarm's aftermath so the joke lands in a quieter beat.

**10. Episode 9, Scene 7 — "Bad luck" is Peg's only response to the turned porridge case, and it may read as too slight for the moment.**

When Kieran proves the sealed case came back the wrong hand — two weeks of food wasted, and the proof that food turns with its carrier — Peg says only "Bad luck" and goes to put the kettle on. It is her character (certainty, deflecting), and the scene moves on quickly. But this is the family learning a fundamental rule of the world (Guess 3, the cargo rule), and it also explains why they can never stockpile mirror food by carrying it. The weight of the discovery may need one more beat.

*Fix:* Before "Bad luck," let Peg look at the case for two seconds longer — or have Danny say "So we can't ever carry their food home" and Peg answer "Bad luck" to *that*, which makes the line a refusal to face a conclusion rather than a shrug at a minor loss.

**Mark: QUIBBLE.**
