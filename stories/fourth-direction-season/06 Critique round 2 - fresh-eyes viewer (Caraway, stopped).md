# Round 2 critique: a fresh-eyes viewer

**Lens.** This reads the plan as an ordinary viewer would meet it: in order, one episode at a time, without having read sections 1–3. It asks where that viewer would be confused, overloaded or bored, and which reveals would land as "wow, of course", which as "wait, what?", and which as "I saw that coming". Round 1 findings that were already fixed are not repeated.

**Verdict.** The season really is mind-bending. Several reveals will land as "wow, of course", and these should be kept exactly as they are:
- thirteen answering eleven;
- 91;
- the password 2-3-5-7;
- the stole's silver tooth fitting the port;
- "He said 'Me'";
- the Litany turning out to be an assembly order;
- the Randall tasting caraway in public;
- the founders laughing at the liturgy.

The risk is not that there are too few twists. It is that the show keeps handing the viewer answers several episodes before the characters find them, and then still counts those moments as twists. About half the twist table will be known to an attentive viewer before it plays. Episodes 7 and 10 currently have almost no reveal the viewer doesn't already hold. Overload peaks in three places: episode 1 (invented words), episode 7 (a whole second world) and episode 9 (too many events).

Four reveals could hit harder without adding any rule:
- The shoulder-tap words are heard for the first time in *Day One* (finding 2).
- An opening epigraph in episode 1 lets the show itself make the Order's mistake (finding 2).
- The finale's 4-in-100 becomes a real shock (finding 3).
- Kit's identity is staged as one reveal (finding 9).

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The episode 6 end card gives away episodes 7 and 8

**Where.** Section 2.7. The credits card after episode 6 lists stipulations 1–7 and 9 "in everyday words".

**Problem.** In everyday words, stipulation 1 says: whatever runs out of time in the dark stays beside the door, still heavy. Stipulation 2 says: a heavy mass near the door makes crossers more likely to turn, and more likely to be dragged off and lost. Those two sentences answer the season's central mystery ("Why is the rate rising?", answered in episode 8) and give away episode 7's horror (the Received are hanging in the dark). The viewer reads them on a caption one episode before Nell finds them.

**Why it matters.**
- Episode 8's "the loving rites are the doom engine" and episode 7's "Received meant lost" become confirmations of a card.
- The audience learns the cause from the show's own narrator instead of watching someone discover it. The brief says "characters discover".

**Fix.** Split the card.
- After episode 6, list only the rules already in play: the tug of home (3), the base drift (4), the First Leaf (5), the ring's reach (6), what turns as one (7), the working number (9), and the founders (8, short form).
- Hold 1 and 2 for a second card at the end of episode 8, after the needle walk and the cellar wall. Title it "…and two more", which are Wells's words in the dark.
- Fair play still holds, because episodes 1–8 show the evidence: Wells's cliff, Mira dragged off, the needles weighing the Given, the cards, and the two records rising together.

### 2. Ream's own words tell the viewer in episode 1 that the Leaves are a phone page about a story, which breaks the meta device

**Where.** The fossil table in 1.3, and episode 1:
- every reading ends "Written for a phone screen";
- the section that holds the handhold verse is called "the Plot". The Randall says "The Plot needs none. It is what will happen" (act 2), and Pim says "The Plot says…" (act 3);
- the shoulder blessing is "Tap the stack at the top right";
- the funeral benediction speaks of "animations" and "reduced motion";
- the Accountants have "the Method of Nolan".

**Problem.** Every viewer knows what a phone screen, a plot, a tap, an animation and reduced motion are. Once a priest calls a verse "the Plot" and ends the reading with "written for a phone screen", no viewer will take that verse as the show's physics.

**Why it matters.** The brief's meta device needs the audience to learn the handhold verse as law in episode 1, then find out in episode 6 that it was a scene from a film. As planned, the viewer is winking at the scripture from minute one. The episode 6 relic then confirms what they suspected instead of turning it over, and "the audience made the city's mistake" never happens.

**Fix.** No new rules, and one rename.
- The Order calls the undotted section **"the Foretellings"**. Only the relic in episode 6 shows the original heading, "In the plot", and that becomes the moment it clicks.
- In episodes 1–5, keep only the fossils that name no technology: "two, three, five, seven"; "the ending writes itself"; "the drift is fifty"; "nobody fakes a mountain"; "paper is a witness"; "the dog knows".
- The shoulder tap stays a silent gesture all season, and its words are first heard in *Day One* (episode 9), laughed aloud by the founders. The viewer has watched that gesture dozens of times and learns what it means in that moment. This hits harder than the current plan.
- "Written for a phone screen" and the animations benediction are first heard at the episode 8 Autumn Giving, after the relic. There they play as grim comedy and set up the laughter in *Day One*. Drop "Nolan" from dialogue.
- *Optional, to make the device bite.* Open episode 1 with an on-screen epigraph of the handhold verse, credited "The Leaves", set in the same type as the episode 6 ledger card. When the episode 6 card appears without it, the show has made the Order's mistake, and corrected it, in front of the viewer.

### 3. Major reversal 3 reverses nothing for the viewer

**Where.**
- Episode 8, Minthome thread: Kit writes in a book at the Mother's needle and hides it.
- Episode 9, Minthome thread: Kit says "Tell them the new place is dirty too… Ours is a handful beside theirs", and Wells says "They'll be standing on our dead".
- Episode 10: cold open, act 1 and act 5.

**Problem.** The viewer already holds all three reversals of the finale.
- They are told what hangs beside the new door (episode 9, said aloud), so 4-not-2 is expected.
- They watched Minthome Give its dead (episode 8).
- Round 1 made Kit's secret count the finale's surprise, but round 1's own plants give it away. He hides a book at the Mother's needle (episode 8), then openly compares the sizes of the two piles (episode 9), which only a man who has measured them could do.

**Why it matters.** The owner asked for mind-bending, and the brief asks for a major reversal in episode 10. As planned, the finale is all suspense and payoff. There is no moment where the viewer's picture of the world turns over.

**Fix.**
- Move Kit's argument and Wells's line out of episode 9 and into episode 10, act 3, which is already a flashback ("earlier that morning").
- In episode 9, Kit's fear stays visible but says nothing specific: "Send them the door and nothing else. Words kill over there." That still meets the round 1 requirement that his silence be seen.
- In episode 8, show Kit writing at the old needle as a habit, not hiding the book. The viewer already knows he "keeps the tally" of arrivals.
- In episode 10, this plays out in four beats:
  - 41 in 1,000 shocks ordinary viewers, and it is fair to attentive ones: episode 7 put the Mother under the Graves, and episode 8 showed a Giving into it.
  - WE. DIFFUSE. HERE. TOO. is the "of course".
  - The flashback explains Kit's silence.
  - His book in the last scene recasts a habit we watched without suspicion.
- This gives up the cold open's suspense of watching her write the prediction, but a surprise the whole finale turns on is worth more.
- Also cut the carved UNTIL WE KNOW BETTER. It states the theme, and Lottie's chalked THE GREY? already shows it.

### 4. In the first ten minutes, the hook shows a loss before it shows the normal, and act 1 is a tour with no clock

**Where.** Episode 1, cold open and act 1.

**Problem.**
- The cold open never shows anyone come back, so the viewer can't tell that Mira's vanishing breaks the rule. The Warden's "Ten. Fourteen. Sixteen." sounds like a slip of the tongue.
- After the cold open, act 1 makes seven stops of world texture: the catechism game, the dash, the weighing, the gloves, Nan's rope and tale, the prime race, the Randall and the dog. The twins cross within hours, and the viewer has just watched a girl vanish, yet nobody in act 1 is afraid of today and nothing in the act has a goal.
- We never learn why Nell would lie to avoid being sent to a paradise.

**Why it matters.** The owner's Anticipation theory says to show the rules, then hide the thing: suspense is care × threat × uncertainty × time. The threat and the time are both on screen, but for ten minutes nothing uses them.

**Fix.**
- **Cold open.** A pair pops back first: gasping, laughing, mint, applause. It takes ten seconds. Then Mira goes in. The Warden counts steadily up to her number, sixteen. The ring is empty. "Favour."
- **Act 1 becomes the twins' last afternoon** before "this day's last six pairs".
  - Nell counts the CALLED lines on the board and asks the Tally Master how many come back. "The rate is the Ancestors'." The dash is now her fear, not a sight on a tour.
  - Every beat at home becomes part of saying goodbye before the crossing.
- **One line plants the lie.** During the prime race: "Has anyone ever come back from Minthome to say it's nice?" "Shh, Nell." In one move it seeds her doubt, Wells's shushing and Nan's Pariah tale.

### 5. Viewers will know the Randall is Turned at the end of episode 1, not in episode 5

**Where.**
- Episode 1, act 1: Plattner growls at the Randall.
- Episode 1 hook: Plattner growls at Nell, and we know she is Turned.
- Episode 2: the kennel scene points at both women, and bread that fits Nell appears.
- Episode 4: the Sundrum fumbles the lid and she doesn't. Her private book reads our way, which is exactly the sign episode 1 taught us means Turned.

The plan still counts this as episode 5's twist, and lists "Why does she write our way?" as an open question in episode 4.

**Problem.**
- The dog's rule is taught and applied to both women within one episode.
- By episode 2 the viewer can also guess who is feeding Nell: who else would have food that fits a Turned body?
- By episode 4 it is certain.

**Why it matters.** Episodes 2–5 are written as if the viewer is level with Nell or behind her. In fact the viewer is ahead, and a reveal held for four episodes lands as "I saw that coming".

**Fix.** Own it; the result is better anyway.
- Give the growl in the hook the same sound as the growl in act 1, so the viewer gets a jolt in the last minute of episode 1.
- From then on, play every scene between Nell and the Randall in episodes 2–4 as dread: we know she knows.
- Episode 5's twist then rests on what nobody can guess: every Randall for generations has been Turned, the table is the leash, and Burnt Row was her own body.
- Update the plan to match:
  - knowledge map: audience ½ in episode 1, ✔ in episode 4;
  - mystery list: drop "Why does she write our way?" and "Why does the dog growl at the Randall?";
  - "Who feeds her?" becomes a question the audience is ahead on.

### 6. Episode 1 still carries about fifty-five invented words, a dozen of them at Wells's goodbye

**Where.** Episode 1, especially act 4.

**Problem.**
- Round 1 cut the names to eight but not the invented words.
- Act 4 alone introduces all of these: the gates barred "until the needle settles"; the Answering; the amber dot; "the holiest voice"; "the neighbour's life is the other hand"; "the ending writes itself"; "Go toward the Ancestors"; Passed; RECEIVED; REFUSED; "walk as a Pariah"; and the Pariah law.
- Act 2 adds "That verse has no voice… The Plot needs none", which relies on a colour code nobody has explained.

**Why it matters.** Act 4 is the emotional peak: Wells leaving. Every new word there takes attention away from the twins.

**Fix.**
- **Cut the colour code from episode 1.**
  - "Is it true?" "It is in the Leaves, child. It is what will happen."
  - At the Passing, show a close-up of an amber dot, unnamed.
  - Name the dots in episode 2's archive scene, where "St." gives them a reason to matter.
- **Cut "barred until the needle settles" from episode 1.** Plant it at the episode 4 Joining, where it already is, and again at the start of the episode 8 Autumn Giving.
- **Keep the Others boy as an image,** but let the word "unweighed" arrive in episode 2 with the shunning.
- **In act 4, keep only what the season needs from that night:** the gloves, the gloss "Go toward the Ancestors", RECEIVED and REFUSED, and the Pariah law.

Aim for fewer than thirty new words in episode 1, and no more than six in act 4.

### 7. Episode 7 dumps a second world on the viewer and buries its one real surprise

**Where.** Episode 7, acts 1, 4 and 5.

**Problem.**
- About twelve new names and places arrive at once: Minthome as a place, the Landing, the Answering House, the station, the Mother, the Long House, "Straight", CHIRAL HOLD, PANTRY 3, MIRROR-LIFE CONTAINMENT and the fence. On top of those come two replayed nights, the 89 plan, the pair test, the long haul, the Weight-book and the Givings.
- Episodes 9 and 10 depend on a map of the two worlds: Ream's Temple sits over Minthome's Answering House, and Ream's Old Graves sit over the Mother. The plan says this once and never shows it.
- The episode's billed climax, "Received meant lost", can already be worked out:
  - episode 1 shows Mira's Calling, the believers' RECEIVED traces and the Giving all as the same rise that stays;
  - in episode 3, Theta says the labels were re-inked.
- The pair test answers a question that episode 6's "He said 'Me'" has already made moot.
- The one reveal the viewer can't already hold is a single bullet in the middle of act 1: we can read Minthome's sign, and Wells can't.

**Why it matters.** The episode that makes the viewer work hardest has the least surprise, and the map they will need for the finale is never drawn.

**Fix.**
- **Merge the places.** The Landing is simply "the Answering House", and the station is simply "the Mother".
- **Use one sign first:** MIRROR-LIFE CONTAINMENT: NO EXIT. It is readable, it is ominous, and it pays off in episode 10. CHIRAL HOLD and PANTRY 3 can stay in the background.
- **Teach the map with a match cut,** reused every time the show crosses: the Temple floor dissolves to the Answering House yard at the same spot, and the Old Graves dissolve to the Mother. By episode 9 nobody needs it explained.
- **End act 1 on the readable sign.** Hold on Wells squinting while we read it.
- **Play the Weight-book as dread, not as a twist.** Keep the camera on Nell as she turns to her father's page, then Nan's, then cut to the two believers' families at their shrine.
- **Compress the pair test** to a sixty-second montage.

### 8. Too many names for one person, and too many words for what becomes of a person

**Where.** Sections 1.5 and 4; episodes 2, 3, 7 and 8.

**Problem.**
- One woman is "Warden Dvali" (episode 2), "Theta" (episode 3) and "Mother Dvali" (episode 7).
  - Her given name, Ada, nearly matches "Adamala".
  - "Mother Adamala keeps the Mother." That makes three Mothers in one story, besides the two real mothers.
- There are eleven on-screen words for what becomes of a person: Turned, Passed, Called, Given, Joined, Received, Refused, Pariah, Straight, unweighed, and the Weight (or the Ancestors).
  - "The Ending" means both the Passing rite (1.4) and the promised day of fifty in a hundred (episode 4: "The Ending comes!").
  - "Weight-makers" (Wells's lock guild) shares its word with the Weight.
- "Straight" has a strong everyday meaning that will pull viewers out of the scene, and its hidden meaning ("not mirrored") won't come across.

**Why it matters.** Viewers track each person by one handle and each fate by a clear contrast. Near-synonyms blur exactly the distinctions the reveals in episodes 7 and 9 depend on: Received against Refused, Called against Passed.

**Fix.**
- Theta is "Warden Dvali" in public and "Theta" in the cellar, never "Mother". Drop "Ada".
- Adamala becomes "Keeper Adamala", so "the Mother" means only the old Lift.
- Stop using "the Ending" as a name for the Passing.
- Replace "Straight" with a plain Minthome phrase, such as "another starver".
- Cut "unweighed"; reason 6 in 1.8 works without the word.
- Call the guild "the lock-makers".

That leaves about seven words for what becomes of a person.

### 9. The season's best "of course" is never staged, and the lore it rests on arrives unplanted

The best "of course" is that the old man in Minthome is Nan's Pariah and the Randall's twin.

**Where.**
- Episode 1: Nan's tale.
- Episode 5 hook: "Ream lore says that when the Ancestors spell a name, that one is Called." This is the lore's first mention.
- Episode 7: Kit says "Eleven? Seven." and says he "spelled CARROLL to reach his sister".
- Episode 8, act 5: the Randall tells of her Tithe, "and Kit, who held her hand".
- It is in neither the twist table nor the knowledge map.

**Problem.**
- The pieces are all there, but they leak out one at a time. The moment the two worlds' stories become one family's story is a clause in a list.
- Separately, the spelled-name lore is introduced in the same breath it is used to threaten the Abbotts, so it plays as "wait, what?".

**Why it matters.** This is the emotional hinge between the two worlds, and it costs nothing: no new rule.

**Fix.**
- **Episode 4.** Nan at the Joining, folding the robe: "The last time the Ancestors asked for someone by name, I was a bride. Old Hanna Carroll went in singing." One line plants the lore, the Joining's precedent and the name.
- **Episode 7.**
  - Keep "Eleven? Seven.": a small click that Kit is Nan's Pariah.
  - Kit says, "I sent a name once. They Joined an old woman for it." An attentive viewer links this to Nan's Hanna.
  - "To reach his sister" can stay. It opens the question "Who is his sister?".
- **Episode 8, the cell.**
  - The Randall ends the story of her Tithe with "His name was Kit." Hard cut to Kit at the Mother's needle.
  - Add this to the twist table (an L twist) and to the mystery table (raised in 7, answered in 8).
  - Episode 9's "Pass him" then lands on a face the viewer knows three ways.

### 10. Episode 9 is still overloaded; here are concrete cuts

**Where.** Episode 9, flagged in the revision log's "Still unresolved" list.

**Problem.** The episode holds all of this:
- a cold open;
- the plates and Year 9;
- the Graves over the Mother;
- the 62;
- the new gloss;
- the Randall's turn, split across three scenes: she reads Ottilie's entry alone, chalks the number at night, and tastes at dawn;
- the board;
- the keys, with a new obstacle ("they tried in 188");
- the Litany;
- the ballast;
- the Minthome thread;
- *Day One*;
- the carrying.

**Why it matters.** *Day One*, the season's quietest image, gets squeezed. The Randall's turn is spread across three small moments instead of one big one.

**Fix.**
- **Merge the Randall's three scenes into one,** at dawn, in front of the city. She reads Ottilie's entry aloud at the board, chalks 24 IN 100 in her own hand, opens the Jar without a thought, and says "Caraway". One scene, and the biggest public turn of the season.
- **Cut Jonah's "they tried in 188".** It is an obstacle raised and solved in the same act. Bea's place on the list is enough to turn his key. Pim's Litany answers the real problem: taking 212 plates apart and rebuilding them in one night.
- **Shrink act 1** to "1 of 212" and the Year 9 log line. The viewer has known since episode 7 that the Mother lies under the Graves and that Pariahs come out of it cured. Nell catching up needs one line, not a scene.

This frees about eight minutes, so Pim's scene does not need to move to episode 8.

### 11. "Minthome, Year One" gives away episode 9's answer in episode 2, and the heroine ignores it

**Where.** Episode 2, act 3: "The first ledger's first page is headed *Minthome, Year One*."

**Problem.** A viewer who reads it knows, seven episodes early, that Ream was once called Minthome. Nell, the curious one, doesn't react, which looks like a writing slip.

**Why it matters.** It spends part of the *Day One* reveal early and makes the heroine look slow.

**Fix.** Choose one of two:
- The heading is in the founders' hand, which reads Ream's way, and it stays unsubtitled, with the camera on the "2 in 100" instead. It becomes a reward for a second viewing.
- Nell notices ("Why would the founders call us Minthome?"), and the question joins the mystery table, answered in episode 9.

The first is simpler.

### 12. Episode 8, act 1, is a lecture

**Where.** Episode 8, act 1. Seven bullet points, "each something the audience has already seen", told to Tau in a cellar.

**Problem.** The heroine recaps seven known pieces to a listener, in the season's most eventful episode.

**Why it matters.** The brief says: "Nobody lectures; characters discover." The viewer already did the discovering during the needle walk.

**Fix.** Two minutes, built on conflict.
- Tau, grieving: "Mira was the lightest girl in her year."
- Nell: "So she was longest beside it."
- She chalks the needle-walk numbers and the two rising records side by side, says nothing, and writes her prediction.
- Tau stops chalking MIRA.
- Go straight to the Autumn Giving.

### 13. Theta's betrayal has no cause on screen

**Where.** Episode 8, act 2. The deal appears only in a bracket: the 89 hauls show up on the drum, a guard sees Tau at dawn, and Theta trades Nell for his pardon.

**Problem.**
- The viewer sees Theta look away but never sees Tau in danger, so her act looks arbitrary.
- The question "Who betrayed Nell?" is closed in episode 8, yet it was never opened.

**Why it matters.** It is a big turn for a principal. Without a cause it reads as plot convenience, and Theta loses the care the season built.

**Fix.**
- End episode 7's long haul with one shot: Wardens take Tau at the Temple door at dawn.
- Early in episode 8, Theta goes into Dimopoulos's room, and the door shuts.
- The audience is still behind, but primed, so the moment he takes the Jar clicks.

### 14. Too many numbers, in too many forms

**Where.** Across the season, especially episodes 2, 5, 9 and 10.

**Problem.** The same kind of fact appears as "in 100", "1 in 5", "nearly 2 in 5", "one in four / thirty / fifty", "41 in 1,000" and "45%", among some thirty different figures. The viewer has to convert between them to know whether the news is good or bad.

**Why it matters.** The numbers are the heroine's voice and the season's evidence. A viewer who can't compare them can't feel the turns: 24, then 62, then 31, then 4.

**Fix.**
- Show every rate on screen "in 100":
  - the episode 5 crates become 20 in 100 and 40 in 100;
  - the episode 10 board reads "about 4 in 100", and 41 in 1,000 appears only in Pim's notebook.
- Keep Arkani's "one in four… thirty… fifty" as speech. It belongs to his character.
- Use the Tally board as the one scoreboard, updated on camera: 2 (Year One), 24 (now), 50 (the Ending), 62 (Pim), 31 (the old door), 4 (the new door).

### 15. The second half of episode 6 is two acts of reading a screen

**Where.** Episode 6, acts 3 and 4.

**Problem.** About ten revelations arrive as text on a screen:
- the three tags;
- "St.";
- "In the plot";
- the cube paragraph;
- the Sources;
- the slider;
- the simulator;
- the line about statisticians;
- the Tithe's purpose;
- *Day One*.

Only the slider is something anyone touches.

**Why it matters.** After the best set piece of the season, the hand in the stone, the energy drops to reading aloud, and some viewers will get lost in the definitions of the tags.

**Fix.**
- Put a visible clock on it from act 2: "Bell's at the half hour", with Owen watching the port.
- Cross-cut with the Randall finding the empty hook where her stole should hang and walking toward the Temple, so her arrival in the hook is the clock running out.
- Cut the Sources beat, or move it to episode 10, when Nell leaves Tau her notebooks. The surname joke is the one piece the reveal doesn't need.
