# 54 Fable's single review of the mended season

*Fable 5.1, at extra-high effort, gave one review of the season after the last mends (file 53), and did nothing else. Spoilers throughout.*

---

*Read from the page, start to finish, against the brief (`00-brief-2.md`), the second owner feedback (`05-owner-feedback-2.md`), the notes on The Catch, and plan version 3. I read a script, not a film, so anything I say about pace is a judgement from the page. Every claim below is checked against the text and quoted; scene numbers are the ones in the document.*

A few words I use, each explained once. A **plant** is a small detail put in early so that a later surprise is fair. A **re-read** is the moment a surprise makes you look back at earlier scenes and see them differently. The **cold open** is the scene before the title. The **drift** is the show's own word for the odds of coming back mirror-turned. The **cargo rule** is the show's rule that whatever you carry through the black turns with you, or doesn't, exactly as you do.

---

## 1. Verdict

SECONDS is a ten-episode crime drama about an Essex family of thieves who can step through walls for a few seconds at a time, whose pride is that they always come back the right way round, and whose every rule was once right and is now quietly killing them. It is finished. All ten episodes are on the page, the voices hold from the first episode to the last, the sums add up (I checked them), the dates line up, the rigs, keys, tickets and letters are all where the story says they are, and every big surprise is planted before it lands. It does what the owner asked: the message is never said, the laws of the world are all used and none is broken, nothing from The Catch is reused, no banned word reaches the screen, and the wider world is ordinary and kind.

I found two things a careful viewer would notice and that weaken the season. One is real and needs a small scene: the family sends Tess to steal the cure's secret, she comes home with it, and for a fortnight nobody in the house asks what it says. The other is a one-word slip about whether a dead man is alive. Everything else is a matter of taste, timing, or something only an audience in a room can settle. After those two fixes I would call this ready.

## 2. How it keeps the owner's rules

### The message is shown, never said

Nobody in the season says that not asking questions kills people. The doom is built out of habits the family is proud of, each of which was once right:

- **"Nothing on paper."** Tickets are burned at the birthday ("a Marlow's ticket never leaves this room") while "Inside the printer the audit roll winds on, keeping its own copy, as it has every night for twenty-five years" (Ep 1 Sc 13). Twenty-five years later the loft is read for the first time: "It's on the paper, Terry. It was on the paper the whole time" (Ep 7 Sc 4).
- **"Make weight."** A starving girl is cheered for getting thin, four weigh-ins in a row, and the star gets bigger each time (Ep 1 Sc 13, Ep 2 Sc 4, Ep 3 Sc 1). Then Liam, who fed his breakfast to the dog, gets "Clean plate, Li. That's a Marlow" (Ep 4 Sc 12).
- **"Never go back twice."** Liam says "Could've gone in twice" in Ep 1 and the table chants him down; Tess works out in Ep 6 that Mayfair was "Twice. Four and four" and her niece would never have turned.
- **The drawer.** Three letters that would have saved three people go into it unopened (Ep 1 Sc 9, Ep 4 Sc 11, Ep 10 Sc 5). The Aherns' version is a stove: Maggie fills in a Register form for her dying uncle and "Declan reaches down the table, picks up the form, opens the stove door and posts it in" (Ep 4 Sc 8).
- **The way a family explains a death.** "Vic come off his food in the winter of '09 and died of it in the March, and the family called it his heart, because that's kinder than the other thing" (Ep 7 Sc 4).

The "always" is earned by Frank: the founder was already dying of the code twenty-four years before the season starts, and his death became the lesson that killed his grandson. And the Aherns end unchanged, cheering a thirteen-year-old on a bathroom scale with the answer folded under it (epilogue Sc 18).

Two lines come close to saying the moral out loud. Terry's "Nobody ever asked why" (Ep 7 Sc 4) and Rosie's "when it's been counted proper, and checked against somebody else's, I'm going first" (Ep 7 Sc 12). Both are about a particular case and neither is a speech, so they pass, but I note them under quibbles.

### Every invented law is used and none is broken

I checked the arithmetic and the rules scene by scene. Some examples:

- **Budget.** "Forty-one eight. Two for the tray. Eleven two. Job's ten" (Ep 3 Sc 9): 41.8 + 2 = 43.8, and (100 − 43.8) ÷ 5 = 11.24. Right. Liam: 62.4 + 1.5 = 63.9 on the lying scale; 63.9 ÷ 0.989 = 64.6 true; plus half a kilo of breakfast = 65.1; (100 − 65.1) ÷ 5 = 6.98 against a 7.0 job (Ep 9 Sc 12). Right, and it is the same 65.1 the enemy's wall printed in Ep 5. The tingle comes two seconds before the end every time, and on the true budget, not the chalk one: Shaun's wrist reads 6.9 for a true 8.9 (Ep 7 Sc 11, Ep 9 Sc 5); Ray's reads 6.3 for a true 8.3 (Ep 10 Sc 12).
- **The lying scale and the honest instruments.** The little bridge reads 1.1 per cent light from 4 April: 20.00 becomes 19.78; a 5-kilo bag reads 4.95 while the brass balance says five exactly (Ep 9 Sc 11); 61.0 on the bridge, 61.7 on the clinic's wall; 58.0 on the bridge, 58.6 on Maggie's steelyard. A body in the black over the plate pulls it up and prints its true mass as a minus (−59.0 for Joe, −46.2 for Rosie, −58.6 for Ray), so "the only true numbers it's printed since April" are "of people nobody could see" (Ep 9 Sc 11). All consistent.
- **The drift table** is stated once, in Ep 6 Sc 7, word for word from the plan, and every outcome sits inside it: seven three-second hops fail (1 in 5,000 each); Rosie's ten seconds comes up wrong (3 in 10); Shaun's three eight-second legs come up straight, straight, turned (1 in 2 each); Ray's 7.5 seconds cures him (about 4 in 10); Tess's 2.5 seconds is nothing.
- **Cargo turns with its carrier.** The painting (Ep 1), the crab (Ep 9), Shaun's sealed case of porridge that Sixpence ignores and Peg's meter can read (Ep 9 Sc 7: "FOOD TURNS TOO").
- **The grip.** Skin only, one hand, a coin toss which; Danny's four fingers (Ep 6 Sc 1); Rosie's offer refused with "Coin says which of us comes back with one hand. Not you" (Ep 8 Sc 2).
- **A field in water cannot carry you; the bite fires only after a minute, blind, for half your seconds.** Planted at the pit in Ep 1 ("Then you'd be carrying the pit with you. Water counts."), taught in Ep 5 Sc 13, paid in Ep 9 Sc 5 when "Her thumb goes to the wrist unit — and stops, an inch off the button. So that is why the Deep keeps you."
- **A turn cannot be felt, only seen afterwards, and never settled by looking.** "I never felt a thing. You never do. That's the trouble with the black" (Shaun, Ep 9 Sc 7). Every identity test is a meter, a photograph of linen, a catalogue plate, a hand reached for a heartbeat and dodged, or a man's own blood after a meal. Never a taste, never a smell, never a mirror.
- **Next door is never shown.** "The screen goes black" (Ep 9 Sc 5); "a step onto a shore we never see"; "The screen stays black for a minute" (Ep 10 Sc 12). What comes back is water, weed, a crab, sand, a breath.
- **The neighbours' life is the other hand.** Proved by an instrument and a man's blood: "The T-meter beeps: 5.6. Peg's meter beeps: LO ... Two meters. Same blood. Both of them right" (Ep 9 Sc 9).

One thing is never explained and does not need to be, but you should know it rests on a stated rule rather than a new one: tungsten beads in a floor slow a crosser "like wading" (Ep 5 Sc 14). The page has already shown that mass pulls at a crosser in the black ("a pull in her gut, the way a lorry passing too close on the A13 tugs at a car", Ep 1 Sc 1; the Hill "like leaning on a lorry"), so a wall packed with dense metal dragging at her from every side follows from what we have seen. No new rule is added anywhere that I could find.

### No echo of The Catch

I searched the whole text. There is no mint and no taste test; "lift" appears only as an ordinary verb (a phone, a bag, a grate) and never for a job, which is always a "dip"; no ledge, no reserve or spare for the bite (the word "spare" appears only in "a second to spare" and "a page spare"); no flask except a tea flask; no dishes except a pie dish and the steel dish a surgeon drops a tumour into; no growth, no mould; no hands meeting on glass; no backwards print seen through a turned character's eyes (the camera stays on the straight side, and the one mirror in the show is a straight boy reading a card in it); no episode ends on a meal that cannot be shared; nobody from next door appears; nobody is counted down or held at the fence ("Nobody reaches for anything, and nobody refuses anything", Ep 10 Sc 12). The one structural cousin, "is the painting a copy or are we the ones who changed?", is the mirror-is-relative idea the owner asked for, and it is settled by linen fibres under a lens, which is the source's own seed, not The Catch's.

### No banned word, no named idea

I searched for every word on the list in plan section 14 (conjecture, criticism, error correction, refute, falsify, fallible, good explanation, hard to vary, growth of knowledge, static or dynamic society, tradition, authority, optimism, "nothing is settled", "question everything", "the problem of", infinity). None appears. The background laws never get their names: they are "step out on purpose, it's twenty years" (Ep 5 Sc 13), "a man had a ring in his stomach ... Ten years of courts" (Ep 3 Sc 5), and "Police don't get the list, love. Not since 2019" (Ep 8 Sc 10). Nobody makes a speech about asking questions. The questions on screen are "Why's it a kilo out?", "How do you know it's not the weight?", "What did my dad weigh?"

### Not a dystopia

The world is pleasant and works. The Register "feeds anybody who walks through the door ... Nobody asks anything" (Ep 8 Sc 6). The fireman goes home to his allotment with his first ration box and sends a postcard of leeks. Mr Pinnock checks the scales with his own crown-stamped weights. Osei keeps a secret for a kind reason and lets Tess walk out. The police are Ray. The only people who never check are two families on two banks of one river, and they are the only people who cross for long.

## 3. What works best

These are the things a reviser must protect, with the reason each is held in place.

- **The count.** "She can't hear you, Tess." / "I know." (Ep 1) becomes "Then why d'you do it?" / "So someone's counting." (Ep 3 Sc 14), and by Ep 7 it is Peg on her knees in a van "and it is her daughter's voice and her daughter's daughter's voice coming out of her". The counting is the family's love and its blindness at once, and the last image of the family is a boy writing "checked against Maggie's" under the day's lorries.
- **"Straight?" "Straight."** Asked before "are you hurt" in Ep 1, it is a lie from Rosie, a hope from Tess ("She can't know that", Ep 6 Sc 6), and finally a confession: "I knew before we were out the marsh. I said it anyhow" (Ep 9 Sc 7). In Ep 10 nobody asks; Kieran holds out the meter. That is the whole season in one ritual, and it dies at exactly the right speed.
- **The sum that kills Liam** (Ep 9 Sc 12). "The lie on its own, he makes it. The breakfast on its own, he makes it. Either one, he makes it. Both, he doesn't. By two hundredths." Two causes, one of them the neighbours' first word and the other a grandmother's love, neither enough alone. Peg's confession is four words: "I made him breakfast." Then she re-chalks the board with the first true number it has ever held: "There's your number, love." It pays off in Ep 10 as "There's your number, Frank."
- **Two meters, one drop of blood** (Ep 9 Sc 9). The proof that next door is food is a starving man's number climbing from 2.8 to 5.6 on the Register's meter while Peg's reads LO, laid side by side "the way Ray laid two photographs on the counter in March". And nobody says what the crab tasted of: "DANNY: What's it taste— He stops."
- **The ferry** (Ep 7). Two enemy weighers with half a record each: "What we never wrote down is who come home wrong. That we kept in our heads. Like you." / "We wrote nothing at all." / "I know. I've watched your fires." The first graph either family has ever made goes on the back of a timetable, and "Nobody counted my girl" is answered with "Then let's count her now."
- **Ray.** "It's what there was to count." A man who wrote everything down for twenty-four years and showed nobody, feeding thieves on dead men's dinners at the going rate because "your mother would sooner starve than owe anybody". The tomato plants he cannot eat: "They need the watering." And the cure played for a laugh at the one moment the family is holding its breath: "Eleven year I've wrote back to front so you lot could read me. Give us a fortnight."
- **Terry.** The gloves, the pot, the washing-day song, the eleven hops, "I could've been eating at this table five year since." His choice is a real one and the page gives both options weight: "Six goes to the end of my count, at my age, and a warrant Monday. Or one go, and I land the right way round. Five year I've been the wrong hand at this table. Over there I'm not." He rolls a die and shows nobody; he holds up his gloves ("I cook with these"); he counts himself off the world: "One." "Two." Nobody watches an instrument afterwards, and that choice was made in the kitchen at four in the morning. Keep his fate open.
- **Rosie's "I went shopping"** (Ep 9 Sc 6), and her held breath at the fence, let out alone twenty yards off. What she saw stays hers.
- **The Twitch cold open** (Ep 2 Sc 1): a hundred City guards coming out into Cheapside with their radios while the roar comes up through the gratings, then "a ticking like rain on a shed roof". The world's strangeness done with sound and pavement lights.
- **Sixpence.** A dog that has growled at Terry for five years growls at Rosie in the first breakfast ("He's jealous. She's been away"), then at Danny, then at every depot case but the turned one, then at a wet jumper. The dog is right first every single time, and nobody explains him until it is too late to matter.
- **The vote** (Ep 10 Sc 14). Danny says "Burn", looks at the boy's second column, and says "No. Give." Peg shuts the stove door on cold ash: "Give. Paper talks." The line she said in Ep 1 to burn the tissue.

## 4. The problems, most serious first

### SUBSTANTIVE 1. The stolen cure sits in the house for a fortnight and nobody asks what it says

**Where.** Episode 6 Scene 5 through Episode 7 Scene 12.

**What is on the page.** In Ep 6 Sc 5 the whole family, at the table, sends Tess to rob the clinic for its method. Tess: "The method's written down somewhere. Price lists, deliveries, how it's done. Whatever a hundred and eighty thousand buys, there's a paper for it under the price." Peg answers with her own line, "Paper talks," and tries to stop her going only in person ("Not you"). Rosie gives her the room. Terry drives. Tess comes back at two in the morning with the binder, and in the van (Sc 7) tells Terry the whole of it: "It's a crossing. Ten seconds in the black, under their roof. That's the whole of it. That's what the money buys." Terry: "Eleven times I went in. On two seconds." Thursday night (Sc 9) Tess sits at the table with the binder; Peg and Danny are in the room; nobody asks. Friday at two in the morning (Sc 10) Rosie "looks at the binder under Tess's flat hands — at the shape of it, the thickness of it".

Then Episode 7 opens on a caption, **TWO WEEKS LATER**. At that morning's breakfast (Sc 2) Peg plans an eight-second job for Shaun "to pay the Laundry" and nobody mentions the book; Tess gets as far as "Eight seconds is—" and is cut off. That night at the stove (Sc 12) Tess lays down the binder and the reveal is played as news to everyone: Danny, "Their book. Their numbers. Her ferry. You went to them"; Peg, "The book burned" (Ep 8 Sc 2), as if she is hearing of it for the first time.

**Why it matters.** Three people at that table are starving at nine hundred pounds a day, the money runs out in "Thirty-eight days", and the family itself commissioned the theft. Peg and Danny would ask the next morning. Terry knows the cure is ten seconds and cooks for a fortnight without saying so. Rosie, who priced the cure at the clinic and asked Osei "But what do you do to him?", sees the book and never asks to open it. Everything that follows in Ep 7 (Peg planning "near enough", Tess put out of the house, the binder burned) depends on the family not knowing, but the page gives no reason for the silence, so it reads as the writers holding the card rather than the family not asking. It also blunts Danny's fair blow, "You had it on your lips at this table this morning", which only covers that morning and not the thirteen days before it. And it is a shame, because a family that does not ask is exactly the season's subject; the silence could be its best example instead of its one hole.

**Fix.** Two routes; the second is better and they can be combined.

1. Shorten the gap. Maggie's "Thursday fortnight" (Ep 6 Sc 4) becomes "Thursday next", the note reads "Thursday 14th", the caption becomes ONE WEEK LATER, and "Two weeks I've had that in my pocket" becomes "A week".
2. Make the silence a choice and show it once. Five lines in Ep 6 Sc 9, the Thursday night, while Kieran weighs his test weight and the adults are in the room:

   PEG: Well? Their paper.
   TESS: When I've checked it against something that isn't theirs.
   DANNY: Terry says ten seconds.
   PEG: Terry says a lot. I'll not have a clinic's book read at my table. When she's checked it, she can burn it.

   Now Tess withholds because it is unchecked (her flaw: she trusts nothing she has not laid beside another number) and Peg refuses it because it is theirs (her flaw: "We don't go to them"), and the two flaws lock. Then Ep 7 Sc 12's fury is earned in both directions, Ep 8's "The book burned" carries the weight of a refusal rather than a first hearing, and Terry's line at the burning, "Eleven goes, that was", gains a second meaning: he told them and was waved away. One line in the loft (Ep 7 Sc 4) can carry that: TERRY: "I told her. Ten seconds, I said. She said she'd had ten seconds off a leaflet."

### SUBSTANTIVE 2. Aoife's father is dead in one scene and alive in another

**Where.** Episode 7, Scenes 7 and 9.

**What is on the page.** Sc 7, Maggie on Aoife's last crossing: "Who? Her dad? Her dad thinks facing home is a charm. She went in alone, in the dark, hungry, with her own sums." Sc 9, twenty lines later, reading her tide book: "Joe's dad, God rest him. Straight." And of the 2019 Mynors run: "Aoife's, her dad on the wheel for her." Nothing on the page says Aoife and Joe had different fathers, so the one man is dead and also holds an opinion in the present tense.

**Why it matters.** It is small, but a viewer who has just been told "God rest him" hears it, and the ferry is the scene where two women are being precise about their dead. It also muddles who, on the Ahern side, let Aoife go uncounted: everything else in the season makes that Declan ("Aherns face home. Nine seconds she was in that bank", Ep 4 Sc 8), and Declan is the one still cheering a child on a bathroom scale in December.

**Fix.** One word: "Her grandad thinks facing home is a charm." It is Declan's line all season and it points the epilogue's last cheer straight back at him.

### QUIBBLE 1. The seven glances may give Rosie away by the middle of Episode 2

**Where.** Ep 2 Sc 7–16. After every hop, "Rosie's eyes go past them, to the catalogue cutting on the wall. She looks at it for exactly as long as it takes to read a line of print", and the action line marks the glance each of seven times, twice adding "nobody sees her do it".

**Why it might matter.** The plan wants ordinary viewers level with the family until Ep 3, reading the glances as care for the painting. On a screen, a camera that singles out the same look seven times is a flag; add Sixpence's second growl ("Still jealous, is he?") and Terry's "Goes through me like water. Since I turned" from Ep 1, and I think many viewers will have it by hop four. That is not fatal (from then on every cheer plays as dread, which the plan wants from Ep 3 anyway), but Ep 3's meter would land as confirmation rather than reversal. Only a screening settles it. If it does give her away, show three glances (hops one, four and seven) and let the others be "and back" in the action line without a shot.

### QUIBBLE 2. Ray is "puzzled" that Rosie looks well, but by then his own ledger has already told him she is fed

**Where.** Ep 5 Sc 3 (the A13, 21 April): "RAY: You look well. ROSIE: I've eaten. That seems to puzzle him more than anything she has said." Ep 8 Sc 6: "The seventh of April, it goes to two. Nobody doubles an order like that for a guest. I knew there was another mouth in that house the week you did."

**Why.** On a re-watch the puzzlement contradicts the ledger: he has known for a fortnight that Peg is buying two dinners. A director can play the look as surprise that she said it aloud, so the page does not settle it. Simplest is to change the action line to what Ep 8 supports: "There's colour in her cheeks now, and he knows whose dinners put it there."

### QUIBBLE 3. "To the gram" is too neat

**Where.** Ep 9 Sc 6: "Minus forty-six two. Who weighed forty-six two?" / "Me. To the gram. Ward scale had me at the same on Wednesday." The ward weighed her that morning "in a t-shirt with her sleeves pushed up" (Ep 8 Sc 12); the plate weighed her at midnight rigged, in her clothes, having hung only her coat. The two numbers can agree near enough, not to the gram, and nothing depends on the match (the writers' own note says so). Cut "To the gram" and let Kieran say "Same as the ward" himself.

### QUIBBLE 4. "Twenty years" is said twice and then Rosie sits her exams

**Where.** "And step out on purpose, it's twenty years" (Ep 5 Sc 13); "It's twenty year, stepping out" (Ep 9 Sc 10); Peg to the sergeant, "my girl stepped out on purpose. Twice" (Ep 10 Sc 15). December: the ward sister, "Your file lives in our kitchen now, love. You eat where you work. And you sit your exams in the spring, like anyone else."

**Why.** The plan's idea is exact and good: the state cannot hold a turned person behind a wall, only feed them, so Rosie's twenty years is a kitchen, not a cell, while her straight aunt is in a cell. The page shows the rule (the ward with "no lock on the door either way", Ep 8 Sc 9) but never joins the two ends, so a viewer who remembers "twenty years" may think she got off. Half a line seals it: SISTER: "Twenty years, the judge said. Twenty years of eating here."

### QUIBBLE 5. Two lines lean towards saying the moral

**Where.** Ep 7 Sc 4, Terry: "Nobody ever asked why." Ep 7 Sc 12, Rosie: "And when it's been counted proper, and checked against somebody else's, I'm going first."

**Why.** Both pass the owner's rule (no banned word, no speech, both about a particular thing), but Terry's line is a general statement about not asking, and Rosie's is the plan's own thesis sentence in her mouth. Terry's next line does the same work better and more in character: "He was off his food, love. Then he wasn't. That was enough for us." I would cut "Nobody ever asked why" and keep that. Rosie's could be "when it's been counted, and not just by Tess". Taste, either way.

### QUIBBLE 6. Why does Shaun go eight seconds deep when two-second hops are filling the crates?

**Where.** Ep 9 Sc 3 and Sc 5. Terry, at two seconds a go from the shed wall: "the crates fill, one case at a time, two seconds a go" (nine cases). Shaun's two eight-second legs bring back two: "Eight for the far stack, Nan." In the black: "At the far end, stacked three high, cases stencilled ARMY. Nobody in his family knows that but him."

**Why.** The long legs are what put Shaun on the eight-second row of the table (1 in 2) and give the "food turns too" reveal, but the reason for going deep is thin on the page: the far stack is never said to be better than the near one, and the ARMY line is a loose end (why does it matter that only Shaun knows?). Two fixes, and the second turns the loose end into another turn of the theme. Either let Peg say the Army's fresh consignment is at the back and the near pallets are the dregs (Mick has already said "Army's in. Whole shed"), or leave the plan as Peg's reflex and let Tess name it on Friday (Ep 9 Sc 11): "Two seconds filled nine crates and you sent him eight for two." That is Tess's own Mayfair mistake, made by Peg, recognised by the one person who has made it.

### QUIBBLE 7. Peg says nothing in the loft while her own handwriting is under the torch

**Where.** Ep 10 Sc 2. "Peg climbs the ladder first." Frank's tickets come out "with Peg's pencil still on them": "12 st 4", then "10 st 2", then "7.1". Kieran reads the numbers out, Terry speaks, Tess speaks. Peg does not react until Ray arrives in Sc 4 ("That's my hand. I pencil'd it on his ticket").

**Why.** The scene is built so that Peg's twenty-four-year legend meets her own pencil, and the page gives her no beat there. It may be intended (she is the one who kept the envelope and could not open it), and her later line is good. But one held shot on Peg's face at "10 st 2", or Peg reading her own pencil aloud instead of Kieran reading the printed numbers, would let the audience watch a memory turn. Taste.

### QUIBBLE 8. The first stroke is an earthquake and the other forty are "Too small to feel"

**Where.** Ep 4 Sc 1 (cups walking off the dresser, a mountain's worth of rock); Ep 5 Sc 4 radio ("another next-door event ... Too small to feel"); Ep 7 Sc 6 ("forty-one next-door events ... regular, like a metronome"); Ep 8 Sc 13 ("the first stroke was on the fourth of April").

**Why.** This is a wonder and should stay one, and the scale change needs the first stroke to be the big one. But a viewer may ask why a message opens with a mountain and continues in whispers. One phrase from the Reading scientist in Ep 8 would settle it without explaining the neighbours: "the first stroke was the loud one; the rest are quieter, the way you'd knock once you had someone's attention." Or leave it. The page does not need it; a curious audience might.

## 5. What I am unsure of

- **Pace** is judged from the page. The ferry (Ep 7) and the night of writing (Ep 10 Act 2) are long stretches of two people and a pencil; both are intercut with a clock (Shaun's crossing; the tide at 05:52) and I did not find a scene without a job in either. Whether they play at fifty-five minutes is a cutting-room question.
- **The baking-tray model** (Ep 10 Sc 9) is the one place where the audience must follow a piece of reasoning to reach the ending. On the page it is clear: flour hill, ruler for our ground, water to Rosie's eight metres, tipped down to Nobody low, the wet edge crossing the ruler in one place. On screen it depends on the blocking. It is worth protecting, not simplifying.
- **The Twitch ticket** reads plus 0.31 on an empty plate (Ep 2 Sc 3) where later readings of bodies in the black are minus. The Twitch is a passing ripple, not a still mass, so the sign need not match, and the plan makes it a wonder. I note it only so that nobody "fixes" it by mistake.
- **Terry's fate** is open on purpose and should stay open. The page holds both readings (his die is never shown; "Whatever I land as over there, I stay") and nothing settles between them. The fainter second message is the season-two hook precisely because it can be read four ways, and the page has shown every rule each reading needs.
- **The "fifteen stone" sum.** The plan relies on the audience being able to do it (95 kilos, a fifth of a second a kilo under a hundred, so about one second). Peg says the line directly under the painted rule (Ep 1 Sc 6) and again beside a ten-second job (Ep 3 Sc 3), and Tess "looks up at Frank's photo ... and back at the thinnest name on the board" (Ep 3 Sc 9). That is as fair as it can be without saying it. Whether viewers do the sum is, again, a room question.
