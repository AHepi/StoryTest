# Episode 5: Delivered

*The war turns lethal, and the family's heaviest boy starves himself to join it.*

---

## Cold open

### Scene 1. The weighbridge office, Grays, Tuesday, ten to seven in the morning.

The week after Easter. Rain on the yard. Two lorries idle at the gate with their lights on, waiting for seven. Inside, the hatch is down and the public door is bolted: family only.

TERRY (66) turns the bacon in his yellow gloves. KIERAN (12), back in his school jumper, copies last night's ticket into his exercise book: 21:00 · LITTLE BRIDGE · 19.78. ROSIE (22) sits at the table with a blue-lidded tub and a spoon, and there is colour in her face. TESS (38) holds a mug and looks at nothing, her lips moving. PEG (72), apron over her cardigan, butters toast for a boy who is already late.

A horn at the back. DILYS (60s, launderette tabard under her coat) bangs in at the back door with the crate of white tubs.

DILYS: Your whites, Terry.

TERRY: Bless you, Dilys.

PEG: *(wiping her hands)* Where's Danny? He's to put his hand in the safe for the money.

TESS: Pouring. Moorgate. Since five. Liam and Shaun with him.

Rosie is already up.

ROSIE: My rig's in the van. I'll post my hand in.

PEG: You'll sit.

ROSIE: It's a hand, Nan. Hands don't turn.

PEG: You'll not put a finger in the black till you're right.

Rosie sits. Dilys waits in the doorway, the rain behind her, tapping the crate with one fingernail. Peg looks at the green safe standing at the end of the little bridge, the steel plate set flush in the lino.

PEG: I've a key, haven't I.

KIERAN: Since when?

PEG: Since before you.

She pulls at the dresser drawer. It sticks, as it always sticks. It's packed with brown envelopes, years of them, unopened: crowns, window envelopes, WEIGHTS AND MEASURES on top. She pushes her whole forearm in under them and digs. A corner of pale green paper with a printed hand on it slides past her wrist and back under. At the very bottom her fingers close on a key ring.

Two keys. A long brass safe key, dark with age. And a small padlock key on a loop of orange baler twine gone pale. Her thumb rests on the small one for a moment. Then she takes the ring to the safe.

She gets down on her knees on the steel plate, slowly, a hand on the safe for balance. Under the counter the printer chatters and prints her weight. Nobody tears it off.

PEG: Not since Blair.

The key goes in stiff. It won't turn. She puts both hands to it. Tess comes and takes the handle, and together they lean, and the lock gives with a clack like a knuckle. The door swings open on a groan.

Inside, on the shelf: a brown envelope of notes. A cash box. And lying flat on its tea towel, face up, *The Assayer's Wife*: the woman at her window, holding her little brass balance up to the light.

On one of the two painted pans of the balance, standing upright on its base like a small brass man, is a bullet.

Peg looks at it. She looks at the key in her hand, and at the lock she has just forced. Behind her Tess has gone very still. Rosie is on her feet. Kieran has stopped writing.

Peg reaches past the bullet without touching the painting, takes out the envelope, gets up off her knees, and counts notes into Dilys's hand at the back door with no tremble in her fingers at all.

DILYS: You all right, Peg? You've gone a colour.

PEG: Same time next week, Dilys.

The back door shuts. Peg turns round to her family.

PEG: It's been delivered.

## Act one: The radius

### Scene 2. The weighbridge office, seven o'clock.

The wall clock clicks to seven. Peg does what she does at seven: she slides up the hatch and unbolts the public door.

TESS: Mum.

PEG: Lorries don't know.

MICK (50s, hi-vis) leans in at the hatch for his ticket, rain dripping off his hood. At the same moment the public door opens, and in comes a thin man in an overcoat too big for him.

MICK: Your copper's back, Peg.

PEG: He's not mine.

DS RAY AMBROSE (54) puts a coin on the counter.

RAY: Hot water, Mrs Marlow, when the kettle's on. *(pleasantly)* Heard someone emptied a cold room in Gravesend without opening the door. Thought I'd come and not ask you about it.

Then he looks past her. At the end of the room the green safe stands open, and the family stands round it like people round a hole in the road.

Peg reaches across to swing the safe door shut.

RAY: Leave it.

Her hand stops.

PEG: It's a copy. You'll have heard.

RAY: I'm not here about the picture.

Peg holds his eye for a long moment. Then she slaps the hatch down in Mick's face.

MICK: *(through the hatch)* Peg!

PEG: Bridge is shut ten minutes.

MICK: What's up with it?

PEG: Scale's playing up.

Out in the yard Mick's lorry sits on the big bridge with its engine running. Peg bolts the public door so no one else comes in. Ray raises the counter flap and walks through into the family's side of the room, where he has never been. He crouches by the safe with his hands in his coat pockets and looks without touching.

RAY: Never been fired. Doesn't need to be. *(beat)* Stood it up on her little pan. You don't do that blind.

TESS: Hand through a door's blind.

RAY: So it wasn't a hand. *(He looks at the bullet a while longer.)* Last one I saw like that was in a man's head. Not a mark on him. No way in, no way out. Sat in the middle of his brain like a stone in a plum. Coroner didn't know what to put. Wrote "delivered".

Nobody says anything. Terry turns off the gas under the bacon.

RAY: To put that there, you've to see it. To see inside a safe you've to be in the black beside it, arm's length. Whoever did this stood beside your safe.

PEG: Nobody stood there. Door bolted, dog in. Nobody's been in this room but family and drivers.

RAY: Not in this room, Mrs Marlow. In there.

Kieran has been standing at the end of the counter with his exercise book clutched to his chest. He opens it, peels a ticket off the page, and holds it out. Not to his mother. To the policeman.

KIERAN: It printed this. Friday. There was nobody on it.

Ray takes it. 02:04 · LITTLE BRIDGE · −59.0 kg.

RAY: You keep these?

KIERAN: Every night. For school.

Ray looks down at the steel plate in the lino, and at the green safe standing at the end of it.

RAY: A plate reads a man in the black as a pull upward. Minus fifty-nine's a man, standing over your scale.

Something passes across his face at the number, and is gone. He takes out his notebook and writes. For a second, over his arm, we see the page above the line he's writing: MAYFAIR · 03:12 · 2ND BODY · 59.0. He shuts the book.

TESS: You've seen that number before.

RAY: I've seen a lot of numbers, Miss Marlow.

He hands the ticket back to Kieran.

RAY: Keep that somewhere safe, son. *(a glance at the safe)* Somewhere safer.

He takes out a clean handkerchief, picks the bullet off the painted pan with it, and folds it into his pocket.

RAY: I'll keep this.

PEG: And the picture?

RAY: I've seen a picture in a safe. Couldn't tell you which way the light fell.

Terry puts a mug in front of him. Hot water, no tea bag. Ray drinks it standing, the way other men drink tea.

RAY: Whoever stood there can stand anywhere. By your bed. By his.

Peg looks at the plate, at the safe, and at the stairs up to where Kieran sleeps.

PEG: Fetch his bed down.

KIERAN: What?

PEG: You're sleeping in here. *(She taps the steel plate with her slipper.)* Nobody stands near that boy without the scale knowing.

RAY: It won't know till the morning.

PEG: Then I'll know in the morning.

Ray puts the mug down. At the public door he stops with his hand on the bolt.

RAY: You'll want to answer this. Don't.

He lets himself out. Peg shoots the bolt behind him, hard.

### Scene 3. The A13, eastbound, Tuesday, 3 p.m.

Rain, spray, lorries. Rosie's white van runs in the slow lane off the bridge approach towards Grays. Her lips move: a sum. On the passenger seat her coat is bundled over something, and a collar strap with a brass disc on it pokes out from under the hem.

Blue lights flicker in the grille of the grey car behind her. She swears softly and pulls into a lay-by beside a shut burger van.

Ray comes to her window with rain on his shoulders. She winds it down.

RAY: Nearside brake light.

ROSIE: Is it.

RAY: It is now. *(He looks past her at the coat on the seat, and the strap.)* Licence for that?

ROSIE: It's for the pit. Training.

RAY: Then the pit'll have to manage.

He walks round, opens the passenger door, pulls her coat aside and takes the rig: belt, collar, wrist unit, the brass disc swinging on its strap. He tears a property receipt off a pad and hands it to her through the window.

ROSIE: That's worth more than the van.

RAY: Then you'll want it back. *(beat)* We don't cuff crossers, love. We take the rig.

She looks at the receipt. Paper, with her name on it.

RAY: Been sightseeing in Gravesend?

ROSIE: Cold-water swimming.

RAY: So I hear.

He looks at her face a beat longer than a policeman needs to. There's colour in her cheeks now.

RAY: You look well.

ROSIE: I've eaten.

That seems to puzzle him more than anything she has said. He goes back to his car with her rig under his arm, like a man carrying a cat he doesn't trust.

### Scene 4. The weighbridge office, Tuesday, 9 p.m.

Supper is cleared. Terry is at the sink. DANNY (41), still grey with concrete dust from the pour, holds the property receipt at arm's length as if it smells.

DANNY: A rig's more than a car.

TESS: More than that van. And Terry's tubs. And the gas.

PEG: We're always short.

TESS: We're shorter.

DANNY: *(the receipt down on the table)* They stood in my mum's kitchen with a bullet.

PEG: Not tonight.

DANNY: When?

PEG: When I know who.

DANNY: Aherns.

PEG: When I *know*.

Behind them LIAM (19) and SHAUN (26) come down the stairs with Kieran's camp bed between them, bumping it round the turn, and set it up in the corner at the end of the little bridge. Its head is a foot from the green safe. Peg brings down the good blankets, the ones from the airing cupboard that nobody's allowed, and makes it up with hospital corners. Kieran watches her do it like a boy watching his first tent go up. He puts his exercise book under the pillow.

Liam straightens up from the bed and pulls his belt a notch tighter. The notch is new: a hole punched through the leather with a nail, rough at the edges. Rosie sees it. Liam sees her see it, and grins, and she looks away first.

On the radio on the counter, the calm BBC voice: "...and the gravity teams report another next-door event in the Thames estuary overnight. Too small to feel. In sport tonight..." Nobody listens.

**Later.** The office in darkness. The display on the little bridge is dark; only the printer's small green light shows. The hatch is down. Upstairs a floorboard settles.

Kieran lies on the camp bed in the good blankets, wide awake, looking at the green safe beside his head. Then he turns over on his side so that he faces it.

## Act two: Con

### Scene 5. The Ahern kitchen, Gravesend, Thursday, 3 a.m.

A baby crying overhead: CIAN, thin and tired. MAGGIE AHERN (44) comes down the stairs in a dressing gown over a fleece, pushing her hair out of her face. The stove is banked low. The marine radio on the sill murmurs river traffic.

CON AHERN (70s) sits in his chair by the stove, as he sits every night, in his cardigan over his pyjamas. He is thinner than he was a fortnight ago; his wrists are sticks. A mug of hot water with gin in it has gone cold on the chair arm. His old mobile lies on his knee, its screen still lit: TEL. The call was never made.

On the table beside him stands a small brown pill bottle with its lid off. It is empty.

MAGGIE: You'll catch your death sat down here.

He doesn't answer. She knows before she touches him. She stands still in the middle of the kitchen for a long moment.

Upstairs, Cian cries.

She fills the pan, puts the bottle in, and counts under her breath the way she always does, until the number. She takes the bottle out and tests it on her wrist. Then she crosses to Con and takes the phone off his knee, gently, as if it might wake him. The screen goes dark in her hand.

SIOBHAN *(from the stairs)*: Mum?

SIOBHAN (13) stands halfway down in her parka and pyjamas.

MAGGIE: Take him his bottle, love.

Siobhan comes down for it. She sees Con. She takes the bottle without a word and goes back up. The crying stops.

Maggie sits down at the table opposite him. On the fridge, under the tug magnet, are Aoife's photo and the long curl of printout. She turns the empty bottle round and round on the oilcloth.

MAGGIE: *(quietly)* Made to your hand.

### Scene 6. The yard at Grays, and the Ahern kitchen, Thursday, 7 p.m.

**Grays.** Terry sits on the upturned crate by the bins with one glove off and the cordless phone in his bare hand. On its screen: CON. Beyond the fence the gravel heaps stand pale in the dusk. Out on the river a tug goes by with a lighter in tow.

This time he presses the green button.

**Gravesend.** On the dresser, Con's old phone buzzes and lights up: TEL. Siobhan, doing homework at the kitchen table, looks at it for three rings. Then she picks it up.

SIOBHAN: Hello.

TERRY: Is Con about, love? It's Tel. About the gravy.

Silence on the line.

SIOBHAN: He's dead.

Terry shuts his eyes.

SIOBHAN: Wednesday night, in his chair. He had you up on his phone. Mum found it on his knee.

TERRY: Was it his heart?

SIOBHAN: His tablets were in the tubs. *(beat)* Grandad says the Marlows took the tubs. *(beat)* Are you a Marlow?

Terry looks across the yard at the office window, at the blind with the shapes of his family moving behind it, and the hatch light.

TERRY: Yes, love.

She rings off.

### Scene 7. The weighbridge office, Thursday, 8 p.m.

Supper. The whole firm is at the table: Peg, Danny, Tess, Liam, Shaun, and Kieran at the end with his homework. Rosie has a blue-lidded tub in front of her. Liam has a full plate he's moving round. Sixpence lies under the table where Liam's feet are, hopeful.

Terry comes in from the yard and puts the phone down on the counter. He doesn't go to the stove. He pulls out a chair and sits down at the table, which he never does at supper, and the table notices.

TERRY: Con's dead.

PEG: Con?

TERRY: Con. Gravy Con. *(beat)* Con used to ring me about gravy.

DANNY: Con who?

TERRY: Con Ahern.

Forks stop.

TERRY: Since I turned. He was turned himself, years back. Neither of us could taste a thing. We'd ring up and tell each other what we remembered. He remembered a sausage he had on Margate front when he was a boy better than I remember my wedding.

PEG: You've been on the phone to them.

TERRY: To him.

DANNY: What was it? His heart?

TERRY: His tablets were in the tubs.

Silence. Then Tess puts down her fork.

TESS: The little tub. The one that rattled. Pills in foil, made to his hand. *(beat)* I put them in the stove.

Every head turns to her.

TESS: Their weigher rang me Friday. I've known since Friday.

DANNY: And said nothing.

TESS: I didn't have a number for it.

Rosie looks down at the blue-lidded tub in front of her. At her spoon standing in it. It's Con's dinner, and she has eaten it every day for a fortnight. She puts the spoon down on the table. Across from her Terry has a white tub from the Laundry, as he has had every day since the raid, and she understands at last why he never touched the blue ones.

At the end of the table Kieran looks at the stove, at the little glass window with the fire behind it.

PEG: We took their dinner. We never killed anybody.

TESS: And his tablets.

PEG: *(hard)* They put a bullet in my safe. Where that boy sleeps.

DANNY: And now they've a funeral. They'll be coming.

Shaun has sat quiet all through. He taps the table with one finger.

SHAUN: Then we go first. Not a person. Boats. Their three tugs lie up on the pontoon off their quay, back off the tow by four. Ebb starts after. I undo every knot on them in the one go, and the river does the rest. They're a tug firm with no tugs for a week. *(beat)* Nobody dies.

DANNY: Nobody dies.

PEG: *(after a moment, a nod)* Boats.

TESS: Every line, one trip.

SHAUN: Never go back twice.

Terry gets up. He takes his coat off the back of the door.

TERRY: I'll drive.

PEG: You don't go on jobs.

TERRY: I've never seen where he lived.

Peg opens her mouth, and closes it.

The table breaks up. Chairs scrape. Rosie stays sitting with the tub in front of her. After a moment she pulls it back towards her, picks up the spoon, and eats, because she has to, without looking up. Terry, passing behind her with his coat on, rests his gloved hand on her shoulder for a second, and goes out.

## Act three: The knots

### Scene 8. The Ahern moorings, Gravesend, Friday, 4:25 a.m.

The river black and wide, turning on the top of the tide. Off the Ahern quay a long floating pontoon, and rafted along it three tugs, back off the night tow, their engines ticking as they cool. Mooring lines run from bow and stern to the pontoon's cleats and bollards. Up the quay, in the Ahern house, a light goes off. Then another.

Next to the yard, a set of public river stairs runs down into the water: worn stone, green with weed. At the top of them the Marlow van waits with its lights off, Terry at the wheel. Down at the waterline stand Shaun, rigged, and Danny, Tess and Liam. Liam holds a torch he has been told not to switch on.

SHAUN: *(low, looking at the tugs)* Round turn and two half hitches on the bow lines. Bowlines on the sterns. Declan's knot. He ties it like he's cross with it.

TESS: Every line.

SHAUN: One trip.

He flexes his fingers once, the way every Marlow does, and steps off the world.

Tess's lips start moving.

**The black.** Sound drops to Shaun's heartbeat and breath. His glow reaches an arm's length and no further. It shows him the inside of the stone step he was standing on, grit and shell in it, and then the river under the pontoon, clear as air in the glow: a flatfish lying on the mud, an old anchor furred with weed, a bicycle wheel. He pulls himself out along the pontoon, small pulls at nothing, and its floats slide past him from the inside, rust and dark water and bolts.

The first cleat. A rope wound round it. In the glow the rope is clear as jelly, every strand, and the knot sits inside itself, loop through loop. Shaun puts his fingers into the knot, not round it but into it, the way Rosie's hand went into the crate at Mayfair, and draws the end back out through the loop as though the rope were smoke. The knot isn't there any more. Neither end of the rope has moved.

The second. The third. He goes along the raft, line to line. A tug's hull slides by from the inside: the engine room, a kettle on a swinging ring, a calendar nobody has turned since Christmas, a child's drawing of a boat taped to a locker. He doesn't look at them.

The sixth. The seventh. The eighth is the stern line of the last tug, a bowline gone hard as wood with salt and years. It takes him longer to see the way out of it. His fingers hunt. Then they find it, and the loop opens like a hand.

Close on his fingertips: the fizz under the nails.

The ninth line runs to the far bollard, an arm's length beyond where he is. He could turn now. He looks at it. Never go back twice. He reaches, and takes the knot apart, and turns, and finds home, the one pull in the black that means home, and faces it, and steps, a hair slow.

### Scene 9. The river stairs, continuous.

TESS: ...eight...

Shaun lands on the stairs with a gasp and falls sideways into Liam. He's lying wrong.

His right leg ends above the knee.

The end of it is a clean flat plane, as if the leg had been cut on a butcher's slicer: the white ring of bone, the dark meat, the yellow of the fat, all perfectly flat and perfectly dry. For one second nobody moves and nothing bleeds.

Then it does.

Terry is out of the van and down the stairs faster than anyone has ever seen him move. His belt is off before he reaches the bottom. He cinches it high round Shaun's thigh, jams the handle of a spanner through it and twists.

DANNY: Get them gloves off, you can't feel.

TERRY: They stay on.

He twists again. The bleeding slows. Shaun is white and shaking and grinning at the sky.

LIAM: *(from habit)* Straight?

SHAUN: Straight. *(beat)* Did they go?

Behind them, on the black river, in silence, the tugs are leaving. Their lines lie round the cleats and bollards with no knot in them, and one after another the ropes slide off into the water as the ebb takes the hulls. The three tugs swing out, turn their sterns to the town, and go off down the river in the dark together, slowly, like cows walking out of a gate somebody's left open.

DANNY: They went.

Liam gathers Shaun up off the stairs the way he carried the painting in that first morning, like a tray of glass, and carries him up to the van. Terry climbs after them with his gloves red to the wrist.

Tess stays a moment on the bottom step and looks at the place where Shaun came back. There's nothing there. Only water slapping at the stone.

### Scene 10. The Ahern quay, Gravesend, Friday, 6:30 a.m.

Grey light. Across the river a harbour launch is nudging one tug off the mud below Tilbury. A second lies hard against the ferry pier. The third is out in the fairway, turning slowly, with a pilot boat circling it and hooting.

On the pontoon DECLAN AHERN (70, cap) holds up a mooring line he has pulled from the water. Its end is whole. No cut, no fray, no knot.

DECLAN: Not a knot on the river.

JOE AHERN (17, skinny, hoodie) coils another line that came to nothing. Siobhan stands on the quay in her parka.

Maggie walks down the public stairs next to the yard. Halfway down she stops. On the stone at the waterline, where the tide hasn't reached, is a dark smear, and a long drag of it up the steps, and a spanner lying in it.

MAGGIE: Somebody paid for that.

DECLAN: *(from the pontoon)* Not enough.

Maggie looks across the river at Grays, where the gravel heaps are coming pale out of the dawn.

## Act four: No margin

### Scene 11. The flooded gravel pit, the following Tuesday, 4 p.m.

The water is cold pewter under a white sky. Rosie kneels at the edge of the pontoon with her phone's stopwatch running. There's no rig on her belt.

Liam stands at the edge in a wetsuit that fitted him at Christmas. Now it hangs in folds at his belly, and his collarbones stand out above the zip like coat-hangers. His goggles are painted black with enamel. He drops in.

**Under the water** it's black, and the bubbles roar. He tumbles. Then he stops fighting. He lets a little breath go, feels which way it runs, and follows it.

He breaks the surface.

ROSIE: Four.

LIAM: Four!

ROSIE: Terry took a month.

LIAM: Again.

He hangs in the water, grinning, his teeth chattering.

LIAM: What's it like? The tingle.

ROSIE: Pins and needles. Only in your nails. It comes about two seconds before you're done.

LIAM: And then you step out.

ROSIE: Then you step out if there's air. *(beat)* If you're in the concrete, you don't. Never. You keep going till there's air.

LIAM: And if there's no air?

ROSIE: There's always air. Tess makes sure.

He grins and goes under. Rosie's lips move as she counts, just like Tess's. At three she opens her mouth to call him out. She doesn't. He breaks the surface.

LIAM: How long?

ROSIE: Four.

Danny comes down the haul road from the plant with concrete to his elbows and stands at the end of the pontoon. He watches his son haul himself out onto the planks, and sees the wetsuit hanging off him.

LIAM: Four seconds, Dad. Find up blind. First time, every time.

DANNY: *(to Rosie)* Is he?

A beat.

ROSIE: Every time.

LIAM: The lock-up. Their money's in it, Shaun says, in a box sunk in the floor. That's Rosie's cure, near as. *(beat)* I'm not the big lad any more, Dad.

Danny looks at him for a long time: the shoulders that used to fill a door, the ribs under the rubber.

DANNY: Yes.

Liam whoops and falls backwards into the pit.

Rosie looks down at her phone. She stops the stopwatch.

### Scene 12. The weighbridge office, Tuesday, 9 p.m.

Shaun lies on the settle by the stove with the stump bandaged and propped on a cushion, feverish and bright-eyed. Terry sits beside him with a bowl of water and a cloth, in his gloves. The rest are at the table.

DANNY: Liam's going. The lock-up.

The table goes quiet.

SHAUN: *(from the settle)* Make it worth my leg.

TERRY: He's nineteen.

PEG: Tess was fourteen.

Terry says nothing more. He wrings out the cloth.

TESS: Can he find up?

ROSIE: *(not looking up)* Four seconds. Every time.

Tess nods. Her eyes go to nothing, and she plans aloud, the way she always does, with no paper on the table.

TESS: Lock-up stands on the river wall. Floor's beads, a metre and more. Box is sunk in it by the river side, and that wall's a weigh-wall. Floods at three. You go in from the mud at low water: through the river wall, through the floor to the box. Bags. Back the way you came. Seven dead.

LIAM: River wall's brick. Brick's quick.

TESS: Brick's quick. Beads aren't. *(beat)* Tingle comes, you're back in the brick.

LIAM: Back in the brick.

PEG: Weigh him in the morning.

Peg looks up at the chalk board. LIAM 72.0 is still there, from Kieran's birthday, never wiped. She rubs it out with her thumb.

On the camp bed at the end of the little bridge, Kieran lies with his exercise book on his chest and watches the grown-ups decide.

### Scene 13. The weighbridge office, Wednesday, 4:30 a.m.

Dark at the windows. The printer's small green light. Kieran asleep on the camp bed by the safe. Sixpence in his basket.

The family comes down the stairs quietly: Tess, Danny, Rosie with no rig, Peg in her apron over her nightie. Terry stays upstairs with Shaun. Liam comes last, in Shaun's rig. It fits him. That says everything, and nobody says it.

He steps onto the little bridge in his socks. The printer chatters. On the camp bed Kieran raises his head. Tess tears off the ticket.

TESS: Sixty-two four.

For a second nobody cheers, because of the hour. Then Danny bangs the table with the flat of his hand, and it isn't quiet any more. Peg picks up the chalk and writes LIAM 62.4 on the board, and beside it, slowly, a star. His first. Liam looks at it like a man looking at a medal.

On the camp bed, up on one elbow, Kieran looks at the star too. Then he lies back down.

TESS: Sixty-two four. One and a half of notes coming out. Sixty-three nine. *(beat)* Seven two.

DANNY: Job's seven.

TESS: Two tenths.

ROSIE: It's not much, Tess.

TESS: It's twice what Nan ever gave anyone.

PEG: *(at the stove, lighting the gas)* A tenth was always plenty.

DANNY: Near enough.

TESS: Near enough isn't. Seven two. *(to Rosie)* Margins are for people who can't count.

Tess takes hold of the brass disc on Liam's collar and gives its tab a hard tug, the way a diver checks a partner's belt before the drop.

LIAM: Can I bite it if I'm short?

TESS: Not in the black. Only once you've stood a minute on the wrong side. *(beat)* And step out on purpose, it's twenty years.

Liam nods, as if he'd always known.

TESS: Van.

Tess goes out into the dark yard to load the van, and Danny after her. Rosie follows them to the door and stops there. She looks back at her brother at the table. She almost says something. Then she goes out.

Liam is alone at the table with Peg and the dog.

The bacon spits. Peg turns from the stove with a plate piled high: two eggs, three rashers, black pudding, fried bread. She puts it down in front of him.

PEG: Lucky breakfast.

LIAM: Nan...

PEG: You're not going over that water on nothing.

He looks at the plate. He looks up at the star beside his name. Under the table Sixpence's tail thumps the lino, waiting, the way it has waited every morning for three weeks.

Liam picks up his fork.

The camera stays at the table. He eats every bite. Sixpence's tail slows, and stops.

PEG: That's my boy.

A horn in the yard. Liam wipes his mouth, kisses his nan on the cheek, and goes. Peg scrapes the plate clean into the bin, though there's nothing on it to scrape.

On the camp bed, Kieran is asleep again, with his face to the safe.

## Act five: The hundredths

### Scene 14. The foreshore below the Ahern salvage lock-up, Gravesend, Wednesday, 5:40 a.m.

First light. Low water. The river has pulled back off a hundred metres of mud that shines like pewter. Above the mud stands a Victorian river wall of soft red brick, green with weed to the tideline, and on top of it the Ahern salvage lock-up: a squat shed of engineering brick with no windows on the river side.

At the foot of the river wall, on the mud: Tess, Danny, Rosie, and Liam in Shaun's rig. Liam has his palm flat on the weed.

DANNY: Straight back.

LIAM: Straight back.

ROSIE: Tingle, you keep going till there's air.

LIAM: I know.

He grins at her, the big lad's grin, in a face that has got too small for it. He flexes his fingers once, the way every Marlow does, and steps off the world.

Tess's lips start.

**The black.** Sound drops to Liam's heartbeat and breath. His glow reaches an arm's length. It shows him the weed from the inside, then the brick: old soft red brick and lime mortar, a clay pipe somebody lost when the wall was new, a dead crab's shell in a gap. He goes through it like a dog through a hedge. Brick's quick.

0.9. 1.3.

Then the floor. Inside it are the beads, packed grey through the grey like frogspawn in jelly, and the going turns thick and slow. He pulls. It's like wading. He knows this stuff; he has barrowed tons of it. His lips move: *not ours*.

1.8. 2.2. A mat of steel mesh slides through his glow, rust-red rods in a grid, and behind him.

2.6. The box. A steel box sunk in the concrete, and inside it, lit through its walls by his own light, bundles of fifties in clear bags. He reaches through the steel and draws the bags out, gently, the way Rosie drew the canvas out of its crate, and holds them flat against his chest.

**The mud.**

TESS: ...two... three. There's the wall.

Through the brick above them comes a roar like a tipper shedding its load. Danny's hands close into fists.

**The black.** Out of the top of Liam's glow comes a silent black hail. Beads pour down into the box's well where his hands were a moment ago and fill it. The room above him is filling. He turns for home with the bags against his chest and starts back through the floor.

3.4. 3.9. The mesh again. 4.4.

4.9.

Close on his fingertips: the fizz under the nails.

His glow is full of beads. Beads ahead of him, beads on every side. At the very edge of the light, ahead, the first course of the brick is only just showing.

Tingle comes, you're back in the brick. He isn't back in the brick.

His face. He knows what Tess said. He knows what Rosie said. Never in the concrete. He pushes.

5.3. The brick. Quick again. He goes through it as fast as he has ever moved in his life.

6.1. 6.5.

The outer face of the wall comes into his glow: the weed on it seen from the inside, pale green, a hand's breadth ahead. Beyond the weed there is only black: the air over the mud, where his dad is standing.

6.9.

He reaches.

The glow goes out. The heartbeat stops between one beat and the next.

Black. Nothing on the screen at all.

He was two hundredths of a second from home.

### Scene 15. The foreshore, continuous.

TESS: ...six... seven.

Nothing. The river, a long way off, talking to itself.

TESS: ...seven two.

Nothing.

Danny steps up to the wall and puts his palm flat on the weed, where his son went in.

TESS: ...eight.

DANNY: Tess.

TESS: ...nine.

Rosie sits down in the mud, the way you sit when your legs decide for you.

TESS: ...ten. Eleven. Twelve...

She keeps counting. At twenty her voice stops. Her lips go on.

Up on the lane a door bangs.

### Scene 16. The Ahern salvage lock-up, Wednesday, 5:50 a.m.

Maggie comes down the river stairs in her coat over her nightclothes. In her hand is a long curl of thermal paper, torn off the printer in her kitchen. Joe comes behind her. At the top of the stairs stands Declan, with a boathook.

Maggie stops on the last step above the mud. Danny turns from the wall and comes at her.

DANNY: Where is he?

Tess steps between them without a word and stands there.

MAGGIE: *(holding up the paper)* It's still printing.

For a moment none of them understands. Then Tess does, and her face goes.

MAGGIE: He's on my wall.

She looks at them: three Marlows on her mud at dawn, and the girl sitting in it. Then she turns and goes back up the stairs, and after a moment they follow her.

She unlocks the lock-up's steel door and swings it open. Inside is a salvage store: chains, a ship's bell, coils of rope, a brass porthole on a nail. Half the floor is buried under black tungsten beads, heaped like spilled coal over the place where the box is sunk. In the river-side wall is a plain steel panel the size of a door: the weigh-wall. There is nothing on it to read.

Declan fills the doorway behind them.

DECLAN: Not in my lock-up.

MAGGIE: Dad.

Just that. He looks at the panel. He looks at the Marlows. Then he goes.

Joe stays at the door, holding it open, not looking at anyone. He doesn't look at Danny at all.

MAGGIE: It's a minute. That's all you get. Then you go home.

She looks at her watch.

They stand. Danny in front of the panel, his hands hanging. Tess beside him with her eyes shut, counting it, because that's the only way she can stand there. Rosie next to her father, and she can't look at him. She looks at the beads. Out on the river a gull cries, and stops.

A minute.

Maggie tears the last length off the curl of paper in her hand. As she folds it we see the last line printed on it: 65.1 KG. She holds it out to Danny.

MAGGIE: It'll read him a month. Softer every day. Then it won't.

Danny takes it. He doesn't look at it. He folds it again, smaller, and puts it in his wallet, behind his driving licence.

Rosie goes out past Joe into the light.

### Scene 17. The weighbridge office, Wednesday, 7:10 a.m.

The hatch is down. Kieran is pulling his school jumper over his head by the camp bed. Peg is at the stove with the kettle, and the fry-up pan is still on the back ring.

Outside, the van rolls onto the big bridge. The printer chatters. Peg tears the ticket off out of habit and pushes it onto the spike, on top of the one that printed at five, when the van went out.

She looks at the two tickets. Then she takes the top one off the spike again and holds it beside the other, one in each hand, and reads them both.

She sits down on the bottom stair.

KIERAN: Nan?

The door opens. Danny. Tess. Rosie, with mud to her hips. Nobody else.

On the board, in fresh chalk: LIAM 62.4, and the star.

Nobody wipes it.

### Scene 18. The weighbridge office, Wednesday night.

Dark. The whole house is in bed, or pretending. The display on the little bridge is dark; the printer's small green light glows. The kettle ticks as it cools.

Kieran is awake on the camp bed. He gets up in his socks and stands on the little bridge. The printer chatters. He tears off the ticket and reads it by the green light: 39.2.

He sits on the edge of the camp bed and opens his exercise book. Down the page runs the column of his test weight, night after night: 19.78, 19.78, 19.78, 19.78. With his school ruler he draws a second column beside it, heads it *me*, and writes in it: 39.2.

He puts the book under the pillow and lies down facing the safe.

Footsteps on the stairs. Kieran shuts his eyes.

Danny comes down in the dark in his vest. He doesn't put the light on. He steps onto the little bridge. The printer chatters. He tears off the ticket and reads it by the green light.

DANNY: Eighty-eight.

He looks at the green safe, and at the wall clock above it, and down at the steel plate under his feet. His right hand opens and closes at his side.

DANNY: *(very quietly)* Two seconds is enough to grab someone.

On the camp bed, Kieran's eyes are open.

**END OF EPISODE 5**
