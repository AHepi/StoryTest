# Round 2 critique: theme and patch check

**Lens.** Theme and hard to vary, using the owner's skill (SKILL.md, "Check the patches"; by-domain.md part 5). Every round 1 fix is treated as a new part. Each is tried on the case that forced it and on the cases it must leave alone. Then the theme, "a culture that never questions always dooms its people", is re-tested as a whole. Round 1 findings that were fixed cleanly are not repeated. Where a finding touches one in `70-critique-round2-viewer.md`, it says so.

**Verdict.** Most round 1 fixes hold. Each of these does its job and breaks nothing I could find:
- the creep from Year 150;
- the sincere Year 235 relabel;
- the copyists' choice;
- the Fable sealed after Burnt Row;
- the unweighed;
- the Joining's plants;
- the ring's reach;
- dropping Earth from "always".

Five fixes do not hold cleanly.
- **Two collide.** Minthome's young pile (Logic 5) and Nell's failed prediction (Theme 3) contradict the episode 8 needle walk, which says the Graves are clean. As written, the heroine's one big error is bad luck, not hers.
- **The barred gates** (Theme 7) pass the doom test but leave the other half of the crush idle: a tested truth would crush the same crowd. So episode 8 teaches that saying a frightening truth kills.
- **The 62** (Theme 2) is not a result the old story forbids. Ream's own Leaves can hold it, in the verse right before the handhold verse. The clause added to stipulation 2 to allow it is a patch with one job.
- **The hem seeds** (Logic 1) tie the hidden Turned Randalls' food to the children's turning. That is a self-interest motive nobody meant.
- **"The mint alone is witness"** (Logic 3) is the one secret, self-serving act left in the chain, as the revision log feared.

Two theme risks run through the season:
- On screen, more people die after someone questions than after someone obeys: Burnt Row, CARROLL, the Joining, the crush, and the grey from Nell's own dropped rule.
- Nell is never wrong about a checkable thing through her own fault. She even scolds the Randall for the Burnt Row mistake she has just repeated.

The fixes below turn both risks without adding a rule.

## Whole-explanation tests on the theme

- **Flip.** Keep the same Lift, the same plague and the same grief, but give Ream the habit of asking.
  - The plan says that Ream would have seen the creep and stopped the Giving long before the Weight took hold. The founders, who counted, met the Year 9 flood and moved the Lift. Result: held, together with stipulation 2.
  - The founders' move is the season's best contrast case, though it is used only as a practical clue (the Litany). Nothing needs adding, but writers should know it is the comparison case.
  - Flip the finale: had the new door read 2, "keep counting" would still be the message. So the ending holds only through what forces the 4, Minthome's pile. Finding 1 has to repair that.
- **Rival: bad luck.** Held, with one leak. As written, Nell's prediction fails because her needle read "nothing" at the Graves, so her failure is luck (1).
- **Rival: scarcity.** No real rival. The Turned starve because of chemistry, and the Straights starve because of Minthome's own rule.
- **Rival: a villain.** Held, except for two small leaks: the mint gloss (7) and the hem seeds (11).
- **Rival: plain grief.** This is now the strongest rival. Grief drives the first Giving, Adamala's Giving, Nan's Joining and Dimopoulos's Fast.
  - The plan separates grief from not asking through three people: Tau (grief that learns), Dimopoulos's crate (grief that tests) and Adamala (grief that doesn't).
  - But the one scene that would show grief kept and the practice changed, a burial, is missing (4).
- **Add a job.** If the theme is right, a questioning culture should also *keep* old rules once it finds their reasons, not only throw rules out.
  - In the present-day story every repair throws something out: the Giving, the door, the Tithe, the Passing, the dash.
  - The only old rule kept for its reason is the Tithe's count, which lives on as the cards. Burnt Row carries that lesson only for the past.
  - Findings 2, 3 and 9 each add a present-day case of a rule that is questioned and kept.
- **Does it teach "questioning is dangerous"?** At risk (6, and 2, 3, 9).
- **Does it teach "the heroine is always right"?** At risk. Nell is wrong through her own fault only once, in episode 1 ("two more"). Findings 1, 3 and 5 give her mistakes she owns.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. Two round 1 fixes collide: the needle walk says the Graves are clean, and Minthome's pile says they aren't

**Where.**
- Episode 8 cold open: "at the river gate by the Old Graves, nothing", and "She stands at the Graves in the cold, where the needle is still".
- Section 2.6: "Young and old clouds" and "Minthome's small weight".
- Episode 10 act 2: "Theta's needle at the Graves shows a small weight".
- The numbers table.

**Problem.**
- Logic 5 made Minthome's 20 tonnes young and tight, a third as far from its door, pulling "like about five hundred tonnes at Ream's distance". Its door is the Graves.
- By the plan's own cube law, a needle at the Graves should read about a third of the Temple steps' reading: roughly 35 against 100. That is neither "nothing" nor "a small weight".
- Theme 3 then has Nell predict 2 in 100 because that needle was still.

**Why it matters.** Try the fix on the case that forced it: Nell's prediction must fail, and she must own the failure. As written, she looked, the needle misled her, and the world surprised her, so her one big failure is bad luck. The fix also breaks a case it must leave alone: the episode 8 needle walk, the season's model test. A viewer who knows the rule will see the contradiction.

**Fix.** Make the miss hers, in the shape of Burnt Row's true lesson: she answered before she looked.
- **Episode 8.** End the walk at the river gate, with a reading of "a hair". The Graves are locked at night, because they are Pariah ground. Cut "where the needle is still". Nell carries the cube law past a gate she never opened, and writes 2 on the cellar wall.
- **Episode 10 cold open.** Theta has only just set her needle down, and Dimopoulos is on his way with the Great Tithe list. Nell writes the board before the needle has settled.
- **After the cards.** Theta shows her the settled needle: about a third of the Temple steps' reading. Nobody states the lesson. The phrase "until the needle settles", which barred the gates in episode 8, now lands on the heroine.
- **Numbers.** Change "a small weight" in episode 10, and add the Graves reading to the numbers table.

This also meets the viewer critique's wish (its finding 3) that 4 instead of 2 should be a fair shock.

### 2. The crush passes the doom test, but its "untested" half does no work, so episode 8 teaches that saying a frightening truth kills

**Where.**
- Episode 8, acts 2–4.
- Section 3, step 8.
- The Randall's "Right about" list in section 4.
- The episode 8 row of the twist table: "one right person without a test the crowd can see is not enough".

**Problem.**
- Round 1 (Theme 7) found two faults in the crush:
  - it failed the doom test;
  - "swap Nell's bare claim for the same claim with the needle walk in hand, and a frightened crowd could crush just the same".
- The barred gates fixed the first fault. The second is still there.
- Tell twelve thousand people that the Ancestors beside the door are killing their children, and they run for the gates whether or not a crate is opened. They are even doing the right thing, getting away from the door, and the rule kills them.
- So the test she didn't show plays no part in the crush.

**Why it matters.** The plan says the lesson is "don't say more than your test shows". What the viewer sees is that the heroine told the truth and eleven people died, just as the Randall warned. Together with Burnt Row, this is the season's loudest "questioning kills".

**Fix.** Give each cost its own cause.
- **The crush belongs to the gates alone.** Stage it on a Warden who won't lift the bar "until the needle settles" while people die.
- **The untested claim has its own cost: what the crowd does with it.**
  - She named a *who* (the Ancestors) with no *where* and no way to check. So the old story fills the gap: "Let the coin sort us", and the Great Tithe.
  - That is episode 4's rule (evidence with no explanation only feeds the old story), with the heroine as its source.
- **Rewrite the Randall's "Right about":** "A truth told without its test is swallowed by the old story." Change the twist-table row to match.
- **Optional, one line in 1.4: give the gate rule the founders' reason.**
  - A moving hall shakes the needles, so a Giving can't be read until the hall is still.
  - In episode 10, while Wells crosses, the city at the Graves goes still for Theta's needle, by choice. That is a rule questioned, understood and kept.

### 3. Nell repeats Burnt Row in episode 5, then scolds the Randall for it, and the plan doesn't notice

**Where.**
- The episode 4 hook: "Burn what leaves your body".
- The episode 5 cold open: "Nell kept the first two commands and ignored the third, because it came with no reason".
- Episode 5, act 4: "You didn't ask it anything. You just stopped burning."
- The grey mould's step-by-step build-up, added in round 1 (Craft 15).

**Problem.**
- At twenty, Iris dropped an unexplained burn rule without testing it, and seven people died.
- Nell drops the same rule, given by the same Randall, for the same reason. A neighbour's child touches the grey, and Hester burns half the house.
- Minutes later, Nell hands Iris's mistake back to her as wisdom, and the plan says "writers must not expand this line".
- The round 1 build-up made the harm Nell causes larger and easier to see, without changing how the scene judges her.

**Why it matters.** This is the season's best present-day proof that questioning a rule is not the same as throwing it away. Left unmarked, it teaches the opposite on both theme checks:
- the heroine is right even while doing the wrong thing;
- the one time she drops a rule, the story lets her off.

**Fix.**
- After Nell's line, the Randall answers in four words: "Neither did you, child."
- Nell looks at her own smoke-blackened hands.
- That makes the Randall right about something (the Randall test), and nobody lectures.
- From then on, Nell burns her waste in the glasshouse brazier: a rule she now keeps because she has seen why.

### 4. The Giving, the doom's engine, is never stopped on screen, which leaves "plain grief" standing

**Where.**
- Section 3, step 10: "Stop the Giving; bury the dead again".
- Section 9: "The Giving has stopped, and the dead are buried in the Old Graves again".
- No episode stages it.

**Problem.**
- The plan names the funerals as the engine of the doom. Moving the door only moves the problem: Give the dead at the new door, and a new Weight grows there.
- Stopping the Giving is the one repair that costs the city its comfort ("they stay with us: you can weigh them"), and it happens off screen.

**Why it matters.**
- Grief is the strongest rival to the theme. The season needs one scene in which grief is kept and the practice changes. Without it, a viewer may leave thinking grief itself was the problem.
- In episode 8, Hester says "Stop" when she won't hear that Da is in the Weight. That line is never paid off.

**Fix.** No new characters, and about a minute of screen time.
- **Episode 10 cold open.** The Order arrives at the Graves with the Great Tithe list, and with the crush dead on biers. The Order had held them back to be Given at the Great Tithe's dawn, so they will be the new door's first rite. Nell's "Cards first" halts both the Tithe and the Giving.
- **After the message.** WE. DIFFUSE. HERE. TOO. shows what Giving does beside a door. The families bury their dead instead: the first graves dug in 170 years, beside the humming Lift.
- **Hester digs.** She Gave Tom.
- **In 1.9, give the Giving the reason it was right once.** In the fever of Year 131 the dead outran the gravediggers, and the Lift carried the bodies out of the city. The plan's claim that every rite was "right once" then covers the Giving honestly.

### 5. The 62 is not "the one result the old story forbids": Ream's own Leaves can hold it, and the clause added to allow it is a patch with one job

**Where.**
- Episode 9, act 2: "No reading of the Ending can hold that number".
- Twist table, episode 9: "More than fair".
- The new last sentence of stipulation 2: "Given long enough beside it, more than half of crossers turn".
- The revision log, "Changes added".

**Problem.** The source page, and so the Leaves, carries two lines the plan never uses.
- A grey line (the Voice that Follows), under the simulator: "The odds needn't be a half — a real field would have a drift."
- A Plot line, right before the handhold verse: "The statisticians find the flip rate isn't fifty percent. A seventy-percent cure exists..."

This has three consequences.
- The doctrine "the drift is fifty" has stood for centuries beside a verse that says the rate isn't fifty. Section 1.8, reason 3, says every bad sign has a home in the doctrine, but this verse has none.
- The Order has a far stronger rescue for the 62 than a tiny new gloss: "Seventy in the hundred: foretold. The Ancestors are generous."
- The clause added to stipulation 2 buys what the grey verse already gives for free.

**Why it matters.** Try the fix on its case: does the 62 force the Randall to turn?
- It can't. A belief that swallows every outcome can swallow this one too, and the plan's own reason 3 says so.
- What turns her is her choice to let a failed answer stay failed. That is the Fable's "closing a question is a choice", which makes it a better scene.
- But the plan describes it wrongly. Writers told that "no reading can hold it" will play it as evidence forcing belief.

**Fix.**
- **Give the verse its standing gloss in 1.3:** "The flip rate isn't fifty — *yet*."
- **In episode 9, Dimopoulos's rescue quotes that verse,** and his carved gloss takes its words from it.
- **The Randall knows every verse.** She sees the rescue is there, and refuses it: "We said fifty. A failed answer stays failed."
- **Cut "no reading can hold"** from episode 9 and from the twist table.
- **Drop the new clause from stipulation 2.** List the 62 in 2.6 instead, as following from stipulation 2's "grows with time" and the Leaves' grey verse.
- **Optional: let the 62 catch Nell too.** Her episode 2 curve stops at "one in two", as if a fair coin were a ceiling. That brings back her flaw from episode 1, using scripture to dismiss what she can't count, for one beat.

### 6. On screen, questioning's death toll is counted, while the rites' toll is only described

**Where.** Episodes 4, 5, 7 and 8; section 1.8, reason 5.

**Problem.** The deaths that follow someone's question or test are shown or counted:
- Burnt Row: 7.
- CARROLL: Hanna Carroll.
- Nell's eleven, then the Joining: 13, Nan among them.
- Nell's dropped burn rule: the grey, a child's hand on it, and half a house burned.
- Nell's speech, then the crush: 11.

The rites' toll appears as three Called children and a montage of horror in episode 7, with no number attached. In episode 5 the Randall says "I asked a question once. It killed seven people." Episodes 4 and 8 then seem to prove her right.

**Why it matters.** The brief's message is the reverse. The plan knows the true cause of each of these deaths, but a viewer weighs what is counted.

**Fix.** Findings 2 and 3 take two of those deaths off questioning's side. Then add two things.
- **Episode 7, act 5.** Nell counts the RECEIVED Passings since Year 235 in the Weight-book and chalks a single number. On the plan's own rates it is roughly a thousand children or more; fix the exact figure in the numbers table. No speech.
- **Episode 4.** Use the viewer critique's Hanna Carroll line (its finding 9). It shows the Order Joining someone on a word from the needles long before Nell sent anything. The Joining then reads as the Order's habit, not the test's fault.

### 7. "The mint alone is witness" is still a secret, self-serving act at a turning point

**Where.**
- Sections 1.3 and 1.4.
- Section 1.8, reason 4: "A hidden Turned Randall carved it long ago... It closes every test a liar could not pass".
- Episode 1, act 3.
- The revision log flags it under "Still unresolved".

**Problem.**
- Every other step in the chain is sincere. This one is a hidden Turned keeper writing law to protect herself and her line: exactly the "one secret villain" the brief rules out.
- Swap it for a sincere origin and nothing else in the season breaks. The self-serving version has no job of its own.

**Fix.** Give the gloss a public, merciful origin that later Turned Randalls happen to shelter behind.
- **The origin.** Long ago, a signature test got a clean left-handed girl Passed, because her writing ran backward, before anyone opened the Jar. The Randall of the day carved "The mint alone is witness" in grief, in daylight.
- **Episode 1.** The Randall closes Pim's proposal with the true reason: "We signed once, child. A left-handed girl was Passed for it." She is right about something, and the scene shows that a test can be wrong too: a reading is not the thing read.
- **Section 1.8, reason 4, becomes:** a mercy rule that every hidden Randall since has had her own reasons to keep.

### 8. The meta device still costs the audience nothing: the verse the audience believed blamed Wells's reach, and the reach was a rescue

**Where.**
- Episode 1, act 2: Wells grabs Nell as she freezes, staring toward the cliff.
- Episode 1, act 3: "The hand that reached".
- Episode 7, act 2: the pair test.
- Episode 8, act 1: "the cliff Wells felt, and Mira: it drags crossers away".
- The twist table, episode 7.

**Problem.**
- The audience learned "the handhold is what did it" as law, and concluded from it that Wells's grab turned Nell. The Plot verse right after the handhold verse says the same: "grab a crosser mid-transit and turn them".
- The episode 7 test proves that a grip changes nothing. But it changes nobody's reading of any scene, because for the twins exactly one of them turned anyway.
- So the moved pair test (Theme 6) still does only one job: showing the method.

**Why it matters.** The brief called this device worth keeping. It only bites if the audience's belief made them misread something that matters.

**Fix.**
- **One beat in episode 8, act 1.** As she works out that the Weight drags crossers off (the cliff, Mira), Nell realises that Wells pulled her away from it. The hand the verse blamed kept her from being Called.
- **At the episode 10 table,** when she gives back his gloves, this stays unspoken or gets a single line.

This complements the viewer critique's finding 2, on the phone words that give the device away early.

### 9. THE GREY? straight after "Let him speak" can read as "the Pariah law was right"

**Where.** Episode 10, acts 3 and 5; section 9, "The residue"; section 2.6, "Carried germs".

**Problem.**
- The Randall ends her arc by undoing "Pass him on sight".
- Minutes later, the boy she let in sneezes Earth life onto the cards.
- To a viewer who remembers Burnt Row, the season's act of redemption looks like the same mistake: an old rule dropped, and the blight back.

**Why it matters.** It would end the season on "questioning is dangerous". The true point is "nothing is settled".

**Fix.** Let the new practice make the check it knows how to make, and still meet what it couldn't know.
- At the reunion, Hester, who burned her own house to stop the grey, strips off Wells's Minthome clothes and burns them.
- Kit's "Burn those" on Earth then mirrors her: two worlds taking the same precaution.
- The sneeze shows what burning clothes can't reach. THE GREY? becomes a new question, not a verdict on mercy.

### 10. In the finale, one voice still settles things

**Where.**
- Episode 10, act 2: the Randall says "A copy keeps its source's history... one voice, not two".
- Episode 10, act 5: "Wait."
- Section 9, "The residue": "Kit's thirty years of counting it in secret. The same doom has begun on the other side".

**Problem.** Three beats.
- **The quotation.** The crowd's claim, "Two peoples Give their dead. It must be holy", is answered by the former high keeper quoting a book. Swap her Fable line for a verse from the Leaves and the scene works the same way, by authority. That is the Changers' failure (episode 3), and it is the very thing the episode's own pitch asks about: "Will a new answer become a new scripture?"
- **The last image.** One right person facing a rite: Kit's old stance.
- **The residue.** It ties Minthome's doom to Kit's secrecy. By episode 4's rule, publishing a number changes nothing, so his secrecy was never the cause.

**Fix.**
- **Answer the crowd with a witness, not a quotation.** Lottie asks, "Did they find it out, or read it?" When Wells arrives, he answers from what he heard in episode 8: "I asked her. She said, 'Because the Leaves say so.'" The Randall's line can stay as her quiet recognition, or go.
- **End with Nell holding Kit's book out to Jory,** not only saying "Wait" to Adamala.
- **Reword the residue.** The doom on the other side is Minthome's unquestioned Giving. Kit's failure was that he had the reasons and built no test anyone else could run.

### 11. The hem seeds (Logic 1) tie the Turned Randalls' food to the children's turning

**Where.**
- Section 1.4: seeds sewn into the Tithe children's hems.
- Section 2.6: "Arkani's seeds" and "Living Turned".
- Brother Arkani: "One seed in four comes back right for the glasshouse now."

**Problem.**
- As written, the Randall's glasshouse is stocked from Tithe seeds that come back turned. Its useful seed has risen from one in fifty to one in four as more children turned.
- So the hidden Turned Randalls eat better as the doom gets worse, and only the Tithe feeds them.
- A viewer can read that as a motive to keep the children crossing.

**Why it matters.** It opens the villain rival the brief rules out, and nothing in the plan uses it on purpose.

**Fix.**
- **Add one line to 2.6:** turned crops breed true, and the glasshouse has grown its own seed for generations.
- **The hem seeds are kept only as a rite** ("for the Ancestors' garden"). Arkani sorts them out of habit, which is the only reason a count exists at all.
- **Both of their jobs survive:** Arkani's count, and a fossil of the founders' experiment.
- **If the link is kept instead,** it must be a horror the Randall discovers in episode 9, never something she knew.

### 12. Three statements of the theme in the plan name the wrong part

**Where.** Section 0 (the spine); section 1.9 (Year 131, and Years 131–235); section 3, "Why 'always' is earned".

**Problem and fix.** Each of these is an instruction to writers. Left as they are, they will leak into dialogue that contradicts episode 4.
- **"The year Ream stopped counting is the year Ream began building its doom", and "A counting city would have stopped the Giving at three hundred tonnes".**
  - But the Randalls kept counting in private, and episode 4 shows the full curve changing nothing. What Ottilie closed was not the count but the question of why the count and the book disagreed.
  - Write "the year Ream chose the book over the count", and "a city that asked why its count moved".
- **"Always": Minthome copied the Giving.**
  - By the show's own episode 10 line, a copy is one voice, not a second case, so it can't count as an independent turn of the wheel.
  - Say that it is the copy, shown on purpose. Point Minthome's own case at what wasn't copied:
    - its own rule, "the Mother is for the dead", which starves the Straights;
    - Kit, who had the reasons and built no test.
- **The spine: "Every rule in Ream was right once."**
  - The handhold law and "the drift is fifty" never were.
  - Write "every rite in Ream worked once".

### 13. The last clause of stipulation 7 is a patch with one job

**Where.** Section 2.5, stipulation 7: "A part still joined to a body that stays on the world... cannot turn". Also rule 4, and episode 6.

**Problem.** It was added only so that Nell's hand survives episode 6. Nothing else uses it.

**Fix.** It can be worked out from the rules rather than simply declared: a mirror-image hand cannot stay joined to a wrist that was not mirrored. Move it to 2.6 with that reason. The show's list of things it asks the audience to accept then shrinks by one clause.
