# SECONDS

*Season pitch, angle A: ten 55-minute episodes.*

**Logline.** An Essex family of thieves who can step through walls has one proud boast: Marlows always come back straight, never mirror-turned. Then their star crosser starts starving at the family table while they cheer every kilo she loses. The woman who keeps the family's numbers in her head must find what is turning them. The answer is the seconds she has squeezed out for them, and the only proof is the thing the family swore never to keep: paper.

The title means the seconds a crosser gets, second helpings at Peg's table, and a second go, the family's cure. None is ever spelled out.

## 1. Genre

**Lead: Crime.** The criminal is the hero, inside a family firm, and the pursuer looks like less than he is.
- **Promise taken from the profile:** crimes worth watching (the Mayfair lift, the clinic hop, the depot raid); a plain-looking master pursuer (Ray); a long fight between equals (the Marlows against Ray, and against the Aherns); deception on both sides (Rosie's hidden turn, Ray's hidden pantry); lines crossed (Ray feeds thieves, Tess goes to "them"); and a reckoning paid in kind: the family that kept nothing on paper is undone, and saved, by paper.
- **Guarantee broken: the family table is safe.** It breaks early, from inside. In episode 3 the table where Rosie is applauded is where she is starving; in episode 5 Peg's lucky fry-up sends Liam out half a kilo heavy. After that, every meal carries threat.
- **Question:** what, if anything, would exempt someone from the rules everyone else keeps? The Marlows' unspoken answer is "People like us look after our own": no police, no clinics, no letters, no paper. The ending shows the exemption was their cage.

**Guests.**
- **Gangster**, all season: the firm, the code at the table (Peg serves breakfast while Danny plans a war), a rival family, turncoats (Tess, then Ray), a successor.
- **Detective:** Ray hunts a "spinner" who doesn't exist. Fair clues; the answer to "who is to blame?" is no one, only habits.
- **Horror**, single scenes: running out, the flat stump, the black water.
- **Science Fiction:** one hinge technology and its traffic jams.

## 2. The world

**The normal.** Grays, on the Thames estuary, today. Thirty-one years ago something arrived in a field on Mucking Marsh: no crater, no trail, already flickering in and out of the world. The field was the Marlows' tenancy. The Ministry took field and object, and paid Peg and Frank enough to buy a gravel pit and a public weighbridge. From the object came the **rig**, a padded belt and collar that lifts you off the world for a few seconds. Crossing is now a trade like plumbing.

**What changed, and what that changed:**
- **Walls mean nothing, so weight means everything.** Gold sits under the City as dust in concrete, much of it Marlow concrete. Locksmiths became weightmen. Vaults have **weigh-walls** that feel a body in the dark within four metres and flood the goods with tungsten beads. Lifts that took two seconds in Frank's day now take eight or nine.
- **The turned.** About forty thousand Britons have come back mirror-turned, from surgery, rescue work or crime. The state's **Register** feeds them free mirror rations and puts them on a list the police can read. Obverse, the firm that makes the food, sells a private "Restoration" for £180,000, seventy per cent success, method confidential. Its share price tripled.
- **Licensed crossers**, such as surgeons who lift tumours out of children without a cut, are held to three seconds "for safety." The trade thinks them soft.
- **The Looking Act** makes looking inside a body a search, so police need a warrant to look in your stomach. Police don't cuff crossers; they take their rigs.
- **Taken for granted:** phones have a "turned mode"; passports read HAND: C or HAND: T; the BBC weather reports "next-door events"; Kieran's school poster calls dark matter "the neighbours' galaxies"; Thames watermen shrug at the Nobody Tide, a slosh on no moon's timetable. Crossers map the next world by weight alone: **the Hill**, a mountain felt over Mucking Marsh ("the Hill takes you"), and **the Deep**, a soft, wide pull under Grays.

**The rules of crossing, shown in action, never lectured.**
- In **the black** between worlds, the only light is what your field carries. Within arm's reach you see the inside of everything; beyond that, nothing. Radio can't cross, so you are alone with your count.
- On a Marlow rig, **every kilo under a hundred buys a fifth of a second.** Your fingertips tingle as the time runs out. Stay past it and you don't come back: you become a weight in an empty room, slowly spreading.
- Any crossing can bring you back **turned**, and you can't feel it. Turned, you get nothing from ordinary food or medicine, and no germ can touch you. You starve in about six months unless fed mirror food. The cure is another crossing, with the same dice.

## 3. The culture that never questions: the Marlows

Marlow Aggregates: a gravel pit, a concrete plant, and a public weighbridge whose office is Peg's kitchen. By night, they lift. The code is spoken, never written, and every rule was right once.

Why each was right, *but* how it grows the doom:
- **Nothing on paper.** Paper is evidence; its fibres even show police whether a thief turned. *But* no pattern across years can be seen, so each turning feels like one person's bad luck.
- **Make weight.** Lighter means more seconds; Peg made it law after Frank "ran out because he was fifteen stone." *But* thinness is admired, so a starving crosser looks like the most devoted one.
- **Lightest goes longest.** The lightest has seconds to spare. *But* the best and youngest get the longest jobs.
- **Face home.** It keeps you on your own world. *But* they believe it also keeps you straight, so a turn means you failed, and the turned hide it.
- **We feed our own.** It kept the turned off the police's list. *But* each turned mouth costs £300 a day: more jobs, longer jobs, more turned.
- **Never go to them.** Clinics and the Register report crossers. *But* it cuts them off from the one number that would save them, and the letter that would have saved Liam.
- **Blood pays blood.** For twenty years it kept rivals away. *But* a turning with an enemy nearby becomes a war.

**The doom, step by step.**
1. When jobs took two seconds, almost nobody turned, and "Marlows come back straight" became the family's pride.
2. Families like theirs got so good that the City moved to weight, and jobs grew longer.
3. The family answered with its rules: lose more weight, send the lightest longest. Tess grew proud of squeezing out tenths of a second.
4. Long jobs turn people. Each turning read as a failure to face home, so the turned hid it or were fed, which meant more jobs.
5. With no paper, nobody saw that every turned Marlow had been on a long job.
6. One turned near a rival, and blood paid blood.
7. Then the world changed again, one world over, and a binned letter made that fatal.

## 4. Characters

**TESS MARLOW, 38, the weigher.** She keeps every number in her head. The family's lightest crosser from fourteen to twenty-six, she never turned; jobs were short then. Now she plans every lift.
- *Want:* keep the family fed and whole; this season, keep Rosie working and get her well.
- *Wound:* the week her father died, Peg made the fourteen-year-old the family's crosser, and made her light. She has counted every mouthful since.
- *Flaw:* she answers every loss with a tighter number, and she decides things for others. "Margins are for people who can't count."
- *Secret:* she found Rosie's extra seconds by pushing her to lose four more kilos.
- *Levers:* competence, silent counting, love for Kieran and Rosie, a pencil behind her ear she never uses.
- *Arc:* she keeps the numbers, then writes them down, then hands the pencil over.

**ROSIE MARLOW, 22, Danny's daughter, the lightest adult crosser.**
- *Want:* to be the best ever. Secretly, to lift tumours out of children. After Mayfair, to fix herself before anyone knows.
- *Wound:* she has been praised for being hungry since she was twelve.
- *Flaw:* pride that hides weakness.
- *Secret:* she has been turned since the pilot.
- *Levers:* competence, humour, and the audience knowing her secret before her family does.
- *Arc:* she hides, then tells, then chooses.

**PEG MARLOW, 72, the founder.**
- *Want:* a family that is together, proud and owes nobody.
- *Wound:* the Ministry took her farm, and the black took Frank.
- *Flaw:* certainty. She bins official letters and remembers what she needs to.
- *Secret:* one kept even from herself: she weighed Frank the morning he died.
- *Levers:* warmth, humour, the fry-up, the chalk board.
- *Arc:* the code in person becomes the first Marlow to write on paper.

**DANNY MARLOW, 41, the foreman.** At 88 kilos he is good for a 2.4-second hop, or for sending just his hand through a wall.
- *Want:* keep his kids safe; then revenge.
- *Wound:* at seventeen he was too heavy to take his father's place, and his little sister was chosen.
- *Flaw:* every loss is an attack.
- *Secret:* he said yes to Liam.
- *Levers:* love for his kids, humour, grief.
- *Arc:* he turns himself trying to turn an enemy, carries his son's death in his wallet for five episodes, and finally asks the question that explains it.

**DS RAY AMBROSE, 54, Met Handedness Unit.** Thin, vodka at his desk, lard on crackers; colleagues think he's a drunk. He turned eleven years ago carrying a child out of a collapsed building, and those are among the few things he can digest.
- *Want:* stop the Marlows dying, and catch the "spinner" he believes is turning them.
- *Wound:* his first case, in 2002. A Hatton Garden weigh-wall read 64.3 kilos where nobody stood, spreading for a month.
- *Flaw:* he hoards what he knows, and thinks feeding people is the whole of caring for them.
- *Secret:* he is "the Chemist," who supplies Peg's back kitchen.
- *Levers:* competence, decency, dry wit, his own hunger.
- *Arc:* from secret feeder to open accomplice, saved by the family's first real question.

**MAGGIE AHERN, 44, the Ahern weigher across the river in Gravesend, Tess's mirror image.** The Aherns run tugs and salvage by the same code; Frank taught it to her father, Declan. One difference: Maggie opens her post.
- *Want:* protect her son Joe, win the war, then end it.
- *Wound:* her uncle Con, turned, lives off her kitchen. *Flaw:* she matches every blow.
- *Secret:* the body in the black beside Peg's office was Joe, sent by her.
- *Levers:* competence, a son, and being Tess's reflection.
- *Arc:* from enemy to the other half of the count. Declan never changes.

**Supporting:** Kieran, 12, Tess's son, who wants to cross and asks the questions nobody answers; Liam, 19, Danny's big-boned son, desperate to be a real crosser; Terry, 66, Peg's brother, turned five years, who cooks food he cannot share; cousin Shaun; Joe Ahern, 17; Dilys of the launderette.

## 5. The ten episodes

**1. "Straight."** *The Marlows pull off the cleanest lift of their lives and get back a painting nobody believes is real.*
- **Cold open.** 3 a.m., Mayfair. Rosie, 44 kilos, steps off the world beside a private bank's art store. In the black we see only what her field lights: the inside of the wall, a guard's ribs, the painting in its crate. Beads pour; she lifts it clear by half a second. At 3:12 something heavy passes in the dark; she flinches. In the van Danny asks, as the family always does, "Straight?" "Straight." She looks a beat too long at a crisp packet.
- **Acts.** Fry-up at the weighbridge, under the chalk board of weights and Frank's one photo, a big man. Tess found Rosie 9.4 seconds, past Peg's old limit of eight. They burn the tissue paper. The Chemist's laundry van drops off Terry's mirror food. On the table, "The Pearl Weigher" has its window on the opposite side from the catalogue photo. Nobody looks. Peg to Kieran: "Your grandad was fifteen stone when the black took him. That's why we make weight." Trading Standards: "Twenty-four years of audit rolls in your loft, Mrs Marlow, by law." "Bloody paper." At Kieran's birthday everyone is weighed on the bridge to cheers. Rosie turns a jam lid the wrong way and signs his card in flawless mirror writing. "Show-off," says Tess.
- **Twist.** The insurer's expert says the light falls from the wrong side, so it's a copy. No money.
- **Hook.** Ray lays two photos on Peg's counter. "Linen has a hand, Mrs Marlow. This canvas came back the wrong way round. It's the original. If the painting turned, so did whoever carried it." Leaving: "I met your dad once." "You never met my dad." "I met what was left of him."

**2. "The Pearl Weigher."** *To get paid, the Marlows must prove the painting is real, and the only proof is that one of them turned.*
- **Cold open.** 4 a.m. Rosie reads her phone in a make-up mirror, then eats a spoon of something white by fridge-light.
- **Acts.** Peg's plan: steal the painting back and turn it back with more crossings. Rosie volunteers at once. Tess, uneasy at how grey she looks, allows only three-second hops. After each of seven the family peers at the painting; Rosie's eyes go to the cutting on the wall. It stays wrong. Terry: "I went again twenty-two times. The black never gave me back." Kieran finds Rosie's phone in turned mode. "Setting's broke."
- **Twist.** Rosie walks into the Obverse clinic on Harley Street. A sign tells patients never to touch staff in transit.
- **Hook.** "A hundred and eighty thousand. Seventy per cent." Outside, Rosie eyes the jeweller's across the road.

**3. "Making Weight."** (First major reversal.) *Rosie's weight keeps falling and her family keeps cheering.*
- **Cold open.** The weigh-in: 41.8. Applause. "That's a Marlow."
- **Acts.** Short of money since the painting, Peg gives Rosie the longest job yet: ten seconds, a Hatton Garden diamond room. Tess argues and loses. Ray tells Tess the Mayfair weigh-wall logged two bodies in the black at 3:12, and that the law won't let him look inside her niece: "So I wait till she comes to me hungry." Tess tests Rosie: write your name, fast. Rosie has practised; she passes. Kieran finds lard in her van. After the job Rosie folds. Tess squeezes a glucose gel into her mouth. Nothing. Another. Nothing. One spoonful of Terry's pot opens her eyes.
- **Twist.** Rosie has been turned since Mayfair. The thinness they cheered was starvation.
- **Hook.** At the table Rosie says, "Something took hold of me in the black." There were two bodies in the log, and the Aherns work Mayfair. Peg: "Blood pays blood."

**4. "The Other Bank."** *The war begins, and across the river the enemy is living the same nightmare.*
- **Cold open.** A quake with no fault shakes Thurrock; the radio suspects "a next-door event." Danny, back from a hop: "The Hill's moved." Peg: "Hills don't move."
- **Acts.** Danny sends his hand, which lasts a long time in the black, through the Aherns' cold-store wall, and the crew lifts their mirror rations. Across the river: Maggie, Joe, Declan, and Con, turned, whose food has just vanished. At Grays a lorry driver says the bridge has him two hundred kilos light. Peg: "Then you've had a good day." A Trading Standards envelope goes in the bin unopened. At 2 a.m. Danny feels a body in the black beside Peg's office, looking into the safe.
- **Twist.** The Aherns lost Aoife to a turn last autumn, with a Marlow crew nearby. They have been at war for months.
- **Hook.** Maggie rings Tess: "Con hasn't eaten in four days."

**5. "Delivered."** *The war turns lethal, and the family's heaviest boy starves himself to join it.*
- **Cold open.** Peg opens her safe to find a bullet sitting on the painting. The safe was never opened.
- **Acts.** Con dies of hunger; the Marlows are killers too. Shaun comes back from a reprisal a hair slow, his leg still in the black when the field closed: the stump is a clean flat plane, bloodless for a second. Liam, who has secretly lost ten kilos, begs for the next lift, at the Ahern salvage lock-up. Danny says yes. Tess weighs him: 62.4, so 7.5 seconds. The job needs 7.4. No margin. Peg sends him off with the lucky fry-up; he eats every bite.
- **Twist.** A tenth of a second from home, Liam runs out. The Ahern weigh-wall goes on reading him.
- **Hook.** Maggie lets the family stand one minute beside the reading, as the lost are mourned now, and gives Danny the printout: 63.6 kg. He folds it into his wallet, then weighs himself: 88. "Two seconds is enough to grab someone."

**6. "Ten Seconds."** (Second major reversal, part one.) *Danny tries to turn an enemy, and Tess breaks into a clinic for a cure and finds a clock.*
- **Cold open.** Two seconds in the black. Danny catches Joe Ahern mid-crossing and wrenches him round. "Got him."
- **Acts.** At breakfast the bacon tastes of nothing and Terry's pot smells like home. A grip in the black turns one of the two, and Danny is the one. The Chemist's bill trebles. Tess decides to rob the cure. Rosie is too weak and Danny too heavy, so Tess crosses for the first time in twelve years: a 2.5-second hop into Obverse's records room. The tingle comes back like an old song. In the car she reads the protocol: "Transit 10.0 s. Expected restoration 0.70." Below it, the odds of turning, second by second.
- **Twist.** The cure is nothing but time, and so is the curse. That is why surgeons are held to three seconds, and why Rosie, sent in for nine, turned.
- **Hook.** Tess eats a biscuit to learn whether her own hop turned her. It tastes of biscuit. She looks at Rosie. "I gave you nine."

**7. "Slack Water."** (Second major reversal, part two.) *Two enemy weighers meet on the ferry and lay their families' secrets side by side.*
- **Cold open.** The Nobody Tide slaps the Tilbury pontoon under no moon.
- **Acts.** In the loft Tess has found what nobody knew the family kept: twenty-four years of bridge tickets, one for every job-day weigh-in. Weight gives the seconds; memory gives who turned. On the ferry, neutral water, Maggie brings the Ahern side. Joe felt a weight at 3:12 and flinched; the log puts the two bodies six metres apart throughout. Aoife turned on a nine-second job; Joe came back from Danny's grab straight. On the back of a ferry timetable the women draw the first graph either family has made. Every turned Marlow and Ahern sits on one line.
- **Twist.** Nobody spun anybody except Danny, and he spun himself. Con died in a war about nothing.
- **Hook.** Peg feeds the timetable to the office stove. Danny: "And Liam? Did your sums do Liam too?" The line can't explain running out. Peg: "You went to them." Tess is put out. Next morning the Chemist's laundry van doesn't come.

**8. "The Chemist."** *With their secret supply cut off, the Marlows find out who has really been feeding them.*
- **Cold open.** Ray, suspended, faces an audit: seven claimants on his caseload have been dead for years and still draw rations.
- **Acts.** The pantry empties; Terry, Rosie and Danny turn grey. Peg's answer is the family cure, "go again": fourteen short hops for Rosie in the flooded gravel pit, blindfolded, facing home. Nothing. Tess traces the van to Dilys's launderette and the ration batch codes to Ray. For five years the man hunting them has fed them on dead people's food: "I watched your dad's weight spread for a month. I wasn't watching it again." He never asked them to change. Tess tells the family: "We never fed our own." Peg's pride breaks, and she answers with more: raid the Register's depot, long crossings. To keep Rosie off it, Tess calls Ray's colleagues. Rosie is taken to the Register's ward: no locks, only food.
- **Twist.** The Chemist is Ray.
- **Hook.** Rosie eats her first real meal in months while the ward TV explains that the fault-free quakes spell a molecule of the other hand. Front pages: "COME ROUND FOR TEA."

**9. "The Deep."** *The family raids the state's pantry while Rosie tries to leave the world.*
- **Cold open.** Peg does the numbers now. Shaun's lost leg has bought him a whole second, so he takes the longest leg of the raid.
- **Acts.** Terry comes back straight and eats his first bacon sandwich in five years; Shaun comes back turned. Peg calls it proof that going again works. On the ward Rosie grows strong and decides to go where the tea is. The Hill takes you; the Deep, she reasons, is soft. Only food holds her, so she walks out through the wall. At night on the bridge, Kieran asleep in the office, Rosie for the first time in her life does not face home. She follows the Deep and steps out one world over into black water: cold, pressure, salt, no floor. She fires her rig, faces home, and lands on the weighbridge in a gush of sea. The printer chatters. "It's sea. Under us. All of it."
- **Twist.** Next door, at the Marlows' address, is the floor of an ocean that doesn't know they exist. In the morning Peg mops it up and tips the bucket down the drain.
- **Hook.** Tess rings Ray. "What did my dad weigh?" "Sixty-four kilos."

**10. "Paper."** (Third major reversal.) *The family reads its own record for the first time and must decide whether to give it away.*
- **Cold open.** 2002, as Peg remembers it: a big Frank on the bridge at dawn, joking while the ticket prints.
- **Acts.**
  - In the loft, Frank's tickets read 12 stone 4 the week after his four-second Farringdon job, then fall every week for six months to 10 stone 2 the morning he died, with Peg's own pencilled "7.1." It is Rosie's curve. The cold open replays as it was: a thin man who can't open the jam jar, Peg laughing as she does it for him. Ray's strongroom trace agrees: 64.3 kilos, held still until the seconds ran out.
  - Peg picks up the pencil and writes beside the ticket: "Frank. 10 st 2. Turned." All night the family writes every crossing they remember onto the rolls. Maggie crosses the river with her count and her own scale.
  - At Liam's line Danny unfolds his printout: 63.6. The roll says 62.4. "Why's it a kilo out?" Kieran remembers the lorry driver; Peg, the envelope. A sack of cement on both scales: the Marlow bridge reads light. A mountain moved next door, a letter in the bin, a fry-up, a number cut to the tenth.
  - At dawn they offer Ray the cure. The bridge gives him 8.4 seconds, Maggie's scale 8.3; a week ago they'd have sent him for 8.4. They send him for 7.5 and tell him the odds. He signs the roll afterwards in plain writing.
  - Rosie chooses to stay turned: "Can't catch anything. There's wards that need that." Danny: "I'm done making weight."
- **Twist.** The family's founding lesson was a memory that had turned in the dark, and the family's own scale killed Liam.
- **Ending.** Cars at the gate. The rolls are twenty-four years of evidence and the only honest record of what time in the black does. Peg's stove is lit. Tess holds the boxes and asks each of them. Kieran: give. Rosie: give. Danny: give. Peg carries them out herself.

## 6. Twist schedule and plants

Twist, plants, and what a swap would break.
- **The fake is the original (1).** Catalogue photo; burned tissue. Only an object that turns with its carrier sets up episode 2 and Ray's hook.
- **Rosie is turned (3).** Crisp packet, jam lid, mirror card (1); make-up mirror, eyes on the wall, turned mode, clinic (2); lard and applause. "Make weight" must already be admired, or her thinness reads as illness.
- **The Aherns mirror the Marlows (4).** Two bodies in the log (3). The ferry needs a symmetrical war.
- **Liam runs out (5).** The fifth-of-a-second rule and the fry-up (1); lorry driver and envelope (4). A spin or a bullet would close his cause too early.
- **The cure is the curse (6).** Seven short hops that fail (2); Terry's twenty-two tries; the surgeons' three seconds. It lands because we watched short hops cure nothing.
- **Danny spins himself (6).** Clinic sign (2); "two seconds is enough" (5). The grab repays him in kind.
- **Nobody spun anybody (7).** Six metres in the log (3); the rolls in the loft (1). It needs the paper nobody knew was kept.
- **The Chemist is Ray (8).** The laundry van (1–7); Ray's thinness; "what was left of him." It turns "we feed our own" inside out.
- **The Deep is a sea (9).** The Hill and the Deep in lore (1); "COME ROUND FOR TEA" (8). It answers the exile fantasy the message planted.
- **Frank's weight (10).** "Fifteen stone" plus the fifth-of-a-second rule (1): 95 kilos buys one second, too few for any job; the jam jar. The sum was fair from episode 1, and no Marlow ever did it.
- **The scale killed Liam (10).** Quake, lorry driver, envelope (4); the printout (5). It gives Danny his question, and saves Ray.

## 7. Fourth Direction items used, and their jobs

Job in brackets.

**The neighbours.** The object with no crater, already unpinned (the family's origin and pride); their ocean at your address (the Deep, which ends Rosie's escape); the earthquake with no fault (the Hill moves, the bridge reads light, Liam dies); first contact in mass, a molecule of the wrong hand, "come over" (COME ROUND FOR TEA, Rosie's temptation); the sloshing lake, dark-matter maps, silent mergers, astronomy as neighbourhood watch (everyday background).

**The budget.** Tingling fingertips (the only clock); the lightest courier goes furthest ("lightest goes longest"); running out as seventy kilos of spreading weight (Liam, Frank, a new way to mourn); the partial return and the amputee's extra seconds (Shaun, and Peg's arithmetic); sending a hand (Danny's craft).

**Walls and eyes.** Gold as dust in concrete, and weight-makers (success lengthens the jobs); the bullet "delivered" (the war's threat); the tumour lifted from a child (Rosie's want); looking is a search (Ray kept at arm's length); privacy has a radius (Joe beside the office); cuffs open (police take rigs).

**Turning.** Slide and turn feel alike, every crossing a coin toss ("Straight?"); wrapping-paper forensics (the linen canvas); the "forged" painting lit from the wrong side (the signature crime); mirror script and jar lids (Rosie's tells, Frank's, Ray's plain signature); a drift that isn't a half, and ten seconds for a seventy-per-cent cure (the second reversal); the grip in transit and assassination by spin (Danny spins himself); murder that looks like a diet ("making weight"); water, vodka and lard (Rosie's secret, Ray's desk); unmedicable yet immune (the useless gel; Rosie stays turned); passports that record handedness (Rosie can't run).

**Feeding and curing.** The mirror kitchen and the tripled stock (Obverse); custody by pantry (the ward, Ray's supply line); blindfold training in water ("go again" in the pit); the wrong pull and exile as a cure ("the Hill takes you"; Rosie's sea); the held breath (salt, then the drain).

**Weight as witness.** Whoever holds the reference sample decides who is normal, and nobody fakes a mountain (the roll outranks memory); the drift as Earth's most valuable secret (a gravel firm gives it away).

## 8. Mysteries and wonders

**Mysteries (pressed hard, all answered):** What is wrong with Rosie? (3) Who is spinning the Marlows? (7) Who is the Chemist? (8) Why did Liam run out, what did Ray see of Frank, will Rosie live, and will the Marlows be caught, at what price? (10)

**Wonders (glimpsed, never chased, left open):** what the neighbours are and mean ("tea" is a tabloid's guess); why the object came to the Marlows' field; what the Hill is; what Rosie tasted besides salt; why Frank held still until his seconds ran out.

## 9. The ending: what it settles and what it costs

**Epilogue, months later.** Obverse's table is on every front page ("The Ten-Second Cure: a gravel firm's records"), and turned people are cured on licensed rigs for the price of an appointment. Tess is in prison, having claimed every number as hers; Danny too, fed by the state he refused. Ray is charged. Peg, on bail, runs the bridge with Kieran, who writes each lorry's weight on the roll and adds in pencil, "checked against Maggie's." Last image: across the river, an Ahern girl steps on a bathroom scale while Declan chalks the number and the family cheers.

**It settles** why the Marlows turned, who fed them, why Liam died and what Frank weighed. The family learns to count and check. The war ends between the two women who compared numbers.

**It costs** Liam, Con and Frank's legend; Tess's and Danny's freedom; Danny, turned for life; Ray's career; Peg's pride; the firm. The Aherns, unchanged, still cheer a child's hunger.

## 10. The show's ledger

**Accepted from The Fourth Direction:** the seven laws as labelled there, and the theorems walls become weights, privacy has a radius, the cure uses the same dice, the mirror is relative, custody by pantry, one honest channel. **Not** asserted: exile as a cure (tested; it finds a sea) and "the neighbours' life is the other hand" (only the world's reading of the message).

**The show's own new guesses, declared once and never added to:**
1. **Drift.** The chance of turning grows with time in the black: 1 in 25 at two seconds, 1 in 8 at four, 1 in 3 at six, even at eight, and 7 in 10 at ten.
2. **Budget.** On a Marlow rig, every kilo under a hundred buys a fifth of a second. Clinic rigs buy more.
3. **The grip.** A grip in the black always turns one of the two crossers, and which one is a coin toss.
4. **Next door.** Here the neighbours have a sea under Grays and a mountain over Mucking Marsh. They move the mountain in episode 4, changing local weight by nearly one part in a hundred: the source's own dial, applied here.

**Derived, with nothing new added:** radio can't cross, so the crosser is alone; a mirrored canvas can't be a forgery; ordinary glucose can't revive a turned body; stepping out inside the neighbours' rock kills you ("the Hill takes you"); facing home keeps you on your world but does nothing about turning; a turned reader sees print right way round in a mirror; a bridge weighs you but a rig counts your mass, so a mountain moved next door makes the bridge lie about your seconds.

**On screen:** guesses 2 and 4 appear in episode 1 (Tess's rule, crossers' lore); guesses 1 and 3 in episode 6 (the protocol, Danny's grab).

## 11. Check against the owner's six rules

1. **Not The Catch.** None of its devices or set pieces; no "the world stayed put, she changed" reveal; no ending about who can share food; no mint test.
2. **Religion.** Left out. The one new rite is secular and native to this world: a minute beside a lost crosser's weight.
3. **The second attachment.** Never referenced: no book, maxims, Greek names or recited ideas.
4. **More of The Fourth Direction.** Close to forty items, nearly all carrying plot.
5. **Never name the ideas.** The theme lives in a chalk board, a burned timetable, a binned envelope and a pencil. The only questions are ordinary ones: "Why's it a kilo out?"
6. **Not a dystopia.** Essex is ordinary and works: the state feeds the turned, inspectors check scales, scientists share data, courts protect bodies. Only one family, and its mirror across the river, never ask. The doom grows from admired habits, not a villain, and Tess is wrong often enough (Rosie's nine seconds, Liam's missing margin, deciding for Rosie) that the heroine is plainly not always right.
