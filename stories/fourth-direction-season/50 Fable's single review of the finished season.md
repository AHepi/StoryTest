# 50 Fable's single review of the finished season

*Fable 5.1, at extra-high effort, gave one review of the finished season (file 49), and did nothing else. Spoilers throughout.*

---

**What I read, and how.** I read your brief, your second feedback, the notes on *The Catch*, and the master plan (version 3) first, then all ten episodes of `full-season.md` from the first line to the last, in order. Everything I say below I checked against the text: every line I quote is on the page, every number I mention I re-did by hand (a fifth of a second per kilo under a hundred, and 1.1 per cent light on the Grays scales from 4 April). I read scriptments, which are episodes written as scenes with the key lines of dialogue, not full scripts, and nobody has filmed them, so where I talk about pace I am judging from the page.

A few words I use, explained once: a **plant** is a detail put in early so that a later surprise plays fair; a **cold open** is the scene before the title; a **reversal** is a moment that flips what the audience thought was going on; an **action line** is the description between the dialogue, which a viewer never hears but which tells the director and actors what to do.

---

## 1. Verdict

*SECONDS* is a ten-part crime drama about an Essex family of thieves, the Marlows, who can step through walls for a few seconds at a time, and whose one boast is that they always come back the right way round. It is warm, funny, exact and frightening, and it does the thing you asked for: a family that never questions its own rules is destroyed by them, one comfortable habit at a time, without anyone ever saying so. The three big reversals (Rosie has been starving at the table where she is cheered; the cure and the curse are both just time in the black; the family's founding lesson was a memory that had turned) each make you re-read everything before them, and each is planted fairly. Every invented law from *The Fourth Direction* is in the show and doing work, and I found no scene that breaks one.

**Is it finished?** Yes, as a season. It is complete, consistent from episode to episode, and ready to hand on. I found two things I would fix before calling it final (one logic seam in the war plot that a sharp viewer will catch, and one implausible gift to Rosie in the epilogue), and seven smaller things that could go either way. None of them needs a restructure; the biggest is a line and a look.

## 2. How it keeps your rules

**The core message is shown, never stated.** Nobody makes a speech about asking questions. The idea lives in habits you can see: the ticket that goes into the stove ("Paper talks. Burn it.", Ep 1 Sc 3), the drawer of unopened envelopes that "won't shut" (Ep 1 Sc 9), the chorus "Never go back twice" (Ep 1 Sc 3), the applause at the weigh-in, "Straight?" "Straight." said after every crossing by people who cannot know. The questions that are asked on screen are the ordinary ones the plan allows: "Why's it a kilo out?" (Danny, Ep 9 Sc 12), "How do you know it's not the weight?" (Tess, Ep 9 Sc 11), "What did my dad weigh?" (Ep 9 Sc 13). The one place it edges towards being said is a refrain in Episode 7 ("Nobody ever asked why", "Nobody counted her"), which I list as a quibble below.

**The twists are fair and mind-bending.** I checked each big one for its plants:
- Rosie turned since Mayfair (Ep 3): the crisp packet she looks at "a beat too long" (Ep 1 Sc 1), the dog, the jar lid, the mirror-script card, Terry's "Goes through me like water, love. Since I turned." (Ep 1 Sc 13), the seven glances at the catalogue cutting (Ep 2), the lard "For the pit" (Ep 3 Sc 11).
- Liam runs out though the numbers said he'd make it (Ep 5): "And everything in you counts, breakfast too" (Ep 1 Sc 3), the test weight reading 19.78 (Ep 4 Sc 1), Mick "two hundred light" (Ep 4 Sc 11), the council envelope into the drawer (Ep 4 Sc 11), and the fry-up the camera stays on (Ep 5 Sc 13).
- The cure is time (Ep 6): the TV surgeon at three seconds, "She's done four thousand. Never turned." (Ep 1 Sc 10); Terry's "Eleven times I went again. Two seconds a go. Hungry every time after." (Ep 1 Sc 8); seven three-second hops that do nothing (Ep 2).
- A Marlow floor turned Aoife (Ep 7): Danny at the table, "Mynors had us back last autumn, for the floor under the one we did in '24. Two floors now. Long time to be nowhere." (Ep 2 Sc 6); Declan, "Their vans were outside it all week" (Ep 4 Sc 8).
- The scale has lied since April, and killed Liam with Peg's plate (Ep 9): Kieran's graph, the "chipped" weight, the brass balance that "Never told me a lie" (Ep 1 Sc 12).
- Frank turned and hid it (Ep 10): "Your grandad was fifteen stone when the black took him" said directly under the painted rule (Ep 1 Sc 6), which any viewer can turn into "95 kilos buys one second"; the jam jar (Ep 1 Sc 13); Ray's "I met what was left of him" (Ep 1 Sc 18).
Every one of these re-reads earlier scenes rather than bolting on a surprise, and every one grows from the book's own logic (cargo turns with its carrier; the mirror is relative; only weight crosses; a slide and a turn feel the same).

**Every invented law is used and none is broken.** I went through the plan's ledger against the scenes. The stack and the neighbours-as-weight: Kieran's poster, the Twitch, the April dig felt in Grays but "In London, nobody felt a thing" (Ep 4 Sc 1). Unpinning and the budget: every crossing counted in tenths, the tingle always about two seconds before the end (9.0 of 11.2 at Mayfair; 9.2 at Marrable Street; 4.9 for Liam's true 6.98; 6.3 for Ray's true 8.3). Dispersal: Liam, Frank and Aoife are readings on a wall, never bodies. Partial return: Shaun's leg, Danny's fingers, both "bloodless for a second". A sent hand that is blind: "We stay on Danny's face, eyes shut" (Ep 4 Sc 5). Knots: the Mayfair crate and the Ahern tugs. Rotation and the reference sample: the linen, the catalogue photo, Peg's meter, the T-meter. No up in the dark: the pit, the bubbles, and Rosie in the Deep. The grip: Danny's fingers. Handedness as fate: Con's pills "Made to his hand" (Ep 4 Sc 14), the passport box, the dog, the milk, Den's "wrong-way flesh" (Ep 8 Sc 10). The other hand next door: proved by an instrument and a man's blood, not a taste ("Two meters. Same blood. Both of them right.", Ep 9 Sc 9). Exile as a cure: Terry. Custody by pantry: the ward with one lock, on the pantry. The only honest channel: a molecule spelled in tremors. The field as the door, worked out on a baking tray from three records (the receipt, the tide tables, the sea). I checked the sums that matter and they hold: Liam 62.4 chalk, 63.1 true, plus 1.5 of notes and 0.5 of breakfast makes 65.1 and 6.98 seconds; Terry 70.6 on the lying plate is 71.4 on the steelyard; Ray 58.0 and 58.6, 8.4 and 8.3.

**No echo of *The Catch*.** No lift or shaft (a job is a "dip"; the rig "takes you off the world"); no kind figure from next door, no ship, no child's drawing, no toy; no backwards signs seen through a turned character's eyes (the crisp packet is "an ordinary packet of salt and vinegar", camera on the straight side); no dishes of germs, flask or mould (the only "flask" is tea; the only "dish" is a pie dish and a surgeon's steel dish); no needle showing a visitor live (the 02:04 ticket and the Twitch ticket are found afterwards; Danny's ambush is played on the wall clock); no hands meeting on glass; and the season's moral question is not "may you decide for someone else" but "did you write it down and check it against someone else's". The one shared law, a turned body's germs, is handled through law, gloves, gel, a stripped-and-washed step next door and a jumper wrung down a drain, never as a growth.

**No banned words or named ideas.** I searched the whole file for every word on the plan's list (conjecture, criticism, error correction, refute, falsify, fallible, "good explanation", "hard to vary", growth of knowledge, static or dynamic society, tradition, authority, optimism, "nothing is settled", "question everything", "the problem of", infinity) and none appears. The Convention, the seed-bank affair, the Looking Act and the Privacy Order are never named; on screen they are "step out on purpose, it's twenty years" (Ep 5 Sc 13), a ring in a man's stomach (Ep 3 Sc 5) and "Police don't get the list, love. Not since 2019." (Ep 8 Sc 10).

**Not a dystopia.** The wider world is ordinary and works, and is mostly kind: Trading Standards inspects, a nurse hands out a meter and tells Rosie the first number will frighten her, a fireman goes home with his first ration box and a postcard about leeks, Dr Osei keeps her secret to keep forty thousand people fed. Only two families on one river never check, and they are the only people who cross for long.

By Episode 9 Shaun says it and Peg's meter reads LO "in front of everyone"; in Episode 10, when Ray comes back, "Nobody asks him anything" and Kieran holds out the meter instead. The ritual dies on screen without a word about it.

## 4. The problems, most serious first

### SUBSTANTIVE 1. The family's "spinner" theory is refuted by a rule they state in the same breath, and nobody notices for three episodes

**Where.** Episode 4, Scene 2 (the war council), then Episode 6, Scene 2, then Episode 7, Scenes 7 and 12.

**The problem.** At the council Tess names how Rosie must have been turned: "Turn her round in the black and send her home to her tea — that's the only way it could be done." Thirty lines later the same scene lays down the grip rule, in full: "TESS: Grab someone in there and one of you turns. Coin says which." "TESS: Collar does nothing. It's skin, or it's nothing." "ROSIE (from the stairs): And whoever turns leaves the hand in there. Girl at the clinic's is plastic." And the camera then shows the fact that settles it: "On the stairs, Rosie turns her own hand over and looks at it — both sides." Rosie has both hands. By the family's own rule, if someone had grabbed her and she had turned, she would have left a hand in the black. So nobody grabbed her. The audience has the rule and the fact in one scene; the four adults at the table have both too, and none of them puts them together. They go to war on it. In Episode 6 Danny loses four fingers to the very rule ("The four fingers are gone. Off clean across the knuckles"), Joe "comes home whole", and still nobody at the breakfast table says "Then nobody grabbed Rosie." The show then treats it as a revelation on the ferry: Maggie, "Nobody touched nobody. And then that second weight in the Mayfair log got called a spinner on both banks, and your family started reaching for coats." (Ep 7 Sc 7).

**Why it matters.** The war (Con's pills, Shaun's leg, Liam's job, Danny's hand) runs on the spinner theory from Episode 4 to Episode 7. A viewer who is paying attention will see the theory is impossible in the scene where it is born, and from then on the family looks not un-questioning but slow, and the Episode 7 "nobody spun anybody" lands as something we already knew. Rosie's own lie was built the other way, as a pull, not a grip: "It pulled me off home, Dad. I lost home." (Ep 3 Sc 17), and the plan says so ("It is a pull, not a grip, so it breaks no rule"). Tess's line turns it back into a grip.

**Fix.** One line and one look, and nothing else moves. Replace Tess's "Turn her round in the black and send her home to her tea — that's the only way it could be done." with the family's own false belief, the one the season exists to break: "Sit heavy by her at the wall till she loses home. She faces wrong, she comes home wrong, and six months later it looks like a diet." That keeps the war, keeps the six-metre truth for the ferry, and makes the family's theory rest on "facing home keeps you straight", which is exactly what Episode 6's table destroys. Then, after Rosie's "whoever turns leaves the hand in there", let Kieran's look at his own hand ("opens it, and shuts it") travel to Rosie's two hands on the banister, and let Danny answer the objection nobody has spoken: "Nobody had hold of her. They sat on her till she faced wrong." Now the family is seen reasoning inside its own rule, wrongly, which is the point of the show, and the ferry line "Nobody touched nobody" answers a belief instead of a hole.

### SUBSTANTIVE 2. The epilogue hands Rosie licensed surgery six months after she said Marlows don't do paper

**Where.** Episode 10, Scene 16, against Episode 2, Scene 11.

**The problem.** In Episode 2 Rosie tells Kieran why she cannot be a crossing surgeon: "They want exams. Certificates. Letters after your name. Paper." / "You could do exams." / "Marlows don't do paper." In the December epilogue, a convicted thief serving her sentence on a fever ward, turned, is shown in a theatre with "A rig's collar over her gown. A child asleep... she is back, and something small and dark drops into the steel dish a nurse holds out. Three seconds." Nothing between June and December shows her doing any paper, and no line says she is training; the plan's "She is training with the licensed crossing surgeons" is not on the page. In a world the season has been careful to make ordinary and rule-bound (licences at three seconds since 2014, Trading Standards, a court that moves a file to a kitchen), this is the one place a viewer will feel the writers giving a character her dream instead of the world giving it.

**Why it matters.** The ending's honesty is its cost: Tess in prison, Danny turned and undecided, Terry gone, Rosie fed only where she works. The surgery softens Rosie's price to nothing and does it by breaking the show's own realism, in the last two minutes.

**Fix.** Two ways, either cheap. (a) Keep the image but earn it: the ward sister's line already there ("Your file lives in our kitchen now, love. You eat where you work.") gains one more sentence, "Exams in the spring. Till then you're allowed the dish.", and in the theatre the licensed surgeon crosses while Rosie holds out the steel dish, still "inside the limit she once called soft", because it is her count the surgeon uses. Her locker then holds the Fever Company card and a prospectus, two roads. (b) If you want her to cross, put the paper on screen: a certificate in her locker beside the card, and a surgeon at her shoulder, "Her first. Count her.", so the crossing is supervised and the licence is seen. I would choose (a): it keeps the sentence a sentence.

### QUIBBLE 1. Episode 7 says its theme too many times, in the same words

**Where.** Episode 7, Scenes 4, 7, 9, 11 and 12.

**The problem.** The line "Nobody ever asked why" is said by Terry in the loft (Sc 4: "TERRY: Nobody ever asked why.") and then repeated by Tess on the ferry in the same words about the same man (Sc 11: "Ours is Lee... Nine. Nobody ever asked why."). Maggie says "Nobody counted her" three times in one speech (Sc 7), then "she never asked why that was" (Sc 9), then "Nobody of mine crosses till somebody's counted it" (Sc 11), and Tess repeats "nobody counted her" at the stove (Sc 12). Eight in one episode. Each is in character and none uses a banned word, but together they become a refrain, and a refrain is the theme being said.

**Fix.** Keep Terry's (it is the first and the best, and he answers it himself: "He was off his food, love. Then he wasn't. That was enough for us."). Keep Maggie's first "Nobody counted her" and her closing "till somebody's counted it". Cut Tess's repeat in Scene 11 (she can just point: "Ours is Lee. Off his food all one winter. Nine."), cut "and she never asked why that was" from Scene 9, and in Scene 12 let Tess say "she went in again on her own" and stop there.

### QUIBBLE 2. Danny asks "How do you turn a picture round?" a week after telling Kieran what "no rotation" means

**Where.** Episode 2, Scene 4, against Episode 1, Scene 3.

**The problem.** In Episode 1 the family burns the tissue because the police read wrapping to see whether the thief turned: Kieran reads the cutting, "wrapping shows 'clean translation, no rotation'", and Danny explains it, "Means we came back straight. Every time." So Danny knows paper turns with the thief. In Episode 2 the same man asks "How do you turn a picture round?" and laughs along with Peg's plan to hop the painting "till it turns" on its own. The scene does half-cover this on purpose, and well: Liam asks the one right question ("Won't she come back with it? Whichever way it goes?") and Peg waves it off with "Not if she faces home." That is the family's habit, shown. But it is Danny's line that jars, because he is the one who said the other thing.

**Why it only could go either way.** For a viewer who noticed Episode 1's cutting, Episode 3's reveal becomes the family catching up; the plan accepts that sharp viewers run ahead, and the real twist of Episode 3 is not "she's turned" but "the applause was starvation", which no viewer can get early. So this is a matter of how far ahead you want the sharpest viewers to be.

**Fix.** Give "How do you turn a picture round?" to Shaun. Let Danny say nothing, look once at the framed cuttings, and then bang the table with the rest. The man who knows is seen not asking, and Rosie is not exposed.

### QUIBBLE 3. The Trading Standards letter is dated the day after it arrives

**Where.** Episode 4, Scene 11 (Friday morning after the reefer raid: "a brown window envelope with the council's crest... she drops into the dresser drawer") against Episode 9, Scene 11 (Tess reads it: "Dated the eleventh of April. It's the twelfth of June."). Episode 4's scenes are dated by the quake on Saturday 4 April and Peg's "Wednesday night, then" for the raid, so the letter arrives on Friday 10 April and carries the date 11 April.

**Why it matters.** Almost nobody will catch it, but it is a plain contradiction, and the season's whole point is that dates on paper can be checked.

**Fix.** "Dated the ninth of April."

### QUIBBLE 4. "No tickets, no trial" claims a little more than the rolls give

**Where.** Episode 10, Scene 13. Tess: "Without the rolls, there's nothing to match Purfleet. Nor twenty-five year of anything. No tickets, no trial." Danny: "Burn them tonight. We lose the bridge. We stay free."

**The problem.** The depot's walls logged two bodies "to the hundred grams, cargo and all" (Sc 6), and Shaun is sitting at the table; anyone with a steelyard can weigh him on Monday and match him to a case. Terry is gone, but Shaun isn't. So burning the rolls does not quite buy "free"; it buys "only Shaun". The choice is still real (the rolls are the only proof of the drift and of twenty-five years of dips), but the line overstates it and a careful viewer will feel the thumb on the scale.

**Fix.** One sentence from Tess: "They can weigh Shaun any day they like. It's the twenty-five year they can't touch without these." Then Danny's "We stay free" becomes "The rest of us stay free," and the vote is honest.

### QUIBBLE 5. Terry says he won't cross, then crosses nine times

**Where.** Episode 8, Scene 7 ("TERRY: I'll drive. (beat) Nobody's sending me in on my seconds, mind. But I'll drive.") against Episode 9, Scene 3, where he does nine two-second hops through the depot wall and explains: "Two seconds I can do, love. It was the long ones I said no to."

**The problem.** The Episode 9 line squares it, but the Episode 8 line reads as a refusal to cross at all, so for a moment in Episode 9 it looks like the writers changed their minds and had Terry explain it away.

**Fix.** In Episode 8: "Nobody's sending me in on my seconds, mind. Twos I'll do. And I'll drive." Then Episode 9's line is a reminder rather than a repair.

### QUIBBLE 6. Three near-identical action lines for Peg's quiet fury

**Where.** Episode 7, Scene 12: "her voice doesn't rise at all, which is the worst thing in the room." Episode 8, Scene 7: "her voice doesn't rise, which is the worst of it." Episode 9, Scene 10: "When she speaks, her voice doesn't rise at all, which is the worst thing in the room." Also "the way everyone in this family counts everything now" in Episode 7, Scene 13 and again in Episode 9, Scene 7.

**Why it matters.** A viewer never hears action lines, so this is a note for the page, not the screen; but the same instruction three times tells an actor and director less each time, and the third reader of the scripts will feel a template.

**Fix.** Keep the Episode 7 one (the first, and the strongest moment). In Episode 8 write what she does instead ("She wipes the fish slice and puts it down square."). In Episode 9 let the silence stand: "Peg does not turn round."

### QUIBBLE 7. A scene is numbered "5A"

**Where.** Episode 8, "Scene 5A. The weighbridge office, Grays, Tuesday, mid-morning." (Mick's line that Purfleet is "shut to us Wednesday fortnight").

**Why it matters.** It is a good, necessary scene (it is where the raid's date comes from), but the label shows the join of a late insertion. Renumber Episode 8's scenes so it is Scene 6 and the rest shift.

## 5. What I am unsure of

- **Frank's memory.** The third reversal asks us to believe that Peg, who pencilled "10 st 2" and "7.1" on his last ticket herself, and Terry, Danny and Tess, who lived with a wasting man for six months, all remembered "fifteen stone". The season argues for it rather than assumes it: Vic's "heart", Maureen's "nerves", "Gary went to Spain" (Ep 7 Sc 4) show this family relabels wasting as a habit, and Episode 10's two cold-open kitchens ("as Peg remembers it" / "as it was") make the turning of memory the subject rather than a trick. On the page it holds. Only an audience can say whether it feels true in the room, and I would not add a plant for it; one more would give it away.
- **Pace in two places.** Episode 2's seven hops and Episode 7's long ferry conversation are the two stretches where a viewer's attention might drift. Both are intercut with something that has a clock on it (the false alarm in hop four; Shaun's eight seconds at Silvertown), and every hop and every line of the count does a job, so I would not cut, but this is a judgement I cannot make from the page.
- **Maggie's price.** Maggie tried to have Rosie run out ("I wanted it to be more than she'd got", Ep 7 Sc 7), sent a bullet, and then hands her own family's tide books to the police with the rolls; the epilogue shows Declan unchanged and Maggie in the doorway, but nothing of what the books cost her. The season says plainly "Nobody's square", and it is not pretending the two women's acts are equal. Whether an audience wants a line about the Aherns' own reckoning, I can't tell from here.
- **How the tugs choose where you land.** The lore says "The Hill takes you. The Deep keeps you." (Ep 1 Sc 8); Episode 9 says you step out at your own address ("Their sea's sitting over this lino"); Episode 10 has Rosie follow the Hill's pull from the field and land on a shore. These agree in every case the season shows, and the plan's ledger backs the address reading. Nothing on screen contradicts, so I have not listed it, but if a second season ever sends someone from Grays after the Hill, decide then which it is.
