# 36 Round 2 - the four critiques

*Three GLM critics, each through one lens, and MiMo v2.6 Pro with fresh eyes, on the season after round 1.*

## Critique: story

1. **The weigh-wall's stated range contradicts the later set pieces it must serve.** In ep 3, scene 5 (the pit), Ray states the rule on screen: the Mayfair wall logs "every body in the black within four metres." But ep 7's Corbett's job (scenes 2, 10–11) has the wall sensing and flooding Shaun while he is "fifty foot into the room" under the floor, and ep 9's depot raid (scenes 3–5) and ep 10's investigators (scene 6) depend on walls logging bodies deep under shed floors. Either the walls read at range — and Ray's line is wrong — or they don't, and two set pieces plus the finale's evidence chain (wall logs matching the rolls) collapse. Ep 5 survives either way: the lock-up box sits by the river wall, inside any range. **Why it matters:** the wall is the season's honest instrument; a stated limit the plot ignores is exactly the kind of uncheck the show is about. **Fix:** cut "within four metres" from Ray's line ("Every body in the black in that room, with its weight and its time"); nothing else needs the figure. SUBSTANTIVE

2. **Maggie's live printout breaches the plan's own no-live-instrument rule.** Ep 5, scene 16: Maggie arrives with the lock-up wall's printout "still printing" — a live reading of Liam, unseen, in the black, chattering through the death-watch. The plan's section 14 bans exactly this ("No instrument showing an unseen visitor live, anywhere: every such reading is found on the roll afterwards"), to stay clear of The Catch's needle; the Twitch ticket and the 02:04 ticket obey it, and the ep 6 ambush is played blind on the wall clock for the same reason. **Why it matters:** it is the one use of the banned device, in the season's most emotional scene, so it will be noticed and remembered as a rule-break. **Fix:** she arrives with the curl already torn off — "It's been printing since ten to six. He's on my wall" — and the last length (65.1) is torn away after the minute, which the scene already does; only the tense changes. SUBSTANTIVE

3. **Ray is handed two decisive facts off-screen.** The plan's knowledge map has Ray knowing "nobody spun anybody" by ep 8, but no scene tells him: the ep 8 flat scene never touches the spinner, and his ep 10 "I'd take mine counted. Before Monday" (scene 9) comes before Tess quotes him any odds (scene 10), so he asks for a counted cure before anyone has shown him the count. His season-long stated want — catch the spinner — and the meaning of his cure both rest on transfers the audience never sees; his ep 3 promise "I wait till she comes to me hungry" also dissolves unpicked-up. **Why it matters:** a principal's arc turns twice on invisible information. **Fix:** two lines in the ep 8 flat — Tess: "Nobody's spinning anybody. It's the seconds. Ten mends seven in ten," and Ray, squaring his files: "Twenty-four year I've wrote things down and never laid one beside another." That also earns his ep 10 request. SUBSTANTIVE

4. **Danny's ambush pays off on a stack of unluck-acknowledged luck.** Ep 6, scene 1: the 02:04 ticket dates Joe's visit to the minute, Danny's true presence is 2.2 seconds, and Joe's coming that night is Maggie's envelope deadline, unknown to Danny. Danny deduces a nightly routine from one visit, picks one night, and his step lands inside Joe's hover at the safe. The script calls it a guess, which covers the gamble but not the minute-resolution timing against a 2.2-second window. **Why it matters:** a careful viewer will feel the hit is too cheap for the cost (four fingers). **Fix:** anchor the step to a sound — the inbound tow's wash or clank against the wharf, heard through the office wall — and add "Third night I've sat here" so persistence replaces coincidence. QUIBBLE

5. **The family sits broke on an unsellable fortune for eight episodes.** From ep 3 (float tin empty) through ep 8 (the last tub), the Marlows need £109,500 to £360,000 while the original *Assayer's Wife* lies in the safe; after the buyer leaves in ep 2 it is never weighed as an asset again until it is given back in ep 10. The wrong-window linen makes it worthless to anyone who checks the catalogue, but the season never says so at a moment of need. **Why it matters:** a careful viewer will ask why Liam's job was "cash for Rosie's cure" with a masterpiece in the house. **Fix:** one line at the ep 6 money table — Peg: "And her? Every dealer from here to Vienna's seen the book photo. She's a copy to the world." QUIBBLE

6. **Rosie's landing should print heavier than 45.7.** Ep 9, scenes 4–6: she steps on the plate (ticket), crosses (−46.2 at 00:45), then lands soaked, with weed and a 0.38 kg crab in her hood, back on the same plate — a third ticket the episode never numbers and which the plan's notes say also read 45.7. Kieran audits every ticket; the sea arriving as kilos on the certified roll is a free, in-world proof of next door. **Why it matters:** the show's own honesty device (the roll) skips the one number that would dramatize it. **Fix:** give the landing ticket roughly fifty and a Kieran line ("Went in forty-five seven, come back fifty-one"), and correct the note. QUIBBLE

7. **The raid van returns at dawn for no stated reason.** Ep 9: Terry's nine two-second legs and Shaun's two are over by about one o'clock (the investigators later date the bodies "between midnight and one"), yet the van rolls in at dawn — which is precisely what leaves Rosie alone with the office and the rack. **Why it matters:** the gap exists to serve the cross-cutting, and a viewer doing the hours will notice. **Fix:** one line — they sat in the reeds until the gatehouse changed shift and the dog settled. QUIBBLE

8. **Month-old print survives in a stove that cooks every day.** Ep 10, scene 13: Danny opens the firebox and finds "the ferry timetable's print, and the corner of a cloth binding" — burned in ep 7, in the stove that fries breakfast and eats tickets daily since. **Why it matters:** small, but it is offered as physical evidence of the burning. **Fix:** have him open the cold firebox and say the line over anonymous ash, or let the corner have fallen behind the firebrick when Peg raked it out. QUIBBLE

9. **A dark office at 05:40 in mid-June.** Ep 10, scene 10: "Dark except the printer's green light and the stove's" — sunrise in Essex on 14 June is before a quarter to five. Nobody low at 05:52 is canon and untouchable; the darkness isn't. **Why it matters:** the finale's dawn is its texture, and the error is checkable on any phone. **Fix:** "Blinds down from the night" in the scene heading; the field scenes already have their fog. QUIBBLE

10. **Rosie once says the season's answer almost flat out.** Ep 7, scene 12: "when it's been counted proper, and checked against somebody else's, I'm going first." It is the theme's whole method in one spoken clause, in an episode that otherwise carries it in a balance, a steelyard and a strip of ferry timetable. **Why it matters:** it is the closest the season comes to stating the idea the brief says must live in events. **Fix:** cut to "when it's been counted proper, I'm going first." QUIBBLE

11. **The finale is over-stuffed for 55 minutes.** Ep 10 carries Frank's revelation, Ray's 2002 file, the noon investigators, the night of writing, the steelyard scene, the baking-tray door, three crossings intercut at 05:52, the rolls argument, the vote, the handover and a four-scene epilogue. Terry's going — five years of a principal — gets roughly ninety seconds by design, and the front-page material duplicates scene 19's radio. **Why it matters:** the emotional beats at the fence and the stove risk being brushed past rather than felt; only a running-time test could settle it. **Fix:** trim, don't reorder — fold scene 16's headlines into scene 19, let the ward carry the public-cure beat in two shots, and shorten the cold open's remembered weigh-in. QUIBBLE

## Critique: people


1. **[SUBSTANTIVE] Peg and Tess never speak to each other again after the exile.** Ep 7 sc 12 ("Out, Tess"), repeated in ep 8 sc 7; from then on every exchange is mediated — ep 9 sc 11 ("Peg opens her mouth. Tess doesn't wait for it"), ep 9 sc 12 (Peg defending the fifteen-stone legend *to* Tess, then Tess leaving), ep 10 (side by side all night, no direct line), epilogue (a letter from Kieran, no visit). This is the season's deepest care-debt: the Frank revelation proves Tess's whole life of counting was built on Peg's misremembered number, and Peg's conversion is complete on every front except the daughter. The audience is left holding an unpaid wound at the exact moment the family pays all its others. **Fix:** one private beat. In the loft (ep 10 sc 2), over box 02: "Nobody made me stand you on that plate. I'll say that part an' all." Or at the station door, and an epilogue image of Peg in a visiting room, turning Kieran's graph so Tess can read it through the table slot.

2. **[SUBSTANTIVE] Nobody responds to Rosie refusing the cure.** Ep 10 sc 12, at the fence: "Seven in ten, it's Nan's fry-up for me on Friday… I'm staying as I am." Danny answers about himself ("I'm done making weight"), Peg gathers Terry's clothes, and the scene moves on. A family that spent nine episodes trying to buy this girl ten seconds — that watched her fold on the plate — hears her decline them and says nothing. A careful viewer will feel the choice orphaned at the moment it's declared. **Fix:** one line each at the fence. Danny: "Seven in ten and you're on my plate Friday." Peg: "Either way, you eat." Ten seconds of screen, and the choice becomes theirs too.

3. **[STANTIVE→SUBSTANTIVE] Rosie's lie is never weighed against what it cost.** Ep 3 sc 17: "Something took hold of me in the black" sends Danny for his coat and lights the war — the reefer raid, the burned pills, Con, Shaun's leg, Liam's lock-up job. In ep 7 sc 12 she retracts it as shame, and the table processes it as being about applause and hiding; in ep 10 she claims only the coaching ("I'm the one taught him to be hungry for it"). No one — least of all Terry, who lost his gravy pal to a raid her ghost story launched — ever connects the confession to the body count. Her guilt arc covers Liam and skips Con. **Fix:** one beat. Ep 7, quietly, after the retraction, Terry: "Con's lot believed it an' all." Or ep 10, in her own mouth at the writing: "And I named a ghost, and my dad went to war on it. Write that."

4. **[SUBSTANTIVE] Maggie's tip is confessed and then never reckoned.** Ep 7 sc 7: "Ten seconds under a road, Tess. I wanted it to be more than she'd got." After that, Maggie crosses the river with tide books and a steelyard (ep 10 sc 8) and is absorbed into the family with no scene of debt — and Joe, the boy whose whole hand cost Danny four fingers, is in the same room with the man who grabbed him, unaddressed. The Crime promise is a reckoning paid in kind; the war's one deliberate attack on Rosie is paid with nothing, and "nobody's square" (one line, ep 7) can't carry it alone. **Fix:** one exchange before the vote. Danny: "You rung the law down on my girl." Maggie: "And your floor turned his sister. Put it all on the pile." Then the tide books go down.

5. **[SUBSTANTIVE] Kieran's starvation is discovered, then dropped as a person.** Ep 9 sc 11: the reveal gets one line ("He's been making weight under your roof"), no scene between mother and son — the boy leads *her* to the graph — and nobody feeds him on screen afterwards; he clerks all night at twelve years old, and the steelyard reads 38.4 with no comment. The pencil handover (ep 10 sc 9) beautifully answers his *role*, but never his wound: a boy starving himself to earn this family's love, in the exact image of the mother who was stood on that plate at fourteen. **Fix:** twenty seconds. Terry sets a plate by the clerk's elbow and doesn't move until it's gone (paying his own rewrapped-lunch thread), and Tess — at the column, not the table — touches the top of his head the way she did in ep 7, and says one un-numbered thing.

6. **[QUIBBLE] Danny's "Yes" to Liam is carried by two lines.** Ep 5 sc 11. The cause exists (Liam's plea, Rosie's vouching, the cure money, the ep 1 plant "They picked your auntie instead of me"), but the most consequential parental decision of the season lands faster than a ticket. Given his want (keep his kids safe) and his wound (too heavy at seventeen), one beat of him doing his own arithmetic aloud — "Eighty-eight. Two and a bit. It can't be me" — would make the surrender to the family's numbers visible and wounding rather than swift.

7. **[QUIBBLE] A cluster of lines edges toward saying the theme.** Terry's "Nobody ever asked why" (ep 7 sc 4) is repeated by Tess on the ferry (sc 7); Rosie's vow "when it's been counted proper, and checked against somebody else's, I'm going first" (ep 7 sc 12) is the plan's own formula for the season's question; Tess's "We never fed our own" (ep 8 sc 7) sums the thesis in five words. Each is defensible in voice; together, and with the repetition, they start to state what the plot should carry alone. **Fix:** cut the ferry instance of "asked why" and trim Rosie's vow to "When there's a proper count, I'm going first off it." Keep "We never fed our own" — it's said over a full fridge, and earns itself.

8. **[QUIBBLE] The detective's pursuit evaporates off-screen.** Ray builds a case across eps 1–5 (the linen photographs, the pour contracts, the notebook, the confiscated rig) and then never mentions it again; his suspension (ep 8) explains his silence in fact but not in feeling, and the family's final surrender is to the Register and Grays uniform, not to him. The Crime promise of a long fight between equals loses its police half for five episodes without a beat of acknowledgment. **Fix:** one line in the flat scene (ep 8 sc 6): "I'd have had you for Mayfair by June. Then they walked me out of Victoria."

9. **[QUIBBLE] The Aherns' interiority rests almost entirely on Maggie.** Declan is public bluster in every scene — the form in the stove, the chalk, "Not in my lock-up" — so his epilogue refusal to change risks reading as a moral wearing a cap rather than a grieving man; and Aoife exists only as photo, printout and bottles. The final Ahern image (Siobhan cheered, the front page unread) needs him legible as a person. **Fix:** one twenty-second private beat — Declan alone with Aoife's printout at night, folding it smaller, then chalking the board anyway — and one live trace of Aoife (a phone video Joe watches: her at the tug wheel, laughing, the baby slung).

10. **[QUIBBLE] Liam and Rosie's mother does not exist.** Ten episodes, a death and a sentence, and no mention of the woman who mothered Danny's two children — no grief at the river wall, no visitor at the ward. In a saga whose whole grammar is tables and feeding, the absent mother of the two central kids is a hole a careful viewer eventually falls into. **Fix:** one line or one object. "Your mum would've had something to say about that," or a photo on Danny's dashboard; anything that admits she was.

11. **[QUIBBLE] Rosie's surgeon want goes dark in the middle.** It is vivid in eps 1–3 (the videos, "She's done four thousand," "Marlows don't do paper") and returns in ep 8 with Den's dressings — but eps 4–7, exactly the stretch where she is starving, lying and jobless, never touch it. A want under that much pressure should show the pressure. **Fix:** a ten-second silent beat in ep 6 sc 10 or ep 7 — at two in the morning, coming down for the pot, her phone propped and playing an operating theatre with the sound off, the blue light on her face while she eats standing up.

12. **[QUIBBLE] Terry never learns how Con died holding his name.** Maggie gives Tess the detail in ep 7 sc 7 ("TEL. Held it in his lap like a hymn book"), and it belongs to the one person on screen who earned it — the friend of five years of two-a.m. phone calls. The season's tenderest plant is never delivered. **Fix:** one line from Tess in ep 7 act four, before the burning. Terry: "…Pass us that box down, then," and he carries the rolls to the ladder himself.

13. **[QUIBBLE] The night of writing shares out Liam's death unevenly.** Ep 10 sc 7: Peg takes the breakfast, Rosie takes the coaching, Kieran writes — and Danny, who said yes, contributes nothing to the accounting of his own son. His silence is in character (he never says sorry), but this is the one night the family says things, and his share of the boy is the largest. **Fix:** one line: "And I said yes. Write that first."

14. **[QUIBBLE] The "nine point four" debt between Tess and Rosie is closed only by implication.** Ep 6 sc 10 ("I gave you nine"), ep 8 sc 9 ("Nine point four, Tess"), then ep 10's odds written "where they can read them" — an apology in the family's own language, which fits, but the two women at the centre of the first reversal never touch it directly, and the ep 6 scene ends on Rosie going upstairs with nothing to say, a loaded gun that never fires. **Fix:** optional, and cheap: at the roll, Rosie reading her odds aloud and adding, "Your count, Tess. First one I ever trusted." If the silence is preferred, keep it — but know it's a choice, not an oversight.

15. **[QUIBBLE] Ep 10 acts four and five are two consecutive kitchen-table acts after the field.** The fog, the black shore and Terry's count are the episode's peak of air and dread; what follows — the case for the rolls (sc 13) and the vote (sc 14) — is the emotional climax delivered at a standing pulse, with the Monday warrant present only as words. **Fix:** one exterior breath between them — loading the boxes into the van in rain, or the investigators' car passing the shut gate on Sunday afternoon — to let the vote arrive with the lungs full.

## Critique: continuity

1. **Episode 3, act two (the pit) against episode 7, act two (the ferry), and the bible's canon table.** Ray states the Mayfair wall's log radius outright: "Every body in the black within four metres, with its weight and its time." Maggie swears Joe "never came within three fathoms of her" — about 5.5 metres; the bible says six metres — and Rosie was working the shelf hard against the very wall that floods. On Ray's figure, a boy holding 5.5–6 metres off a crosser at that shelf should be outside the log, yet the two-body reading at 3:12 is the season's founding clue: the spinner theory, the war, Ray's whole case. The bible attaches four metres to *flooding* (rule 13); episode 3 has welded logging to it. **Fix:** cut the radius from Ray's line ("Every body in the black, with its weight and its time. At twelve past three it logged two") or say "within six metres", and align Maggie's "three fathoms" with the bible's six metres. **SUBSTANTIVE**

2. **Episode 2, sc. 3 and 13, against episode 6 sc. 9, episode 7 sc. 5 and episode 9 sc. 5.** Kieran's test-weight line is dead flat for months ("Same as every day. That's the point"), his stated aim is to see the neighbours' tide, and in March he admits he hasn't ("Not yet"). From May it wobbles, 19.77 to 19.79, and in episodes 7 and 9 he says "The tide's in my graph" — a claim Miss praises. The Nobody Tide is decades old: if the little bridge can resolve it in May it could in February, so the flat line is wrong; if it can't, nothing shown explains what changed, and Kieran's tide claim is a misreading nobody corrects. This sits inside the proof-spine — his log is what exposes the April step and Liam's two hundredths. **Fix:** in episode 9, one honest line — "It wobbles since April. That's not my tide. My tide never come. I don't know what it is" — leaving the audience to connect it to the strokes; or give the change a cause on the radio. **SUBSTANTIVE**

3. **Episode 5, sc. 2 — never paid.** Over Ray's arm we see MAYFAIR · 03:12 · 2ND BODY · 59.0 as he writes up the minus-59.0 ticket, and "something passes across his face at the number": the detective joins the two fifty-nine-kilo bodies and files it. It never returns. He never identifies the boy, never revises the spinner, and the bible's knowledge map ("It was Joe… Ray: 8") has no scene delivering it. A clue shown being loaded and never fired; his flaw — writes everything down, shows no one — should make this pay, not swallow it. **Fix:** in episode 8, sc. 6, squaring his files: "Second body at Mayfair, fifty-nine kilos. Same as the one stood over your safe. I've known since April it was a lad from across the river, and I sat on it like everything else." **SUBSTANTIVE**

4. **Episode 10, sc. 6 against the bible, §8, episode 10 act one.** The bible says the Purfleet walls "logged three bodies in the black"; the episode says two — one nine times, one twice. Only Terry and Shaun crossed at Purfleet, so the episode is right and the bible's exact number is wrong, and the bible claims its numbers win. **Fix:** correct the bible to two; or, if three is wanted, Danny must take one two-second case, which changes his "I'll come for the carry" and his margin — not worth it. **SUBSTANTIVE**

5. **Episode 6, sc. 4.** Joe: "Went in. I heard it land." The crossing grammar everywhere else (episodes 1, 3, 5, 6, 9, 10) is that an unpinned crosser hears only heartbeat and breath — beads fall "in silence", Tess's counting can't reach Rosie, "She can't hear you." Joe, in the black, cannot hear an envelope land on a leaf. **Fix:** "I put it on the picture," or "I felt it go." **SUBSTANTIVE**

6. **Episode 8, sc. 6.** The ledger is described as "a date and a number of tubs", and Ray says "Your uncle. One a week, five year… The seventh of April, it goes to two." Set against the season's food arithmetic the words clash: £300 a day a mouth, Dilys's weekly crates, and Terry's "Nine days. Less, there being three" all make one tub one mouth-day, so Terry drew seven a week and April doubled it to fourteen. "One a week… goes to two" survives only as one standing order becoming two drops — which fits Terry's "Fridays as well now", but not a column of tub-counts. **Fix:** Ray says "Seven a week, five year… the seventh of April it goes to fourteen", or the description becomes "a date and a number of drops". **QUIBBLE**

7. **Episode 5, sc. 13 against episode 9, sc. 4.** The bite's minute and its uselessness in the black are taught to Liam in episode 5; its tariff — half your seconds — is first spoken in Rosie's whisper seconds before she uses it ("Half my seconds. Five point four"). Every other rule is on screen well before the moment that depends on it. **Fix:** one clause in Tess's teaching — "It's half your count when it goes" — or Terry naming it at the pit in episode 8. **QUIBBLE**

8. **Episode 10, sc. 2 against the bible ("Farringdon, September 2001") and episode 7, sc. 4 ("01, 02, 03, year by year to 26").** The roll from the box marked 02 opens "September: FRANK 78.0" and runs through the new year to the spring 64.4 — September 2001's tickets filed in the 2002 box. **Fix:** a half-line — rolls are changed when they run out, not at New Year — or Tess pulls both boxes down onto the boards. **QUIBBLE**

9. **Episode 7, sc. 9 against episode 10, sc. 8–9.** Tess's pencil "has never been sharpened" (the whole point of borrowing Maggie's), yet in episode 10 she "writes the odds" on the roll in pencil before handing the same pencil to Kieran. An unsharpened pencil can't write, and the symbol is muddied if she has already used it. **Fix:** she writes the odds with Kieran's school pencil and gives him her own; or one beat of Kieran sharpening it. **QUIBBLE**

10. **Episodes 5 to 10.** Peg's keys migrate: dug from the drawer on a ring (5, sc. 1), the safe key alone on a bootlace (8, sc. 2; 9, sc. 1), then "her key ring" with both keys from her apron pocket at Mucking (10, sc. 11). The small padlock key — the one that opens the field — drops out of sight between episode 6 and the moment it matters. **Fix:** when Peg strings the bootlace in episode 7, sc. 12, both keys go on it. **QUIBBLE**

11. **Episode 8, sc. 6 — never closed.** Five years of Laundry money sits untouched in an account in Terry's name — at the going rate the best part of half a million — and after Terry steps over it is never mentioned: not for lawyers, not for Danny's March card, not in the epilogue. In a Crime season a fortune in a vanished man's name is a live object. **Fix:** one epilogue line — the passbook pays Danny's licensed cure, or Tess's appeal. **QUIBBLE**

12. **Episode 9, sc. 7.** Shaun turned on the raid, so the case he carried turned with him: the Register's mirror porridge came home straight, feeds only the straight, and Peg's "Bad luck" lands the irony. But Kieran's line over the ordinary reading — "It's ours now. This case is food for us" — plays on first hearing like a gain for the starving side of the family, the opposite of the beat. **Fix:** "It's turned with him. It's our food now — no use to them." **QUIBBLE**

13. **Episode 1, sc. 17 against episode 2, sc. 4.** Peg's copy theory changes shape eight scenes apart: "Bank's hung a copy. Kept the real one in Switzerland" in the van, then "Bank hung a copy the wrong way round" with no Switzerland on Tuesday. Motivated nonsense is in character, but two unchallenged versions read as a seam, not a self-serving story settling. **Fix:** keep Switzerland and let "the wrong way round" explain it, or have Danny say "that's not what you said Saturday". **QUIBBLE**

14. **Episode 3, sc. 17 into episode 4, sc. 1 (the 3–4 seam).** Episode 3 buttons on "Blood pays blood" and Danny reaching for his coat — a promise of immediate violence; episode 4 opens ten minutes later with him returning from a practice hop. The swap is sound (it plants his 2.2 seconds) but the button oversells what the next episode must soften. The rest of the seam is clean: the dates, the knowledge split ("When did he tell you that?" / "Wednesday"), the tray, the pot and the half-drawn 10.0 all carry. **Fix:** end episode 3 on Peg's line with Danny stopped at the door by the first lorries, or open episode 4 mid-hop. **QUIBBLE**

15. **Episode 8's plant notes against its scenes.** The notes claim the spinner is killed here with the line "There's no such person. There never was." No such line exists in the episode; the theory actually dies on the ferry in episode 7. Notes citing unshot lines will mislead the next writer. **Fix:** cut the quotation, or restore the line to Tess in sc. 7. **QUIBBLE**

16. **Episodes 7 to 10.** Tess's agreement word drifts: "Yeah" (7, sc. 12), then "Aye" on the night line (8, sc. 8), at the car (9, sc. 13) and over the rolls (10, sc. 13). "Aye" belongs to Terry, Ray and Maggie — the people she allies with — so it may be deliberate absorption, but nothing marks it as a choice and it reads as dialect bleed in the late episodes. **Fix:** keep "Yeah" for Tess throughout, or plant one borrowed "Aye" on the ferry. **QUIBBLE**

17. **Episode 1, sc. 3.** Terry sings "'Twas on a Tuesday morning" from "Dashing Away with the Smoothing Iron"; the traditional line is Monday. If Tuesday is a joke for Dilys's day, it is a lovely unflagged one; if not, it is a wrong lyric. **Fix:** flag it once — Terry, winked at: "Tuesday, when the whites come" — or sing it straight. **QUIBBLE**

18. **Episode 10, sc. 10–11.** The headings run backwards: the office at 05:40, then Mucking at 05:20, converging on 05:52. The cross-cut will play, but the order invites a continuity query in prep, and the field scene's washings need their head start stated. **Fix:** head sc. 10 at 05:30 running to 05:52, or head sc. 11 "earlier, running parallel". **QUIBBLE**

## Critique: mimo


**1. Episode 10, scene 6 — the investigators say "two bodies" at Purfleet; the master plan says "three."**
The plan's Episode 10 spec reads: "The Purfleet depot's weigh-walls logged three bodies in the black." The episode's INVESTIGATOR says: "the Purfleet depot's walls logged two bodies in the black. One of them nine times… the other twice." Only two people cross at Purfleet (Terry and Shaun); the episode's own raid scene confirms this. The plan mandates "Where a number appears here, it is the number," so the episode is in breach — or the plan has miscounted. Either way the two disagree, and the episode is internally coherent while the plan is not. **Fix:** change the plan to "two bodies" (the correct count for the scene as written), or — if the plan's third body is intended — identify who the third crosser is and add them to episode 9's raid. **SUBSTANTIVE.**

---

**2. Episode 8, scene 8 — Tess phones the Register to take Rosie to the ward without asking her, echoing The Catch's moral question.**
The plan's "Never echo The Catch" section states: "Nobody's moral question is whether you may decide for someone else; the season's question is whether you wrote it down and checked it against someone else's." Yet Tess, acting alone, calls the Register night line and reports "a turned woman… Marlow Aggregates" so a crew arrives at dawn. Rosie does not know. Her parting shot — "Nine point four, Tess" — reads as blame for a decision made over her head. The dialogue was rewritten to remove "You decided. Again.", but the *action* of deciding someone's fate without telling them is unchanged. A viewer who knows The Catch will read the echo; a viewer who doesn't may still feel the consent problem and attribute it to the theme. **Fix:** have Rosie overhear or initiate the call, making it a shared decision — so Tess's flaw remains "she trusted her own sums without checking" (SECONDS's theme) and not "she decided for someone else" (The Catch's). **SUBSTANTIVE.**

---

**3. Episode 7, scene 9 — Terry's eleven 2-second hops are drawn as "ticks" on the ferry graph, which elsewhere marks "straight."**
Maggie pencils "eleven small ticks in a row above the 2, like a fence." Tess has just said "Nothing. Eleven times, nothing. Not turned, not mended." The ticks are meant to encode "no change" (Terry stayed turned). But earlier in the same scene, a tick above the 4 means "came back straight" (Joe's dad) and a cross means "came back turned" (Brendan). A first-time viewer who reads ticks as "straight" will see Terry — a turned man — marked as straight on the central reveal. The dialogue ("Nothing… nothing at all") partially clarifies, but the graph is the episode's thesis and must be readable at a glance. **Fix:** use a third mark for "no change" — a short dash, or open circles — so the fence at 2 reads differently from the straight ticks at 4 and 6. **SUBSTANTIVE** (the reveal is the episode's spine).

**4. Episode 9, scene 5 — Rosie states her bite will give her "5.4 seconds" while standing in wet clothes; the budget rule says everything inside the field counts.**
