## The theme under test

**The question.** Why do the Marlows and the Aherns suffer and die? The plan's answer: rules that were right once, kept after the world changed, and answered with more of the same.

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| **Remove "never questions":** a family that counts, in the same world | Liam lives (the letter, Kieran's log, a margin). The war never starts (the Mayfair log, a phone call to Maggie). Rosie's turn is avoided only once finding 2 is fixed. Frank dies anyway as written (5). The door stays hidden anyway as written (3). |
| **Swap the family:** the Aherns, same code with one difference | Same outcome, because the difference does nothing (4). |
| **Rival: bad luck** | Mostly beaten. The drift table makes turning a matter of time, and the family chose the time. Luck remains at Frank's one in twelve (5) and Marrable Street's three in ten, which is fine. |
| **Rival: a villain** | Not yet beaten. If the whole working world never noticed that long crossings turn people, Obverse becomes a hidden villain keeping a cheap cure secret (7). Peg is close to being a single culprit (11). Ray passes: a feeder, not a villain. |
| **Rival: scarcity** | Beaten. The Register feeds the turned free; "we feed our own" made the scarcity. |
| **Rival: plain grief** | Beaten for the code. Grief bent Peg's memory, but the "fifteen stone" sum stood on the chalk board for twenty-four years and nobody did it. |
| **Does exile undercut the theme?** | Not in the events. The door is found only by laying three records side by side, it costs a coin toss and a whole life, and only the turned can use it. It does undercut in the plan's claims (3). It will look like a shortcut until the dice-against-door sum is on screen (9). |

**The twists, swap by swap.**
- *Pass:* the "forgery" that is the original; Ray turned; Rosie turned; nobody spun anybody; the Laundry is Ray; Ray's mirror signature.
- *Best in the season:* the honest scale that lies. Only a weighbridge measures pull rather than mass, so nothing else explains Liam's missing kilo.
- *Wobble or fail:* Frank as "the code killed him" (5); the field as the door (13); next door is food, which is right but is proved by taste (6); the final choice about the rolls (8).
- *Loose but harmless:* Danny spinning himself. It is a coin toss, and either face of the coin serves the theme.

## Findings, most serious first

1. **The exile finale is pitch B's ending, and the plan doesn't declare it (rule 7).**
   - *Where:* ep 10, act four; section 15, last paragraph ("Taken from pitches B and C. Nothing.").
   - *Problem:* lay the two endings side by side.
     - **Pitch B:** a scout crosses at the tide and comes back holding his breath ("salt, and cut grass, but wrong"). Then the family's old turned elder, Nan, having just been told a hard truth, goes at the 4:12 tide onto a floor the neighbours' land-moving made. Her son dances with her. Whether anyone receives her is left open.
     - **SECONDS:** Rosie scouts at Nobody low water and brings back one breath. She breathes it out for Terry ("Southend"). Then the family's old turned elder, just after Peg says "turned" to him for the first time, goes at 05:52 onto the shore of the Hill the neighbours moved. Peg blesses him: "Go on, then." Whether he arrived straight is left open.
     - The parts come in the same order. Pitch C adds the held breath as proof (Jess "comes back with her cheeks full").
     - Section 15 names only the general resemblance, "a turned person going to live next door".
     - Separately, Clarke's "Technical Error" ends with a mirror-reversed man sent back through for a cure and lost. Terry's open fate repeats that.
   - *Why it matters:* the owner chose A over B and said not to graft in B's material. He read B's summary, and its ending is the part he will recognise. It is also where the theme lands.
   - *Fix:* keep Terry going, since the owner asked for exile to be true, but build the sequence from SECONDS's own parts.
     - **Make it a dip.** The Ministry has fenced the Mucking field since 1995, with weigh-walls round the old landing site. The family's last job is to put a man into their own stolen field for five seconds: weigh-in, count, beads, clock. Peg's hidden want, "her field back", pays off. She stands on it for the first time in thirty-one years, on the wrong side of the law. The Crime lead holds to the end.
     - **Let Terry decide at the crab in ep 9,** where his want ("something that tastes of itself") is paid. Then the fence needs no sniff test.
     - **Give the held breath a job B and C never gave it.** Rosie keeps it. She breathes the neighbours' air alone at the fence, then tells them she is staying. What it smelled of stays hers, beside "what Rosie saw". Her choice is made knowing what she is giving up.
     - **No farewell at the water.** He goes on the count, like any job.
     - **Declare in section 15** what resembles B and C, and why each difference is there.

2. **The doom's engine: the two new causes are loose, and the old one, Caraway's Fast, still turns Rosie.**
   - *Where:* section 6 (the rows "Never go back twice" and "Make weight"; steps 2-3); the ep 1 cold open (the weigh-wall trips); ep 3, act one (Tess's two trips); Tess's secret (section 7); ledger, theorem 1; section 15, item 10.
   - *Problem:* run remove and swap on the three causes the plan names.
     - **Marlow Mix.** Swap Danny's floors for any other firm's bead concrete. Jobs lengthen just the same, because every bank moved to weight. The irony is real, but the family is not the cause.
     - **Never go back twice.** At Mayfair the weigh-wall trips on the first visit and the beads bury the shelf. A second trip would find the goods under tungsten. So the vault forces one trip, not the family's rule. Tess's two-trip plan at Marrable Street could never have worked, and the rule costs nothing.
     - **Make weight.** The plan says it only hides the starvation. But Tess's secret is that she "found Rosie's 9.4 seconds by pushing her to lose four more kilos". So the inciting turn still runs on the chain the originality review called Caraway's Fast: admired thinness buys a longer count, and the longer count turns you.
     - What is actually held is only "jobs got long and nobody counted".
   - *Why it matters:* this is the theme's causal chain, and the owner has read Caraway.
   - *Fix:*
     - **Marlow Mix.** Danny sells the family's night-time knowledge by day: "I know what slows a crosser down." The City buys Marlow Mix because thieves designed it, so the longest jobs in London are the ones the Marlows built. Take away the family's know-how and the floors are thinner.
     - **Weigh-walls.** They flood only when a body stays within four metres for more than three seconds, so that licensed couriers and surgeons passing next door don't flood every vault in the City. Short visits slip under; one long visit always trips the wall. Now the one-trip rule caused the Mayfair beads, and Tess's two short trips at Marrable Street would have dodged the beads and the turn. The rule costs twice.
     - **Tess's secret.** Rewrite it: she planned Mayfair as one 9.4-second visit when two short ones would have done, because one trip is the rule and tenths are her pride. Rosie's weight loss starts after Mayfair. It is the starvation the cheers hide.

3. **"The cure missed for thirty-one years" fails the flip test, and Rosie survives where the lost died by luck.**
   - *Where:* the logline; section 6 (the "Face home" row and step 8); section 15, item 1; ep 9, act four; guess 5 (the spare); section 3.2 ("Nobody who followed the Deep has come back").
   - *Problem:*
     - **Flip it.** By the plan's own world, a Marlow who broke "face home" at any time before 2026 would step out into rock (the Hill) or into eight metres of black sea (the Deep), as the lost did. Nobody knew next door's life was the other hand until the message in ep 8. So "face home" was not hiding a cure; it was keeping people alive. "Nobody could have known" explains the thirty-one years as well as "they never asked" does.
     - **The spare.** If every rig has always carried one, the lost could have fired theirs, and "nobody has come back" makes no sense. If Rosie has one and they didn't, the plan never says why. Either way, the season's one reward for breaking a rule is luck.
   - *Why it matters:* this is where exile meets the theme. As written, the ending half-says the rule was right. A line like "it was under our feet all along" would make the audience side with Peg.
   - *Fix:*
     - **Date the spare.** Spares came in around 2016, after the Deep's lost. Since then a crosser who stepped into the Deep could fire home, but nobody tried, because the rule and the saying were made in the years without spares. That is a rule right once and never re-asked when the world changed. It cost Terry five years: one spare-backed step in 2021 would have brought back a sea he could eat.
     - **Rosie's pit training** at eight metres (already planted) is why she keeps her head in black water.
     - **Rewrite the logline and step 8** to "since the spare came in". Nobody on screen says "thirty-one years".

4. **The Aherns' one difference does no work, and their paper saves nobody. What the theme really rests on is comparing records, not paper.**
   - *Where:* section 6, "Why 'always' is earned"; Maggie (section 7); ep 4, act three; ep 7, act two; epilogue, scene 5.
   - *Problem:*
     - **The plan has the Aherns backwards.** It says they "keep the same code with one difference (Maggie opens her post), show where it goes without that difference". But they are the family *with* the difference.
     - **Remove the difference and nothing changes.** Aoife still turns and runs out, Con still starves, Siobhan is still cheered on the scale. The season's one controlled comparison then says that opening your post changes nothing, which is the opposite of what Peg's drawer is meant to prove.
     - **A hole.** Maggie must have read the 2019 Register letter, so why is Con not on free rations?
     - **The Aherns keep paper.** Their tide books time every job to the second, and Ray keeps a folder of every Marlow weight since 2002. Neither saved anyone.
     - **What saves people** in this plan is always two records laid side by side: the ferry, the bridge against the brass balance, Ray's 64.3 against Frank's ticket, "checked against Maggie's". So comparing is the part that is held, and the logline ("The only proof is... paper") names the loose part.
   - *Why it matters:* the Aherns carry "always". If their one difference does nothing, the season's only comparison muddies the theme.
   - *Fix:*
     - **Ep 4:** Maggie has filled in Con's Register form, and Declan puts it in the stove: "We feed our own." Her difference is real, and it is overruled. That shows one right person is not enough, three episodes before Tess learns it. This matches the craft critique's finding 10.
     - **Ep 7:** Maggie sees that her tide books held the seconds for twenty years but never who turned. The Marlows' memory held who turned but never the seconds. Neither family could see it alone.
     - **Logline:** point at the comparison, for example "paper, and somebody else's to check it against".
     - **Epilogue:** on the Ahern table, under Siobhan's scale, lies December's front page, THE TEN-SECOND CURE, unread. The answer is printed and in their house.
     - **Correct the sentence** in section 6.

5. **On the plan's own dates, the code didn't kill Frank. And his reveal is Caraway's secretly turned keeper unless nobody knew.**
   - *Where:* section 6, "Why 'always' is earned" and step 1; ep 10, act one (the 2001 letter); section 3.2 (the first mirror kitchen, 2009); canonical numbers.
   - *Problem:*
     - **How Frank died.** He turned on a four-second job in 2001, a one-in-twelve chance: bad luck in the short-job era. Then he starved, because no mirror food existed. Obverse opened the first mirror kitchen in 2009, and the Register's rations come from Obverse.
     - **The drawer did nothing.** It is the only family habit on his path, and the 2001 letter offers only screening. Had he opened it, Frank would have learned he was turned and starved anyway. So "the code was already killing its founder" fails the remove test. "Make weight" was born from his death; it didn't cause it.
     - **What Frank does prove,** and it is strong, is that the code was built on a turned memory.
     - **The Caraway echo.** The culture's founder revealed as secretly turned is close to Caraway's high keeper. It is also the season's third "secretly turned" reveal, after Ray and Rosie.
   - *Why it matters:* "always" leans on Frank, and the owner has already flagged the keeper.
   - *Fix:*
     - **Change the letter.** The 2001 letter offers screening and a place on the Ministry's small lab-ration scheme for turned crossers (a few dozen mouths, costly, years before Obverse). It says applicants will be interviewed by police. In 2001, "never go to them" meant choosing between prison and hunger, and the drawer chose hunger. The drawer now kills in 2001, 2019 and 2026: one habit, three letters.
     - **Make it plain that nobody knew,** not even Frank: "off his food", "his sugar", the jar. He hid nothing; the family's memory did the hiding. That separates him from Caraway's keeper, who knew and ruled anyway.

6. **The smell-and-taste proofs in episodes 9 and 10 bring back the mint (rule 1, near miss).**
   - *Where:* ep 9, act five ("wrong... the seaside... Both are right... It tastes of crab"); ep 10, act four (Terry breathes in: "Southend"); ledger 4.8; the theorem 4 row.
   - *Problem:* The Catch's key reveal turns on a flavour tasted by someone who has turned ("nothing has happened to the mint"), and Caraway's turned on a mint taste test. The plan bans a taste test "as a ritual" and says identity is settled by the meter, the catalogue, the strip or food. Yet the season's biggest discovery, that next door is food, and Terry's decision are settled by smell and taste three times. "It tastes of crab" even has the rhythm of the mint line. It is the same device, one substance with two hands and two verdicts, in thin disguise.
   - *Why it matters:* a rule 1 near miss at the season's turning point.
   - *Fix:* settle it with instruments the season has already planted.
     - A drop of the sea on Peg's meter reads LO, as Rosie's blood did.
     - The same drop on the T-meter the nurse gave Rosie in ep 8 gives a normal reading.
     - Two meters give two answers, and both are right. That is theorem 4 ("whoever holds the reference sample decides") with objects in shot.
     - Keep Sixpence growling. Terry eats the crab and asks for a second helping, with no verdict line; his face does the work.
     - Cut the "Southend" breath (see finding 1).

7. **The world's ignorance of the drift is a rival explanation, and it turns Obverse into the villain.**
   - *Where:* section 3.2 (40,000 turned, licensed surgeons, rescue crews, the Register, insurers, Ray's unit); the theorem 3 row; section 14, tone ("Only two families... never ask"); epilogue, scene 1.
   - *Problem:*
     - **The scale of the miss.** Tess and Maggie find the drift from eighteen cases on the back of a timetable. Britain has 40,000 turned people, a Register, a police unit, rescue crews with logs, and insurers pricing every job. If none of them ever noticed that long crossings turn people, the whole working world is as incurious as the Marlows, and "only two families never ask" is false.
     - **The villain it makes.** The one party that knows, Obverse, keeps a cure worth "the price of an appointment" secret at £180,000 and leaves 40,000 people living on rations for profit. That is the single hidden villain the brief rules out, and a viewer can blame the Marlows' doom on it.
     - The family itself passes the test: its own loft held the answer. What the plan lacks is a reason nobody else's records did.
   - *Why it matters:* until the plan says why the world didn't know, this rival beats the theme.
   - *Fix:* make the blind spot come from the trade's own code.
     - Licensed crossings are capped at three seconds (the craft critique's finding 14 gives a reason), and so are rescue crews. The legal world has almost no long crossings.
     - The only people who cross for nine seconds are thieves, and thieves keep nothing on paper.
     - The Register records who turned, never for how long, because saying how long would confess the crime.
     - So the drift is a secret because every crossing family keeps "nothing on paper". The Marlows' rolls become the only long-crossing record in the country, and Obverse is a firm guarding a recipe, not a villain. Give it the face the craft critique proposes.

8. **The final choice can be dodged by leaking the Obverse binder.**
   - *Where:* ep 6, acts two and three (Tess lifts the binder); ep 10, act five; epilogue, scene 1.
   - *Problem:* swap "hand the rolls to the police" for "post Obverse's binder to a newspaper". The drift still goes public, cures still cost an appointment, Obverse's shares still halve, and nobody goes to prison. The season's closing sacrifice becomes avoidable, so it reads as needless.
   - *Why it matters:* if a swap can undo the climax choice, it isn't doing the work of a climax.
   - *Fix:*
     - **Burn the binder.** In ep 7, act four, Peg feeds it to the stove along with the timetable (Tess kept them together).
     - **Make the rolls the only proof nobody can call a forgery.** They are certified by Trading Standards, dated, and printed on a public weighbridge: twenty-five years of real crossings. They also carry what the binder never did: when the Hill moved, and where the door is.
     - Now the only way to make the drift public is to confess.

9. **Exile looks like a shortcut until the dice-against-door sum is on screen.**
   - *Where:* ep 10, acts three and four; canonical numbers (Terry 71.4 kg, Ray 58.6, Danny 88, Rosie 46.2); the theorem 3 and 7 rows.
   - *Problem:* the plan has the numbers but never lays them side by side, so viewers will ask why Terry doesn't just do what Ray did. The answer is in the budget.
     - **Terry,** at 71.4 kg, has about 5.7 seconds. A five-second hop at home cures him one time in six. A five-second step to the shore feeds him five times in six. Same five seconds, same coin, read from the other side.
     - **Ray** is lighter. He can reach 7.5 seconds, and he has a life here.
     - **Danny** can reach neither.
     - **Rosie** could take a ten-second hop at seven in ten, and chooses neither.
   - *Why it matters:* this answers "does the easy way out undercut the theme?"
     - **With the sum,** exile is not an escape from the dice. It is the same dice, and it costs everything Terry has here. Weight, the family's old measure of worth, finally decides honestly who gets which cure.
     - **Without it,** the door reads as a way out that skips the reckoning.
   - *Fix:* on the night of writing, Tess writes each turned person's two odds on the roll ("Terry: here 1 in 6, there 5 in 6") and asks each of them. Nobody decides for anyone. One line of arithmetic, no speech.

10. **The Register's texture is drifting toward a dystopia (rule 6).**
    - *Where:* section 3.2 (sealed loos, bans on food work and swimming, cremation, border refusals, strip tests at every gate); Ray's unit (section 7: "finding unregistered turned people, because of what they carry"); epilogue, scene 1 (licensed crossings next door "for turned volunteers only").
    - *Problem:* each rule has a reason, but together they describe a marked minority:
      - separate toilets;
      - barred from jobs;
      - refused at borders;
      - cremated by law;
      - hunted by a Met unit if unregistered;
      - and in December, invited to emigrate to another world.
      That is the shape rule 6 rules out, however kind the nurses are.
    - *Why it matters:* rule 6 is one of the owner's six points.
    - *Fix:* keep the laws, which do jobs, and change who enforces them and how it feels.
      - **Ray's unit** investigates crossing crime: dips, spins, "delivered" bullets. That also gives the Crime lead a pursuer who actually pursues crime.
      - **The Register** is a health service people are glad of, like a diabetes clinic. Ray shows his card without shame. One scene shows it working well for an ordinary turned person, such as a surgeon or a rescue worker.
      - **The epilogue's consultation** becomes crossing next door as one option offered at the Register's cure clinics, not a scheme "for turned volunteers only".

11. **Peg carries almost every wrong answer.**
    - *Where:* episodes 7-9; Peg in section 7.
    - *Problem:*
      - **In eps 7-9, every "more of the same" is Peg's alone.** She burns the timetable, puts Tess out, orders the fourteen hops, plans the depot raid, sends Shaun on the nine-second leg, chalks yes to Kieran at 38 kilos, and tips the sea down the drain.

    - *Fix:* give each act a second pair of hands.
      - Danny is the one who says "You went to them" and wants Tess out, out of grief for Liam.
      - Shaun asks for the long leg himself: "Eleven tenths, Nan."
      - The table cheers Kieran's 38 before Peg chalks it.
      - Danny lifts the drain grate for her.
      - Peg still leads. The family votes with her.

12. **Kieran does the adults' asking for them.**
    - *Where:* episodes 9 and 10 (the log, the bucket on two scales, the graph, the door, the dates); Kieran in section 7; epilogue, scene 5.
    - *Problem:*
      - **What Kieran supplies:** the proof that the bridge lies, the two-scale test, the door ("The thing in Nan's field didn't fall. It stepped out"), and the season's last written line. The adults ask two questions between them.
      - **The stock figure.** That is the child who sees what grown-ups can't, which is the originality review's worry about his label.
      - **The Caraway echo.** It recalls Caraway's Lottie, who asks the question and chalks it up, and Caraway's ending, with children at the Tally board.
      - **The blunted point.** It weakens the brief's point that the people themselves must become able to ask.
    - *Why it matters:* the brief's "one right person is not enough", and a Caraway echo.
    - *Fix:* keep Kieran's log and his bucket, which pay off his want. Give the door to three adults laying their memories side by side.
      - Peg remembers the Ministry men in waders, and the field flooded "at the funny tide" the morning the thing came.
      - Maggie brings the Nobody Tide tables.
      - Rosie gives the depth.
      - Peg, whose memory turned Frank, now remembers true, checked against Maggie's. Kieran writes it down.

13. **The field-as-the-door twist collides with the Hill moving.**
    - *Where:* guess 4; ep 4, cold open; ep 7 ("Your Hill moved our tide"); ep 10, act two; twist 13.
    - *Problem:* the door is "where the Hill's shore meets our ground level... the Marlows' old field", found from where the object landed in 1995. But on 4 April the neighbours move the Hill.
      - Move a mountain-island and its shore moves with it, so the 1995 landing no longer marks the door.
      - Maggie's tables, which the plan says the move shifted, no longer time it either.
      - The twist needs both "the Hill moved" (Liam) and "the shore is where it was in 1995" (the door), and the plan never squares the two.
    - *Why it matters:* this twist is planted in episodes 1, 7 and 9, and sharp viewers can break it before episode 10.
    - *Fix:*
      - **Say in guess 4 what moved.** The neighbours took down the Hill's far side, a quarry the size of a hill. Its pull on Grays changed; its shore at Mucking did not.
      - **Or keep a bigger move** and let Kieran's April step tell Peg how far the shore now lies from the old gate.
      - **Don't let the move create the shore.** A floor raised next door is pitch B's.

14. **Four ledger rows claim jobs that a swap shows they don't do (rule 4).**
    - *Where:* section 4.11, rows 2 (SETI), 26 (the Hand Company), 29 (dark-matter maps) and 30 (the silent black-hole merger).
    - *Problem:* the owner's checklist says "An item with no job fails." Swap each claimed job:
      - **The Hand Company**, claimed job "Rosie's final choice has somewhere to go". But she goes to a hospital isolation ward with surgeons, not to the Company. Cut the Company and her choice is unchanged.
      - **The SETI poster**, claimed job "why he points his telescope down". His log is a school project on the Nobody Tide, and any poster would do.
      - **The dark-matter maps and the silent merger** are texture, which the ledger half admits.
    - *Fix:* give each one a move in the plot, or declare it surplus in the ledger with a reason.
      - **The Hand Company:** on the ward in ep 8, a recruiter offers Rosie good pay and rations for life. In the epilogue she has chosen the surgeons instead, the three seconds she once called soft. The Company is the road not taken.
      - **The BBC's "next-door event" on 4 April** is what prompts Danny's "The Hill's moved" and Peg's "Hills don't move". It links the quake to next door.
      - **SETI and the maps:** give them one line from the scientist who reads the message in ep 8 ("sixty years listening to the sky"), or declare them surplus.

15. **The epilogue keeps the check but drops the kindness.**
    - *Where:* epilogue, scenes 2-5; section 12, "What it costs".
    - *Problem:* the theme review warned all three pitches that when kindness is what kills, the ending must keep the kindness and add a check, or the audience learns "don't be kind".
      - **The check survives:** Kieran's "checked against Maggie's".
      - **The kindness doesn't.** There is no table, no fry-up and no feeding, only a prison, a ward and a weighbridge. The only family still cheering round a table is the Aherns.
      - **What viewers take away:** the family that learned to ask lost its table, and the one that didn't kept it.
    - *Fix:* in scene 5, a December breakfast at the bridge. Peg serves Kieran his fry-up, then weighs him after it, not before ("Everything in you counts"), and writes it on the roll. The kindness kept, with a check, in one shot.

16. **The reason given for guess 1 can't produce its own numbers.**
    - *Where:* section 5, guess 1.
    - *Problem:* "the longer you tumble in the dark, the likelier you are facing the wrong way." A random tumble evens out towards one in two and never passes it. But the table climbs to three in five and seven in ten, and the source's seventy-per-cent cure needs it to. If a writer gives this reason to Tess or puts it in the protocol, a sharp viewer will catch it at the second reversal.
    - *Fix:* make the reason a slow, steady roll in the dark, like a boat swinging on its mooring, so the odds climb past a half as the roll nears half a turn. Better still, keep the reason off screen: only counting reveals the drift, which is the book's point and the season's.
