# Episode 4, "The Other Bank": critique of the draft

*Draft reviewed: `80-episode-04-draft.md` (the 08:39 version). This replaces the earlier critique that had the same filename. Checked against `70-plan-v3.md` (episode 4 beats, sections 9, 10, 13 and 14), the owner's brief and second feedback, The Catch notes ("The short answer" and faults 1-4), the finals of episodes 1-3, and, for dates going forward, the episode 5 draft.*

## Verdict

This is a strong draft. Every beat the plan gives episode 4 is on screen, most of the plan's lines are word for word, and every number matches the canon table. It adds some very good things of its own: the woman walking a baby at the lit window during the raid, "It's only my hand", "Nobody weighs a pantry", Peg wiping Rosie's column, "I'm turned, Nan", the two old men on two banks, and "The other one". No banned word reaches the screen (I searched for each one). Next door is never shown, no reading is seen live, and nobody's moral question is deciding for someone else.

The serious problems are about control of information:
- the last line's stakes are weakened by the draft's own earlier lines;
- one scene gives away the lying scale, which the season needs hidden until episode 9;
- the 02:04 mystery is solved in the same episode that sets it.

After those come one line that echoes The Catch, a cure the episode offers and never rules out, and some small physics and continuity fixes.

---

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The last line's threat has already been taken away (Scenes 9 and 14)

**Problem.** In Scene 9 Con says the rest of his pills were "in the cold", then adds "Enough till the boat." Maggie: "Boat's Tuesday." Con: "Then Tuesday." So the audience has just learned that Con has enough pills until Tuesday's boat. When Maggie's call ends on "Made to his hand. There's no more," it sounds like she's exaggerating. The draft's notes say "There's no more" means the boat has none. That is never on screen. It also gets harder later: in the episode 5 draft Con dies ten days on with an empty bottle, which needs at least one boat to have come without pills, and nobody ever says why.

**Why it matters.** This is the episode's last line and the war's first death. If the audience believes more pills are coming on Tuesday, the hook doesn't bite. Con's death in episode 5 then looks like bad luck instead of the price of the raid.

**Fix.** Plant, in Scene 9, that the pills can't be replaced, and take out the reassurance. For example: MAGGIE: "How many's a few?" Con doesn't answer. He rubs his chest. MAGGIE: "That chemist in Rotterdam took all winter making you them." Cut "Enough till the boat / Boat's Tuesday / Then Tuesday." Then the plan's line, "Made to his hand. There's no more," lands at full weight. Play Maggie's call as a plea. She rings to ask for the box back, so Tess's slow look at the stove is the answer, and the audience knows it before she does.

### 2. Scene 11 gives away the lying scale, and makes the family's checker look slow (cold open and Scene 11)

**Problem, part one.** The cold open writes 19.78 "under a long column of 20.00s". Episode 1 showed that column and "a dead flat line" of a graph, and episode 2 showed 20.00 every night in March. So Peg's "You dropped that on the step at Christmas" is plainly contradicted by the boy's own book. Yet the draft has Kieran, whose whole character is checking, "nod, satisfied" at a paint chip.

**Problem, part two.** Within about ninety seconds the same scene stacks:
- Mick's "two hundred light";
- Kieran's "light too";
- a readable envelope marked "TRADING STANDARDS · IMPORTANT", from the office whose Mr Pinnock checked these same scales in episode 1;
- a drawer gag that holds the camera on the envelope.

**Why it matters.** The plan wants the audience surprised when Liam runs out in episode 5 (twist 5: the fry-up must look like the whole answer). It also wants the audience to learn that the scales lie only in episode 9 (twist 12, the start of the third reversal); the knowledge map says "9 (plants from 4)". As drafted, any viewer who notices that Peg's date is wrong has been told the weight is fine and the scale isn't. Two of the season's twists are exposed in episode 4.

**Fix.** Give the chip a cover story that holds up, and blur the plants:
- **The least change:** cut "at Christmas" from Peg's line: "You dropped that on the step. It's chipped. Stop wasting roll." Kieran hauled the weight about in the scramble after the quake, so he can reasonably believe it.
- **Better:** in the cold open, the weight slips off the counter's edge onto the step with a clang before he weighs it. Then the chip is real and dates from the same morning, and most viewers will believe it along with him. Episode 9 still breaks it with Peg's flour on the brass balance.
- **Blur the plants:** show the envelope as "a brown window envelope with the council's crest" and drop the drawer gag. Either take "too" out of Kieran's line, or move Mick's line to another morning (Scene 2's queue of lorries is right there).

The plan's job for this beat, Kieran believing her until episode 9 ("Nan says it's chipped"), is kept.

### 3. The 02:04 mystery is solved the same night it's set (Scenes 2, 8 and 13)

**Problem.** In one episode the audience gets four pointers to the same answer:
- the Ahern chalk board reading "JOE 59, with a star" (Scene 8);
- a tow that "comes past ours just after two" (Scene 2);
- a ticket at 02:04 reading −59.0;
- "The camera stays on the safe" (Scene 13).

Episode 2's "Fifty-nine!" is a fifth pointer.

**Why it matters.** The knowledge map has the audience learning that someone stood by the safe at episode 5 (only sharp viewers at 4), and that it was Joe at episode 6. "Who stood by the safe at 02:04?" is a listed mystery, answered in episodes 5 and 6, and Maggie's "Fifty-nine. That's my Joe" is episode 7's payoff. With all of this together, ordinary viewers solve it tonight. Episode 5's bullet and episode 6's blind ambush then lose their question.

**Fix.**
- Take the numbers off the Ahern board: names and stars only. Chalk Siobhan's number with Declan's back to the camera. Episode 2's single shout stays as the fair plant for sharp viewers.
- End Scene 13 on Kieran's pencilled "GLITCH?", not on the safe.
- Keep the tow line where it is. Episode 6 needs it, and it sits in act one, far from the ticket.

### 4. A line that echoes The Catch, and explains (Scene 3)

**Problem.** The clerk says: "Your germs are the wrong way round and all, aren't they? Nothing over there would know what to do with them." This restates, nearly word for word, the idea behind The Catch's two dishes of turned germs that nothing will eat. It also has a minor character explain a rule that the passport box and the strip test are already showing.

**Why it matters.** Nothing from The Catch is the owner's first rule, and the owner named mirror germs as the law The Catch used. Rule 11 lets mirror life appear only as law, borders, gel, gloves, gowns and risk. Here the border is doing that work, so the words are both redundant and risky.

**Fix.** Cut both sentences and keep "*(lower)* They're frightened what you'd bring in with you." Cut the pun "They turned him round at Stansted" as well, because it's the writer winking with the family's key word. Use "They sent him home from Stansted."

### 5. The episode sets up a free cure and never rules it out (Scene 2)

**Problem.** The war council sets out the grip rule: grab someone and one of you turns, a coin says which, and whoever turns leaves the hand behind. Shaun's "Shook on it" then explains the ferry man. Two turned people sit at this table, Rosie and Terry. Rosie spends the rest of the episode chasing £90,000 in Lisbon and £180,000 in London. A viewer who applies the rule just taught will ask why she doesn't shake hands with Terry. The plan answers that only in episode 8 ("We could hold on, in there") and episode 10 ("I cook with these").

**Why it matters.** If an obvious way out is left open, the heroine looks slow and her desperation looks optional, in the very scene that teaches the rule.

**Fix.** Add one silent beat. At "Shook on it", Rosie looks at her own hands, the hands she wants for surgery, then at Terry's yellow gloves. Terry turns back to the stove and fills the kettle. Nobody says anything. That keeps the question closed until episode 8, and plants episode 10's "I cook with these". Kieran's open-and-shut hand beat can stay beside it.

### 6. The raid's numbers and marks don't add up (Scenes 5 and 7)

**Problem, in three parts.**
- **The count.** At fifteen Tess says "It came." By the budget rule, an arm to the elbow (about 2 kg) plus a tub has about nineteen seconds, and the tingle comes about two seconds before the end, as it did in episode 3. So the tingle can't have come by fifteen. Tess, who never gets a number wrong, gets one wrong, and the script doesn't mean her to.
- **The reach.** An arm to the elbow reaches about 40 cm into a forty-foot container from one spot. The crew never moves along the wire, yet Danny "sweeps the container's floor" and they conclude "that's all of it."
- **The frost.** His hand sweeps the floor, but the next morning "the frost on the floor is smooth as a pond."

**Why it matters.** The owner asked that every law be true (here, rule 4, the budget). Sharp viewers do these sums. And the smooth frost throws away the best clue Scene 7 could have.

**Fix.**
- Count to eighteen: "Seventeen. Danny. Eighteen. Out."
- Either make it a twenty-foot reefer with one rack of shelves against the fence side, or have Danny shift a pace along the wire between reaches.
- In Scene 7, replace "smooth as a pond" with finger-tracks raked through the frost along the lowest shelf, an arm's length in from the wall, and no bootprint. Then Maggie's "That's a hand" is read off the frost, not guessed from a count.

### 7. "Terry has never had a column" contradicts the canon, and the camera can't show it (Scene 2)

**Problem.** Terry was a crosser. He was made to weight at 63.0 kg for his 2021 job and hopped eleven times after it, and the board holds "everyone's weight". So he had a column once. The line, and the notes' "Terry never had one", are also captions: the camera can't film a column that never existed.

**Why it matters.** It's a small break with canon, and it weakens the beat. The real point, that the family wipes out the turned, hits harder if it has happened before.

**Fix.** Show it. After Peg's three strokes, the camera finds the ghost of an old chalked TERRY at the edge of the board, under years of wiping, and Rosie sees it. Do the same for other lines only a reader gets: "It's the first time she's told them", "The last time he asked her that, she said 'willpower'", "Liam has watched that hand go into the safe a hundred times", and "Nobody has ever said it to him before". Give each one a look the camera can film, or cut it.

### 8. The radio claims more than the physics allows (Scene 1)

**Problem.** "Instruments in London recorded nothing at all." Shaking strong enough to walk cups off their hooks in Thurrock would show on London's seismometers. And episode 2 taught viewers that ripples from next door are "recorded by gravity meters around the world". So the line raises a question the season never answers. The plan says only that the dig is felt at Grays and Gravesend and not in London, and that London's scales read true.

**Fix.** "In London, nobody felt a thing." That keeps the hint and loses the contradiction.

### 9. Terry reacts to a name he can't hear (Scene 14)

**Problem.** "At the stove, Terry's spoon stops" when Maggie says her name, but only Tess can hear the handset.

**Fix.** TESS *(flat, aloud)*: "Ahern." Then the spoon stops. Or let Terry react to Tess going still and turning her back, which he can see.

### 10. The Ahern scenes tell people what they already know (Scenes 7 and 9)

**Problem.**
- In Scene 9 Maggie tells Con that the wall read Aoife for a month and that "we stood our minute on the pavement". Con stood it with her.
- "Boat's been short since Aoife" is never explained. Why would her death cut the Rotterdam supply?
- In Scene 7, "All gone on the ebb" doesn't mean anything a viewer can use.

**Why it matters.** This act carries the episode's twist. Lines spoken past the listener to the camera read as exposition, and links that are never explained leave viewers puzzling when they should be feeling.

**Fix.**
- Let the fridge printout carry "less every day"; the camera already shows it. Keep Maggie to "So she could feed him" and the bottle.
- If "short since Aoife" stays, add a clause: "since Aoife stopped doing the Rotterdam run."
- Replace "All gone on the ebb" with a line that does a job. For example, Maggie looks at the empty berth: "Tow was out. They knew." That also foreshadows Danny's sum about the tow in episode 6.

### 11. Burning the pills changes the plan without saying so (Scenes 6 and 14)

**Problem.** In the plan, Con's pills "go with the stolen tubs" (ledger; section 15, item 25). In the draft, Tess burns them by accident under Peg's "Paper talks." It's a good change: it ties Con's death to the family's no-paper rule, and gives Tess a guilt that fits her flaw (she never checks). But it takes away the choice the plan's version leaves open for episode 5, whether to hand an enemy's medicine back. And it adds duties the plan never allowed for: episode 7's ferry must decide whether Maggie learns that the woman across from her burned Con's pills.

**Fix.** Keep it, but record it as a declared change in the revision log and tell the writers of episodes 5 and 7. The episode 5 draft already uses it ("I put them in the stove").

### 12. Continuity with the finals and the next episode (the notes, and Scene 3)

**Problem.**
- **A missed promise.** Episode 3's final promises that Rosie still has the Obverse leaflet in her coat in episode 4, with her practised plain signature on its back. This draft brings in a new Lisbon printout instead, and never uses the leaflet.
- **Wrong citations.** The notes cite Con lines that are in no final ("Ours do that"; "'We feed our own,' my lot say") and quote Shaun as "Aherns. All winter" (the final has "All winter").
- **Going forward.** The episode 5 draft says episode 4 ends on Friday 17 April, gives Con "his old mobile" and calls the pills "the little tub... pills in foil". This draft ends on Saturday 11 April, with the Aherns' cordless landline and a white carton.

**Fix.**
- Have Rosie write LISBON and the half price on the front of the Obverse leaflet. At the counter she signs the passport form in the practised plain hand, a quiet payoff of episode 3's writing test, and the clerk's eyes go to her wrists anyway.
- Correct the notes against the finals.
- Pick one set of dates and props, and hand it to whoever revises episode 5.

- **Liam's "Nan says never go back twice" and Danny's "It's only my hand."** The rule bends the moment it's inconvenient, with no comment.
- **"Nobody weighs a pantry."**
- **Peg wiping Rosie's column, "You're poorly." / "I'm turned, Nan."**, and the fish slice that stops and then goes on.
- **Rosie's "They'll clap you." / "They never clap me." / "They will."**, and her hearing it as she says it.
- **Declan's "Light as Aoife was, at your age", and Maggie's answer in bread rather than words.**
- **Two old men on two banks, not ringing off.**
- **"Is that the weigher?" / "The other one."**
