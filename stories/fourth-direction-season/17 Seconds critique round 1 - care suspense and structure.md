# Critique, round 1: craft (care, suspense, structure)

*Reviewer's lens: the Bond, Anticipation, Gap and Implied World theories and the Crime profile. Plan read: `30-plan-v1.md`.*

**Rule check, every page.** Rules 2 (religion left out), 3 (no trace of the theory), 5 (no named ideas; the banned words appear only in the writers' list) and 6 (not a dystopia) pass throughout. Rule 7 has a breach (finding 3) and rule 1 has two near misses (finding 4). Every checklist item under rule 4 has a job, but two items break the show's own rules as staged (findings 12 and 13). **Mind-bending:** the physics reveals (the "forged" original, the honest scale that lies, the sea that smells wrong to one sister and right to the other, Ray's mirror signature) are the season's strength. The weak spots are the first reversal, which the pilot gives away (5), and a finale whose reveals arrive as sums and documents (7, 11).

---

1. **Rosie's lie starts the war that kills her brother, and the plan never makes her face it. She is also passive for five episodes.**
   - *Where:* ep 3 hook ("Something took hold of me in the black"); ep 7 twist ("was pride"); eps 4-8; Rosie's arc in section 7.
   - *Problem:* Rosie lies to hide what she thinks is her failure to face home. Peg answers "Blood pays blood", and the war follows: Con dies, Shaun loses a leg, her father Danny turns himself, and her brother Liam dies stealing "cash for Rosie's cure". The plan settles all of that in half a line of the ep 7 twist. Rosie herself never hears it, owns it or pays for it. Meanwhile she is missing from ep 4, "too weak" in the cold open of 5 and in 6, appears only in a twist line in 7, and is taken away in 8. Her arc says "hides, then tells", but the only telling is forced out of her by a meter.
   - *Why it matters:* she is the character the audience cares about most after ep 3. Bond theory says suffering with no response turns into pity and then fatigue. The Crime profile says a debt the story has made loud must be paid, or its non-payment shown. This is the loudest debt in the season, and it falls on the wrong people.
   - *Fix:* give her a thread she drives through 4-8.
     - **Ep 4:** she is barred from the cold-store raid, so she coaches Liam to make weight instead: "Skip the fry-up. Sleep in your coat." She passes on her own wound.
     - **Ep 5:** she times his runs in the pit, and the 7.4 for the job comes off her stopwatch. At the minute beside his reading she cannot look at Danny.
     - **Ep 7, act four:** as Peg reaches for the stove, Rosie tells the table: "Nothing took hold of me. I thought I'd faced wrong, and I was ashamed." Peg burns the timetable anyway and puts Tess out. Two people asking are still not enough, and Tess's betrayal in ep 8 ("You decided. Again.") now lands on the one person who had just stood with her.
     - **Ep 8:** Danny and Rosie, both turned, split the last tub. He pushes his half to her: "I'll sort it." That is his forgiveness, never said aloud.

2. **Liam is fridged, and the broken guarantee is only felt in hindsight.**
   - *Where:* ep 5; section 7 (Supporting); section 14 ("Liam gets a whole scene in episode 5"); section 2 (the guarantee).
   - *Problem:* Liam does not appear in eps 1-4. In ep 5 he is introduced, wants something, is weighed and dies. After that his death drives Danny's revenge (5-6), Danny's "And Liam?" (7) and the ep 9 reveal. His ten-kilo loss happened over months the audience never saw. And because the fry-up's part in his death stays hidden until ep 9, the plan's claim that "after that every meal carries a threat" is not true for the audience in eps 5-8.
   - *Why it matters:* this fails the Bond theory's fridging test. Cut his one scene of wanting and his death does exactly the same job for Danny and the plot. The season's horror beat, "the boy weighed a tenth of a second short", lands on a stranger.
   - *Fix:*
     - Put Liam in every episode from 1 to 4, with a want of his own: to stop being "the big lad".
       - **Ep 1:** at the weigh-in, 72 kilos, he is the only one who gets no cheer. He carries the crate.
       - **Ep 2:** he is on Danny's bead-floor pour. He knows the Marrable Street floor.
       - **Ep 3:** he carries Rosie off the bridge.
       - **Ep 4:** he slips his fry-up to Sixpence under the table.
     - **Ep 5, the bomb under the table:** Tess weighs him before breakfast and goes to load the van. The camera stays at the table while Peg piles his plate and he eats every bite. The audience was taught in ep 1 that "everything in you counts, breakfast too". Now they are ahead of the family, and the table itself is the threat, as it happens.

3. **Rule 7 breach: pitch C material is grafted in and not declared.**
   - *Where:* ep 7, act three ("Your Hill moved our tide. Forty minutes, since April."); ep 7 cold open ("Ferry. Nobody slack."); ep 2, act three (the flu); section 15 ("Taken from pitches B and C. Nothing.").
   - *Problem:* three pieces come from pitch C without a word:
     - The neighbours move a mountain and a tide shifts by about forty minutes for good. That is pitch C's beat almost word for word: "the loch's slack moves forty-one minutes, for good".
     - "Slack" as the meeting clock. The plan retitled the episode because "Slack Water" was C's title, but kept the word.
     - A flu that skips the turned and is "noticed only by the audience" is C's episode 2 beat.
     The forty-minute line also has no job: nothing later uses the shift.
   - *Why it matters:* the owner said to take nothing from B or C unless it solves a problem, and to say so.
   - *Fix:*
     - Cut the forty-minute line.
     - Make the envelope say "Ferry. Nobody low water. Thursday." That plants the exact phrase that times the door in ep 10, so the line now has a job.
     - Vary the flu so it is SECONDS's own. Rosie and Terry are the only two left on their feet. That is required anyway: if Terry is in the house, the law says he cannot catch it. Terry brings her a tub and gives her a look (a plant for his secret), and Rosie nurses Kieran. That seeds her closing choice ("There's wards that need that").
     - Declare the flu in section 15 if it stays.

4. **Rule 1 near misses: two images sit close to The Catch.**
   - *Where:* the ep 8 hook; ep 4, act five.
   - *Problem:*
     - Ep 8 closes on Rosie, turned and sent away by her family, eating alone on a ward. That is the meal that cannot be shared, the one image the plan's own section 14 forbids.
     - Ep 4's "02:04. The printer chatters. 59 kg. Nothing on the bridge. Danny with a torch: nobody." stages the same scare as The Catch's needle climbing in an empty room: an instrument shows an unseen visitor, live, at night.
   - *Why it matters:* the owner's first rule, and the theme review's one warning about this pitch.
   - *Fix:*
     - End ep 8 on Rosie walking out instead (finding 7).
     - Drop the live scare in ep 4. Kieran finds the 02:04 ticket among the lorry tickets in the morning and logs it as a glitch. It then works as paper read later: Ray's "whoever did it stood beside your safe" (5), and Maggie's "He's fifty-nine, my Joe" (7). Ep 6's ambush at 02:04 can stay, because a stakeout is a Crime use, not a haunting.

5. **The first major reversal is given away in the pilot.**
   - *Where:* ep 1 hook; section 10 ("sharp viewers suspect from 1"); section 15 item 11 (the claim that the meter "lands on the audience with the family").
   - *Problem:* Ray says it outright in the last minute of ep 1: "If the painting turned, so did whoever carried it." The audience watched Rosie carry it. Every clue in ep 2 then reads as confirmation, not as a plant: the seven glances, the backwards roll, the flu, the clinic, and Ray's "Tell your uncle". The meter in ep 3 confirms rather than reverses. Ray's line also spends the carrier rule two episodes before Tess is meant to teach it ("Then you'd be eating it", ep 4).
   - *Why it matters:* a reversal needs a gap the audience did not know was there (Gap theory). As planned, ep 3 is a reversal only for the family.
   - *Fix:*
     - End Ray's ep 1 hook at "It's the original", then keep "I met what was left of him". The audience's question is now the painting and the money, Peg's "copy" against Ray's "original".
     - Give ep 2's seven hops a money clock: the buyer leaves on Friday.
     - Move "so did whoever carried it" to ep 3, act two, said to Tess alone at the plant. She answers it with the writing test, a check built so that Rosie can pass. The audience suspects along with her for two acts: dread through the ten-second diamond room, then horror at LO.
     - Tess's "Then you'd be eating it" in ep 4 then re-reads ep 2: the painting was Rosie's thermometer all along.

6. **Episode 1 overspends the attention budget.**
   - *Where:* ep 1, all acts; sections 3.2 and 3.4.
   - *Problem:* the pilot has to teach or plant about thirty items: the black, the lamp's radius, knots, weigh-walls and beads, the budget, breakfast counting, the tingle, the spare, face home, "Straight?", the window, Terry's tub, gin and dripping, Sixpence, the burned tissue, the forensic cuttings, Marlow Mix, fifteen stone, the pit, Terry's eleven hops, coming apart, the audit rolls, Kieran's test weight and the Nobody Tide, the SETI poster, the glucose meter, the surgeon on TV, the brass balance, the weigh-in, the jar lid, two mirror-script cards, Terry's pot, the insurer and Ray. Across the season, about twenty coined names are spoken, including the Hand Convention, Almaty, the Looking Act, *R v Adeyemi* and the Register Privacy Order.
   - *Why it matters:* every departure uses attention the plot needs, and film pays most for institutions and rules (Implied World theory). The two rules that suspense depends on, "breakfast counts" and "a turn can't be felt", get lost among the surplus.
   - *Fix:* show each rule where it first bites.
     - **Keep in ep 1 (needed to fear anything in eps 1-3):** the black and the lamp; the budget on the chalk board, with "everything in you counts, breakfast too" said once over the fry-up; the tingle; "Straight?"; face home; running out (Terry's line); the window; the cheers at the weigh-in; Terry's tub; Sixpence; Pinnock's rolls.
     - **Background only, with no line:** the posters, the BBC, the meter in the drawer, the brass balance, the cuttings.
     - **Move:** Kieran's test weight debuts in ep 2's cold open, where the Twitch gives it its first job. The spare moves to ep 5 (finding 13).
     - **Never name on screen:** the Convention, Almaty, the Act, the case and the Order. Each becomes a prop or a single anecdote: "Then you'd be eating it"; the ring in the stomach; "Police don't get the list, love."

7. **Episodes 8-10 are unevenly loaded: 8 spends its answer in the cold open, 9 and 10 are crowded.**
   - *Where:* the ep 8 cold open and twist (twist schedule row 9); ep 9, act five; ep 10, acts one to five, and the epilogue.
   - *Problem:*
     - **Ep 8:** it opens on Ray's audit ("seven claimants... dead for years"). The audience already knows he is turned (since ep 2) and knows about the van, so Tess's tracing is a walk to a door the audience has already opened. The twist is labelled a twist but is not one.
     - **Ep 9:** act five delivers three reveals in about ten minutes (next door is food; the bridge lies; Liam's sum), plus two drawer letters.
     - **Ep 10:** four climaxes (Frank; the field as a door; Ray's toss; Terry's toss) come before the vote on the rolls, and then six epilogue scenes.
   - *Why it matters:* time is a factor in suspense. Ray's four in ten and Terry's five in six each need minutes of held doubt, and they get seconds.
   - *Fix:*
     - **Ep 8, cold open:** the last white tub split three ways at Peg's table with a teaspoon. That is the starvation clock, with care before the threat. Ray's audit becomes the act-four confirmation after Tess knocks on his door.
     - **Ep 8, hook:** Rosie, fed and strong, reads "COME ROUND FOR TEA", looks at the unlocked door and walks out. The last shot is her empty bed.
     - **Ep 9:** open on Rosie facing the Deep on the bridge that night, intercut with the depot raid, so Shaun's nine-second leg and her black water share one clock. The food proof (the crab, the two smells) moves to act one, which frees act five for Liam alone.
     - **Ep 10:** intercut Ray's and Terry's tosses in the same minute at 05:52. Tess and Kieran are with Ray on the bridge; Peg and Rosie are with Terry at the fence. The two cures, the dice at home and the door next door, share one sequence, and the rolls stay the true climax.
     - **Epilogue:** four scenes. (1) Front page over Rosie on the ward. (2) Tess and Danny in the visiting room, and Ray signs in plain writing. (3) Peg and Kieran at the bridge, then Siobhan on the scale across the river. (4) The second molecule, and the outfall.

8. **Nothing ratchets after episode 5, and the best bomb is hidden.**
   - *Where:* Kieran's secret (section 7); ep 9, act one; section 10.
   - *Problem:* after Liam's death, the season's most dangerous secret is kept from the audience until Danny spots it in ep 9. A twelve-year-old is starving himself to qualify, on a scale the audience may already suspect is lying. Meanwhile eps 6-8 put no principal in danger the audience can see coming. Ep 6's turn is a reveal, ep 7 is talk, and ep 8's hunger falls on people Terry's pot can still feed.
   - *Why it matters:* show the bomb (Anticipation theory). A secret revealed at the payoff gives fifteen seconds of surprise. Shown early, it gives four episodes of dread, and it takes away the one refuge left, the child.
   - *Fix:* from the night of Liam's minute, give one glimpse per episode.
     - Kieran adds a second column to his log under the test weight: 40.0, 39.6, 39.1, 38.4.
     - His school dinner lies in the office bin.
     - Tess, living in her car in 7-8, never sees any of it.
     - In ep 9, Peg's chalk says yes. Danny's "Not him. Not after Liam." is then the release the audience has been begging for.

9. **The Crime lead's pursuer never pursues, and the final choice has no way out to refuse.**
   - *Where:* section 2 (the promises); Ray in eps 3-8; twist schedule row 8 ("the log's positions (3)"); ep 10, act five.
   - *Problem:* Ray makes no move that the Marlows have to answer, so there is no blow for blow between criminal and pursuer. Worse, the plan gives him the log's positions (six metres apart) in ep 3, so the master pursuer spends four episodes chasing a spinner his own evidence rules out. After ep 8 there is no pursuer at all. The depot raid, a theft from the state's own pantry, draws no investigation on screen, yet Peg is "on bail for the depot raid" in the epilogue. The vote is "give, give, give", with no outside pressure and no way to refuse.
   - *Why it matters:* the Crime profile promises "a long fight between equals" and "closing in". A final decision proves most when the outside reasons to choose rightly have been cleared away, and here there were never any outside reasons to clear.
   - *Fix:*
     - **Ep 3:** Ray reads the Marlow Mix pour contracts (paper is his craft) and stakes out Marrable Street. Tess reroutes the dip the long way, so his pressure adds the seconds. Give him only "two bodies at 3:12" here; the positions arrive in ep 7, from the letter Maggie opened.
     - **Ep 5:** Ray takes Rosie's rig at a traffic stop ("We don't cuff crossers"). The family's need for cash rises, and Liam begs.
     - **Ep 10:** the Register's investigators hold the depot weigh-walls' readings, four bodies. The only match is Peg's chalk board, and Peg wipes it. For once, "nothing on paper" works: without the rolls, nobody can be charged. They hand them over anyway.
     - Let Danny say "no" first, and turn.

10. **Con's death breaks the show's own starvation rule, and the Ahern code has no face.**
    - *Where:* ep 4 hook, ep 5 act two; section 13, rule 9 ("starves in about six months"); Declan in section 7; epilogue scene 5.
    - *Problem:*
      - Con dies of hunger within days of losing one store, although the audience has learned that a turned body lasts months on spirits and fat (Ray, Terry).
      - Maggie, who opens her post, would know that the Register's list is closed to police. So why isn't Con on free rations? The plan never says.
      - Declan, "who never changes", has no scene of his own.
      - Siobhan, the season's last human image, first appears in the epilogue.
    - *Why it matters:* a death that breaks a taught rule reads as cheating. Con then exists only to give the Marlows guilt, which is fridging again. And the closing image of "always" lands on strangers.
    - *Fix:*
      - **Eps 1-3:** Terry swaps gravy recipes by phone with "Con". Ep 4 reveals he is an Ahern.
      - **Ep 4, act three:** Con is already thin, because the Ahern supply has been short since Aoife. Maggie holds out a Register form, and Declan puts it in the stove: "We feed our own." The theft takes Con's last weeks, and the code does the rest.
      - **Ep 4:** Siobhan asks Declan to chalk her weight. **Ep 5:** it is she who answers Con's phone to Terry.

11. **Frank "held still": a loud question filed as a wonder. This is where a reveal could hit hardest.**
    - *Where:* ep 10, act one (Ray: "held still in the black until the seconds ran out"); the wonders list in section 11.
    - *Problem:* the heaviest line about the founder is spoken at the season's emotional peak and then promised never to be answered. The emphasis makes it a mystery, and the audience will chase it (Gap principle 5).
    - *Fix:* answer it on Ray's paper, with no new rule. Ray's 2002 file: "In and out needed five seconds. He had seven. The wall had him still for the last two." Turned, starving, and costing the family a job a week to feed, Frank took himself off the table.
      - The legend is re-read three times over: he was not heavy; he was turned; he stayed.
      - "We feed our own" killed its founder through his own love.
      - It rhymes with Terry. Frank left in secret; Terry leaves in the open, after Peg says "turned" to him for the first time, which is Peg refusing to repeat 2002.
      - If that is too heavy for the owner, cut "held still" and keep 64.3.

12. **Shaun's load turns with him, and the plan misses it.**
    - *Where:* ep 9, act two; new guess 3 ("whatever rides inside one field... turns or slides with its carrier, always").
    - *Problem:* Shaun is straight when he carries the sealed mirror rations on his nine-second leg, and he comes back turned. So his whole load came back in the ordinary hand: food no turned Marlow can eat. The plan says "Three weeks of food" and "Bad luck."
    - *Why it matters:* it silently breaks a declared rule (section 13, rule 8), and it throws away the raid's best beat.
    - *Fix:* at the table Shaun opens his case, and Terry tastes it: "That's just porridge." The longest leg, the most food, the same dice: the raid has turned its own loot. Peg: "Bad luck." Kieran writes it on the roll. Theorem 3 is felt at the table a whole episode before the rolls are handed over.

13. **The spare is taught where it does no work, and it makes Liam's death look like a cheat.**
    - *Where:* ep 1, act one ("Always carry your spare"); new guess 5; ep 5, act five.
    - *Problem:* the audience learns the spare as a safety device. Then Liam runs out "a tenth from home" and nobody fires it. Nothing on screen says the spare can fire only from a leaf and is useless in the black.
    - *Why it matters:* at the season's first death the audience will think "the spare!" and feel cheated or confused.
    - *Fix:* cut it from ep 1. In ep 5, act four, Tess checks Liam's spare light. Liam: "Can I use it if I'm short?" Tess: "It's for if you step out wrong. In the black it's no use to you." One line gives the rule and the dread together, and it is still in place for ep 9.

14. **The surgeons' three-second rule gives the secret away, and the secret's keeper has no face.**
    - *Where:* section 3.2 (licensed crossers); twist 6 ("that is why surgeons are held to three seconds"); theorem 3.
    - *Problem:* if regulators hold surgeons to three seconds because long crossings turn people, anyone can reason that long crossings also cure. Then the drift is not "the most valuable secret on Earth", and the ep 6 reversal is something the state already knew. Obverse, the only party that knowingly keeps the table, is never a person, so its payment (its shares halve) is abstract.
    - *Why it matters:* it undercuts the second major reversal, and a keeper with no face makes the reckoning feel abstract.
    - *Fix:*
      - Make the three-second rule right for a different reason. In 2014 a surgeon ran out mid-operation with a child's tumour in her hand, and the board set three seconds as a margin against running out. Nobody counted the side effect: surgeons hardly ever turn. In ep 6 Tess sees it in the table. The healthy world has the same pattern, a rule right for the wrong reason, but its people wrote things down.
      - Give Obverse a face: the ep 2 clinician, herself a Restoration patient. In ep 6 or the epilogue she defends the secret: "That price pays for the Register's kitchens. That secret feeds forty thousand people." She keeps it for a kind reason, which is the theme again, now with care.

15. **Episode 7 sags, and Peg leaves the rolls alone for no reason.**
    - *Where:* ep 7.
    - *Problem:* straight after Danny's turn comes an episode with no crossing, no clock and no danger: the loft, the ferry, the graph, the stove. Episodes 7 and 8 both run without a crime in a Crime-led show. And Peg, who burns the timetable, leaves twenty-five years of the same evidence in the loft for three episodes.
    - *Why it matters:* the ratchet eases right after its peak, and the missing burning is a hole viewers will notice.
    - *Fix:*
      - Intercut the ferry with a job Peg plans without Tess ("near enough"): Shaun's first job since the leg, eight seconds. As the women pencil "most over eight", his wrist unit passes 8.0. He comes back: "Straight?" "Straight." That is a false alarm that pays off in ep 9, when his nine-second leg does turn him.
      - In act four, Peg carries a roll box to the stove and stops at Pinnock's certificate on the wall. Without the rolls she loses the weighbridge licence, so the ordinary world's law protects the paper.

16. **"Straight?" is retired offscreen, at the wrong moment.**
    - *Where:* section 14, crossing grammar ("After the ferry the family stops asking").
    - *Problem:* after the ferry the family burns the graph and throws Tess out, and Peg's ep 8 hops are still "facing home". Nothing has changed their minds, so they would still ask. The season's best ritual would die with no scene.
    - *Fix:* keep "Straight?" through ep 9, and make its death two beats. In ep 9 Shaun answers "Straight" and Peg's meter reads LO in front of everyone. In ep 10, when Ray comes back from his toss, nobody asks: Peg holds out the meter instead. The question is replaced by a check, on screen.

17. **Niamh is a girl's name.**
    - *Where:* ep 4 ("Aoife's baby, Niamh... He screamed"); ledger row 28 ("her son Niamh").
    - *Fix:* call the boy Cian or Oisín, or make the baby a girl. An Irish-rooted family would not name a son Niamh, and viewers will notice.
