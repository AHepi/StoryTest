# SECONDS plan v2: round 2 critique, patch check and owner rules

**What was checked.** Every round 1 fix in `51-revision-log-r1.md` was treated as a new part of the plan. Each was tried twice: on the case that forced it, and on the cases it had to leave alone. Then every page of `50-plan-v2.md` was checked against the owner's seven rules, line by line. The word check: no banned word reaches the screen. The only hits are §14's own list of banned words and plan scaffolding such as "Ray's spinner theory". There is no religion and no trace of the theory of explanation. The breaches and failed patches are below, most serious first.

---

1. **The grip's price (guess 6) doesn't close the hole it was made for, and together with the sent-hand rule it opens a sure cure.**
   - *Where:* §5 guess 6 and the derived line on sent hands; §3.2 "Handshakes"; §4.5 row 6; §4.9 row 3; ep 8 act one; ep 10 act two. Log: laws 1 and laws 12.
   - *Problem:*
     - The price is "whatever of them was doing the holding or being held", so it shrinks with the hold. Two turned people who pinch fingertips cure one of themselves for a fingertip. The log's defence ("most would rather be fed free than lose a hand") falls, and the Register empties in a few rounds. That is the very case that forced the patch.
     - Laws fix 12 says a hand sent from the leaf "has an up and never turns". Suppose that hand closes on a turned crosser in the black. The grip must turn one of them relative to the other, and the hand cannot turn, so the crosser turns back every time. Any straight Marlow could send a hand and cure Rosie, Terry and Danny for certain in ep 8, at a fingertip each, while they starve. Take the other reading, that the anchored hand gives the crosser an up. Then a partner's sent hand becomes a tether that stops thieves turning on long jobs, and the doom goes away.
     - Either way the owner's theorem 3 ("the only cure is another crossing with the same odds") is false. The handshake is a cure at a fixed one in two that never touches the drift, so the drift is no longer the most valuable secret.
   - *Why it matters:* this breaks rule 4, because theorem 3 must be true and do work. The second reversal and ep 8's hunger both need there to be no cheap cure.
   - *Fix:* revise guess 6 in place, with no seventh guess:
     - A grip takes only when a closed hand holds flesh. Anything less slips, so the price is never less than a hand.
     - Only two crossers wholly in the black can grip. A field joined to the leaf, like a sent hand's, shears at its edge the way a rope does and turns nobody.
     - Then say plainly in the theorem 3 row that the handshake is a second, dear cure at fixed odds, and let the world show it: one-handed cured people in the background, and Obverse's price set against it.

2. **The ep 6 cold open is The Catch's needle.**
   - *Where:* ep 6 cold open ("At 02:04 the readout jumps to 58.4 with nobody on the plate"); §14 "Never echo The Catch"; log craft 4 and unresolved item 4.
   - *Problem:* at night we watch, live, a readout climb where nobody stands. That is The Catch's gravity needle climbing in Iona's empty room. The plan's own rule bans "an instrument showing an unseen visitor live in an empty room", then lets this scene through because it is "a stakeout". Calling it a stakeout changes the reason for the image, not the image.
   - *Why it matters:* rule 1. The owner rejected the first attempt as a rip of The Catch.
   - *Fix:* Danny watches the wall clock, not the scale.
     - He has done the sum from Kieran's ticket. The Aherns time every job to the second for the tug pickup (§7), so Joe will come at 02:04 again. At 02:04 by the clock, Danny hops blind.
     - The second ticket turns up on the roll next morning, as in ep 4.
     - This also gives the Ahern habit a job in the plot.

3. **"The world is printed funny" is The Catch's central reveal, told twice, and it breaks "Frank included".**
   - *Where:* §7 Rosie's secret; §6 step 1; ep 2 act two; ep 10 act one; twist 12; rule 6. Log: laws 15 and theme 5.
   - *Problem:*
     - Laws fix 15 has the turned see print reversed at once. Rosie then tells herself "the world is printed funny", Frank grumbles that the papers are "printed funny", and each reveal is that the world didn't change, the person did. That is The Catch's "nothing has happened to the mint": backwards print, the wrong theory that the world changed, then *you* changed. Ep 2 ("the roll's printed backwards" / "Tess checks it: fine") plays the same beat in small.
     - It also works against theme fix 5. A man who sees every newspaper backwards for six months, in a family whose ritual is "Straight?", cannot fail to know. "Nobody knew, Frank included" doesn't hold.
   - *Why it matters:* rule 1, and the Frank twist loses its logic.
   - *Fix:*
     - Rosie knows at once and lies. She already prices a cure in ep 2 and checks the painting, so the self-deceiving line adds nothing.
     - Keep the source's example once, as a slip seen from outside: Rosie says the roll's printed backwards, turns it into a joke, and nobody checks it.
     - Cut Frank's "papers printed funny". The jar, the weight curve, "off his food" and 64.3 carry him.
     - Let Frank know and hide it, as §6 doom step 4 says the turned do. He is the code's first victim, not a keeper holding a secret over others, so it stays clear of Caraway's keeper.

4. **The fence beat brings pitch B's ending back in, through the grip fix.**
   - *Where:* ep 10 act three ("Rosie reaches for his hand... Danny stops her... He goes alone... Danny counts him down"); §4.5 row 6; §15 declared likenesses. Log: laws 1 and theme 1.
   - *Problem:* pitch B's finale reads "The 4:12 tide. Tam counts, and will not hold her hand." SECONDS has a family member counting the old turned elder down at Nobody low water, with no hand held. The theme critic flagged pitch B's shape (scout, breath, elder at the tide). The revision trimmed that shape, then the grip fix added its most specific beat. §15's list of differences from pitch B doesn't mention it.
   - *Why it matters:* rule 7. Nothing may come from pitch B unless it solves a problem nothing in SECONDS solves.
   - *Fix:*
     - Cut Rosie's reach and Danny's line at the fence.
     - The grip already does its ep 10 job at the table: Tess's list offers "a handshake: one in two, and a hand", and Terry's "I cook with these" answers it.
     - Keep the count, which every Marlow crossing has had since ep 1, and add it to §15's declared likeness.
     - With this beat gone, guess 6's rope-and-pole clause has no job left. Cut it too.

5. **The spare, as revised, is The Catch's reserve, and it makes the Deep survivable for everyone since 2016.**
   - *Where:* §5 guess 5; ep 5 act four ("Tess checks the spare light on his rig... 'Can I use it if I'm short?'"); ep 9 act one; §4.5 row 1; §4.10 "weightlessness" row; §6 "Face home". Log: laws 4 and theme 3.
   - *Problem:*
     - The spare is a reserve charge on the wrist, with a light and a warning line, that brings the heroine home at two climaxes. That is The Catch's "a reserve I would rather you did not touch" and its reserve bar.
     - The revised spare fires in water, at once. So Rosie had no need to swim up: she could have fired on the seabed. What saves her is the spare, not her training. Three claimed jobs are therefore false: the ledger's "Rosie survives the Deep by [blindfold training]", the corollary row's "how Rosie finds up in black water", and theme fix 3's "survives by her pit training".
     - Any crosser who followed the wrong tug since 2016 would have come home soaked, so "It's sea. Under us." can't be news in ep 9.
   - *Why it matters:* rules 1 and 4 (two ledger rows claim jobs they don't do), and the ep 9 discovery.
   - *Fix:* revise guess 5 in place.
     - After stepping out onto a leaf, a rig can fire again only once it has stood a full minute there. The tight field stays, so it works in water. No light, no reserve.
     - A crosser who steps out eight metres down in black water must then live a minute with no up. Before 2016 and since, almost all drowned, so "the Deep keeps you" stays true. Rosie lives because the pit taught her to follow her bubbles.
     - Liam's exchange becomes: "Can I go again if I'm short?" / "Not from the black."

6. **Custody by pantry fails for Danny.**
   - *Where:* §12 scene 2; §4.9 row 5; §3.2 (the Register "asks no questions"; the 2019 Privacy Order). Log: laws 13, and §6's claim that "custody by pantry is now true in the ending too".
   - *Problem:* the only lock on Danny's secure ward is the pantry door. But the Register feeds any turned person free and asks no questions, and since 2019 the police can't read its list. Danny can walk out through an unlocked door and draw rations at any clinic in the country. Two round 1 fixes cancel each other out: the Register made kind and private, and the prison made a pantry.
   - *Why it matters:* rule 4, at the one place the plan says shows theorem 5.
   - *Fix:* add one line. The court moves Danny's Register file to the ward, so no other clinic will feed him. The Privacy Order keeps the police out of the list; it doesn't stop the Register obeying a court. Now the pantry door really is the lock.

7. **The flu from pitch C still fails the owner's test.**
   - *Where:* ep 2 act three; §15 "Taken from pitches B and C"; §4.6 row 1. Log: craft 3.
   - *Problem:* the owner allows a small idea from B or C only "if it solves a problem in SECONDS that nothing else solves". The reason given, "the law makes it true whatever we do, since Terry lives in the house", explains why a flu would skip Terry. It doesn't explain why SECONDS needs a flu. Rosie's turn already has about a dozen plants: the crisp packet, the dog, the cards, the jar, the cheers, the glances, the roll, Harley Street, the lard and the pot. Her immunity already does its work through the Hand Company poster, the fireman and her last line.
   - *Why it matters:* rule 7.
   - *Fix:* cut the flu. Seed Rosie's nursing inside SECONDS's own material: on the ward in ep 8 she sits with the fireman, near the fever beds the recruiter talks about. Remove "the flu" from §4.6 row 1.

8. **"It took both" is false: the scale alone killed Liam.**
   - *Where:* ep 9 act five; §14 Liam row; twist 5. Log: laws 6 and craft 2.
   - *Problem:*
     - The scale reads 1.1% light, so Liam's 62.4 is really 63.1. With the notes that is 64.6 kg, which buys 7.08 seconds: short of a 7.1-second job even without the fry-up.
     - The plan says the fry-up alone would have left him two hundredths "and it took both". In fact the scale alone was enough. With any margin under about 0.14 seconds, the scale alone always kills him, and the season teaches its viewers to do these sums (twist 12 relies on it).
     - Also, Kieran's ep 5 figure, 39.6 on the same scale, is 40.0 in truth, so the first glimpse of his hunger shows no loss at all.
   - *Why it matters:* the answer to the season's central death rests on a sum that doesn't add up, and the point that two habits were both needed is lost.
   - *Fix:*
     - Make the job 7.0 seconds. Tess's 7.2 is then two tenths of margin. The fry-up alone leaves 7.12, the scale alone 7.08, both together 6.98, and Liam runs out two hundredths from home. "It took both" becomes true.
     - Start Kieran's column at 39.2 (39.6 in truth).

9. **The 02:04 ticket can't read 58.4 under the plan's own rule.**
   - *Where:* §5 derived (a platform scale feels a body in the black "the way a weigh-wall does"; a weigh-wall feels a body's own pull, "so none of those lies"); ep 4 act five; ep 7 act two; twist 11; §14 Joe row. Log: laws 7.
   - *Problem:* if the little bridge feels Joe in the black by his own pull, as a weigh-wall does, the Hill's change can't touch the reading. It shows his true 59.0, just as the Gravesend wall shows Liam's true 65.1. The 58.4 plant, and Maggie's "He's fifty-nine", contradict the rule written in the same fix.
   - *Why it matters:* the third reversal's fair clue breaks the show's own rules.
   - *Fix:* the ticket reads 59.0 in eps 4 to 7, and Maggie says "Fifty-nine. That's my Joe." The clue gets better in ep 9: the only ticket since April that agrees with Maggie's steelyard is the one where nobody stood on the plate.

10. **The Twitch can't flood the walls under the three-second rule.**
    - *Where:* ep 2 cold open ("every weigh-wall floods its vault with beads at the same moment"); §3.2 (a wall floods "only when a body stays within its four metres for more than three seconds"); rule 13. Log: theme 2.
    - *Problem:* a twitch lasts an instant, so under the new rule nothing floods. The Twitch's jobs (thicker floors, Danny's order book, longer jobs) all rest on the flood.
    - *Fix:* add a detail of the world, not new physics: a wall floods after three seconds for a body, or at once for any reading over a tonne ("nobody carries a tonne"). The Twitch registers for an instant as a vast mass and trips every wall. Add this to §3.2 and rule 13.

11. **Danny's grab breaks the grip's own "flesh holding flesh" limit.**
    - *Where:* ep 6 cold open ("grabs Joe by the collar"); §5 guess 6. Log: laws 1.
    - *Problem:* the fix says only flesh holding flesh turns anyone, and a collar is cloth. By the rule nothing happens, and Danny keeps his fingers. The limit does no other job anywhere in the season.
    - *Fix:* Danny grabs Joe by the wrist. With finding 1's fix, a closed hand round a wrist is the clean case.

12. **Frank's death has had no cause on paper since "held still" was cut.**
    - *Where:* ep 10 act one; §6 step 1; §14 Frank row. Log: craft 11.
    - *Problem:* at 64.3 to 64.4 kg, Frank had 7.12 to 7.14 seconds for a 7-second job. The scale was honest in 2002, and the wall shows no cargo. On the plan's own numbers he should have come home. The log calls it "the same arithmetic that killed Liam", but Liam's sum had a hidden error and Frank's has none. Viewers trained to do the sums will notice.
    - *Fix:* give the wall's time log one line, since walls log times (Mayfair's 3:12). Ray: "Job was planned at seven. The wall had him in there seven and two tenths." A starving man is slow. Nothing new is needed.

13. **The 2014 limit gives the drift away in hospital records.**
    - *Where:* §3.2 licensed crossers; ep 6 act four. Log: craft 14 and theme 7.
    - *Problem:* the fix has surgeons crossing as long as they liked before 2014 and held to three seconds since. Operating theatres log every operation to the minute, and a turned surgeon ends up on the Register. Those are before-and-after records in the best-kept files in the country: surgeons turned often before 2014 and almost never after. Any routine audit would find the drift. That reopens theme finding 7 (the whole world as incurious as the Marlows) and weakens the claim that the rolls are the only record.
    - *Fix:* crossing surgery began only in 2014, licensed and held to three seconds from its first day, because a surgeon in a private trial ran out with a tumour in her hand. Before then, rescue crews crossed as volunteers and logged nothing. No hospital file then holds a before and after.

14. **Dark-matter maps still do no plot work.**
    - *Where:* §4.11 row 29. Log: theme 14 and unresolved item 3.
    - *Problem:* the owner's checklist says each "In the plot" example must either appear doing work, or be left out with its reason in the ledger. Row 29 does neither. It admits to a teaching job only.
    - *Fix:* choose one of two.
      - Give it one beat. In ep 10, after Rosie won't say what she saw, she stops at Kieran's poster and puts a finger on the dark-matter map. That is the only hint that next door has a night sky. It shows nothing of next door (rule 2) and keeps the wonder.
      - Or leave it out and state the reason in the ledger.

15. **"Register loos" put back the image of a marked minority that the theme review took out.**
    - *Where:* §3.2 Register; §4.6 row 2; §15 item 4. Log: laws 5, which works against theme 10.
    - *Problem:* theme finding 10 named separate toilets first among the signs of a dystopia. Laws fix 5 then kept sealed "Register loos" as the Register's main rule, for a risk the plan itself calls slow and rare.
    - *Why it matters:* rule 6. Separate toilets for one class of people is the image most viewers know from segregation.
    - *Fix:* make the precaution look like ordinary care: claimants carry a Register hand gel and never share towels, and the gloves and gowns stay. The law still does its jobs: borders, Terry's gloves, and stripping and washing before going next door.

16. **Two Caraway echoes are back.**
    - *Where:* §3.2 departure 1 ("Crossers wear slip-ons and buckles, never laces: 'Knots don't hold in the black'"); §3.1 ("It lifts you off the world for a few seconds"). Log: laws 14.
    - *Problem:*
      - Caraway's everyday details include "Knots are distrusted: crossers tie nothing ('knots come undone')", and its machine "for a few seconds... lifts a body 'off the world'". Laws fix 14 added the first of these to SECONDS, and the rig line matches the second.
      - The slip-ons do no job the Mayfair crate and Shaun's tugs don't already do.
      - The plan also uses "lift" and "lifts" about twenty times for thefts. Writers will carry the word to screen, and §14 bans it for a job.
    - *Fix:* cut the slip-ons and the saying, and reword the rig line ("It takes you off the world for a few seconds"). In action lines, use "take", "draw out" or "raise".

17. **The final choice rests on the police not thinking of the rolls.**
    - *Where:* ep 10 act four ("without the rolls, nobody can be charged") and act five. Log: craft 9 and theme 8.
    - *Problem:* Mr Pinnock says in ep 1 that the rolls are kept "by law". Investigators who know a certified weighbridge exists can get a warrant and take them. Not handing them over is no way out; only burning them is.
    - *Fix:* make that the choice. Tess: "They'll be back Monday with a warrant." Burn the rolls and lose the licence and the bridge (Pinnock's certificate, planted in ep 7), or hand them over and lose Tess and Peg. The cold office stove then means something.

18. **Taking the top off the Hill makes Grays scales read heavier, not lighter.**
    - *Where:* §5 guess 4; ep 4 cold open; rule 14. Log: laws 11 and unresolved item 2.
    - *Problem:*
      - The Hill is about eight kilometres off and rises above Grays' ground. Its pull on a Grays scale is mostly sideways and slightly upward. Remove its top and you remove that upward part, so scales would read heavier.
      - A pull strong enough to change weight by 1.1% from that angle would be several times stronger sideways. "Down" would tilt across Thurrock, and nobody on screen notices.
    - *Fix:* use the log's own alternative, made sharper. The neighbours dredge the floor of the Deep under Grays.
      - Mass taken from below makes Grays and Gravesend read light and London not, with no tilt.
      - The sea's surface, Rosie's eight metres and the shore at Mucking stay where they are.
      - Danny's "The Hill's moved" becomes his wrong guess, and Peg's "Hills don't move" turns out right. The wonder becomes why they dug.
