# SECONDS, round 5: an editorial review of the whole season

*Reviewed against: the brief (`00-brief-2.md`), the owner's second feedback (`05-owner-feedback-2.md`), the notes on "The Catch" (`the-catch-notes.md`) and the master plan, version 3 (`70-plan-v3.md`). Read: all ten episodes of `round-5/full-season.md`, start to finish, including the "Plants and payoffs" notes after each episode. Every claim below is quoted from that file. I read a script; nobody has watched a film of it, so where I talk about pace I am judging from the page.*

A few words I will use, in plain terms. A **plant** is a small detail put in early so that a later surprise is fair; the **payoff** is the moment it comes due. A **cold open** is the scene before the title. **Intercut** means two places shown in turns inside one scene. The **budget** is the show's own word-free rule for how long a crosser can stay off the world (a fifth of a second for every kilo under a hundred). The **drift table** is the plan's list of odds of coming back mirror-turned, by seconds in the black.

---

## 1. Verdict

SECONDS is a ten-episode crime drama about an Essex family of thieves who can step through walls for a few seconds at a time, and whose one boast, "Marlows come back straight", turns out to be the thing killing them. Its engine is not a villain but the family's own habits: never write anything down, never go back twice, make weight, face home, feed our own, never go to them. Each rule was once right; each one now costs a body, and the cost is found, every time, on a piece of paper somebody refused to read.

It achieves what the brief asked for. The core message is never spoken and is everywhere in the events. The three big reversals (Rosie was turned all along; the cure and the curse are both just time; the family's own scale, and then the family's own founding legend, were lies) each make you re-see everything before them, and each is planted fairly and early. The invented physics is not decoration: every law in the owner's checklist does plot work, and I could not find a place where the season breaks one of the plan's twenty "rules the physics must never break". Nothing echoes The Catch. No banned word or named idea reaches the screen. The world is ordinary and works; the only thing wrong with it is one family, and the family next door.

**Is it finished?** Very nearly. The season is complete, coherent and consistent across all ten episodes, which for a two-author hand-over (Claude's writers on 1 to 3, GLM on 4 to 10) is itself an achievement: the later episodes honour the early plants closely, including small ones (the padlock key on baler twine, the pencil behind Tess's ear, "So someone's counting"). I found **two substantive faults**, both local, both fixable in a few lines, and both about a crossing's geometry contradicting the show's own tenths on screen. The rest are quibbles. This is a polish pass, not a rewrite.

## 2. How it keeps the owner's rules

### The core message is shown, never said

The season never says "a culture that never questions dooms its people". It shows it as a chain, and the chain is on screen link by link:

- The pride is planted as a chorus in episode 1, scene 3: "PEG, DANNY and ROSIE *(together, a chorus they've done a thousand times)*: Never go back twice." / "KIERAN: Lightest goes longest!" / "ROSIE: Face home." / "PEG: And Marlows come back straight." And in the same scene, the family's own concrete lengthens every job: "Every inch is a tenth of a second they haven't got." / "LIAM: Us included." / "DANNY: Then we'll get lighter."
- The cheering is lethal from the first breakfast, and the audience is told so without anyone knowing it: "PEG: And everything in you counts, breakfast too" (ep 1, sc 3), and the same episode's "Goes through me like water, love. Since I turned" (ep 1, sc 13).
- The refusal to ask is shown as a habit, not a speech. Liam asks the one question that would have saved the season in episode 2, scene 4 ("Won't she come back with it? Whichever way it goes?") and Peg answers it away ("Not if she faces home"), and the table laughs. Terry, in the loft in episode 7: "He was off his food, love. Then he wasn't. That was enough for us."
- Every rule pays its bill in a body: Rosie (make weight), Liam (make weight plus a scale nobody re-verified plus a letter in a drawer), Shaun's leg (never go back twice), Danny's fingers (blood pays blood), Con (blood pays blood, the other side), Frank (all of them, twenty-four years earlier).
- "One right person is not enough" is kept: Tess, alone and trusting her own sums, hands Rosie to the ward (ep 8, sc 8); the way out comes only when the whole family writes and votes (ep 10). And "asking is not punished" is kept: Rosie's rule-break finds the food (ep 9); Kieran's homework proves the scale lies (ep 9, sc 11).
- The Aherns are the mirror that makes "always" earned: the same code with one difference ("Maggie has the day's post and a butter knife, and she opens every envelope", ep 4, sc 8), and in December Siobhan is still cheered on a bathroom scale "Under the scale, folded, lies December's front page, unread" (ep 10, sc 18).

The nearest the season comes to stating its thesis is one clause of Rosie's in episode 7 (see quibble 8). Everything else is questions of the ordinary kind the plan allows: "Why's it a kilo out?" (ep 9, sc 12), "How do you know it's not the weight?" (ep 9, sc 11), "What did my dad weigh?" (ep 9, sc 13).

### The twists are fair

I checked each of the plan's sixteen twists against its plants. All are planted before they land, and the plants are hidden in plain sight rather than buried:

- **Rosie turned since Mayfair (ep 3).** Planted in ep 1 with the crisp packet ("She looks at it a beat too long"), the dog ("He's jealous. She's been away"), the jam lid, the mirror-script card that "flows" beside the old one that is "careful and a little wobbly", "Since I turned", and the level in Terry's pot; in ep 2 with the seven glances at the catalogue cutting and the roll "joke"; in ep 3 with the lard and the dodge. When the meter reads LO and Tess says "It's not low. It can't read her", the camera goes to the two cards on the fridge and lets the audience do the rest.
- **The cure is time (ep 6).** Planted by the surgeon on TV ("She's done four thousand. Never turned", ep 1, sc 10), Terry's eleven two-second hops ("Hungry every time after", ep 1, sc 8), and seven three-second hops that do nothing (ep 2). Osei's "The licence came in the year after, at three, as a margin against running out" (ep 6, sc 8) closes it.
- **The floor turned Aoife (ep 7).** Planted by Danny's boast at the table in ep 2, sc 6 ("And Mynors had us back last autumn, for the floor under the one we did in '24. Two floors now. Long time to be nowhere") and Declan's "Their vans were outside it all week, MARLOW on the side" (ep 4, sc 8). The ep 7 flash back to the March table is earned.
- **The scale lies (ep 9).** Planted by 19.78 on the morning of the quake, Peg's "It's chipped" (and a real chip, because the boy really dropped it), Mick's "two hundred light", the council envelope in the drawer, the brass balance from ep 1 ("These were my mother's. Never told me a lie"), and Osei's "Sixty-one kilos, seven hundred grams" against "Point nought on my bridge" in ep 6.
- **Frank turned (ep 10).** Planted by "fifteen stone" said directly under "A FIFTH OF A SECOND A KILO UNDER A HUNDRED" (ep 1, sc 6: ninety-five kilos would buy one second, a sum any viewer can do), Ray's long look at the photo, "I met what was left of him", the jam jar, Terry's "It's the one thing in this family everybody's sure of" over the unopened box marked 02 (ep 7, sc 4), and "Sixty-four kilos" at the end of ep 9.
- **Ray's mirror signature (ep 10)** is planted by Rosie's practised plain hand down the back of the leaflet in ep 3.

Each twist is "hard to vary" in the plan's sense: swap it for a different one and earlier scenes stop making sense. A real spinner, for instance, would have had to put two bodies together in the Mayfair log, and Maggie's "He never came within three fathoms of her" (ep 7, sc 7) is exactly what the ep 1 pull ("the way a lorry passing too close on the A13 tugs at a car") allows.

### Every invented law is used and none is broken

I went through the owner's checklist against the text. All of it is on screen and doing work, most of it more than once:

- **The stack; only gravity crosses; the space between is black.** Every crossing is glow-lit and arm's length; "Radio can't cross" is felt as "She can't hear you, Tess" (ep 1, sc 1); the neighbours are only ever weighed (the Twitch ticket, the quake, the message).
- **Unpinning, the budget, everything in the bubble counts.** The chalk rule; Tess's "Forty-four dead at midnight. Eleven two, less the lady" (ep 1); the fry-up that kills Liam; "Then you'd be carrying the pit with you. Water counts. Everything counts" (ep 1, sc 7), paid when Rosie's thumb stops an inch off the button in the sea (ep 9, sc 5).
- **Dispersal, the tingle, the partial return, the sent hand, the amputee's seconds.** Liam on the wall ("It'll read him a month. Softer every day. Then it won't", ep 5, sc 16); the fizz under the nails in every crossing; Shaun's leg ("a clean flat plane... For one second nobody moves and nothing bleeds", ep 5, sc 9); Danny's blind hand in the reefer (ep 4, sc 5); "My leg weighed five and a half. Eleven tenths, Nan" (ep 9, sc 1).
- **Free translation, knots, sight inside things.** The sealed crate "banded, knotted and sealed, and empty" (ep 1); Shaun's nine mooring knots ("The knot isn't there any more. Neither end of the rope has moved", ep 5, sc 8); the guard's "slow fist of his heart"; Tess reading spine labels through steel (ep 6, sc 6).
- **Rotation mirrors, a slide never does.** The painting's window; "Linen has a hand, Mrs Marlow"; the framed "clean translation, no rotation".
- **No up in the dark; turn and slide feel alike; the coin; the drift; the tug of home; the grip.** The pit training; "Straight?" "Straight." said by a turned man in ep 9 ("I knew before we were out the marsh. I said it anyhow"); the drift table read aloud in ep 6; three tugs in ep 9; Danny's fingers.
- **Handedness is fate.** The meters; Con's pills "made to his hand"; Aoife's milk ("He screamed three days before we understood"); the dog at every turn of the plot, including the depot dog in ep 9 ("He can smell me. Sixpence's done the same five year"); mirror germs as law and habit only (gloves, gel, HAND: T, stripping and washing at the standpipe) and never as a dish or a mould.
- **Neighbours weigh.** The Nobody Tide, the Hill, the Deep, the Far, the quake, the metronome, "COME ROUND FOR TEA", the minus tickets.
- **The seven theorems and the corollaries** are all present, and the ending stipulation (next door's life is the other hand) is proved the way the plan demands, by an instrument and a man's blood: "Two meters. Same blood. Both of them right" (ep 9, sc 9).

Two things I checked hard and found consistent: the odds. Every crossing's outcome sits honestly in the drift table (seven three-second hops fail at 1 in 5,000; Shaun's two eight-second legs come up one each way at 1 in 2; Rosie's 9.4 at about 3 in 5; Ray's 7.5 at "about four in ten"). And the numbers. The canonical numbers in plan section 14 are used exactly and never contradicted across episodes (62.4 + 1.5 = 63.9; 64.6; 65.1; 6.98; 61.0 against 61.7; 58.0 against 58.6; 38.0 against 38.4). The one place the tenths in the black do not add up is finding 1 below, and the one place the tenths quietly contradict a stated alternative is finding 2.

One note for the owner rather than a fault: the "minus" readings (a body in the black over the plate prints as its mass with a minus sign, and prints true while the plate lies about everything standing on it) rest on the plan's own stipulation that gravity is felt at human scale. The show keeps to that stipulation consistently and uses it well; a physicist in the audience will have to take it as the world's rule, which the plan says it is.

### No echo of The Catch

I checked against the list in the brief and the plan's "Never echo The Catch" note. No mint and no taste or smell test settles anything ("Nobody says what it tasted of", ep 9, sc 8; Danny's "What's it taste—" dies unfinished). No backwards print seen through turned eyes: the roll "joke" is shown from the straight side ("ordinary purple print, the right way round", ep 2, sc 10). No dishes, flask, mould or growth. No hands on glass. No reserve bar: the bite "has no light and no gauge" and is never called a reserve. No ship, no kind figure, no child's drawing, no lift shaft. No episode ends on a meal that cannot be shared. The mirror-script beat that the source allows is turned inside out: a cured man signs backwards.

### No banned word, no named idea

I searched the whole file for every word and phrase in plan section 14 (conjecture, criticism, error correction, refute, falsify, fallible, good explanation, hard to vary, growth of knowledge, static, dynamic, tradition, authority, optimism, "nothing is settled", "question everything", "the problem of", infinity, and dystopia and Popper for good measure). None appears anywhere, on screen or in the notes. The background laws stay as single lines ("And step out on purpose, it's twenty years", ep 5, sc 13; "Police don't get the list, love. Not since 2019", ep 8, sc 10). Nobody makes a speech about asking questions.

### Not a dystopia

The world is ordinary and kind. The Register is "run like a diabetes clinic": "We don't take nobody that won't come, Mrs Marlow. There's a bed, there's a kitchen, and no lock on the door either way" (ep 8, sc 9). A fireman goes home with his first ration box and talks about his leeks. The police take rigs, not people ("We don't cuff crossers, love"). Trading Standards writes letters. The one oppressive thing in the season is Peg's drawer. Religion is left out entirely, as the plan says.

## 3. What works best

**The check-in that becomes a lie.** "Straight?" / "Straight." is the family's whole belief in two words. It is played straight in ep 1, becomes a hope in ep 2, a lie in ep 3, and dies on screen in ep 9 when Shaun says it and Peg's meter reads LO in front of everyone: "I never felt a thing. You never do. That's the trouble with the black." In ep 10 nobody asks Ray; "Kieran holds out Peg's meter, unopened, on the flat of his hand." That is structure doing the work of theme, and a reviser should not touch a syllable of it.

**Counting as love.** "She can't hear you, Tess." / "I know." (ep 1) returns as "Then why d'you do it?" / "So someone's counting." (ep 3, sc 14), and then as Peg counting Shaun home in ep 7 "and it is her daughter's voice and her daughter's daughter's voice coming out of her, generation after generation of it". Tess's lips moving with no number on them is the season's picture of grief.

**The meter.** Sugar tastes sweet in either hand; a glucose meter cannot be fooled. "It's not low. It can't read her" (ep 3) is the first reversal in five words, and the same instrument settles Danny (ep 6), Tess (ep 6, "5.4... Straight"), Rosie's fourteen useless hops (ep 8), Shaun (ep 9), Ray (ep 10) and, side by side with the ward's T-meter, next door itself. Every identity question in the season is answered by a thing that never crossed, never by a look.

**The fry-up.** "The camera stays at the table. He eats every bite. Sixpence's tail slows, and stops. PEG: That's my boy." (ep 5, sc 13.) The audience has known since ep 1 that breakfast counts; nobody at the table does; and then the sum in ep 9 makes the plate only half the answer: "Either one, he makes it. Both, he doesn't. By two hundredths." Peg's own confession, "I made him breakfast", is the best-earned line in the season.

**The ferry.** Two enemy weighers laying twenty-five years side by side on the back of a timetable, one out of books and one out of a head: "Marlow jobs out of a memory; Ahern jobs off paper; and neither woman remarks on which is which, because both of them know." Maggie's "Nobody counted her, Tess. That's what I've come to say" and the ending, "Nobody spun anybody." / "Except Danny. And the only one Danny ever spun was himself." The whole doom, and the whole cure, are on one sheet, and Peg burns it.

**Ray.** "You counted us in dinners." / "It's what there was to count." (ep 8, sc 6.) A pursuer who has fed his suspects for five years, charged them the going rate so that Peg would not smell charity, and never touched the money. His flaw is the family's: "Twenty-four years of watching us, and you never laid it beside a soul." / "It's evidence." / "Of what?" And his cure is a joke at the tensest minute of the season: "Eleven year I've wrote back to front so you lot could read me. Give us a fortnight."

**Frank.** The cold open of ep 10 "as Peg remembers it" (a big man, "fifteen stone if he's an ounce") replayed "as it was" (a thin man turning a jam lid the wrong way, "carefully, as if patience were the trick"), and "Cut to Rosie in the loft hatch, sitting on the top rung, wearing exactly that look." Then Peg's pencil: "FRANK. 10 ST 2. TURNED." / "There's your number, Frank."

**The proof by blood.** The crab, the hour, the two meters, and Terry pushing "his empty plate out into the middle of the table, towards the middle of the family, the way a man asks for more." It obeys the plan's ban on taste, and it is better for it.

**Small things to protect.** Peg's "Then you've had a good day" to a driver two hundred kilos light; Kieran's second column headed *me*; "Print me proper" as Rosie steps off the plate on purpose; Peg wiping Rosie's column and "an older name shows through the chalk dust: TERRY"; "Mind, her window's on the wrong side. She came back like that. She's staying like that."

## 4. The problems, most serious first

### SUBSTANTIVE

**1. Tess's crossing into the clinic breaks the show's own geometry (ep 6, scene 6).**
*What is there.* Tess "stands on the pavement by the black door" and goes down: "0.4. Stone steps from the inside. 0.9. A coal chute, a mouse run in old lath. The glow slides her down through the dark of the house. 1.3. A void, warm... 1.7. The cabinet... 2.2. She draws the PROTOCOL binder out through the steel." Then, at once: "Home is one pull, the strongest thing in the world. She faces it and steps out onto the pavement." Osei later reads the wall: "Two point six seconds."
*The problem.* The season has taught, three times over, that you step out where you are and that the way back takes as long as the way in. Rosie's Mayfair route is "past the guard at 1.9... the pull at 5.0, and back the same way" (ep 1 notes); at Marrable Street she has the tray at 4.9 and is home at 10.0; Rosie tells Liam "Then you step out if there's air. If you're in the concrete, you don't" (ep 5, sc 11). Tess is a floor down and a wall in at 2.2 seconds and on the pavement at 2.6. Either she should have stepped out inside the basement, or the trip back should have taken as long as the trip in, about four seconds, which is past the wall's three and into the one-in-twelve row of the table she is about to read.
*Why it matters.* The whole scene's tension is "I'm two and a half. Not a tenth more" and Terry's "That's their wall. It floods at three." As written, a careful viewer sees her cheat the rule the scene is built on, and the later "5.4... Straight" beat loses its honest odds.
*Fix.* Start her at lower-ground level, not the pavement: Harley Street houses have a sunken area in front with steps down. Rosie's casing already puts the records room on the lower ground ("Stairs down, lower ground. Toilet's under the stairs. And past it a steel door"). Have Tess go down the area steps and stand at the front wall of the lower ground floor, so the crossing is one wall in (about 1.0), the cabinet (0.5), and one wall out (1.0): 2.5 seconds, exactly what she planned, and back into the area where Terry's van cannot see her. One line of Rosie's briefing ("Area steps. Their door's below the pavement") sets it up.

**2. "Twice. Under three." would not have reached the Mayfair shelf (ep 1, scene 1; ep 6, scene 9; ep 7, scene 12).**
*What is there.* At Mayfair the wrist unit reads "1.8. 1.9" as Rosie passes the guard, the wall floods at three, and the canvas leaves the shelf just before "4.9. 5.0." In ep 1 Liam says "Could've gone in twice. Quick in, quick out. Wall never floods." In ep 6, when Tess runs the season through the drift table, "Her lips over both: *Twice. Under three.*" In ep 7 and ep 8 this becomes "I gave you nine" and "Nine point four, Tess", which is the moral hinge of the second reversal.
*The problem.* From the wall Rosie went through, the shelf is nearly five seconds in. Two trips under three seconds each, which is what "Wall never floods" and "Under three" both require, could not have reached it. Tess's guilt is real (9.4 seconds at about three in five is far worse than two shorter trips), but the alternative the script names is not one the shown route allows, and this is a show that teaches its audience to count tenths on a wrist unit.
*Why it matters.* The second reversal rests on Tess having chosen one long trip when a safer split was available. If the safer split is impossible as described, the guilt looks manufactured.
*Fix.* Two choices. (a) Change the alternative, not the route: make it two trips at about four seconds each ("Twice. Four and four." in ep 6; Liam in ep 1: "Could've gone in twice. Quick in, quick out. Half the beads."). At four seconds the table says one in twelve each, against three in five for 9.4: the guilt is exact, and it no longer depends on the wall. The wall-tripping cost of "never go back twice" is still shown at Marrable Street and at the lock-up. (b) Keep "under three" and move the shelf: put the art store against the wall the van parks on, so the canvas is out at about 2.8 and the remaining seconds are the guard's corridor Tess chose to route through. (a) is the smaller change and touches nothing else.

### QUIBBLES

**3. Liam's way out is shown much slower than his way in, with the cause only half on the page (ep 5, scene 14).**
*What is there.* In: brick "0.9. 1.3", the floor, the box at "2.6". Out, after the wall floods at three: "3.4. 3.9. The mesh again. 4.4. 4.9" (tingle), "5.3. The brick. Quick again. He goes through it as fast as he has ever moved in his life. 6.1. 6.5... 6.9", and the outer face of the wall "a hand's breadth ahead" when the glow goes out at 6.98. The brick took under a second going in and about a second and a half coming out at his fastest.
*Why it matters.* Nothing hangs on these tenths for the plot (the 7.0 job and 6.98 budget are the canonical numbers), but the audience has been shown symmetrical routes in eps 1 and 3, and this is the crossing the season asks them to re-run in their heads. The text half-supplies a cause ("Beads pour down into the box's well... His glow is full of beads. Beads ahead of him, beads on every side") but it is not made the reason.
*Fix.* One beat, using a rule already planted. In ep 3 Rosie "doesn't hurry, because hurrying wastes pulls." Give Liam the opposite: "He pushes. He hurries, and hurrying wastes pulls, and the brick that was quick going in is not quick now." Or trim the outbound tenths so brick-out matches brick-in and the loose beads take the difference.

**4. The family's theory of what happened to Rosie contradicts the rule they state a minute later (ep 4, scene 2).**
*What is there.* Tess: "Turn her round in the black and send her home to her tea — that's the only way it could be done." Then, in the same scene: "Grab someone in there and one of you turns. Coin says which." and Rosie from the stairs: "And whoever turns leaves the hand in there."
*Why it matters.* Rosie has both hands. If the family had listened to itself it would know she was not turned by a grip. The intended theory (the plan's, and Rosie's own words in ep 3: "It pulled me off home") is a pull that made her face the wrong way, since the family believes facing home keeps you straight. "Turn her round" can be heard that way, but it can also be heard as a wrench, and the scene teaches the wrench's price in the next breath without anyone noticing the contradiction.
*Fix.* "Pull her off home in there and send her back to her tea." One word, and the family's theory matches its own rules.

**5. Joe's way to and from the safe is never placed (ep 5, scene 4; ep 6, cold open and scene 4).**
*What is there.* Danny's sum: "He came off that boat, stood in my mother's kitchen, and rode away down the river for his breakfast." The tow "comes past ours just after two". Joe at 59 kilos has about eight seconds. Facing home puts a crosser back where he stands, so Joe must go from the passing tow to the safe and back to the water inside eight seconds, or step out in the office with a bolted door, a dog and a boy. The script hints at the right answer ("JOE (17), white to the lips, the river cold still on him", ep 6, sc 4) and never states the distance.
*Why it matters.* Danny's deduction is the premise of the season's best set piece. A viewer who has been taught the office is a room with a scale in it may wonder how a boy gets in and out of it unheard.
*Fix.* One line when Danny does his sum in ep 5: "Our wharf's forty foot off that safe. Eight seconds is there and back for a lad his weight." It sharpens the sum and closes the question.

**6. Aoife's father is dead and alive in the same conversation (ep 7, scenes 7 and 9).**
*What is there.* "MAGGIE: Who? Her dad? Her dad thinks facing home is a charm." (present tense, sc 7). Then "MAGGIE: Joe's dad, God rest him. Straight." and "Aoife's, her dad on the wheel for her" (sc 9). Joe and Aoife are both Maggie's children.
*Why it matters.* Nothing turns on it, but the ferry is the scene where the audience is being asked to trust every number and name these two women say.
*Fix.* "Her grandad thinks facing home is a charm." Declan already says "Aherns face home" in ep 4.

**7. Rosie describes a weigh-wall panel at the clinic that the audience was not shown (ep 2, scene 18; ep 6, scene 5).**
*What is there.* Ep 2: "Past the toilet stands a steel door: RECORDS. STAFF ONLY. A keypad beside the handle." Ep 6: "And on the wall by the toilet, one of them steel panels. Like the one in their lock-up."
*Why it matters.* The ep 6 notes say the panel is "armed beforehand by... Rosie's casing", but the casing scene does not show it. Small, and a cross-writer seam.
*Fix.* Half a line in ep 2, scene 18: "Beside it, a plain steel panel the size of a door, with nothing on it to read." The audience has just seen the same object described at the Ahern lock-up in ep 5, so it will register.

**8. The baking-tray model implies an eight-metre tide next door, and nobody says so (ep 10, scene 9).**
*What is there.* "Peg runs water from the tap into the tray, slow, until the water stands well above the ruler's edge — and Rosie's hand stops her. ROSIE: There. That deep over our lino. Eight metres. I counted it." Then Maggie tips it "and brings it level again, and the water lies dead along the ruler's top edge. Nobody low." So at Nobody low the sea sits at our ground level everywhere, and when Rosie crossed it was eight metres up.
*Why it matters.* It is consistent (the neighbours' sea runs to their moon; the Thames itself moves six or seven metres at Tilbury), and it is why the field is a door only at low water. But a viewer who remembers "Their sea's sitting over this lino" may hear the tray contradict it.
*Fix.* One line from Maggie as she tips: "Their tide's the size of ours, near enough. Eight metre between full and dry." It also quietly explains why "the Deep keeps you" on most nights and not this one.

**9. The one clause that comes close to saying the message (ep 7, scene 12).**
*What is there.* Rosie: "And when it's been counted proper, and checked against somebody else's, I'm going first."
*Why it matters.* It is one clause, it grows out of the ferry sheet she has just seen, and it breaks no rule in the plan's list. But "checked against somebody else's" is the plan's own thesis in a character's mouth, and it is the only line in ten episodes I would call a statement of the moral rather than a picture of it. The owner cares about this more than most.
*Fix.* Make it concrete instead of general: "When Tess has counted it, and Maggie's counted it too, I'm going first." Same meaning, two names, no rule.

## 5. What I am unsure of

- **Pace.** Judged from the page only. Episode 7 (loft, ferry, Silvertown, the stove) and the night of writing in episode 10 are dense; on the page every beat earns its place, and I would not cut, but a director will feel the length.
- **The minus tickets.** As said above, the plate printing an unseen body's true mass rests on the plan's stipulation. The show uses it consistently and it pays off beautifully ("Those two are the only true numbers it's printed since April, and they're both of people nobody could see"). Whether a general audience follows why the minus is true while the plus lies is something only a screening can tell you; Tess's line in ep 9, sc 11 is as clear as it can be made without a lecture.
- **The wonders.** What Rosie saw on the shore, what her last breath smelled of, whether Terry arrived straight, what went down the drain, the Far, the second message: all are left open on purpose, and each open reading is held by something on the page. Do not let anyone "fix" them.

## 6. One next step

Fix finding 1 and finding 2 first, since both are a few lines and both concern the exact thing the season trains its audience to watch, the tenths on the wrist unit. Then the quibbles in order. After that the season is ready to go to the owner as finished.
