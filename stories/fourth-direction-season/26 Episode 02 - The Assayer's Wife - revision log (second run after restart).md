# Episode 2, "The Assayer's Wife": revision log

The draft (`80-episode-02-draft.md`) is left exactly as it was. The revised episode is `82-episode-02-final.md`.

**How I worked.** There was already an earlier revision of this episode in the folder. The finished episode 3 was written to fit it. Since then, episode 1 has been revised again (`82-episode-01-final.md`). So I didn't take the earlier revision on trust. I went back to the critique (`81-episode-02-critique.md`) and checked every finding from scratch against the master plan (`70-plan-v3.md`), against the draft, and against the latest episode 1. I agreed with nearly every decision in the earlier revision and kept them, which also means episode 3 still fits. Then I made some more small fixes of my own, marked **(new in this pass)** below.

Every line the critique asked me to protect is still there, word for word. I added no new rule and no new canonical number, and none of the plan's banned words reaches the screen (I searched for all of them).

## Other changes, and why

**New in this pass**

- **Kieran finds the Twitch ticket for a reason.** In episode 1 he sorts the night's tickets after school. Here the radio says "next-door event", his spoon stops, and he goes through the pile before school instead. That squares the two episodes, and it makes finding the ticket something he does because of his hobby, not luck. He's also now listed at breakfast, at his end of the counter, since he's in the room.
- **Dilys's Tuesday greeting.** "Your whites, Terry." / "Bless you, Dilys." It's the exchange from episode 1, and the episode 5 draft uses it too, so it now reads as a weekly routine.
- **Peg pays Dilys out of the float tin, not "the drawer".** In episode 1 the drawer is where Peg stuffs unopened official letters, and in episode 3 the float tin is upended empty. This keeps both straight.
- **Liam's drawing.** The draft said beads were "Thickest at the back, where they'd come in from the street side", which contradicts itself. Now: "Thickest at the back. That's where they'd come in, off the lane." It also matches episode 3, where the Marrable Street floor is thickest on the mews side.
- **The word "turn".** In this show "turn" means coming back mirror-turned. In the black, Rosie "turns to" the tug of home "the way you turn in bed", which could be read as her turning right there. Now she "faces it, the way you roll over in bed towards a warm back". The same goes for "Every head turns to the window" and "Then they turn to the painting" in hop four, which now "go to" and "look at". Episode 1 made the same fix.
- **The tea on the step.** The scene used to end with Rosie holding the mug to her mouth "for the warmth", as if she couldn't drink it. A turned body can drink water and tea perfectly well; it just gets nothing from the milk and sugar. So that was a false hint. Now she drinks, and leans into Tess.
- **Peg's "Don't you sign nothing."** It comes after Rosie's joke call to the clinic. The family's rules are "never go to them" and "nothing on paper", and it would be odd for Peg to watch a call to a clinic, with Terry's name given, and say nothing. One line from her lets the rule sit over the joke without stopping it, and it fits episode 3's "We don't go to them".
- **Small fixes.** At hop five Kieran now "sits on the bottom stair" (he'd just come down to weigh, so he couldn't "stay" there). "After dinner" is gone from hop seven, which is at half past four. The headings now read "MAIN TITLE: SECONDS" and "END OF EPISODE 2", like episodes 1 and 3.
- **The plants list.** I removed a claim that the printer is "the family's doorbell" leading to the 02:04 ticket. The plan's rule is that an unseen visitor is only ever found on the roll afterwards, and I didn't want this episode to promise otherwise (see the note for episode 6 below).

**Kept from the earlier revision (not asked for by the critique)**

- **Tess's reaction to "New roll's printed backwards."** In the draft her face was "completely open" for a second, which suggested she was afraid for Rosie. The plan has Tess suspect nothing until episode 3. Now she half rises and says "Show me", and when Rosie laughs "Joke", Tess says "Not funny. That roll's certified." She's worried about her legal audit roll and Trading Standards, which is exactly what Tess would fear. A viewer watching a second time can wonder whether that's all it was.
- **Matching episode 1.** Frank's photo is "a big man in a donkey jacket laughing at whoever held the camera". The surgeon in Rosie's videos wears a rig's collar over her gown, and the tumour comes back in her gloved palm. Rosie's 43.1 gets a star on the chalk board like her 43.6, which episode 3 also counts.
- **Times.** The radio scene is at 06:30 and breakfast at 06:45, before the hatch opens at seven. In the radio scene Peg turns the bacon instead of handing eggs to a driver, because no drivers are inside before seven. Hop two moved from 12:30 to 13:10, after dinner, to fit Tess's meal rule.
- **Putting the painting away.** "Put her away" comes straight after hop seven, and Rosie, still rigged, posts it into the safe. The draft's "Nobody in this family has used the key to that safe in years" is cut, because episode 1 already says it ("Nobody's had that door open since Blair").
- **A slip in the draft.** On Friday "Peg looks at the order book, and the thin envelope in the drawer", but that envelope went to Dilys on Tuesday. Now she looks at the order book and the till.
- **"It's his birthday" became "It's for his birthday"**, on the phone and at the clinic desk, so nobody wonders why there's no party on Saturday.
- **The Ahern shed.** The lad stands on a wooden board hung by chains from the steelyard, rather than sitting in a sling. The plan says he "stands", and episode 10 weighs grown adults on the same steelyard.
- **Wording.** "The weave of the linen like a woven field" is now "like a ploughed field" ("woven" twice). Peg's "There's nothing left when the black takes you. That's the point of it." now ends "You know that." The old ending didn't mean anything clear; the new one is Peg shutting the subject down.
- **One stage direction cut.** At the weigh-in the draft said "She was fourteen when her name first went at the top of that board." Tess tells us this herself on the step two acts later, so the earlier line gave it away.

**Length.** The episode is about 950 words longer than the draft, and most of that is the fuller plants list. The new beats (the false alarm, the call on the step, the City cold open) roughly balance the cuts, so screen time per act is about the same.

## Notes for other writers and for the plan's keeper

- **Episode 3.** Everything your finished episode relies on is still here: Rosie walking after the van saying its plate, the call on the step as a birthday joke, "Better. See?" after the glucose sweet, Peg's copy theory and her picture idea, and the painting kept in the safe.
- **Episode 4.** Liam hasn't started to diet; he has only looked at the bathroom scale in the dark. Slipping his fry-up to Sixpence is still yours, after Rosie's "Skip the fry-up." Your draft gives Kieran a desk; in episodes 1 and 2 he has his end of the counter, with his graph and poster on the wall above it.
- **Episode 5.** Rosie and Danny both reach the painting through the safe door, and nobody uses the key, so Peg's key in your cold open is still the first time in years. Dilys's greeting matches yours.
- **Episode 6, and the plan's keeper.** Osei says a clinician "looks in on him in transit, a second or two at a time. Nobody touches him." That's the only way the sign on her door fits the odds table, and it can pay off when Tess reads the clinic's protocol. Separately: this episode shows again that the printer chatters by itself whenever something settles on a scale, even at night. That's how the 02:04 ticket comes to exist in episode 4. In episode 6 Danny waits in the office at 02:04, so if Joe settles over the plate before Danny steps in, the printer would tell Danny live that someone is there, which breaks the plan's rule. Your stakeout should have Danny step in by the clock before any ticket prints.
- **Episode 8.** Dilys's line is "He'll want it, Peg. He always does." Ray's cover as a supplier who wants paying holds until your reveal.
- **The plan's keeper.** Peg's idea that a picture held loose can turn by itself is a family mistake, not a rule of the world. The rule that a thing always turns with its carrier is untouched, and episodes 3 and 4 correct her on screen.
