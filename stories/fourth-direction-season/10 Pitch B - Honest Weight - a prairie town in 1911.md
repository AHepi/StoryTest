# HONEST WEIGHT

*Pitch B. Ten episodes of about 55 minutes. Saskatchewan, autumn 1911 to autumn 1912, with cold opens reaching back to 1882 and a frame in 1935.*

## Logline

On a July noon in 1882 a grey stone appeared in the Carricks' wheat: no furrow, no scorch, nobody who saw it fall. Thirty years later the rust-proof wheat that grew beside it has made the family the pride of the prairie, and the district's children are wasting on full bellies. Seventeen-year-old Bel Carrick, who hands out the family's free bread every Saturday, has to get a kind, proud town to weigh the thing it loves most, beginning with her grandmother.

## Lead genre and guests

**Lead: Coming-of-Age** (the Memoir and Coming-of-Age profile), with Memoir's Storyteller Structure used lightly: Bel at forty-one, in 1935, opens episode 1 and closes episode 10.

*Why.* The culture that never questions is a family and its town: the profile's *unspoken rule*, a belief nobody inside can see, which shapes every want and grinds everyone. Here it is "Nan's mouth is never wrong." The ending settles Bel's question, and the town's with it.
- **Promise.** Strong feeling, above all the bridge: someone long cut off joined to others at last. Nan has eaten apart, in secret, for thirty years; the season joins her to her family through the truth and to strangers through a dance. The pressed question, "what was the pattern, and what did it mean?", is answered.
- **Guarantee.** The teller survived. I spend it on purpose: the frame moves suspense off Bel and onto Nan, Kirsty and Tam. The expectation I overturn early is the teller's innocence: Bel handed out the loaves and hid the photographs that showed the thinning.
- **Question.** What does she believe makes her unfree, and what act proves she has stopped believing it? Bel's "I can't" is "I haven't the mouth, and the district leans on us." Her proving act is double: she puts the family's bread on a public scale, and she tells Nan a truth a kind lie would have spared her.

**Guests.**
- **Horror**, all season. Promise: the squeeze, and the monster in us. Guarantee broken in episode 2: *an everyday need is harmless*. The need is bread, and *the child is safe* goes with it. Question: where does the human end and the inhuman begin? Here the monster is the town's own kindness.
- **Detective turning Thriller**, episodes 1-6. Expected mystery: what is killing the children? Tam is the rival investigator with a pet theory (someone is turning people) who is nearly right. From episode 4: can Bel trust the person she loves most? Every clue is on screen before its reveal.
- **Western**, visiting: the setting and Tam. The frontier closes on its own schedule; a stranger rides in with a dangerous skill, uses it for others, and cannot stay; the showdown becomes a public weighing, decided when the beam settles.
- **Love Story**, visiting: Nan and Andrew, in cold opens and one waltz.

## The world

**The normal.** Carrick, Saskatchewan, 1911: six hundred people, a siding, three elevators, the Carrick mill, a curling rink, Mr. Lee's Star Café, a Mounted Police constable, and a schoolmaster who sketches the canals of Mars. Ukrainian settlers ("Galicians") farm the poorer land north; Métis families live in the valley. Wheat is king and the town is doing well.

**Taken for granted.** Bread at every meal: bread and dripping, bread and syrup, porridge. Hens fed on wheat sweepings. Rust as a kind of weather. Charity as a civic duty. Temperance, so Nan's whisky goes unmentioned. And the Dominion Cerealist, the nation's judge of wheat, who judges it by chewing a few grains into gum: the nation's measure is a mouth too.

**Three departures, with deep ripples.**
1. **The Stone** (1882). Grey, loaf-sized, warm in January, impossible to shift. When the High Field slough is at its highest (twice a day, on a clock about forty minutes longer than the moon's) the Stone "breathes": whatever touches it, or lies within arm's reach, is taken for a few seconds and set down within a few yards, sometimes as its own reflection. Ripples: Nan came back turned; a ring of turned wheat grew round it; pencil tallies on the granary beam; Tam's boyhood thefts; a boy dead of "consumption"; a fence round "Nan's lucky stone".
2. **Carrick's Mercy**, the turned wheat. Nothing on Earth can eat it, so no rust touches it, no weevil lives in it, no loaf of it moulds, and no hen, horse or child is fed by it. It fills the belly and passes through. It breeds true and will not cross with other wheat, which the catalogue calls purity. It rises only with Nan's leaven. Ripples: the fortune; a town named after the family; the Saturday loaf; "keeping loaves" for survey crews; hens that stopped laying; the slogan "No mouse will live in Carrick flour"; a bid to make Mercy the Dominion's famine reserve; the famine.
3. **The neighbours**, felt only as weight. The slough has tides. One March night in 1909 every pendulum clock in Carrick stopped at twenty to three. The old-house kitchen clock skips some nights at nine. An earthquake shakes a prairie with no faults.

The main departure carries the theme: a bread that fills and does not feed, which is what a comfortable, admired habit becomes once its world has changed.

## The culture that never questions

The Carricks and their town: generous, hardworking, proud. Every habit that dooms them was once right.

| Habit | Why it was right |
|---|---|
| Nan chooses the seed by mouth | It kept her alive, and kept the strain pure |
| Save only the cleanest ears | Good husbandry for a thousand years |
| Never buy seed from strangers | It kept out smut and weeds |
| A free loaf for every family on Saturday | It held the district through the hungry 1890s |
| Nan's leaven for every kitchen | Generous, and Mercy flour needs it |
| Trust Nan | In 1891 she nursed the district through diphtheria and never took sick |

**How the doom grows.**
1. 1882: the Stone turns Nan. Her milk starves her baby, Tam; the Métis neighbour Marguerite Lafleur nurses him.
2. 1883, a rust year: starving, Nan finds three ears by the Stone that taste sweet, the only food that feeds her, and plants them.
3. Each harvest she picks her ears by taste. Her palate finds only turned grain, so it keeps the strain perfectly turned, and the rule of saving the cleanest ears agrees with her every year, since no blight can touch a turned ear. Andrew sees the pigs won't fatten on it and keeps it for her alone, telling no one.
4. 1902: Andrew crosses the Stone to look for "the country underneath" and her kind of bread, and never returns. The family decides he deserted. Nan, sure she was sent back to feed people, widens her acre.
5. 1904: rust blackens the district; only Nan's quarter stands. Rab founds Carrick Seed & Milling, sells Mercy across the West, and his catalogue invents its antiquity: "chosen by mouth since 1790."
6. 1905-10: the mill blends Mercy with other wheat, so bread still half-feeds. Hens lay less; nobody asks why.
7. 1911: rust again. Every wheat but Mercy fails, the district's flour is pure Mercy for the first time, it won't rise with baker's yeast, and Nan's leaven goes into every kitchen.
8. Winter: the poorest, who live on bread, waste first. The town answers with more, more lovingly: bigger loaves, a sick ward fed Mercy porridge, a quarantine on the healthy Galician farms, a bid to make Mercy the nation's reserve.

**The mirror.** The Hnatiuks keep their own rule, "seed is not for strangers", and cut before Illia's day because they always have. It saves them by luck: the rust comes later in August. Olena learns *why* in episode 9, which is what lets her change the rule when her town needs it.

## Main characters

**Isobel "Bel" Carrick, 17.** *Want:* to keep her people fed and be worthy of Nan. *Wound:* the family gift skipped her ("you'll get the mouth when you need it"). *Flaw:* she answers trouble with more of what the family has always given, and trusts Nan's certainty over her own eyes. *Secret:* since spring her Saturday photographs have shown the loaf line thinning; she hid September's because they "didn't look well for the catalogue." *Levers:* Desire, Decency, Competence (camera, bread, sums), dry Humour; the 1935 frame gives Interiority. *Arc:* from the kindest daughter of a kind culture to the one who puts what she loves on the scale, learning that proof is the easy part and that others must weigh for themselves.

**Mercy "Nan" Carrick, 60**, turned at 31. *Want:* to feed people; she believes she was taken into "God's dark palm" for a count of eleven and sent back for that. *Wound:* the baby she could not feed; the husband who "left". *Flaw:* pride in her gift, and thirty years of secrecy about a body to which the world tastes of chalk. *Secret:* she knows Andrew went into the Stone for her, and let her son hate him rather than explain. *Levers:* Competence (midwife, nurse, farmer), tart Humour, Connection, Specificity (dripping and whisky, a ledger in a hand nobody else writes, jars she can never open, "Morning" said to the Stone like a neighbour over a fence, a waltz alone at nine that stumbles on the turn). *Arc:* from certain saint to a woman who tells the truth and chooses where she can be fed.

**Tam Carrick, 29**, the baby of the field, nursed by Marguerite, milk-brother to her son Joseph. *Want:* to find who is turning people, because the wasting looks like Joseph's death; underneath, forgiveness. *Wound:* a mother who couldn't feed him; Joseph. *Flaw:* he takes, hides and leaves. *Secret:* he has crossed since fifteen (a fingertip lost when the Stone closed early); at sixteen he held Joseph's hand through it and the grip turned Joseph; at seventeen, stealing from the old granary, he followed the wrong pull, stepped out into black salt water, and kept a jar of it. *Levers:* Competence (the crossing is his gun), Humour, Vulnerability. *Arc:* the stranger who rides in, uses his skill for others, confesses, and cannot stay.

**Dr. Owen Pryce, 34**, district health officer, Welsh. *Want:* to prove the soul has weight, as a Massachusetts doctor claimed in 1907, because his wife died in childbirth. *Flaw:* he sees what he looks for; the germ theory is his faith. *Secret:* he has weighed the dying without telling every family. His sealed balance and his polarimeter (a brass tube for reading sugar in diabetic urine, which shows whether a sugar turns light right or left) are the show's honest instruments. *Arc:* from weighing the dying to weighing the living. He never finds his wife; he finds people a hair's breadth away.

**Robert "Rab" Carrick, 40**, Bel's father, mayor, head of the firm. *Want:* the Dominion contract. *Wound:* "deserted" at thirty with the debts. *Flaw:* hears doubt as betrayal; answers every threat by building more. *Secret:* he has mortgaged everything on the contract. *Arc:* from builder of the myth to the man who reads the weights aloud, burns his seed and picks up his father's pencil.

**Olena Hnatiuk, 17.** Wants the normal school in Regina. As rule-bound as Bel, in her family's way. Smuggles bread to Kirsty, works out why her family cuts early, and breaks her grandmother's rule to save a town that barred her from school.

**Supporting.** *Kirsty, 8*, Bel's sister, who sees the dog won't touch her crusts, buries them, and so eats less Mercy than anyone. *Janet*, Bel's mother. *Harry Pask*, hired man, Boer War amputee, Nan's whisky runner. *Agnes Tait*, a drayman's wife, who loses a child and is the first to weigh her own. *Marguerite Lafleur*, who tasted Nan's milk in 1882. *Major Hollis*, War Office, who watched fever kill more men than bullets did in South Africa and wants turned soldiers on Mercy rations, whom no disease can touch. *Dr. Mayne*, the Dominion Cerealist, honest, who judges wheat by chewing it.

## The ten episodes

**1. "The Saturday Loaf."** *The rust has blackened every farm but the Carricks', and the town celebrates the wheat that saved it.*
Cold open: 1935, a dust-dark kitchen at nine; a woman of forty-one watches a clock tick evenly, stops it with a finger, and weeps. Then July 1882: young Mercy sets her baby in the shade, finds a stone that was not there at breakfast, touches it and is gone. The men count to eleven. She is back, standing dry in the slough, laughing: "It's all lamps inside."
Beats: Rab's reserve bid at the harvest supper; Nan's tasting walk, Bel carrying the basket and tasting no difference. Bel photographs the Saturday line for the catalogue; Mary Tait, six, wants to be "the Mercy girl". The baker's flour "has gone dead"; Bel carries Nan's leaven to every kitchen. Nobody's hens lay but Olena's. Tam comes home broke and charming; a knotted rope of his comes back straight; Marguerite shuts her door on him; seeing the Tait children, he asks Pryce to test their sugar "for which way it turns." In her darkroom Bel hides September's prints. At nine Nan waltzes alone, stumbling on the turn; the clock skips.
Twist: as the slough rises with no rain, Tam lays his palm on the Stone and vanishes for a count of nine.
Hook: Tam, soaked to the knee, looking up at his mother's window: "Who's been using you?"

**2. "Twenty-One Grams."** *A child dies with a full belly, and the town reaches for its usual answers.*
Cold open: 1898. Tam and Joseph, sixteen, hold hands at the Stone for a dare and vanish; back, Joseph bites an apple and spits: "Chalk."
Beats: Mary Tait dies. Pryce, with Agnes's leave, weighs her at death: nothing leaves. Shaken, he falls back on what he trusts: an infectious "Carrick decline", a ward in the curling rink, and Mercy porridge, the best wheat in the West, for the sick. The Galician children are plump: carriers? The council quarantines the northern farms; Olena is barred from school; her grandmother throws Bel's gift loaves to the pigs. Tam tells Pryce what "turned" means. Pryce tests the ward: everyone turns the right way. Bel watches Tam cross and agrees to keep it.
Twist: nobody is turned, so Tam's murderer seems not to exist, until he sees Nan at the Stone at midnight, talking to it.
Hook: "Your saint goes to it every night. Ask her why."

**3. "Dead Flour."** *A broken instrument and a kitchen experiment say the same impossible thing.*
Cold open: 1883. Gaunt Mercy chews ear after ear in a black field and spits. By the Stone one ear stops her. She eats it whole. Andrew watches.
Beats: Bel watches the Stone to prove Tam wrong and sees only Nan saying good night to it. Pryce tests the ward's flour for chalk: boiled in acid, it turns light left, the reverse of every flour ever tested; a confiscated Hnatiuk loaf reads right. Bel, trying to prove the baker's yeast bad, finds baker's yeast won't raise Mercy and Nan's leaven won't raise Hnatiuk flour: "like two kinds of life." Olena's pigs, fed Bel's loaves, are thinning; Mr. Lee's children, raised on rice, are fine.
Twist: Tam: "It isn't people. Somebody turned the wheat." The wasting is the bread. There is no murderer.
Hook: Bel lays her prints, April to October, across the floor: a town thinning, each holding a loaf she handed them.

**4. "The Keeping Loaf."** MAJOR REVERSAL 1. *Bel has proof, and learns that proof is the easy part.*
Cold open: 1909. A survey crew in the snow living on Carrick keeping-loaves, thin as rails. The foreman's log: "They eat and eat. Scurvy?"
Beats: Carrick's first fair test: two litters of Olena's piglets, one on Mercy, one on Hnatiuk bread, weighed each morning on the mill scale. By day twelve the Mercy litter lags, round-bellied. Rab refuses (the medal, the mortgage). Nan eats a crust: "Sweetest bread in the West." The council trusts Nan's mouth over "a machine that says our bread is backwards," replaces Pryce with a Regina doctor who confirms infection, and doubles the ward's porridge.
Twist: the beloved bread starves whoever eats it, the charity has fed the famine, and the town, shown this, chooses more of the same. Then Tam finishes it: Mercy grew from Nan's three ears, and a turned ear tastes sweet only to a turned mouth.
Hook: through the window at nine, Nan waltzing, the clock skipping, Bel whispering "No."

**5. "Looking-Glass Milk."** *Bel investigates the person she loves most, and Tam tells what he did.*
Cold open: 1882. Marguerite takes the starving baby, touches Mercy's milk to her tongue, frowns, and nurses him herself.
Beats: Bel's quiet case: jar lids, the ledger, "lost her letters after the fever", dripping and whisky, never ill, a garden no insect touches, the dog's growl. The 1880 wedding tintype, which reverses whatever it shows, matches Bel's 1911 print exactly, mole and parting. Bel calls it a later fake; Rab shows her Andrew's thumbprint in the varnish: the original. Marguerite: "It tasted like the wrong side of a leaf." At the High Field, Pryce's balance finds a steady weight of about twelve stone where nobody stands, and at nine a second weight moving in threes. He writes "presences." Janet sees Kirsty filling out on Olena's bread and takes Bel's side.
Twist: Tam confesses. The grip turned Joseph. Tam took him through eleven more times, Joseph tasting bannock after each; he never came right, and starved in six months "of consumption."
Hook: Nan cuts her hand on the bread knife and, for the first time in thirty years, lets Pryce stitch it. He pockets the cloth.

**6. "The Mouth."** MAJOR REVERSAL 2, part one. *The blood says what the family won't, and Bel asks Nan to her face.*
Cold open: 1891, diphtheria. Mercy carries a dead child out, singing, untouched. The district starts calling her a saint.
Beats: Nan's blood turns light the opposite way to anyone's. Bel sits on it a day, the family habit, then confronts Nan. Nan denies, then breaks: since the field the world has tasted of chalk; in the dark palm everything shone from inside, her baby's heart like a lamp, and she was sent back to feed people. And Andrew did not desert: he went into the Stone to bring her bread, and she let Rab hate him ten years. Bel tells the family; Rab rages; Janet stands with Bel; Tam, for once, is gentle with his mother. Agnes Tait, on her own, weighs her children on the elevator scale and brings Bel the numbers.
Twist: the sacred mouth is a turned mouth that has picked its only food for thirty years and fed it to everyone; the deserter was a man looking for his wife's bread.
Hook: at dawn the prairie shakes. The slough drains and fills.

**7. "No Fault."** MAJOR REVERSAL 2, part two. *Something underneath has moved a mountain, and the family decides it is Andrew.*
Cold open: 1902. Andrew at the Stone with lantern and watch, tide times pencilled on the beam. He lays his hand on it. The count passes eleven, twenty, sixty. The lantern burns on in the empty field.
Beats: chimneys down; the schoolmaster, eyes on Mars for years, insists the prairie has no faults. Pryce's balance finds a huge new weight under the High Field. Tam produces his jar: black salt water under a moon not ours. It turns light the same way as Nan's blood. Tam: "Nobody raises an island for a joke. There's a floor down there now." At nine the clock skips in three-four; Nan: "That's Andrew's waltz." The truth leaks; a few families switch bread; in May the farmers sow Mercy again, because the rust frightens them more. Major Hollis arrives ahead of the Cerealist and asks about "the woman who never takes sick."
Twist: the world a hair's breadth under Carrick is inhabited, someone has built a place to stand, and the weight dances Andrew's tune.
Hook: Nan packing a basket: "He's calling me."

**8. "Six Gloves in Twenty."** *To send Nan across the family must learn the odds, and the man who already knew them was the "deserter".*
Cold open: 1900, South Africa. Captain Hollis in a fever tent; Sergeant Pask's leg.
Beats: Bel sends forty prints through at the tide; twelve come back reversed. On the granary beam, in "Grandad's counting", she finds a drawn glove, two columns, "6 in 20": the same odds, ten years apart. Rab stands a long time before his father's hand. Nan gets about ten seconds; Tam reached the sea in seven. Pask volunteers to scout, his missing leg buying him more than a second, and trains blindfold in the stock tank ("Your ear will swear you're falling. Follow the cold pull"). Tam tests the tide with his arm alone. Hollis asks his old sergeant to help him take the Stone and the seed. Rab locks Nan's foundation seed in the strongroom.
Twist: Pask comes back holding his breath and lets it out in the kitchen: salt, and cut grass, but wrong. Wet new rock; a moon not ours; far off, lanterns, waiting.
Hook: at dawn the foundation seed is gone from the locked strongroom. No door was touched.

**9. "The Still Weight."** *The town turns on its saint, and a sum turns Nan's hope inside out.*
Cold open: 1899. Tam, seventeen, bursts soaked from the Stone clutching a jar. Only Mercy sees. She wraps him in her shawl and says nothing.
Beats: the torn seed-bag tags turn right: the thief slid through unturned, someone practised. Tam, handcuffed: "Take me to the Stone at the tide and see what these are worth." The town, knowing now, finds a new way to do the old thing: it blames Nan. The leaven is her kind of life, so the council confines her to the old house, food by permit, the key with the constable, while every kitchen keeps its leaven because nothing else raises the flour. Olena brings her grandmother's seed and the reason they cut early. Bel plans a weighing at the fair. Pryce maps both weights for the crossing: the still one is 79.8 kilograms and spreading at the same slow rate for years. Rab says what Andrew weighed: "Twelve stone eight."
Twist: Andrew is the still weight, spread thin under the High Field since 1902. Whoever dances underneath, it isn't him.
Hook: Bel at the old-house door, with a truth Nan is packing to go without.

**10. "Honest Weight."** MAJOR REVERSAL 3. *The town weighs its bread in public, and Bel tells Nan the truth.*
Cold open: 1935 again, the even clock.
Beats: (1) The fair. The Cerealist chews Mercy: superb. Bel's answer is the elevator scale: the two litters, one fat, one down to two round-bellied survivors; then Agnes lifts her children onto the platform; then other mothers, unasked. Rab reads the weights aloud. The Cerealist withdraws Mercy; the farmers vote to burn it and sow Hnatiuk seed, cut early; Rab drops the charge against Tam. (2) Bel tells Nan. Grief. Then Pryce's records: the dancing weight follows Nan's own waltz, a day late, down to the stumble on the turn from the knee she broke on the ice in '84. Someone underneath has felt her dance for thirty years and learned it. (3) To stay, Nan would need Mercy kept alive and her leaven in every kitchen; going, she can take both. Tam hid the seed in her flour bin. Every twenty pounds costs a second; she straps on all forty and has eight. Hollis comes for the Stone; Pask blocks him. The constable brings her key; Nan: "It was never the door." (4) The 4:12 tide. Tam counts, and will not hold her hand. She dances one turn with Rab, touches the Stone, and at eight is gone. On the balance her weight arrives underneath, stands a long moment, then moves slowly away until the beam rests. (5) Households carry their leaven to the fire, some slowly. Tam tells Marguerite about Joseph; she tells him to go. The first Saturday weighing. Tag, 1935: a man from Ottawa brings a drought-proof seed, and the women try it on the hens and the scale before anyone sows it; a child asks why they weigh everything, and Olena, now the teacher, tells her to find out. That night the clock, even since spring, skips at nine: one-two-three, a stumble on the turn. Someone underneath is dancing Nan's waltz. Bel stands and dances back.
Twist: the call was never Andrew's. Strangers with only her weight to go on learned her dance and answered it; the High Field's ghost was two things all along, a lost husband and a listening neighbour.

## Mysteries and wonders

**Mysteries, pressed and answered:** what is killing the children (eps 3-4); what turned the wheat, and why Nan can eat it (eps 4-6); Joseph (ep 5); where Andrew went (eps 7-9); who took the seed (ep 9); who dances underneath (ep 10); whether Nan reaches the floor and lives (ep 10 and the tag); why a woman wept at an even clock (ep 10).

**Wonders, mentioned lightly and never chased:** what the Stone is and why it came; who the neighbours are, and why they would raise land for one old woman; the night the clocks stopped; what Nan saw in the dark palm; the lanterns. Nobody's goal is to solve them.

## The ending

**Settled.** The famine's cause is named and stopped by the town's own weighing, not Bel's say-so. Mercy is burned, the leaven poured out, the foundation seed carried to the one place where it is bread. Andrew is found. Nan tells the truth and lives, as twenty-three years of dancing prove. The Stone stays, fenced, held in common by the farmers; Hollis leaves with nothing.

**Cost.** Mary Tait and thirteen other children. Nan, gone for good. Andrew, dead. The Carrick fortune and name. Pryce's hope of weighing his wife. Tam, sent away by the woman who nursed him.

**The theme, earned.** Every habit that killed was a virtue, and the world kept moving: rust, the Stone's grain, the drought of the Thirties. A town that could not change its mind would only have waited for the next blow. By 1935 Carrick weighs a new seed before sowing it.

## The show's ledger of new guesses

*Established:* sugars turn light right or left, a test doctors of 1911 already used; plants build themselves from air, water, light and minerals, none of which has a hand, so turned wheat grows well; wheat was judged by chewing; a doctor weighed the dying in 1907.

*Taken as The Fourth Direction stipulates:* leaves carry worlds; a field unpins for seconds, longer for less mass; gravity between leaves is felt at human scale; for the ending, the neighbours' life is the other hand.

*The show's own guesses, declared once, never added to:*
1. The Stone breathes only at the neighbours' high tide, and sets things down within a few yards.
2. A grown person gets about ten seconds.
3. Its drift is about three in ten.
4. Under Carrick the neighbours' leaf is sea, and they can raise land in it.

*Derived, and shown before it pays:* turned seed breeds true and won't cross; nothing here eats it, so it resists rust, weevil and mould and feeds nobody; Nan's leaven is her own turned yeast; the dog smells her; a slide leaves paper unturned.

## Check against the owner's six rules

1. **Not The Catch.** No lift, shaft or ledge; no husband-and-brother trio; no figure or ship (the neighbours never appear and take no one); no toy, drawing, needle kicking at a vanishing, wristband, dishes, flask, oxygen or reserve bar; no "you changed, not the world" reveal, and the camera never sees through turned eyes; no ending on unshareable food or hands on glass. The balance measures slow weight and pays off with a still man and a dance. Nor Caraway: no walled city, rite, twins, taste-test lie, scripture or hauled stone.
2. **Religion, fresh.** Nan's private faith grew from a real experience inside the Stone; Pryce hopes to weigh a soul. No scripture, priests, heretics, rites or liturgy.
3. **No second attachment.** No book of ideas, maxims, creeds or Greek letters. The only documents are a seed ledger and pencil tallies.
4. **Far more Fourth Direction.** Over forty items, each with a job.
5. **Ideas never named.** Nobody talks about asking; people weigh pigs, children and seed.
6. **Not a dystopia.** A friendly boomtown at peace. The doom grows from good husbandry, charity, loyalty and love.
