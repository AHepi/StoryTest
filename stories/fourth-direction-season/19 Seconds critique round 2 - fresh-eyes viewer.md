# Critique, round 2: the fresh-eyes viewer

**Lens.** An ordinary viewer watching SECONDS (plan v2, `50-plan-v2.md`) one episode at a time, who knows only what is on screen. I also checked the owner's seven rules on every page. Findings 2, 3 and 8 are near-breaches of rule 1 (The Catch). I found no religion, no dystopia, no banned word on screen and no trace of the theory of explanation. With every law now in the show, the risk to rule 7 (a patchwork) is real but small (finding 16).

**Does episode 1's first ten minutes hook?** Yes. The cold open works: a count in the dark, a guard's ribs, a sealed crate left sealed, beads roaring down, something heavy passing at 3:12, "Straight?" "Straight." and one look too long at a crisp packet. The trouble starts after it (finding 14).

**What lands as "of course!", and must be protected.** The painting's window. LO read as "she's hypo". Con's call ("Your lot took my dinner, Tel"). Liam's 7.2 against 7.1 while Peg piles his plate. Two meters, two answers, both right. Frank's tickets making Rosie's curve. Ray's mirror signature. None of the fixes below touch these.

**What lands as "wait, what?", or lands too early.** The first reversal (ep 3) and the second reversal's second half (ep 7) come after the viewer already has the answer. Ep 9's food twist is read out on TV in ep 8. Ep 10 finds the door through talk about a place we can't see.

---

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The first major reversal is spent by the end of episode 2.
- **Where:** ep 2, acts three and four and the hook; ep 3's twist; section 10 ("sharp viewers suspect from 1").
- **Problem:** in one episode the viewer gets all of this:
  - Rosie ticks HAND: C while the clerk warns about the strip test at the gate.
  - A Lisbon clinic on her phone offers Restoration at half price, and she closes it.
  - The flu leaves only her and Terry, the one person we know is turned, on their feet, and Terry gives her a look.
  - She walks into the cure clinic.
  - She looks at the jeweller's across the road.
  - Ray says "Tell your uncle."

  Anyone who noticed the painting's window has added this up before ep 2's credits. In ep 3 the LO reading confirms what the viewer knows instead of turning it over, and three acts of Tess's tests play as the family catching up.
- **Why it matters:** the brief asks for reversals that make the audience re-read everything. A reversal that only the characters experience is suspense, not a turn. This is the first of the three, and it teaches the viewer whether to trust the show's twists.
- **Fix:**
  - Cut the Lisbon phone beat.
  - Move the passport beat to ep 4, after the family knows. Barred from the Ahern job, Rosie tries to get to Lisbon for the half-price cure, and the Post Office clerk tells her about the strip at the gate. The passport now does sharper work: it closes her last cheap way out, so the family needs £180,000 here, which is why Liam's lock-up job in ep 5 is "cash for Rosie's cure".
  - Give Harley Street a cover the viewer has seen. In ep 1 Terry, at the stove, looks at an Obverse ad on the paper and says "One day." Rosie says she'll price it for his birthday. At the clinic desk we believe her, and ep 3 re-reads the visit.
  - Rosie fakes the flu: a hammed-up cough, a day in bed "like everyone", then she nurses Kieran "since she's over the worst". Terry's look is at the fake cough. The viewer sees a cold, and ep 3 re-reads it as cover. This fits her voice ("When she is lying she gets funnier").
  - Pay off the look at the jeweller's. In ep 3, act one, Rosie pitches Marrable Street, not Peg. She becomes active, and her secret drives the longest job.

### 2. Two beats reuse The Catch's gravity needle.
- **Where:** ep 2's cold open (Kieran watches the test weight read 20.00, then 20.31, then 20.00); ep 6's cold open (Danny watches the little bridge's readout "jump to 58.4 with nobody on the plate"). Section 14 already says "No instrument showing an unseen visitor live in an empty room."
- **Problem:** The Catch has a needle that kicks when an object vanishes, and later climbs in Iona's empty room as an unseen crosser arrives. Ep 2 is a live gravity reading that kicks. Ep 6 is a live gravity reading that climbs as an unseen crosser arrives in a room at night. Danny sitting in the room doesn't change the picture. The plan breaks its own rule and the brief's list ("a gravity needle that kicks").
- **Why it matters:** rule 1. The owner knows The Catch scene by scene, and the ep 6 cold open is the image he will recognise first.
- **Fix:**
  - **Ep 2:** show the Twitch through the beads roaring down in a hundred City basements at once, and through the office radio. Kieran finds "04:17, 20.31" on the roll the next morning among his nightly tickets, the same way he finds the 02:04 ticket in ep 4. It does the same job (his log's first entry that matters) and sets a rule the season keeps: the roll catches what nobody saw.
  - **Ep 6:** no readout. Danny asks Kieran when the Nobody Tide was low on the night of the 02:04 ticket. The answer is 02:04 to the minute, because the Aherns are watermen and time everything to it. Kieran's chart gives the next night-time low water. Danny stands rigged by the safe in the dark and steps into the black on the minute, blind, on a guess. If Joe isn't there, he has wasted a crossing. That adds suspense rather than losing it, and it plants the Ahern tide habit that the ferry envelope (7) and the door (10) depend on.

### 3. The season's moral spine now echoes The Catch's.
- **Where:**
  - Tess's flaw ("she decides things for other people") and her arc ("asks each person instead of deciding").
  - Ep 8: Rosie's "You decided. Again." and Tess to Ray, "You fed us so you'd never have to tell us anything."
  - Ray's flaw ("thinks feeding people is the whole of caring for them"), and Dr Osei's kind secret.
  - Ep 10, act two: Tess "asks each of them instead of deciding".
- **Problem:** the owner's notes name The Catch's one question: may you decide someone's fate without telling them? Eli hides his hand, the figure takes people and then tends them, and Iona asks before she turns. SECONDS now has a kind secret feeder who tends people (Ray), a heroine who decides for others, and a finale that turns on asking first, with two lines that come close to saying it outright. Each piece is small. Together they are the same moral frame.
- **Why it matters:** rule 1 covers premise and ending, and the owner already rejected one attempt as a rip. It also pulls the theme off SECONDS's own ground (a family that keeps nothing on paper and checks nothing against anyone else's) and onto consent.
- **Fix:** point every one of these at paper and checking. The plan already carries that theme.
  - Tess's flaw: she trusts the numbers in her head and never checks them against anything ("Near enough isn't", yet she never writes it down). Keep her arc: head, then paper, then the pencil handed to Kieran.
  - Rosie's ep 8 line: "Nine point four, Tess." Her Mayfair number, thrown back.
  - Tess to Ray: "Twenty-four years of our weights in your hand, and you never showed a soul." This matches his existing trait ("Writes everything down, and shows no one").
  - Ep 10: Tess writes each person's odds on the roll, where they can read them. What changes is that the number is on paper, not that she asks. Drop "instead of deciding" from the plan.

### 4. Episode 7's twist is one the viewer already has.
- **Where:** ep 6, act four and twist; ep 7's twist ("Nobody spun anybody"); section 9, row 8.
- **Problem:** in ep 6 Tess runs the whole season through the Obverse table, and the show tells us Rosie turned because of her 9.4 seconds. The "spinner" is dead for the viewer an episode early. Ep 7 then spends most of its hour on two women working out ep 6's answer again on the back of a timetable. Only the family is surprised.
- **Why it matters:** "second reversal, part two" has no reversal for the audience. Ep 7 risks being the dullest hour, and it comes straight after the best one.
- **Fix:** give the ferry something only the Ahern side knows, and have it re-read the Marlow Mix thread. The plan says Aoife turned on a nine-second job "in a bank where a Marlow crew was working the floor below." Make that the reveal: the crew was Danny's day crew pouring Marlow Mix, and the floor that made her job nine seconds long was theirs. Maggie: "Why was it nine seconds? Your floor." Nobody spun Aoife. The Marlows' concrete turned her, legally, by day. This re-reads Danny's "I know what slows a crosser down" (2), the tripled order book and the Marrable Street floor. It makes Danny's "And Liam? Did your sums do Liam too?" in act four cut both ways, and Con's death becomes part of a war about concrete, not "a war about nothing". It adds no new rule.

### 5. Episode 10 is overloaded, peaks early, and teaches by lecture.
- **Where:** ep 10, all acts, and the epilogue.
- **Problem:**
  - One hour holds Frank's reveal, a night of writing, two crossings, the investigators, the rolls and the arrests.
  - In the night of writing, odds are read aloud for five people: three sets for Terry, then Ray's, Shaun's, Rosie's and Danny's.
  - The door is found by talk about a geography nobody can see: a sea eight metres above our ground, and an island whose shore meets our ground at their low water.
  - The emotional peak, Terry stepping off the world and Ray's mirror signature, comes in act three. Acts four and five are procedure, and the viewer can guess the outcome ("give").
- **Why it matters:** the three biggest payoffs (Frank, Terry, the rolls) blur together. Fractions spoken at speed don't land, and a door found by describing geography plays as "wait, what?".
- **Fix:**
  - **Move the investigators to the end of act one.** The night of writing then becomes a night of confessing: every line in pencil is evidence they will ask for in the morning. The odds become stakes instead of a recital, and act five's choice becomes the pay-off of a night-long risk.
  - **Say only Terry's odds aloud, and show them.** He takes a die from Kieran's board game: "Six, I stay hungry. Anything else, I get my tea." That is the same dice with the stakes reversed, understood at a glance. Keep Shaun's "I've given it a leg" and Rosie's silence. Ray's number goes on the roll without being read out.
  - **Show the door on the kitchen table.** Peg's baking tray, a heap of flour for the Hill, water from the tap up to Kieran's tide mark. Maggie drains it down to Nobody low water, and the wet edge stops at the foot of the heap. Peg puts the salt pot there: "Our field." One image replaces a page of geography, and it stays in the kitchen.
  - Cut Rosie's "the Hill an island over Mucking". It tells us what she saw next door, which the plan keeps as a wonder. Let the tray show it instead.

### 6. Frank "not knowing" breaks a rule the viewer learned in episode 1's cold open.
- **Where:** ep 10's twist ("nobody knew, not even Frank"); section 6, step 1; the derived rule that the turned see the world reversed afterwards; ep 1's cold open (the crisp packet).
- **Problem:** the first scene of the show teaches that a turned crosser sees print reversed at once: Rosie knows from a crisp packet. Frank was a working crosser in 2001. Forensic reports on turning hang framed on the wall, and the Ministry was offering screening that year. Yet he grumbles that the papers are "printed funny" and never works it out. At the season's last twist the viewer thinks, "He must have known."
- **Why it matters:** the final reversal has to feel inevitable. A hole here makes the founder's death look like the writers' convenience.
- **Fix:** Frank knew and hid it, as Rosie did. "Printed funny" was his joke, the way "the roll's printed backwards" was hers. Peg is the one who didn't know, and she is the one who buried the ticket. The re-read then runs the right way: Rosie didn't invent the hiding, she inherited it from the man the family's pride is built on. The blame stays on the habit (a turn means you failed to face home, so you hide it), not on Frank's choice. If the plan keeps "nobody knew", it needs an on-screen reason why the print didn't tell him, and there isn't a good one.

### 7. The rescue arrives by coincidence, and its meaning is read out on TV.
- **Where:** ep 8's hook; ep 9's twist; ledger 4.7.
- **Problem:**
  - A message nobody has mentioned lands on the ward TV in the same week the family's food runs out, already decoded: "The Register's people say it may mean the neighbours' life is the other hand," with "COME ROUND FOR TEA" on the front pages. The viewer feels luck, and ep 9's "next door is food" becomes a confirmation.
  - "The tremors since April" will make every viewer link the message to the 4 April quake, which the plan keeps as a wonder.
- **Why it matters:** the brief wants the doom to come from habits, not luck, and a rescue timed by the neighbours is luck. A reversal read out an episode early is no reversal.
- **Fix:**
  - **Build the tremors in the background from ep 5:** a BBC line about "another next-door event", a small regular wobble in Kieran's nightly numbers, a scientist on the car radio in ep 7 saying there's a pattern. The world gets on with its own business, and ep 8's decoding lands as the end of something the viewer watched grow.
  - **Cut the Register's line.** Let the TV say only "a sugar, of the other hand" and keep "COME ROUND FOR TEA" as a tabloid joke. The viewer makes the leap with Rosie as she looks at the T-meter the nurse gave her.
  - **Decide on the April link.** Either the scientist says "It started on the fourth of April": then the neighbours' invitation is what made the scale lie and killed Liam, a big re-read with no new rule, and "why they moved the Hill" moves from wonder to answer. Or the tremors start in May and the wonder stays. As written, viewers will make the link and the show will ignore it.

### 8. The spare looks like The Catch's reserve.
- **Where:** guess 5; ep 5, act four ("Tess checks the spare light on his rig", "It's for if you step out wrong"); the climaxes of eps 9 and 10.
- **Problem:** The Catch has a reserve bar on a wrist display, a line about a reserve best left untouched, and a climax that turns on it. SECONDS has an indicator light for a one-shot reserve, a line about when it may be used, and a climax (Rosie in the Deep) that turns on firing it. The mechanism is different, but what the viewer sees is the same.
- **Why it matters:** rule 1, a device echoed at a climax.
- **Fix:** keep the rule and change the object. Make it a separate thing with its own look and no display: a brass disc on the collar, required by law since 2016, fired by biting a tab. Crossers call it "the bite". Tess's check in ep 5 is a hard tug on the tab, like a diver checking a partner's belt. Never call it a reserve, never mention charge, never show a gauge. In the Deep, Rosie bites it. That works underwater, and it's a stronger image than a button.

### 9. Three rules the early twists depend on are never shown before they matter.
- **Where:** eps 1-2; ep 3, act five; ep 6's twist.
- **Problem:**
  - (a) **A turned body starves on ordinary food.** Terry eats from tubs and says "Fat and spirits. It's all that sticks," but nothing says or shows that the fry-up would starve him. His gloves are never explained either.
  - (b) **A turned body catches nothing.** So the ep 2 flu is a clue only to viewers who guess the rule.
  - (c) **Licensed surgeons hardly ever turn.** This is the plant ep 6's reversal needs most, and on screen we only get "Three seconds. Soft."
- **Why it matters:** the dread of eps 1-3 needs (a). Without it, cheering a thin girl looks unhealthy, not lethal. Without (c), ep 6's "of course!" shrinks to "oh, OK."
- **Fix:** one shown moment each.
  - (a) At the birthday Terry is the only one with no cake. Kieran pushes a slice at him. "Goes through me like water, love. Since I turned." Peg's knife stops at the word. This also plants Terry's wound, that Peg never says it.
  - (b) In the flu act, Terry, carrying trays: "Not had a cold since '21." Together with Rosie's fake cough (finding 1), it stays a fair clue without giving her away.
  - (c) Ep 1, act three, over the TV surgeon. Danny: "Three seconds. Soft." Rosie: "She's done four thousand. Never turned." Danny: "'Cause she faces home." Three lines give the family's false theory and the true clue.

### 10. Episode 4's first act is a lecture.
- **Where:** ep 4, acts one and two.
- **Problem:** in one scene Tess explains that cargo turns with its carrier, tells an anecdote about an army and a seed bank, and states the price of the grip. She says all this to Danny, a veteran crosser who would know all three. Act two adds "His hand's tied to him. It knows which way's up. It won't turn." That is four rules told in ten minutes.
- **Why it matters:** this episode must make the viewer fear Danny's grab in ep 6, and rules that are told don't frighten.
- **Fix:**
  - "Then you'd be eating it" is enough if Tess nods at *The Assayer's Wife* on the wall, because the painting has already proved the rule. Cut the seed-bank anecdote: Danny's plan already is the seed-vault example, so the anecdote only repeats it.
  - Give the grip to Rosie, barred from the job and listening in. She saw the Obverse receptionist's hand in ep 2: "Girl at the clinic. Her hand's plastic. That's what a grab costs." The rule is shown through something we saw, Rosie gets a line from her secret life, and she becomes party to Danny's plan.
  - Cut the "It won't turn" line. Nothing depends on it.
  - Ep 6: Danny grabs Joe "by the collar", but guess 6 says only flesh holding flesh triggers the grip. Make it Joe's wrist.

### 11. Two characters defined by checking miss the checks the plot needs them to miss.
- **Where:** ep 7, act two (Maggie: "He's fifty-nine, my Joe." "Neither of them stops on it."); eps 4-8, where Kieran logs 19.78 every night for two months, including after Liam dies.
- **Problem:** Tess's catchphrase is "Near enough isn't" and Maggie is a weigher, yet they hear 59 against 58.4 and move on. Kieran's hobby is the test weight. Its reading stays 220 grams light for two months and he never raises it again after "Stop wasting roll." From ep 5 the viewer is ahead of the lying scale and will shout at the screen, and not in the good way: it plays as the writers needing them to be dim.
- **Why it matters:** the culture's blindness has to come from its habits, not from characters acting against type. The viewer forgives misses only when each one has a reason.
- **Fix:** give each miss a believable wrong answer from inside the culture.
  - Maggie: "Fifty-nine in his boots. He's been off his food since his sister." Tess is satisfied and moves on. It's fair, and it re-reads in ep 9.
  - Peg to Kieran in ep 4: "You dropped that on the step at Christmas. It's chipped." He finds a chip and believes her. In ep 9 the brass balance reads 20.00, and the chip turns out to weigh nothing.

### 12. Liam's sum is wrong by the plan's own numbers, and too much for dialogue.
- **Where:** ep 9, act five; section 14 (Liam).
- **Problem:** the plan says "The fry-up alone would have left him two hundredths of a second. It took both." But the scale error alone would also have killed him. His true 63.1 kilos plus 1.5 of notes is 64.6, which buys 7.08 seconds for a 7.1-second job. The fry-up wasn't needed, so the plan's claim that the viewer's fry-up theory is "only half the answer" is false. And "two hundredths" is arithmetic nobody can follow by ear.
- **Why it matters:** the show promises sums a viewer could do, and a sharp viewer will do this one.
- **Fix:** with a margin of one tenth, "it took both" can't be true, because the scale's error (about 0.14 seconds) is bigger than a tenth on its own. Choose one:
  - Keep "a tenth to spare" (it rhymes with Frank) and change the line to "The scale did it. The fry-up made sure." The drawer then carries the blame, more than Peg's plate.
  - Or keep "it took both" and give him two tenths. For example: true weight 62.8, little bridge 62.1. Tess's sum: 62.1 + 1.5 = 63.6, 7.3 seconds for a 7.1-second job. Scale error alone: 64.3 kilos, 7.14 seconds. Fry-up alone: 64.1 kilos, 7.18 seconds. Both: 64.8 kilos, 7.04 seconds, and he runs out. The printout reads 64.8.

  On screen, show one sum on the back of the letter and say only: "Either one, he makes it. Both, he doesn't." (or the other line).

### 13. Maggie arrives in episode 4, and the war has cost the Marlows nothing on screen before it is revealed.
- **Where:** ep 4, act three and twist ("They have been at war for months, and the Marlows never knew"); section 7 (Maggie is a principal); section 14 ("Ray and Maggie get scenes of their own").
- **Problem:** a principal, set up as Tess's mirror image, first appears 40 per cent of the way into the season. She arrives in the same act as Joe, Declan, Con's face, Siobhan, Aoife's photo and Cian. And "at war for months" gives the viewer nothing to re-read: no Marlow setback in eps 1-3 turns out to have been the Aherns.
- **Why it matters:** care is built over time, and a mirror only works if we have looked into it. A twist that re-reads nothing is not a twist.
- **Fix:**
  - In ep 1 or 2, add a thirty-second scene the viewer can't place yet. Across the river a woman weighs a skinny lad on a salvage steelyard while a family cheers, and she doesn't. It plants the steelyard, the mirror weigh-in and Joe's weight, and ep 4 reveals who they are.
  - Make Ray's ep 3 stakeout of the basement next door come from an anonymous tip, which ep 4 shows was Maggie's. The war was already making Marlow jobs longer (it sent Rosie the long way round, ten seconds), and neither side could see it.

### 14. After the cold open, episode 1 has no want until act five.
- **Where:** ep 1, acts one to four.
- **Problem:** after the cold open come four acts of household life: rules as banter, the painting on the table, the pit, Pinnock, the surgeon on TV, the birthday. The buy-back that gives the painting its stakes only turns up in act five. A viewer who misses the flipped window has nothing to lean toward for thirty minutes.
- **Why it matters:** a pilot is judged in its first twenty minutes, and hanging out with the family after a heist lets the air out.
- **Fix:** put the sale on the table in act one, with a date and a need. The insurer's man comes Saturday, and the buy-back pays the Laundry for Terry's year (£300 a day, said once, over his tub). The flipped window in act two becomes dramatic irony with a clock, every family scene sits under it, and the cost of "we feed our own" is planted where it will bite.

### 15. The show never says why nobody else has gone next door.
- **Where:** ep 9 (Rosie follows the Deep); section 3.2 (the 2012 Convention, never named on screen); section 6 ("face home" made it unthinkable).
- **Problem:** there have been ten years of spares, forty thousand turned Britons, and crossing surgeons and rescue crews all over the world. Has not one of them stepped out next door on purpose and come back? The only reason is a Convention the plan keeps off screen. Halfway through ep 9 the viewer asks, "Nobody ever tried?"
- **Why it matters:** the ending says a rule made the cure unthinkable for the Marlows. If the viewer doesn't know why it was unthinkable for everyone else, the discovery feels too easy.
- **Fix:** add one line where the spare is taught in ep 5. Liam: "Can I use it if I'm short?" Tess: "It's for if you step out wrong. Step out on purpose and it's twenty years." Now the lawful world has a reason, and the thieves who break every law turn out to obey only their own rule. Peg's "Get rid of it before they come" (9) and the epilogue's charge ("release of other-hand matter") make sense at once.

### 16. Some checklist beats read as footnotes (a rule 7 risk).
- **Where:**
  - Ep 5, act one: Ray's 2019 Southend coroner's report.
  - Ep 8's hook: a scientist says "Sixty years we pointed dishes at the sky."
  - Ep 2: two posters, dark matter and SETI.
  - Ep 4, act two: "It won't turn."
  - The word "hand", doing five jobs: HAND: T, the Hand Company, a sent hand, the grip's hand, "Linen has a hand".
- **Problem:** with every law in play, a few beats exist only to tick a ledger row and touch nothing any Marlow wants. The viewer feels the show stop to show its homework. And "hand" said in the same breath as "the Hand Company" and "leaves the hand in there" muddles a word the season needs to keep clear.
- **Why it matters:** rule 7. SECONDS must feel like one crime family's story, not a tour of a rulebook.
- **Fix:**
  - Ray's report becomes one line at Peg's safe: "Last one I saw like that was in a man's head. Coroner wrote 'delivered'."
  - Cut the TV scientist's SETI line. Kieran's poster already carries it, and in ep 8 he can tap it.
  - Keep one poster and put the other in the background.
  - Rename the Hand Company (for example, the Fever Company; keep the poster "IMMUNE. FED. NEEDED."). Then "hand" means handedness on paper and a body's hand everywhere else. "Linen has a hand" is the season's best line and should have the word to itself.
