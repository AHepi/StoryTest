# Critique of plan v2, round 2: laws and logic, second pass

**Lens.** I re-ran the full checklist from `05-owner-feedback-2.md` against `50-plan-v2.md`. I looked for contradictions introduced by the round 1 fixes (`51-revision-log-r1.md`), checked every number, date, name and rule wherever it appears, checked the knowledge map and twist table against the episode beats, and settled each "Still unresolved" item (finding 18).

**The owner's rules, in short.** Religion: none (passes). The theory of explanation: no trace (passes). Banned words: they appear only in the writers' ban list (passes). Dystopia: none (passes). **Breaches:** rule 1, nothing from The Catch (finding 1); rule 7, SECONDS and not a patchwork (finding 2); rule 4, every checklist item true and doing plot work (findings 5 and 13, and in part 4 and 8).

Findings, most serious first.

**1. Three props still echo The Catch, and one of them breaks the plan's own ban on taste tests.**
- *Where:* ledger 4.11, row 18 ("a spoon of Peg's marmalade after each"); episode 6, cold open ("At 02:04 the readout jumps to 58.4 with nobody on the plate"); episode 5, act four ("Tess checks the spare light on his rig"); section 14, the Catch ban list.
- *Problem:*
  - (a) Terry tastes marmalade after each of his eleven hops. That is the source's "tasting mint after each" with the fruit swapped: a taste test that tells a crosser whether he is still turned. The scent molecule in orange peel has a hand too, so this is the carvone trick itself, which means The Catch's mint and Caraway's mint test. Section 14 forbids "any taste or smell test that settles anything".
  - (b) Episode 6 still shows an instrument jumping, live, for a visitor nobody can see. Section 14 bans exactly this ("No instrument showing an unseen visitor live in an empty room"). The room only avoids being empty because Danny is sitting in it. This is unresolved item 4.
  - (c) A "spare light" is a reserve indicator on the rig. It is a close cousin of The Catch's charge reserve bar, which section 14 also bans.
- *Why it matters:* any echo of The Catch breaks the owner's first rule. A ledger row is an instruction to writers, so the taste test will reach the screen unless it is removed here.
- *Fix:*
  - (a) Terry: "Eleven times I went again. Hungry every time after." He learns he is still turned from days of hunger, not from a taste.
  - (b) Take the readout out of episode 6. Danny sits in the dark watching the wall clock ("Aherns work to the minute") and steps in at 02:04 without knowing whether anyone is there.
  - (c) Tess checks the spare by feel, a latch that clicks, with no light.
  - Optional: episode 2's cold open, with Kieran watching the digits jump at the Twitch, is the source's own event and can stay. It is safer, though, to lead with the City's walls flooding and have Kieran find the three readings on the printout.

**2. The finale borrows more from pitch B than section 15 admits, and one of its listed "differences" is false.**
- *Where:* episode 9, act three (the jar of sea; the weed on two meters); episode 10, acts two and three (the door at the object's field, timed by the neighbours' tide; the count; the hand); section 15, "Taken from pitches B and C".
- *Problem:*
  - In pitch B's episode 7, a thief produces "his jar: black salt water under a moon not ours", and an instrument shows it matches the turned elder's blood. B's ending has the turned elder leave at a stated tide time, through the fenced site where the object arrived, while "Tam counts, and will not hold her hand".
  - Version 2 now has all of it: a jar of the neighbours' sea whose weed is tested on two meters and matches the turned (episode 9), and Terry leaving at Nobody low water through the fenced field where the object arrived, with Danny counting and nobody allowed to hold his hand (episode 10).
  - Section 15 declares only "resembles pitch B's ending". It lists "he goes on the count" as a difference, but B's Tam counts too. The jar test and the tide-timed door at the arrival site are not declared at all.
  - Crossing timed to the neighbours' tide is also pitch C's central device.
- *Why it matters:* the owner said the season must be SECONDS, not a patchwork, and that anything taken from B or C must be declared. Left undeclared, this reads as grafted.
- *Fix:* change the two beats that are most specifically B's, and declare the rest accurately.
  - (a) Prove that next door is food through Terry's body, not through a jar. Terry eats the crab. An hour later the ward's T-meter on his finger reads 5.6, his first normal reading in five years, while Peg's meter still says LO. Kieran wrings Rosie's jumper out straight into the drain, so the residue stays.
  - For the 1.1 per cent, Kieran weighs Peg's 5 kg bag of flour (her balance is planted in episode 1) on her brass balance and on the little bridge: 5.000 against 4.95. A jumper's worth of sea could never show 1.1 per cent anyway. On a one-kilo jar that is 11 grams, one step on a scale that reads in 10-gram steps.
  - (b) Terry counts himself down. Danny holds the gate.
  - (c) In section 15, declare the neighbours'-tide timing and why it stays (a door at a shore can only open at a tide time), and the no-hand rule (guess 6 forces it). Remove "he goes on the count" from the differences.

**3. Danny's grab breaks the grip rule it is meant to prove.**
- *Where:* episode 6, cold open ("grabs Joe by the collar and wrenches him round"); guess 6; rule 9.
- *Problem:* guess 6 says: "Only flesh holding flesh does this... a rope or pole between two fields is simply cut where they part." A collar is cloth. By the plan's own rule, the grab turns nobody. Danny should come back whole with a strip of Joe's jacket in his fist, and twist 7 cannot happen.
- *Why it matters:* this is the only scene that proves the sixth guess on screen, and it breaks the guess in the same shot. Viewers heard Tess state the rule in episode 4.
- *Fix:* Danny grabs Joe's bare wrist. Not his neck: under guess 6 the one who turns also leaves behind whatever "was being held", so a grip on the neck is a coin toss on beheading Joe, which is not "something slower". A grip on the wrist makes the toss a hand for a hand.

**4. Taking the top off the Hill would make Grays scales read heavy, not light. (This settles unresolved item 2.)**
- *Where:* guess 4; episode 4, cold open; rule 14; ledger 4.7, row 1; 4.11, row 32; twist 11; section 15, entry 9.
- *Problem:*
  - A scale reads the downward pull on what stands on it.
  - The Hill's top stands about eight kilometres east of Grays and hundreds of metres above it, so it pulls a sack at Grays sideways and a little *upward*. Remove it and the sack weighs slightly more.
  - Almost all of that pull is sideways. A 1.1 per cent drop in weight would need a sideways pull about thirty times larger, enough to slide cups along every table in Thurrock.
  - The round 1 log called the effect "generous". In fact it points the wrong way.
- *Why it matters:* the lying scale drives the third reversal, and the ledger says it follows from Law 7. As written it contradicts Law 7.
- *Fix (preferred):* put the change directly under the scales.
  - On 4 April the neighbours dig a mountain's worth of rock out of their sea floor under Grays and Gravesend. This is the Deep, already "a soft, wide pull under Grays"; extend it under the river.
  - With less rock below, there is less pull down, so every scale over the Deep reads 1.1 per cent light. London, far off under the cube law, reads true.
  - The Hill and its shore at the field are untouched, so the door still works and the "top off the Hill" reason in section 15 falls away.
  - Keep both lines in episode 4. Danny's "The Hill's moved" is a wrong guess, and Peg's "Hills don't move" is, for once, right. It was the Deep, the sea Rosie falls into in episode 9.
  - *Fallback,* if the Hill must be the source: the neighbours pile rock *onto* the Hill, which gives the right sign. Section 5 would then have to admit the sideways problem openly.

**5. Law 1's "many leaves" does no plot work.**
- *Where:* ledger 4.1, row 4, and the whole season.
- *Problem:* the checklist says "there are many leaves". Every beat needs only two, ours and next door. The ledger gives this row the job "The price of the whole premise", which is not plot work. The owner's rule is that an item with no job fails.
- *Fix:* give the black more than one tug besides home. This is derived from Law 1 and Law 7, so it adds no guess.
  - Episode 1: one line of lore about a third, faint pull on the far side, "the Far", which nobody follows. Some of the lost from before spares followed it.
  - Episode 9: Rosie feels it and chooses the Deep.
  - Epilogue: the second message gains a reading that only many leaves allow. The sugar of our hand may come from further off, from someone who has our hand. Kieran notes it is fainter than the first.

**6. Liam's and Frank's sums don't say what the plan says they say.**
- *Where:* episode 5, acts four and five; episode 9, act five; twist 5; section 12; canonical row "Liam"; episode 10, act one; twist 12.
- *Problem, Liam:*
  - Episode 9 says: "The fry-up alone would have left him two hundredths of a second. It took both."
  - But the scale's error alone kills him. His true 63.1 kg plus 1.5 kg of notes is 64.6 kg, which gives 7.08 seconds, short of a 7.1-second job.
  - So "It took both" is false, and Peg's plate, the beat that breaks the promise that the family table is safe, did no harm.
- *Problem, Frank:*
  - Peg's "7.1" for a seven-second job is a tenth to spare.
  - The wall read 64.3 kg, less than his morning 64.4 kg, so he carried nothing hidden.
  - By rule 4 he should have come back, and nothing in the plan says why he didn't. "The same arithmetic that killed Liam" is not the same: Liam carried hidden mass and Frank carried none.
- *Why it matters:* the audience learns the fifth-of-a-second rule in episode 1 and is invited to check the sums.
- *Fix, Liam:*
  - Make the job 7.0 seconds. Tess's 7.2 is then two tenths to spare.
  - The scale's error alone leaves 7.08 seconds and the fry-up alone leaves 7.12, so either on its own is survivable. Both together leave 6.98, two hundredths short. "It took both" becomes true, and Tess, who gave twice Peg's margin, still loses him.
  - Update episode 5 ("The job needs 7.0"; "two hundredths from home"), episode 9, twist 5, section 12 ("margin cut to two tenths") and section 14.
- *Fix, Frank:* Ray adds one fact in episode 10: "Job planned at seven. At seven he was still at the tray. Starving men are slow." A tenth covers nothing when the crosser is weak.

**7. The 02:04 ticket cannot read 58.4 under the plan's own derived rule.**
- *Where:* section 5, derived ("a load-cell platform also feels a body standing in the black over it... the way a weigh-wall does"; "a weigh-wall feels a body's own pull, so [it does not] lie"); episodes 4 to 7; twist 11; 4.7, row 3; 4.11 seeds; section 14, "Joe".
- *Problem:*
  - The plan says the plate feels Joe the way a weigh-wall does, by his own pull, and that walls don't lie. The change next door alters Earth's pull on things standing *on* the plate. It does not alter Joe's pull on the plate. So the reading should be his true 59.0.
  - Worse, a body in the black *above* a plate pulls the plate up. A load cell would read him as a lift, a minus, not a load.
- *Why it matters:* the round 1 fix made 58.4 a plant for twist 11, but the rule written to allow it contradicts it.
- *Fix:*
  - The ticket prints **−59.0**, which is honest under Law 7 and stranger. Kieran files it as a glitch, and Ray in episode 5 knows better: "A plate reads a crosser as a lift."
  - In episode 7 Maggie: "Fifty-nine. That's my Joe."
  - Drop it from twist 11's plants. Plenty remain: Kieran's 19.78, Mick's lorry, the drawer, Danny's early tingle.
  - If there is room in episode 9, act four, add a beat that makes the audience re-read the season: the only ticket since April that told the truth is the boy nobody could see.

**8. Law 7's cube law, which explains the lying scale, is contradicted by the Twitch and the message.**
- *Where:* episode 2, cold open; episode 8, hook; epilogue scene 4; rules 13 and 14; 4.11, rows 4, 30 and 33.
- *Problem:*
  - The cube law explains why Grays feels the change and London doesn't.
  - Yet the Twitch reaches "every gravimeter on Earth", floods "a hundred City basements" and moves Kieran's Grays reading by 1.5 per cent, all at once.
  - And the message, made of tremors near Grays, is read by "the detectors that heard March's silent merger", which are overseas. A pull that fades with the cube of distance can't do both.
  - Separately, a weigh-wall floods only after a body stays for three seconds, so a single twitch would flood none.
- *Fix:* add one derived line. A mass standing still next door is felt only close by. A sudden, violent event sends out a ripple that travels and is felt everywhere, as the silent merger was.
  - The Twitch is such a ripple. It lasts several seconds (Kieran's three readings span it), which is why the walls flood.
  - The message is slow mass moving near Grays, so it is read on the estuary's own gravimeters, by the team that read the merger.
  - Reword episode 8 and the epilogue to match.

**9. Episode 9's crossing breaks guesses 4 and 5, and the ledger gives Rosie's training the wrong job.**

  - (c) Add one line to 3.3: the lamp is the field's own glow and dies with it. That is also why the spare's tight field is blind.

**10. The rolls can't prove the drift on their own, and the choice to hand them over needs its real alternative.**
- *Where:* episode 10, acts four and five; episode 7, act one; epilogue scene 1; theorem 6.
- *Problem:*
  - The rolls print dates and weights. The seconds of each job exist only in Tess's head, and who turned is pencilled in from memory the night before.
  - "The only record of what time in the black does, and nobody can call them forged" is true of the print, not of the pencil, and a drift table needs what's in the pencil.
  - Also, the law requires Peg to keep the rolls (Pinnock, episode 1), so investigators can get them with a warrant. "Without the rolls, nobody can be charged" is true only if the family burns them.
- *Fix:*
  - Make the rolls the key to other people's paper. Their dates let investigators pull the vaults' own weigh-wall logs for every Marlow job, and each wall logs a body's time and mass, as Mayfair's did.
  - Where a turned crosser went on working, the rolls show the same falling curve as Frank's and Rosie's. The Register's claim dates confirm who turned.
  - Printed weights plus someone else's printed seconds cannot be dismissed as invented. This is the logline's "someone else's paper to check it against".
  - Make Danny's "no" name the stove: burn them tonight, lose the licence, stay free.

**11. Ray's "which side her heart is on" hands Tess a test she never uses.**
- *Where:* episode 3, act two, then act three ("Tess tests Rosie: write your name, fast"); rule 6; ledger 4.6, last row.
- *Problem:*
  - A turned body's heart is on the right. Ray says so in act two.
  - In act three Tess, now suspicious, tests Rosie's handwriting, which Rosie can practise, instead of putting a hand to Rosie's chest, which she can't fake.
  - Rule 6 calls the catalogue photo "chemistry" and the ledger says "no looking can settle who flipped". But a heart on the wrong side, compared against someone who never crossed (Kieran), settles it exactly as the photo does.
- *Fix:*
  - Reword rule 6 to the source's meaning. The turned person can't tell by looking whether she flipped or the world did. Anyone else can tell by comparing her with something that never crossed: a meter, a photo, a heartbeat.
  - Then either shorten Ray's line to "look inside her" (the ring anecdote stays), or keep it and let Tess reach for Rosie's chest while Rosie laughs and dodges: "Get off, you'll want a kiss next." Rosie fails the test by dodging it, and sharp viewers get a clue.

**13. "Mirror germs can barely feed here" is a seventh guess passed off as derived.**
- *Where:* section 5, derived list; rule 11; ledger 4.6, row 2.
- *Problem:* nothing in the laws fixes how well mirror germs grow here. The scientists behind the 2024 warning that the source cites expected them to find enough food with no hand to grow. Sewers are full of fat, which the plan itself says they can eat. Calling "a slow, rare risk" derived adds a guess in secret, which the owner forbade.
- *Fix:* keep the gentle world, but move the claim from physics into history. "In thirty years no mirror outbreak has been recorded, and the Register's modest rules rest on that record." It becomes the world's experience, not a law, and the sea down the drain becomes a real unknown.

**14. By the plan's own numbers, Terry's step is almost free, so "even exile costs dice" and his wonder ring hollow.**
- *Where:* episode 10, acts two and three; rule 18; section 14, "Finale morning"; section 11, wonder; section 12, residue and hook; section 15, entry 2.
- *Problem:*
  - Rosie steps next door in two seconds (episode 9) and in three (episode 10), where the odds of turning are nil or 1 in 5,000.
  - Terry is "allowed 5 seconds: one in six if he takes all five", and nothing says why he would need all five.
  - A viewer who watched Rosie will judge his risk to be almost nothing. "Whether Terry arrived straight" is then not really open, and the hook's "someone who arrived the wrong way round" becomes a long shot.
- *Fix:* give the five seconds a cause the season has shown. Finding a tug you have never followed takes time. Rosie took three because she had already followed the Deep. Terry has only ever faced home and was always slow to find his bubbles in the pit (plant this in episode 1). Tess: "Rosie did it in three. You'll take longer." His odds then sit between 1 in 12 and 1 in 6, and the wonder is honest.

**15. Tess can't trace the batch codes the way episode 8 says.**

**17. Smaller slips in numbers and wording.**
- **Terry's ticket.** His last ticket reads "71.4", but 71.4 was the reading on Maggie's steelyard, which doesn't print tickets. The little bridge would print 70.6. Make it 70.6, with Peg's pencil beside it: "71.4 on Maggie's. Went next door."
- **Terry's 2021 job.** He turned on "a seven-second job", but at 71.4 kg his budget is 5.7 seconds. Give his 2021 weight (63 kg, made for the job) and let five years of dripping put it back on.
- **Shaun's weight.** 58.0 kg (true, March) minus a 5.5 kg leg is a true 52.5, so the light bridge prints 51.9, not 52.5. And 5.5 kg is nearly a tenth of a 58 kg man, too heavy for a leg taken below the knee; take it above the knee.
- **Tess's tingle.** Her "tingle comes back like an old song" on a 2.5-second hop, with about 7.7 seconds to spend. The tingle comes only near the end. Make it "the black comes back like an old song".
- **Frank's tickets.** Scales used for trade in Britain have had to weigh in metric since 2000, so the 2001 and 2002 tickets print 78.0 and 64.4. Peg pencils the stones beside them.
- **Jobs in Frank's day.** "Jobs that took two seconds in Frank's day" sits beside Frank's own four- and seven-second jobs. Say "most jobs".
- **The two halves.** Section 6 says "Neither family could see it alone", but in episode 7, act one, the Marlows hold the dates, Tess's seconds and Terry's memory of who turned, and the Aherns hold their seconds and their own memories. Reword: either family could have seen it, neither ever laid it out, and together it is too big to call luck. Maggie's books also cover twenty years while her count covers twenty-five.
- **"Nobody knew."** Episode 7's "what nobody knew the family kept" contradicts Pinnock saying it to Peg in episode 1. Make it "what nobody had ever read".
- **Kieran's weight.** His 38.0 is a reading from the light bridge (true 38.4 kg, 12.3 seconds). Section 14 should say which.
