# Episode 3: Making Weight

*Rosie's weight keeps falling, her family keeps cheering, and the detective starts adding seconds to her jobs.*

---

## Cold open

### Scene 1. The weighbridge office, Grays, Friday, a minute to midnight.

Job night. The lorries are parked up in a row outside, and the yard lights lay orange bars through the blinds. Inside it's warm and loud and smells of bacon.

TERRY (66) wraps bacon sandwiches in foil for the van crew, in his yellow gloves. DANNY (41) and LIAM (19) are in dark work clothes. SHAUN (26), who isn't on tonight, sits on the bottom stair with a mug of tea. KIERAN (12) has been allowed up for it, in pyjamas under his school coat. SIXPENCE, the old lurcher, lies in his basket by the stove. TESS (38) stands by the ticket printer with the pencil behind her ear. PEG (72) has her apron on over her nightie and a stick of chalk in her hand.

ROSIE (22) steps onto the little bridge in her socks.

At night the displays are dark, so the printer does the telling. It chatters. Peg tears off the ticket and holds it under the lamp, glasses on the end of her nose.

PEG: Forty-one eight.

The room goes up. Danny bangs the table with the flat of his hand. Shaun whistles through his fingers. Kieran bounces on the settle. Liam claps his big hands. Rosie bows from the waist like a conductor.

Peg chalks it at the foot of Rosie's column on the board: 44.0, 43.6, 43.1, and now 41.8. Beside it she draws the biggest star that board has ever had.

PEG: That's a Marlow.

Rosie comes up out of her bow. For half a second she rests her fingertips on the edge of the dresser, the way you touch a wall on a boat. Then she's grinning again, and bowing again, and the room is still clapping.

**MAIN TITLE: SECONDS**

## Act one: The longest job

### Scene 2. Harley Street, Saturday morning, two weeks earlier.

Caption: TWO WEEKS EARLIER.

Where we left her: Rosie hurrying down Harley Street after the jeweller's grey van, collar up. At the corner the lights change and the van gets away. She stands on the kerb, out of breath, and watches it go.

**Monday, 6 p.m.** Closing time. Rosie's own white van is parked across from the jeweller's with its engine off. The guards carry out the steel case. The grey van pulls away, and three cars back, so does Rosie.

Through the City. Into a narrow street of diamond dealers: shutters, buzzers, brass plates, men in black coats with cases chained to their wrists. A street sign: MARRABLE STREET. The guards carry the case down iron steps to a door under the dealers' exchange. Next door stands an empty shop, TO LET, with a grating over its basement window.

**Tuesday night.** The cobbled mews behind Marrable Street, one lamp. Rosie sits in her van with the engine off, watching the back of the exchange. She drinks from a bottle of water. Her breath has fogged the windscreen.

She reaches up and writes on the fogged glass with one fingertip: ROSIE. Slowly, carefully, the right way round for anyone sitting beside her. She looks at it. She wipes it out with her sleeve and writes it again, faster. And again, faster, until it comes quick and easy and looks careless.

Then she wipes the glass clean and sits back in the dark.

### Scene 3. The weighbridge office, Wednesday, 6 p.m.

Tea. Sausage and mash. The gate is shut and the hatch is down. Terry is at the stove in his gloves, with his blue enamel pot on the back ring. The whole firm is at the table: Peg, Danny, Tess, Liam, Shaun, Kieran with his homework.

Peg upends the float tin over the counter. Coins, a button, and a raffle ticket.

PEG: That's the float. Dilys comes Tuesday.

DANNY: Order book's full, Mum.

PEG: Order book's paper. Concrete money goes in the bank, where the taxman can see it. Dilys wants notes. *(She sweeps the coins back in.)* Concrete pays the bills. Concrete doesn't pay the Laundry.

The back door bangs. Rosie comes in on a gust of cold air, drops into her chair and steals a chip of fried onion off Liam's plate.

ROSIE: I've got one.

Forks stop. She tells it like a story at a bar, with her hands.

ROSIE: Jeweller's on Harley Street. Grey van, one steel case. I've had it three nights running. Every night the shop shuts, the stones go to bed in a diamond room. Weekends they sleep there Friday to Monday. One tray. Two kilo, tray and all.

DANNY: In where?

ROSIE: Marrable Street.

Peg's knife stops on her plate. She looks up at Frank's photo on the wall, the big man laughing. The table has gone quiet the way it went quiet at the birthday.

PEG: *(to Rosie)* Your grandad's street.

ROSIE: I know.

PEG: Last job he ever did. *(beat)* He'd have liked that.

KIERAN: Is that where the black took him?

PEG: Fifteen stone, and he went in anyway, 'cause somebody had to. *(She taps the painted rule at the top of the board with her fork.)* That's why we make weight.

Tess looks down at her plate. She was fourteen that week. It was the week her own name went at the top of the board.

DANNY: Under the exchange? Iron steps, door with no handle?

ROSIE: That's the one.

A slow grin spreads over Danny's face.

DANNY: I poured that. Twenty-three. Diamond room, floor and back wall in one go. Best floor I ever did.

LIAM: Thickest at the back wall.

Everyone looks at him.

LIAM: I barrowed every bead. They wanted it thick on the mews side, 'cause that's where you'd come from.

DANNY: He did an' all. All-night pour.

Liam's ears go pink.

TESS: And the side?

LIAM: Empty shop next door. Party wall's old brick with one skin of Mix on it. They ran out of money.

Tess's lips move. The table waits; they all know this face.

TESS: From next door, then. Two trips, under three each. Half the parcels, home, the other half. The wall never feels her long enough to flood.

LIAM: That's what I said at Mayfair.

PEG: Never go back twice.

DANNY and ROSIE *(with her, the old chorus)*: Never go back twice.

PEG: Your dad's mate went back for seconds on the Old Street job. There was a copper stood in the room the second time. Your dad never said his name again. *(to Tess)* One trip. The whole tray.

TESS: One trip, the wall floods and she's racing the beads. Mayfair was half a second.

PEG: And she came home with it.

ROSIE: I'll do it in one.

PEG: There.

Tess looks at Rosie properly: the shadows under her eyes, her wrists in Danny's old sleeves.

TESS: Then she makes weight.

ROSIE: I always make weight.

KIERAN: Lightest goes longest!

The table laughs. Tess has lost, and she knows it.

DANNY: Want it done before the Aherns smell it. They were working Mayfair at Christmas.

PEG: Declan Ahern learnt everything he knows at this table. Frank taught him.

SHAUN: And he never said ta.

ROSIE: *(through a mouthful of mash)* That tray's Terry's year. And his cure after, if you'd let him.

PEG: We don't go to them.

ROSIE: I priced it, for his birthday. Hundred and eighty thousand. Seventy per cent.

At the stove, Terry's spoon stops in his pot. He doesn't turn round.

PEG: Then it's his year. *(to Tess)* When?

TESS: Friday week. I want to walk it.

## Act two: The pursuer

### Scene 4. Marlow Aggregates, the batching plant cabin, the following Wednesday, 4 p.m.

A tin hut at the foot of the concrete plant, shaking gently as the drum turns overhead. The BATCHER, an old boy in a flat cap who has worked the controls since Frank's day, watches a thin man in an overcoat that is too big for him turn the pages of a lever-arch file on the desk. MARLOW MIX: POUR CONTRACTS.

DS RAY AMBROSE (54) stops at a page. MARRABLE STREET. VAULT FLOOR AND REAR WALL. HIGH-DENSITY BEAD. 2023. Signed with a big, looping D. MARLOW.

He copies it into a notebook.

BATCHER: Nothing to hide in concrete, Mr Marlow says. Proud of it.

RAY: He should be. *(He closes the file.)* Where would I find Miss Marlow? The older one.

BATCHER: Pit. With the boy.

### Scene 5. The flooded gravel pit, Wednesday, 4:30 p.m.

The water is cold pewter under a white sky. Kieran, in a wetsuit and swimming goggles painted black, drops off the edge of the pontoon and is gone. Tess kneels at the edge with her eyes on nothing and her lips moving.

Ray comes down the haul road and sits on an upturned drum at the end of the pontoon. Tess doesn't look round.

RAY: He kicks down.

TESS: Everyone kicks down.

Kieran breaks the surface, gasping, and pushes up his goggles.

TESS: Breathe out. Follow your bubbles. Again.

He goes under. Tess's lips start again.

RAY: The Mayfair wall kept a log. Every body in the black within four metres, with its weight and its time. At twelve minutes past three it logged two.

TESS: Two what?

RAY: Bodies. Hers. And one more.

Tess's count falters for a second, then picks up.

TESS: Whose?

RAY: A wall gives you a weight, not a name.

TESS: What weight?

RAY: I'll keep that.

He takes a green card out of his inside pocket and lays it on the planks between them, face up, the way you'd lay down a winning hand. His photo. HANDEDNESS REGISTER. CLAIMANT. Tess looks at it for a long moment.

TESS: You're turned.

RAY: Since 2015. Seven seconds, with a kid under my arm. *(He takes out a packet of crackers and a pocket knife, and spreads lard on one.)* It's all that sticks.

Tess has heard Terry say those exact words at the stove every morning for five years.

TESS: Why show me?

RAY: So you'll know I know what it looks like. *(He eats, catching the crumbs in his palm.)* There's a way to kill a crosser without leaving a mark on her. Turn her round in the black and send her home to her tea. Six months later she's dead, and it looked like a diet. And her family cheered every kilo.

TESS: Nobody can turn anybody. You face home.

RAY: When a crosser comes back turned, and there was somebody else in the black beside her, I stop calling it luck. I've watched your family's weight since 2002, Miss Marlow. Your niece gets lighter every time I see her.

Kieran surfaces. "Again." He goes under.

TESS: Then look at her. You've got a rig.

RAY: I could. Two seconds beside her in the black and I'd see which side her heart's on. The law calls that a search. *(He brushes crumbs off his coat.)* We had a man once with a ring in his stomach. We could see it plain as day. Couldn't see why he'd swallowed it. Ten years of courts. *(beat)* So I wait till she comes to me hungry.

He stands. He looks out over the water at the conveyor gantry, black against the sky.

RAY: There's a car outside an empty shop on Marrable Street. It'll be there all week.

Tess goes very still.

TESS: Why would I care?

RAY: Had a call.

TESS: Who from?

RAY: Didn't leave a name. *(beat)* First job I ever caught was on Marrable Street.

TESS: I know what you caught.

She opens her mouth to ask the next thing. She doesn't ask it. Ray waits, and when nothing comes, he goes back up the haul road.

Kieran bursts up out of the water and tears off his goggles.

KIERAN: How long was I?

Tess looks at him. For the first time in her life, she doesn't know.

At the top of the haul road Ray opens his car door, then turns.

RAY: Miss Marlow. *(She looks up.)* If the painting turned, so did whoever carried it.

He gets in and drives away. Across the yard, the big bridge prints the weight of his car.

KIERAN: Mum? How long?

TESS: *(too late)* Long. Good.

### Scene 6. The weighbridge office, Wednesday, 7 p.m.

Tea. Liver and onions, mash, gravy. Tess comes in late from the pit with Kieran's wet towel over her shoulder and takes the chair where she can see Rosie.

Rosie eats. She clears her plate, wipes the gravy up with bread, and holds the plate out to Peg for more. Peg piles it, delighted.

PEG: Look at her. Eats like a horse and never puts a gram on.

DANNY: Burns it off in the black.

Rosie grins with her mouth full and eats the second plate too, every bite, steadily, the way you'd do a job.

Tess watches her. At the stove end Terry spoons from his white tub; he hasn't touched the family's food in five years. Rosie has eaten everything put in front of her. Something Ray put in Tess's head goes quiet, and she picks up her own fork.

### Scene 7. Marrable Street, Thursday, 11 p.m.

Tess sits in her own car at the dark end of the street with the engine off. The dealers' shutters are down. Across from the empty shop, TO LET, sits a grey saloon with a man in it and a coffee on the dashboard. He isn't hiding. He's there to be seen.

At the bus stop a thin lad of seventeen or so, in a hoodie, lets one bus go, then another.

Tess gets out and walks round the block into the mews, hands in her pockets, as if she lives there. She stops on the cobbles over the back wall of the exchange. She paces it to the wall and back, and down to where the diamond room must be beneath her feet. Her lips move the whole time.

Back in the car, she sits with both hands on the wheel.

TESS: *(to nobody)* Ten.

### Scene 8. The weighbridge office, Thursday night, late.

Peg waits up at the table in her dressing gown with a pot of tea. Danny is there, still in his work clothes. Tess comes in with the cold on her coat.

TESS: There's a car on the basement.

DANNY: Whose?

TESS: Old Bill's.

DANNY: How d'you know?

TESS: I went and looked.

Peg's cup stops halfway.

PEG: Who's talked?

DANNY: Nobody at this table.

Tess says nothing about Ray. She sits.

TESS: So we go the long way, from the street. The mews at the back, down through the road and in through the back wall. Ten seconds.

DANNY: Through Liam's beads.

PEG: She did nine four at Mayfair. Ten's near enough the same.

TESS: Near enough isn't.

PEG: *(evenly)* Stones sleep there till Monday, and Dilys comes Tuesday. Is it ten or isn't it?

TESS: It's ten.

PEG: Then it's the longest ever, and she'll be the one that did it.

Peg rinses her cup and goes up. Danny follows.

**Later.** The office is dark except for the printer's small green light. Tess sits alone at the table, as she has the night before every job since she was fourteen, walking it in her head: down through the road, in through the wall, the tray, out. Her lips move in the dark.

A creak on the stairs. Rosie, in Danny's old jumper, stops on the bottom step when she sees her.

ROSIE: Thought everyone was in bed.

TESS: Walking it.

ROSIE: Course you are.

Rosie looks past her at the fridge. A beat. She crosses to the sink instead, runs the cold tap, drinks a glass of water standing up, and goes back upstairs.

At the top of the stairs a door stands an inch open. Terry's. Behind it, in the dark, a slice of his face.

### Scene 9. The weighbridge office, Friday, a minute to midnight.

The same minute as the cold open. This time the camera stands where Tess stands.

The printer chatters. Peg holds the ticket to the lamp.

PEG: Forty-one eight.

The cheer goes up, and now we see what we didn't see before. Tess's hands stay at her sides. Her eyes are on Rosie's wrists where the sleeves have ridden up, and on the cords standing out in her neck. At the stove Terry raises the lid of his blue pot a crack and looks inside: the level is exactly where it was last night, and the spoon lying across it is clean. He lowers the lid slowly. Under the clapping, down in his basket, Sixpence growls at Rosie, low and steady, and nobody hears him.

Peg draws the star. "That's a Marlow." Rosie bows, and her fingertips find the dresser.

Tess's lips move.

TESS: Forty-one eight. Two for the tray. *(beat, aloud, to Peg)* Eleven two. Job's ten.

PEG: Second and a bit to spare. Your dad never had a second to spare in his life.

Tess looks up at Frank's photo, the big laughing man, and back at the thinnest name on the board.

## Act three: Tests that pass

### Scene 10. The scullery behind the office, job night, five past twelve.

A narrow back room: the twin tub, coats on hooks, boots, Rosie's rig laid out on the drainer. Through the open back door Danny and Liam are loading the van in the yard. Rosie clips the collar on, the brass disc at her throat, and checks her wrist unit.

Tess comes in and shuts the door to the office behind her.

ROSIE: What?

Tess puts one hand flat on her own chest. With the other she reaches for Rosie's, to feel for the beat against her own.

Rosie laughs and twists away, quick as a cat.

ROSIE: Get off. You'll want a kiss next.

TESS: Stand still.

ROSIE: What's got into you?

TESS: Nothing.

Tess looks around the scullery. Then she tears a blank ticket off the new roll on the shelf, and takes the pencil from behind her ear. It's the first time we've ever seen it come down. She holds both out.

TESS: Write your name.

ROSIE: What for?

TESS: Fast.

ROSIE: *(a grin)* Normal, or showing off?

TESS: Normal.

Rosie writes on the drainer, quick and careless: ROSIE MARLOW. Plain as a shop sign. Tess looks at it, and something in her shoulders lets go.

ROSIE: And showing off.

Under it, without a pause, she writes it again in mirror script, every letter running backwards and every one flowing.

TESS: Show-off.

ROSIE: Show-off, me.

She screws the ticket into a ball, opens the door to the office, leans through to the stove, opens its little door and posts the ball inside.

ROSIE: Paper talks.

She comes back and tucks the pencil behind Tess's ear.

ROSIE: Don't lose that. You've never used it.

She goes out to the yard, calling to Liam about the kit bag. Tess stands alone in the scullery. Through the doorway she watches the ball of paper catch in the stove and go. She touches the pencil behind her ear. She breathes out, as if she's put something heavy down.

### Scene 11. The weighbridge office, job night, twenty past twelve.

Peg fills Thermoses at the counter. Terry is wrapping the last of the sandwiches. The back door opens and Kieran comes in from the yard with Rosie's woolly hat.

KIERAN: Nan said get your hat, it's bitter. *(He holds up a block of lard in torn greaseproof paper, a corner cut away.)* Why've you got lard in your van?

Tess, at the printer, turns her head.

ROSIE: For the pit. Cold-water swimming. Channel swimmers grease up.

KIERAN: Can I have some for the pit?

ROSIE: You'll smell like a chip shop.

She takes the lard off him and drops it on the dresser as if it's nothing. Tess is still looking at it. She saw a knife spread that same white fat on a cracker two days ago.

Rosie meets her eyes and winks.

As Rosie crosses the room for her hat, Sixpence raises his head in his basket and growls at her, low.

PEG: He's still jealous.

ROSIE: *(ruffling the dog's ears as he backs away)* Everyone's jealous of me.

Tess turns back to the printer. Her lips move. The number hasn't changed. Eleven two.

### Scene 12. The yard behind the office, job night, half past twelve.

Terry sits on an upturned crate by the bins with the cordless phone. Beyond the fence the gravel heaps stand pale in the dark. He has taken off one yellow glove to hold the phone.

TERRY: Can't sleep, no. You?

CON *(on the phone, faint and old, a voice with a river in it)*: Never do now.

TERRY: They've been clapping again.

CON: What for?

TERRY: Weight.

CON: Ours do that.

A pause. A lorry goes by on the A13.

TERRY: Pot's going down, Con. Every night since the boy's birthday. Two fingers. I top it up.

CON: Mice.

TERRY: Big mice. *(beat)* Last night it never moved. Nor today.

CON: That's good, then, isn't it?

TERRY: Is it?

Silence on the line.

CON: My lot are the same as yours. "We feed our own." Fed on what, I say. Boat's late again.

TERRY: Come here, I'll feed you.

CON: You don't know where here is, Tel.

TERRY: *(a small laugh)* No. I don't.

He rings off, pulls his glove back on and goes in. At the stove he takes the lid off his blue pot, stands a clean spoon upright in it, and leaves it on the side where anyone could reach it.

### Scene 13. The yard, job night, ten to one.

The van's back doors are open. Liam stows the kit bag, the sandwiches and the Thermoses, everything in its place. Rosie, rigged under her coat, sits on the tailgate with her hat on.

LIAM: How d'you keep it off?

ROSIE: Willpower, me.

LIAM: I've got none.

ROSIE: You've got loads. You just spend it all on breakfast.

He laughs. She laughs.

LIAM: What's ten like?

ROSIE: *(a beat)* Long.

She hops down and goes back inside for her gloves. In the office she passes the stove, and the blue pot with the spoon standing up in it. She stops. Her hand moves.

The door opens behind her. Tess, with the car keys.

Rosie's hand goes past the pot and picks up the last foil sandwich instead.

ROSIE: For Liam.

She walks out. At the stove Terry looks at the spoon and doesn't move.

Peg stands at the office door in her dressing gown as the van doors close.

PEG: Face home.

ROSIE: Always.

Tess gets in last. As the van rolls across the big bridge and the printer chatters behind them, her lips are moving.

TESS: Eleven two.

## Act four: The diamond room

### Scene 14. The mews behind Marrable Street, Saturday, 2:58 a.m.

The van sits on the cobbles over the back wall of the exchange with its lights off. Danny at the wheel with the stopwatch on his phone. Liam in the passenger seat. In the back, Tess kneels facing Rosie.

TESS: Forty-one eight, two for the tray. Eleven two. Job's ten. Down through the road, in at the corner, drawers on your left. Wall's at three.

ROSIE: Don't be pretty about it.

TESS: Don't be pretty about it.

Rosie flexes her fingers once, the way every Marlow does, and steps off the world.

**The black.** Sound drops to her heartbeat and her breath. Her glow reaches an arm's length round her, grey-gold, and past it there is nothing at all. Her ears scream that she's falling. She moves the way the pit taught her: small pulls at nothing, down by count.

The wrist unit counts tenths. 0.4. In the glow, the van's floor from inside, then the cobbles from underneath, packed sand, a gas main like a sleeping snake. 1.1. Clay. An old brick sewer, and inside it black water moving. 1.9. Then the concrete.

It is Danny's concrete, and inside it are the beads, packed grey through the grey like frogspawn in jelly, the whole thickness of the wall. She pulls herself through, slow. 2.6. On a seam between two pours, deep inside the wall, she passes a handprint the size of a shovel blade, and beside it, drawn with a finger in the wet mix three years ago, the letters LM.

She puts her hand flat inside her brother's, through it, the way a hand goes into water. Then she goes on.

**The van.** Tess counts under her breath.

TESS: ...two... three. There's the wall.

Somewhere under the cobbles comes a rumble, like a train going by a long way down. Liam feels it through the seat.

LIAM: Can she hear you?

TESS: No.

LIAM: Then why d'you do it?

TESS: So someone's counting.

**The black.** 3.8. She is out of the concrete and into the room. The glow shows steel drawers from the inside, their locks, their runners. Through a steel door, a sleeping guard in a chair, and the inside of him: ribs rising and falling, the slow fist of his heart. For one beat she watches it, the way she watches the surgeons on her phone.

The first beads fall through the top of her glow, silent, like black hail. She reaches into a drawer through its steel face and draws out the tray: velvet, and folded papers, and in the papers a hard glitter. The beads climb the drawers. 4.9. The tray is flat against her chest, and the beads close over the drawer where it lay.

She turns and goes back the way she came.

**The van.** At the mouth of the mews, a thin lad on a bike has stopped under the lamp with one foot on the kerb. His hood is up. He's watching the van.

DANNY: Kid.

LIAM: Just a kid.

Danny's hand goes to the door.

TESS: *(not looking up, still counting)* Nobody moves.

Nobody moves. The lad watches a moment longer, then pedals off into the dark.

**The black.** 7.2. The concrete again, the beads again. The handprint slides past. 8.6. The sewer. 9.0. Clay.

**The van.** Danny, eyes on the stopwatch:

DANNY: Nine four.

Nobody breathes. It's the longest a Marlow has ever been gone, and the count goes on past it.

**The black.** 9.6. Close on her fingertips as the tingle starts, a fizz under the nails, while she is still in the clay under the road, where stepping out means dying inside the ground. She doesn't hurry, because hurrying wastes pulls. She finds the tug of home and follows it up.

**The van.** Rosie lands on the floor of the load bay with a gasp, the tray against her chest. The van smells of bacon and diesel.

TESS: Ten dead.

Danny stops the watch. He turns round in his seat.

DANNY: Straight?

ROSIE: Straight.

She's shaking. Liam pulls off his coat and puts it round her, and she laughs up at him with her teeth chattering.

ROSIE: Longest ever.

DANNY: Longest ever.

Liam holds out the foil sandwich she brought him. She pushes it back.

ROSIE: Big lad needs it.

Danny pulls out of the mews. At its mouth, under the lamp, there's nobody.

### Scene 15. The weighbridge office, Saturday, ten past four in the morning.

Peg has waited up with the kettle on, and Shaun with her. Terry sits at the stove end in his dressing gown. Kieran is asleep sideways in the armchair under his coat. The van rolls onto the big bridge, and the printer chatters.

They come in low and happy, so as not to wake the boy. Peg kisses Rosie on both cheeks and goes straight to the board with her chalk. Beside the big star she starts to write the job, the way the family writes its best nights in chalk and wipes them off later: 10.0.

Danny rolls up his right sleeve and goes to the green safe at the end of the little bridge, ready to post the tray through the door. Rosie carries the tray across to him. She steps onto the plate.

Peg's chalk is on the second nought when Rosie's knees go.

She folds onto the steel the way a coat slides off a hook. The tray skids across the plate and stops against the safe. Under the counter the printer chatters and prints a ticket nobody tears off.

LIAM: Rosie?

He is there first. He gathers his sister up off the plate the way he carried the canvas in from the van that first morning, like a tray of glass, and carries her to the settle by the stove.

## Act five: The meter

### Scene 16. The weighbridge office, continuous.

Rosie lies on the settle, grey, her skin wet and cold, shaking in long shudders. Her eyes roll and come back and roll again. She's murmuring about home. Danny kneels at the end of the settle and holds her stockinged feet in his big hands to warm them.

Peg puts two fingers to Rosie's neck.

PEG: Going like a bird. When did she last eat?

DANNY: She had a fry-up yesterday. I watched her.

Tess yanks open the cutlery drawer and digs Peg's glucose meter out from among the spoons. She pricks Rosie's finger, squeezes a bead of blood onto the strip and waits for the beep.

The screen reads **LO**.

TESS: She's hypo.

Peg takes over at once. She has done this for herself in lay-bys and car parks for years.

PEG: Gel. Top drawer of the dresser. In her cheek, rub it on the gum.

Tess squeezes a tube of glucose gel into Rosie's cheek and rubs it along her gum with a finger. They wait. Peg watches the clock. Tess tests again.

**LO.**

PEG: Another.

A second tube. They wait. Kieran is awake now, standing on the armchair cushion to see over the adults' shoulders.

**LO.**

PEG: *(quieter)* She should be coming up.

Danny has his phone out. His thumb is on the nine.

DANNY: I'm ringing someone.

PEG: With that on the floor?

The tray lies against the safe where it skidded, glittering. Danny looks at it. He looks at his daughter.

PEG: Nobody's ringing anybody. We look after our own. *(to Shaun)* Tea. Four sugars.

Shaun makes it with shaking hands. Tess holds the cup to Rosie's mouth. The tea runs out of the corners and down her neck. She can't swallow.

Terry is standing at the stove with his blue enamel pot in his hands. He picked it up off the side at the first LO, and he has been holding it ever since.

TERRY: Shift.

Nobody moves.

TERRY: Shift, Tess.

He kneels by the settle, a big soft man in yellow gloves, and puts a spoonful from the pot between Rosie's lips.

A beat. She swallows. Her eyes open, and they find Terry, and stay on him. Another spoonful. She swallows again. The shaking slows.

Colour creeps back into her face as if someone were filling her up from the bottom.

Terry puts the spoon back in the pot.

TERRY: She's been at my pot since the party.

Nobody speaks. The fridge hums.

PEG: Don't be soft, Terry.

TERRY: Every night, Peg. Two fingers. I've been topping it up.

Tess looks down at the meter in her hand. LO. On Peg, in lay-bys, it has always meant sugar, now.

TESS: It's not low. It can't read her.

From the armchair, in a small clear voice:

KIERAN: Is Rosie turned?

Peg flinches at the word as if he had thrown something. Nobody answers him.

Tess's eyes go round the room, and the room is full of it. On the fridge, the old birthday card in careful, wobbly mirror writing, and beside it the new one, where every backwards letter runs as easily as breathing. The catalogue cutting on the wall, the woman at her window on the left. The chalk board: 44.0, 43.6, 43.1, 41.8, and four stars. The lard on the dresser. Sixpence in his basket, still growling, as he has growled for weeks, and everyone said he was jealous.

Tess takes Rosie's coat off the hook to lay over her. Something slides out of the pocket and drops on the lino: a leaflet, folded in four. She unfolds it. OBVERSE. RESTORATION. £180,000.

TESS: The job. You brought us the job.

ROSIE: *(barely a voice)* It was for Terry.

TERRY: *(gently)* No it wasn't, love.

Peg looks across at the green safe, where the painting lies. For a moment she looks very old.

PEG: That picture was never a copy.

### Scene 17. The weighbridge office, Saturday, 5 a.m.

Grey light at the blinds. The whole family is at the long table where they cheered her, under the board. Rosie sits wrapped in a blanket with Terry's pot in front of her and eats from it slowly, openly, for the first time, and cannot stop. The tray of stones sits beside the pot, and nobody looks at it.

PEG: When?

ROSIE: Mayfair.

DANNY: Mayfair.

He counts it in his head, the weeks of fry-ups, and puts his face in his hands.

PEG: You've sat at this table every morning.

ROSIE: Every morning.

PEG: And we clapped.

Nobody has anything to say to that.

PEG: You'll go again. Short ones. It'll come up.

TERRY: *(very quietly)* Eleven times, Peg.

Peg doesn't look at him.

PEG: *(to Rosie)* You faced home.

It isn't quite a question. Rosie looks round the table: her dad, her brother, Tess, the stars on the board, Terry.

ROSIE: Something took hold of me in the black.

Silence.

ROSIE: At Mayfair, after the wall. Something heavy, right up by me. It had hold of me, Dad. It pulled me round. And when I found home again...

She doesn't finish. She doesn't need to.

Terry looks at her across the table. Five years ago he came back from the black turned, and told this table so, and nobody has said the word since. He knows exactly what she has just done, and why. He says nothing.

DANNY: *(low)* Took hold of you.

TESS: The wall logged two.

Every head turns to her.

TESS: At Mayfair. Two bodies in the black at twelve minutes past three. Hers, and one more.

PEG: Who told you that?

TESS: The copper. Wednesday.

DANNY: Wednesday. And you sent her tonight.

TESS: I tested her.

ROSIE: She did. I passed.

A long beat. Danny pushes his chair back.

DANNY: Who works Mayfair?

SHAUN: Aherns. All winter.

Danny is already reaching for his coat on the back of the door.

Peg looks up at Frank's photo. Then she looks at her son.

PEG: Blood pays blood.

**Cut to black.**

**END OF EPISODE 3**
