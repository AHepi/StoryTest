# Pitch review: The Fourth Direction and mind-bending

**Lens.** How each pitch uses the book, whether it keeps the book's physics and its own ledger, whether its twists bend the mind by the book's own logic (the mirror is relative; a slide and a turn feel the same; sight has a radius; weight is the only honest channel; a neighbour a hair's breadth away), and whether its engine can run ten episodes. I checked every number in each pitch against its own rules. Theme, religion and dialogue are left to the other reviews except where they touch the physics.

One thing to hold in mind throughout: the owner wrote *The Fourth Direction*. A twist that replays one of its "In the plot" seeds exactly will please an audience, but it cannot surprise the owner. The twists worth most here are the ones that combine two laws into something the book never wrote down.

---

## Pitch A: SECONDS (Essex, a family of thieves)

### Strengths
- **The doom is built from a theorem.** Walls became weights, so the City stored its gold as dust and flooded vaults with tungsten beads; jobs that took two seconds in Frank's day now take nine; long jobs turn people. The book's own logic makes the family's success lengthen its jobs and turn its children. No villain is needed.
- **The numbers add up, everywhere.** Liam weighs 62.4 on the bridge, eats half a kilo of fry-up, and the moved Hill makes the bridge read about one per cent light. His true 63.6 kilos buy 7.28 seconds for a 7.4-second job: "a tenth of a second from home." Frank's tickets (12 st 4 after a four-second job, falling for six months to 10 st 2, with Peg's pencilled "7.1") fit the family rule to the decimal, and Ray's 64.3 matches. "Fifteen stone" buys one second, too little for any job, so a viewer with a pencil could have caught Peg's memory in episode 1. That is fair play of a rare kind.
- **The best single idea in any of the three pitches.** A weighbridge measures pull; a rig counts mass. A mountain moved next door pulls them apart. Weight, the one honest channel, told the truth about the mountain and a lie about the boy. The book never saw this, and it follows from the book.
- **"Straight?" "Straight."** The family's check-in asks the one person who cannot know, since a turn cannot be felt. It is a ritual and a plant at once.
- **It tests the book's ready-made ending and it fails.** Rosie follows the Deep and finds a sea, not a welcome. For this owner, that is the surprise.

### What it uses, and whether each item works
- **Load-bearing:** gold as dust and weight-makers (the jobs grow); the lightest goes longest (the family's law); murder that looks like a diet (the applause at weigh-in); running out as spreading weight (Liam, Frank, and a new way to mourn); the drift that isn't a half and the ten-second, seventy-per-cent cure (the second reversal); the painting called a forgery, and paper as witness (episode 1, the loft, the logline); the earthquake with no fault (the bridge lies); whoever holds the reference sample (two scales; the rolls outrank memory).
- **Working, lighter:** the object with no trail (the family's origin and pride); privacy has a radius (the weigh-wall log: two bodies, six metres apart); the grip in transit (Danny turns himself); partial return and the amputee's seconds (Shaun); sending a hand (Danny's craft); looking is a search (keeps Ray waiting); vodka and lard (Ray's cover); the mirror kitchen and tripled stock (Obverse); custody by pantry (the ward); blindfold water training ("go again" in the pit); their ocean in your house and first contact in mass (the Deep; COME ROUND FOR TEA); mirror script and jar lids (Rosie's tells, Frank's jam jar, Ray's plain signature at the end).
- **Decoration:** dark-matter posters, the BBC's "next-door events", silent mergers, the Nobody Tide, passports (said to stop Rosie running; never shown doing it), the tumour-lifting want (never paid), the "delivered" bullet (a threat that leads nowhere). As surplus detail these make the world feel lived in, which is their job, but none moves the plot.
- **Unused:** the dog, looking-glass milk, the seed vault, plague-proof soldiers, SETI, the global gravimeter twitch, and, most costly, mirror life.

### Physics and ledger
The ledger is honest: four new guesses, each declared, and a list of derivations that really are derived. Problems:
1. **Mirror life is missing.** Forty thousand turned Britons, some for thirty years, each carrying gut bacteria that nothing on Earth can eat or fight. The book calls this its gravest danger. A pleasant, working Britain must have an answer on screen. *Fix:* one ledger line and one visible habit (the Register's rations come with sealed toilets; turned people may not work with food), or make it the season's residue: in episode 9 Peg tips a bucket of the neighbours' sea down the drain. Let the last shot of the epilogue be the Thames.
2. **The grip and the painting.** The ledger says a grip in the black always turns one of the two. Rosie held the painting through the black, yet both came back turned together. *Fix:* one line: the grip rule is between two fields; whatever rides inside one bubble turns with its carrier.
3. **Small sums.** Shaun's lost leg is about ten kilos, which buys two seconds by the family rule, not "a whole second". "Twenty-four years of audit rolls" must reach back past Frank's last six months (2001-02): make it twenty-five. On the ferry, "every turned Marlow and Ahern sits on one line" cannot be right for a coin toss; show a cluster instead: nobody turned under three seconds, most over eight.
4. **Tess's biscuit.** Sugar tastes sweet in either hand and vanilla has no hand, so a biscuit cannot tell her whether she turned. *Fix:* use the family's own sample: Terry's pot. If it smells of nothing to her, she is straight.

### Twists
- **Ep 1, the "forged" painting.** The book's seed, used well; it sets up episode 2 and Ray. Planted by the catalogue photo. The owner will recognise it.
- **Ep 3, Rosie is turned (the first major reversal).** Not a reversal for the audience. By then we have watched her sign in mirror script, read her phone in a make-up mirror, eat by fridge-light and walk into a cure clinic. The family learns; the audience re-reads nothing. This is the pitch's biggest weakness under this lens.
- **Eps 6-7, the cure is the curse; nobody spun anybody.** Strong. Planted by the seven short hops that fail, Terry's twenty-two tries, the surgeons' three seconds and the six metres in the log. The swap test holds: a real spinner would leave two bodies touching in the log.
- **Ep 8, the Chemist is Ray.** A good crime turn with no book logic in it. It re-reads "we feed our own", not the world.
- **Ep 9, the Deep is a sea.** Planted by the crossers' lore; modest, and it changes nothing that follows.
- **Ep 10, Frank's weight and the lying bridge.** The best pair of reveals in any pitch, both planted in episodes 1, 4 and 5, and neither survives a swap. But the finale is crowded: two big reveals, Ray's cure, and the vote on the paper.

### Can it fill ten episodes?
Yes. A heist of the week inside a war inside a family: every episode has a crossing set piece and a clock measured in seconds, and the family's rules keep producing fresh disasters. The soft spots are episode 8 (mostly a reveal), episode 9 (an exile detour with no consequence) and the overloaded finale.

### Serious problems and fixes
1. **The first reversal isn't one.** Hide Rosie's tells in plain sight. In episode 1, show that mirror writing has always been her party trick (an old birthday card on the fridge, years old, in mirror script; "show-off" is what the family always says) and that she has always been cack-handed with lids. Make her episode 2 clinic visit read as pricing a Restoration for Terry. Then the glucose gel that does nothing in episode 3 hits the audience along with the family, and every cheer and every "show-off" re-reads as the book's own point: nobody can tell a turn by looking.
2. **The neighbours are wallpaper until episode 9, and episode 9 pays nothing.** Let Rosie's landing on the weighbridge in a gush of sea be the moment the bridge is caught lying: the ward weighed her that morning, and the printer is a kilo out. The Liam reveal moves to the end of episode 9, and episode 10 carries Frank and the paper alone.
3. **Mirror life, the grip rule and the sums**, as above.

### Verdict
The closest to ready through this lens. Its world is the most thoroughly worked out, its arithmetic is honest, and its closing twist is the best use of the book's weight logic anywhere in the three pitches. Fix the first reversal and give the neighbours a job before episode 9, and it stands.

## Pitch B: HONEST WEIGHT (Saskatchewan, 1911)

### Strengths
- **The most original home for the book.** No rigs, one Stone, and a famine grown from the laws themselves. Mirrored wheat is rust-proof, weevil-proof and mould-proof, breeds true, won't cross with other wheat, and feeds nobody. Every virtue and the doom are one fact. It is the book's mirrored seed vault turned from a weapon into a gift, which is far crueller.
- **Nan's leaven.** Her own turned yeast, the mirror life the book warns about, carried into every kitchen as a kindness, and kept in check because it can live only on Mercy flour. This is the only pitch that turns the book's gravest warning into plot, and it stays consistent.
- **The deepest twist that comes from the book.** The town judges good bread by Nan's mouth, and Nan's mouth is turned. "Whoever holds the reference sample decides who counts as normal" becomes the story. It re-reads the tasting walk, the sainthood of 1891 (she never took sick because she can't), and every Saturday loaf.
- **Period instruments that do the book's work.** The polarimeter (a sugar turns light right or left), the torsion balance, and the tintype, which really does reverse what it shows. The 1880 tintype matching a 1911 print "mole and parting" is the forged-painting seed reborn, and better. Andrew's gloves, counted in two columns ("6 in 20"), are the cleanest picture of handedness in any pitch: a right glove comes back left.
- **Episode 2's misdirection.** Pryce tests the sick and "everyone turns the right way". The audience expects turned people; the turned thing is the bread.
- **The dance.** Strangers who can only weigh Nan learned her waltz, down to the stumble from a knee broken on the ice in '84. Nobody fakes a stumble.

### What it uses, and whether each item works
- **Load-bearing:** the object with no trail (the Stone); the mirrored seed vault (Mercy); mirror life (the leaven); looking-glass milk (Tam's origin, Marguerite); the grip that turns (Tam and Joseph); running out as spreading weight (Andrew); the lake that sloshes to their moon (the slough times the Stone); the fault-free quake and "nobody fakes a mountain" (the raised floor); contact in mass (the waltz); the reference sample (mouth against scale); the original called a forgery (the tintype); exile as a cure (the ending).
- **Working:** the dog (Kirsty and her crusts); jar lids, a mirror-hand ledger, dripping and whisky (Bel's case against Nan); knots undone (the rope, the cuffs); partial return (the fingertip); sending a hand (the gloves, Tam's arm); the amputee's seconds (Pask); blindfold training (the stock tank); the held breath (Pask); a drift that isn't a half (6 in 20, then 12 prints in 40); custody by pantry ("It was never the door"); the lost thief's wrong pull (Tam's jar of sea); sight inside the bubble ("It's all lamps inside").
- **Decoration:** SETI (the schoolmaster and Mars), the night the clocks stopped (a wonder), seed certificates (the epilogue), Nan's garden, and Major Hollis's plague-proof soldiers, who arrive late, make no move that matters, and leave with nothing.
- **Unused, rightly for 1911:** vaults, gold dust, courts, bullets, passports.

### Physics and ledger
The chemistry is right to a surprising degree: acid turns starch to sugar, the mirror sugar turns light left, Nan's blood sugar is mirror sugar because she lives on Mercy, and turned wheat grows well because air, water, light and minerals have no hand. Problems:
1. **The baby's heart.** In the dark Nan saw "her baby's heart like a lamp". But sight works only inside the bubble, and whatever is inside the bubble crosses with you. The baby would have crossed and turned with her, and her milk would have fed him, which breaks the looking-glass-milk thread. *Fix:* she sees something else inside the bubble: her own milk shining in her breast (a quiet plant for episode 5), or a lark's heart in the wheat.
2. **"A practised crosser slides unturned."** The book's fifth law says nobody can steer a turn. At B's own odds, seven crossings in ten come back unturned whoever makes them. The seed-bag tags say nothing about skill and cannot convict Tam. *Fix:* convict him by weight. Pryce's balance logged a body at the 4:12 tide that weighed exactly what Tam weighs: the book's counter-spy gravimeter, read on the season's honest instrument.
3. **The strongroom is out of range.** The Stone sets things down "within a few yards". A theft from a locked strongroom in town is impossible. *Fix:* say the strongroom is in the old granary beside the Stone (the tallied beam is already there), and have Rab move the seed there in episode 8 because he trusts the fence.
4. **A rule added in secret.** Tam stepped into the sea and came back; Pask scouts and returns; Nan goes and stays. The ledger never says a crosser can dip into the next leaf and come back while the field lasts, yet three set pieces rest on it. *Fix:* add it to the ledger and show it early. Tam, soaked to the knee in episode 1, is the right moment.
5. **Joseph's eleven tries, and the cure nobody tries for Nan.** At three in ten, failing eleven times running is a two-in-a-hundred fluke, so the tragedy becomes bad luck. Worse, it is the only evidence against the cheapest remedy for Nan: cross her back. *Fix:* four tries, ending when the Stone closes early and takes Tam's fingertip (which plants the fingertip). Then give Nan an on-screen choice: she is offered the dice and refuses, because she would rather go where her bread is bread than stay where she is cured. Without that beat, the audience asks "why not just cross her back?"
6. **A cloud still readable after nine years.** "Spreading at the same slow rate for years" is something Pryce, who has had the balance for weeks, cannot know. *Fix:* let the spreading rate date the cloud ("at this rate it began about ten years ago"), and add "a cloud spreads for years" to the ledger, since a twist rests on it.
7. **The kitchen clock.** A pendulum that skips to a dance next door is a far finer instrument than Pryce's balance. *Fix:* declare that under the old house the neighbours stand closer than anywhere else (which also says why the Stone came here), or make the skips Pryce's readings.

### Twists
- **Eps 3-4, the bread.** It re-reads every Saturday loaf. Widely planted (dead flour, hens, "no mouse", the Lee and Hnatiuk children, the dog, Bel's prints) and it survives the swap test: a poisoner would make the leaven and the polarimeter meaningless.
- **Eps 4-6, the turned mouth.** The strongest, but Tam says it outright at the end of episode 4 ("a turned ear tastes sweet only to a turned mouth"), so episode 6's blood test only confirms what the audience already holds. *Fix:* hold that line back to the blood result; end episode 4 on the pig test and the town's vote.
- **Andrew didn't desert.** Thinly planted before episode 6 (the 1902 cold open comes in episode 7). *Fix:* make Nan's "Morning" to the Stone, and her midnight talks in episode 2, plainly addressed to someone.
- **Ep 7, the raised floor.** Sound, and trusted because it cost so much.
- **Ep 9, Tam is the thief.** Unsound as written (see physics). **Andrew is the still weight:** the audience gets there first, by design.
- **Ep 10, the dancer is a stranger.** Planted three times, survives the swap test, and it is moving. It re-reads the clock thread rather than the whole season.

### Can it fill ten episodes?
Mostly. The famine mystery carries episodes 1-4, Nan's secret 5-6, the neighbours and Andrew 7-9, the weighing 10. But crossings are rare: the Stone is tied to the tide and only Tam uses it, so from episode 3 to episode 7 there is no crossing set piece at all, only cold opens. The middle is lab, kitchen and council. *Fix:* bring Bel's forty prints through the Stone forward to episode 5 (she needs the odds to understand Joseph anyway), and bring Hollis in by episode 4 with the Dominion reserve bid as an outside clock, so the famine threatens to go national before the fair.

### Verdict
The most original use of the book, and the one whose central twist is most truly made of the book's physics. It has more slips than A in places that carry weight, including one rule added in secret, and its middle runs on truths the audience holds early. Every fix is cheap. Worth doing if they are made.

## Pitch C: SLACK WATER (the West Highlands)

### Strengths
- **It teaches the audience to read a weight trace, then uses the lesson.** Episode 5 shows a true running-out: 147 kilograms, centred, spreading evenly for weeks. Episode 1's trace faded in forty minutes and drifted north-east. A cloud can only spread; a man can walk away. Then the ghost gains weight, and a cloud cannot eat. The memorial's brass traces have the same shape, so the first Lost walked away too, and the town's founding law, the Hand, was built on a misread line. That is the best end-of-season re-read in the three pitches.
- **The Hand is a lovely machine:** a rule made of grief, right for a reason nobody knew (the ferryman was heavier), undone by kindness (feeding the pilgrims). The Strengthening turning the ferrymen follows exactly.
- **Fair clues the audience sees before Jess:** ferrymen who never catch the flu (immune), the dog, the jar, 90 kilos down to 76.
- **"I never let go of him."** True: Callum let go of her.
- **The Long Crossing uses a detail most readers miss:** the outcome is fixed the moment you return, so extra seconds buy only the risk of running out.

### What it uses, and whether each item works
- **Load-bearing:** the lake that sloshes to their moon and their ocean at your address (the Tide); the fault-free quake (the world moves); bounded time and the weigh-plate (allowances); tingling fingertips (the Long Crossing kills Ewan); running out as spreading weight (the grammar of traces); the grip (the Hand, and the first reversal); the seventy-per-cent cure (the pilgrim economy); the lost thief's wrong pull (Del in 2019); the held breath (Jess's crossing); the mirror kitchen and custody by pantry (the Strengthening, the padlock); the lightest courier (the teenage recruits); immunity (the flu clue); first contact in mass (the pacing); the reference sample (the Night of Leaves); exile (Callum).
- **Working:** partial return (Del's leg, the ferryman's fingers); jar lids and the dog (Callum's tells); the anticlockwise watch (a turn and a slide feel the same); looking is a search (episode 1); the counter-spy gravimeter (Aditi's false trail); assassination by spin (Aditi's first theory); looking-glass milk (Shona: more grief than plot); sight inside the bubble (the heart, which is broken as written; see below).
- **Decoration:** the object with no trail, SETI (Dr Ansah), bullion on CCTV, wrapping paper and gold dust (Del's past), the amputee's eleven seconds and the eleven passport amendments (both wrong by C's own rules), blindfold tanks, whisky and dripping.
- **Unused:** the seed vault, soldiers, the painting, mirror script, dark matter, mergers, mirror life.

### Physics and ledger
The ledger is mostly honest, but the breaches sit inside clues, where they do the most harm.
1. **The heart on the right.** Del came as a mirrored pilgrim, and Callum is mirrored. The mirror is relative: to her eyes his heart sits where hers does, so she would see nothing odd. As written, the clue says he was straight. *Fix:* "His heart was on the same side as mine. And I'm the one who's turned." That is better, because the audience has to work the relativity out.
2. **A world of coin tosses.** "Everywhere else the odds are a coin toss", yet frames do surgery and pipe repair. Every working crosser would turn on half their jobs, and Britain would be full of turned surgeons eating from Chirality. *Fix:* declare that elsewhere a short crossing seldom turns you (one in twenty, say), and only Farrach at slack raises it to seven in ten. That also makes the Cure a real miracle, not a twenty-point edge over any clinic.
3. **Del's eleven crossings.** Before 2019 the pilgrim was always the lighter one, so each crossing cured her seven times in ten. Failing eleven times running is about one chance in six hundred thousand. And eleven passport amendments means she turned every time, which would leave her straight. *Fix:* make her a thief who keeps being cured and keeps going back to work: cured four times, turned again on jobs, passport amended nine times. That is the book's pariah, and it fits her flaw: she runs.
4. **Del's eleven extra seconds.** The allowance is "a few seconds" and a lower leg weighs about five kilos. *Fix:* "eleven tenths" (an accountant would count tenths), or drop the number.
5. **Where does Callum stand?** Their leaf is sea at the town, and Del stepped into black water. A man pacing three steps and a turn needs a floor at the Pool. *Fix:* one ledger line: the Pool sits on their shore, with the tideline passing under the Ferry House. That also explains why the Pool, of all places on the loch, cures.
6. **"Every gravimeter on Earth twitched."** The pull falls with the cube of the distance and is felt only nearly overhead, so a mountain moved at Farrach cannot twitch the world. *Fix:* keep it local (the Ferry House, and one gauge in Fort William), or make the world twitch a separate, bigger event next door.
7. **Mirror life.** Twenty thousand mirrored pilgrims a year for twenty-five years, and one loch. *Fix:* a line (the Ferry House's sealed drains), or a wonder (weed in the loch that nothing grazes).

### Twists
- **Ep 2, the watch runs anticlockwise.** Solid and planted.
- **Ep 4 (first reversal), the lighter one turns.** A strong re-read of the town's kindness, and it survives the swap test. But it rests on a new rule the audience meets only as a finding. *Fix:* shadow it earlier: the weigh-plate slip prints both weights; old ferrymen joke "never be the light one" at the Ferryman's Breakfast without knowing why. Also cut the episode's one-line pitch, "The Tide is fine. It's us." It has the exact shape of The Catch's reveal (the world didn't change; the people did), and the owner will hear it.
- **Ep 6, the tide is next door's sea.** The weakest. In a world that has had crossing frames since 1994 and knows the neighbours exist, a famous loch with a tide that matches nothing in the sky is the first place any physicist would look, and "it's next door's sea" would be on the tea towels. *Fix:* make it common lore from episode 1 (the Neighbours' Tide). The reversal becomes what nobody ever asked: the Cure's odds come from their sea, and a sea can move. The world changes by someone else's business, which is the book's point and the season's.
- **Ep 7, Callum let go on purpose.** Strong and planted.
- **Ep 10, Callum is alive next door.** The best, but episode 8's hook ("the ghost weighs three kilograms more than last week") hands the audience the answer two episodes early. *Fix:* end episode 8 on the north-east drift matching Isa's framed trace, and keep the weight gain for episode 10.

### Can it fill ten episodes?
The detective spine holds through episode 7, and every episode has either a crossing or a reading. Episodes 8 and 9 sag into institutions: a pantry, an audit, a church-hall meeting where Jess "lays out everything", which risks a lecture. *Fix:* fold the audit into episode 8, and give episode 9 a crossing: Jess and Del secretly cross a Pensioner the new way, with Del belted to be the heavier, and he tastes home. The town calls it luck, and the meeting says no in the face of a living result. That makes "one right person is not enough" something we see.

### The mint
The pilgrims' jars, the Night of Leaves, Shona's mother's mint: mint that tastes of caraway. The owner's two rejections were The Catch ("nothing has happened to the mint") and a first attempt called CARAWAY that turned on a mint test. *Fix:* use the town's Table card instead. Every household pins one by the door. On the Night, each household crosses its own card and chalks whether it came back in mirror print. Print shows a turn to anyone who didn't cross, so no tasting is needed, and the object the town trusted without a thought becomes the instrument. Del, whose Geneva job was betrayed by wrapping paper, is the one who suggests paper.

### Verdict
The best-built twist machine of the three. It sets up its own way of reading evidence and cashes it in at the end. But its physics breaks inside its clues (the heart, the coin-toss world, Del's crossings and seconds), its mid-season reversal is the thing any informed character would already assume, and it leans on mint and a Catch-shaped line. It needs more repair than A to stand, and the repairs are clear.

## Across the three
- **Two pitches end the same way.** B and C both close on the book's own suggested ending (exile is a cure), with a person next door answering through weight in rhythm: a waltz, and three steps and a turn. They cannot both go forward. And the owner wrote that ending; A is the only pitch that tests it and finds it wanting.
- **Mirror life:** only B uses it; A and C must at least account for it.
- **Rules first seen at their payoff:** A's drift that grows with time and C's "the lighter one turns" are declared in the ledgers but first shown when they pay off. Both are shadowed indirectly. Give each one clear early glimpse.
- **The best mind-bend in each, worth keeping whichever pitch goes ahead:** A's lying weighbridge; B's turned mouth as the town's measure of good; C's grammar of traces and the memorial that re-reads the town's founding law.
