# CARAWAY

*Season pitch, Angle A: the seed concept, tightened until each twist is hard to vary.*

**Logline.** When her twin is exiled for the mirror-change she secretly carries, a starving seventeen-year-old record-keeper finds that her walled city's most loving rite, giving its dead to the dark beside its crossing machine, is the thing turning and killing its children. Her brother can only come home if a city that has never asked "why" learns how to.

**Format.** Ten serialized episodes of 55 minutes. Major reversals fall in episodes 4, 6–7 and 10.

---

## 1. The world

**The normal.** Ream is a walled hill city of twelve thousand people, three hundred years old, built around the Lift: 212 bronze plates that lift a body off the world for a few seconds into the black between worlds. Ream has markets, weddings and debts. Its bank keeps its gold as dust in concrete, and nobody remembers why. Mirrors are contraband, because a mirror is "a small Turning." Ream does not bury its dead. It Gives them.

**The book.** Ream's scripture is the Leaves. Children learn it by leaf, line and word through a catechism game ("Leaf four, line three, word six?" "Mint!"). It holds the Seven Laws, the Theorems, and the Plot, a book of cases read as law and prophecy ("One comes back mirrored. The handhold is what did it."). Each verse carries a small coloured dot: green, amber or grey. The Order calls these "the three voices" and treats the amber *Caraway verses*, about the Lift, the stack of worlds and the Ending, as the holiest. The book closes with "Written for a phone screen." Funerals end with "May the animations respect your reduced motion." Nobody knows what an animation is. The book was hand-copied long ago from the First Leaf, a dead glass tablet now sealed in a block of stone.

**The rites.**
- **The Tithe.** At seventeen every citizen crosses once. Children go in pairs and may not touch. Each one first taps the right shoulder: "tap the stack at the top right."
- **The Mint Test.** On return each child tastes a leaf from the Sealed Jar, whose lid is unscrewed "against the sun" out of reverence. Mint means clean. Caraway means Turned: the body has come back as its own mirror image and can no longer use ordinary food.
- **The Passing, or the Ending.** The Turned are fasted light and sent on a long crossing to Minthome, "where the mint tastes right." The rite is joyful. Its name comes from the verse "For the ending only," and its refrain is "The ending writes itself."
- **The Giving.** The dead are laid in the Lift and its time is let run out. They "do not die so much as diffuse." The needles, Ream's weighing instruments, show them staying. This Weight of the Ancestors rises every year, and the rise is read as favour.
- **The Chant.** At every Passing and Giving the Order swings a twenty-tonne stone in pulses of two, three, five and seven. The needles answer two, three, five, seven: the Ancestors reply. Eleven is never sent, because the Plot says "The pariah crosses eleven times." In Nan's bedtime tales, Pariahs walk out of the Old Graves at night begging to be let in. They are Passed on sight, unheard.
- **The Tally.** A board in the Tally Hall records every crossing and every Turning since Year One (its first page is headed *Minthome, Year One*). Its third line, "Observed rate", has held a dash for three centuries. Writing a number there is forbidden: "The rate is the Ancestors'."

**Offices.** The **Randall** keeps the Leaves and the **Sundrum** keeps the Lift, and both their keys are needed to open its plates. **Accountants** work out each crosser's time budget from body weight. Their creed, the Method of Nolan, runs: "A premise, once paid for, pays back interest." Families take their names from the Leaves' list of sources: Abbott, Wells, Carroll, Plattner, Dvali, Adamala, and one poor clan called Others.

**The heresy.** A second book was sealed at the founding: the Fable. Its secret readers, the **Changers**, take Greek letters as names. Their creed is "What changes when things are changed?" They recite the Fable's formulas like spells. None of them has ever changed anything to find out.

**Why nobody questions.**
1. *The rites worked.* For two centuries the Giving comforted and nothing visibly went wrong. The Passing really did protect the city (see point 5).
2. *The book sounds equally sure everywhere.* The dots that once told proven verses from guesses have become ornament.
3. *The one instrument averages the change away.* The first century's thousands of work-crossings swamp the grand totals. Divided, they give a comfortable 7 in 100. The Randall worked it out once, at twenty-five, and never again.
4. *Every bad sign has a home in the doctrine.* Children lost in the dark were called by the Ancestors. More weight means favour. A new number is a new command. A story that absorbs any outcome can never be caught out.
5. *Memory says questioning kills.* Forty-five years ago a girl hid her Turned brother instead of Passing him. A grey mould that nothing would eat spread through Carroll Row. The street was burned and seven people died. Burnt Row is still black.

**The show's visual rule.** Everything written in Ream appears on screen as mirror-image English, and the audience accepts it as strangeness. Only four things read the way we read: the numbers stamped on the Lift's plates ("the Ancestors' hand"), the Sealed Jar's embossed base, the First Leaf's screen, and the signs in Minthome. Once Nell is Turned, she sees Ream the way the audience always has: backwards.

## 2. The doom engine, step by step

1. **The rite begins.** In Year 131 a fever killed people faster than the Old Graves by the river could take them. The Randall of the day read "A crosser who runs out doesn't die so much as diffuse. The gravimeter in the empty room still registers seventy kilograms," and gave the dead to the Lift. The needles showed them still there, and the fever passed. The rite was "proven" by a history in which the door was still light.
2. **The dead stay put** (ledger item 1). They hang just off the world beside the door as a cloud of bare atomic cores. After three centuries the cloud weighs about two thousand tonnes.
3. **Nearness is everything.** Between worlds, pull fades with the *cube* of distance (Law Seven, a green verse). A cloud sitting at the door outweighs everything a crosser can feel, including the tug of home.
4. **The cloud twists and calls** (ledger item 2). Its lopsided pull turns bodies over, so more children come back Turned. It also out-pulls home, so some children never land. These are the Called, and each one adds weight.
5. **The cure feeds the disease.** More Turned means more Passings, the longest crossing, straight past the cloud. Many of the Passed are Lost, and each adds weight. A Passing is also a coin toss: some of the Passed are flipped back in transit and arrive in a world whose food their bodies can't use. Ream never learns this, because it sends back anyone who returns.
6. **Every answer the city owns makes it worse.**
   - *The Fast* (episode 2). The Accountants reason that the Called were too heavy to get home before their time ran out, and a lighter body gets more time. But a crossing lasts exactly as long as its time budget, so more time means longer beside the dead. More children come back Turned and more are Called.
   - *The Joining* (episode 4). When the needles "speak" a new number, thirteen elders give themselves early to the Ancestors. That adds more weight.
   - *The Great Tithe* (episode 7). The crowd demands that everyone cross and "let the coin sort us." That would mean twelve thousand crossings past the cloud. Nell's sums show a third of the city lost or exiled, the weight doubled, and every child of the next generation Turned.
7. **Why no adjustment can work.** Every rule Ream owns adds weight, or adds time spent beside it. The fault is not how hard the city prays. It is where the door stands and what the city has put next to it. You cannot fast away two thousand tonnes.
8. **What can.** A new explanation: the Ancestors are a thing with a place and a weight, so distance is the cure. Then three acts the rules forbid: stop the Giving, move the door a kilometre, and test with paper cards instead of children. And a public board where anyone may write a number and a prediction.

**Theme, carried by the mechanism.** A culture that never questions keeps doing what worked, and what worked changes the world it works on. There is no villain, only love, grief, prudence and scripture read as command. "Always" is earned three times over. Earth fenced and rationed its Turned instead of asking how to feed them. Ream exiled its Turned and Gave its dead. Minthome, founded by Ream's exiles, took the Giving with it.

## 3. Main characters

**NELL ABBOTT (17), apprentice Tallykeeper.**
- *Want:* bring Wells home alive; later, make the city able to count.
- *Wound:* the three seconds at the Mint Test when she said "mint" and let him go.
- *Flaw:* she hides. Her lie grows into a way of life (secret food, secret tests, secret truths), which is the Randall's way.
- *Levers:* numbers, which she counts under her breath when afraid; a body that can't eat, read or bless the right way round; our view of Ream through her reversed eyes; a stolen shard of mirror; tallow candles eaten in the dark.
- *Arc:* from liar to secret experimenter, to a public truth-teller who causes a disaster, to the builder of a practice a city can use, to a girl who refuses to be its new Randall.

**WELLS ABBOTT (17), her twin.**
- *Want:* to meet his Ending with grace, then to live, then to get word home.
- *Flaw:* faith. Nell always did the questioning for both of them.
- *Levers:* decency, humour, strong hands, and their childhood counting game, in which Nell always went past seven to "eleven!" and he shushed her.
- *Arc:* starving in Minthome, he asks his first question. The surprise that stays in character: at the Mint Test he already knew Nell had Turned, and he mouthed "Me" to the Randall.

**THE RANDALL (Iris Carroll, 65), keeper of the Leaves.**
- *Want:* to keep Ream calm and whole.
- *Wound:* at twenty, as a Changer called Sigma, she hid her Turned brother Kit, and Carroll Row burned. At twenty-five Kit walked out of the Old Graves shouting "Move the door!" and she had him Passed before he could say why. She has carried that sentence without its reasons for forty years.
- *Flaw:* she believes mercy means certainty, and closes questions to spare people pain.
- *Right about:* hidden Turned people really can breed blight, and truth without a plan really does kill (episode 7).
- *Levers:* competence, real tenderness, a secret glasshouse where she grows the wrong-handed seeds Kit carried back, and her love for Nell as her successor.
- *Arc:* the keeper of the dash writes the number in her own hand. At the climax she says what she could not say for Kit: "Let him speak."

**HESTER ABBOTT (44), their mother, an Accountant.**
- *Want:* to keep what is left of her family.
- *Wound:* her husband Given, her son Passed, her mother-in-law Joined.
- *Flaw:* silence.
- *Arc:* she wrote the Fast's weight tables and learns they kill. She stands silent in the crowd while her daughter is exposed, the way Nell once stayed silent. She breaks that silence by working out the one number that brings her son home: his ballast.

**TAU (Tobin Dvali, 19), Tally clerk and Changer.**
- *Want:* to know why his sister Mira was Called.
- *Flaw:* he wants a villain.
- *Arc:* he moves from conspiracy theory to evidence, breaks with his grandmother, leads the Changers in carrying the plates, and stays to keep the public board. Warm and funny, he loves Nell in a way neither can say aloud while she hides.

**KIT CARROLL (62), in Minthome.**
- The Randall's brother, Passed twice.
- He keeps Minthome's own tally of arrivals, including the "Straights," who arrive un-Turned and starve.
- The only person who has crossed through both doors and noticed the difference.

**Supporting cast.**
- **Theta (Old Mother Dvali):** Needle-Warden and head of the Changers. She is sure the Order fakes the needles because she once saw it happen. She is honest to instruments and dishonest to people.
- **The Sundrum (Jonah Adamala):** his daughter is on the Great Tithe list, and his yearly Litany of Plates turns out to be an assembly manual.
- **Nan:** the twins' grandmother, who tells Pariah tales and later Joins.
- **Mira Dvali:** Called in the first minute.
- **Plattner, the dog:** he growls at Turned Nell. By episode 8 he has learned her new smell and lies down beside her again: a small change a creature can adjust to, unlike the city's.

## 4. The season

**1 · "The Tithe."** *On crossing day, twins break the rule about holding hands, and one of them comes back wrong.*
- **Cold open:** Mira taps her shoulder, steps into the Lift and doesn't return. A Needle-Warden calls out "Sixty-one" as her weight registers. The crowd sighs: "Favour." Nell writes her first Tally mark: CALLED.
- **Acts:**
  1. Ream through its rite: the catechism; the dotted verses; the Plot taught as law; plate numbers that are the only print we can read; the Temple's sealed glasshouse; blackened Burnt Row; Nan's Pariahs coming from the Graves.
  2. The crossing. In the black, the lantern-light around each twin lets them see inside each other. Nell panics, and Wells takes her hand.
  3. The Mint Test. Nell tastes caraway and says "mint." Wells says "mint." "One comes back mirrored": one of them is lying. Wells mouths something to the Randall that we can't hear. She chooses Wells.
  4. The joyful Passing and the chant. The needles answer. Nell says nothing. At home Plattner growls at her, and she taps her mother's *left* shoulder.
- **Twist:** the wrong twin is exiled, by a verse the audience has just learned to trust.
- **Hook:** darkness, concrete, a hand on Wells' face. A voice: "Taste." A mint leaf on his tongue. His face says caraway.

**2 · "Observed Rate."** *Nell must hide a body that can't eat, read or bless the right way, and her numbers say the city is running out of time.*
- **Cold open:** Nell at night, eating a tallow candle, retching, eating again.
- **Acts:**
  1. She reads the Tally through a shard of mirror and fakes her own handwriting.
  2. The Fast is declared, built on Hester's tables. Hester notices the wrong-shoulder blessing and says nothing.
  3. Doing the year-end sums, Nell asks a new question: not what the rate is, but what it was *each year*. It was flat for a century, then it climbed, then it climbed faster.
  4. In Minthome, Wells lies in the Long House among the Straights. Kit tells him he has six months.
- **Twist:** the Turning rate has doubled in her lifetime. Within three Tithes it will pass one in two, the "fair coin."
- **Hook:** a cloth bundle on her desk: bread that tastes right. Someone knows what she is.

**3 · "Two, Three, Five, Seven."** *The heretics who share her secret believe the Order fakes the voice of the dead, so Nell designs a test no liar could pass.*
- **Cold open:** Tau catches Nell reading with a mirror, and hands her a better one.
- **Acts:**
  1. The Changers' cellar, where the Fable is chanted like a spell.
  2. Theta swears the Order moves hidden weights to fake the Answering; she saw a reading falsified in the Troubled Years, when the needles once answered wrongly. The audience half-believes her.
  3. Nell's design: send eleven, the one number the Order never sends. If the Order fakes the answers, eleven gets none. Any reply means someone outside Ream; any reply that is not an echo means someone thinking.
  4. At night Nell and Tau heave the stone eleven times while Theta watches the needles. In Minthome, Wells has joined the stone-haulers who answer every chant.
- **Twist:** the Changers' creed is a question none of them has ever asked of anything.
- **Hook:** at 3 a.m. the needle trembles thirteen times.

**4 · "Thirteen."** *Major reversal 1. Nell has proof someone is listening, and the city turns her proof into a reason to die.*
- **Cold open:** Minthome, the same night. Wells recognises their game, refuses to send the echo, and hauls thirteen alone until his palms split. The others join him at nine.
- **Acts:**
  1. Nobody in Ream moved the stone. The voice is real and the conspiracy theory dies.
  2. Theta won't accept it ("They're answering you to trap you"). She left the eleven out of her log to protect Tau.
  3. So the Order sees thirteen from nowhere and proclaims a new number from the Ancestors. Thirteen elders volunteer to Join them, Nan among them, serene, funny and sure. Nell can't stop her without confessing.
  4. Nan is Given. The needles rise.
- **Twist:** evidence with no explanation only feeds the old story. Nell's test has added thirteen bodies to the weight.
- **Hook:** the next bundle of bread, with the Randall waiting beside it. "Eat. And close the Tally."

**5 · "Custody by Pantry."** *Her food now costs silence, while a test with paper shows the city's own Fast is killing children.*
- **Cold open:** the Randall's sealed glasshouse, full of wrong-handed mint, wheat and beans. Every scrap of its waste is burned.
- **Acts:**
  1. Nell accepts the Randall's terms in public.
  2. In secret, with Tau and a Lift clerk, she sends crates of cards through the Lift in the maintenance hours. Each card is printed with an R. Heavy crates bring back a few Я, light crates bring back many. More time in the dark means more turning, and the Fast is Hester's work.
  3. A grey film spreads on the Abbott privy stones, and nothing will eat it. The Randall's rule, "burn what leaves your body," was never explained, so Nell never kept it. Hester burns half the house. The Randall covers it up as damp rot.
  4. Nell asks the Randall how she knew exactly what to do.
- **Twist:** the Randall is Sigma, the girl who hid Kit. Burnt Row was hers.
- **Hook:** the needles bring numbers in threes, as leaf, line and word, the catechism game. Nell decodes WELLS. ALIVE. STARVING.

**6 · "The First Leaf."** *Major reversal 2, part one. To read the holy relic, the city needs a Turned girl's eyes.*
- **Cold open:** the relic in its block of stone, a "wall of weight" that a crossing hand passes straight through.
- **Acts:**
  1. The block stands at the Lift's foot. In the maintenance hours Nell "sends a hand, not a body" (a Plot line), and her unpinned hand lifts the relic out of solid stone.
  2. Powered at the Lift's port, it unlocks to the chant, 2-3-5-7: the password had become a prayer.
  3. Its text looks reversed to Tau but plain to Nell, and to us. Green: *Established, real physics.* Caraway: *Stipulated, the film's own axiom, where the science runs out.* Grey: *Derived.* The holiest verses are the guesses. "For the ending only" was a note on ending a story. "In the plot" means "in the story," so the hand rule is only a scene.
  4. A slider reads "Field drift 50%": the city's fair coin is somebody's setting. Nell taps "Cross twenty times" and the relic fills in its own observed rate. Then its power dies.
- **Twist:** the law that exiled Wells was a moment in a story. The audience, who learned it as law in episode 1, made the city's mistake.
- **Hook:** the Randall, not the guards, finds Nell. "It wasn't the rule. He asked me. He said 'Me.'"

**7 · "The Weight of the Ancestors."** *Major reversal 2, part two. Nell understands what is killing the children and tells the whole city at once.*
- **Cold open:** Nell carries a needle out of the Temple street by street, though needles must never be moved. The reading fades with the cube of the distance. The Ancestors are not everywhere: they are one thing, in one place, at the door.
- **Acts:**
  1. The pieces click. The Year of the Diffusion. The curve that stayed flat until the weight passed a threshold. The cards. The Plot's line "the grip turns them," because a pull is a grip. Her father is part of that weight. Hester will not hear it.
  2. At the Autumn Giving, Nell climbs the Lift's steps and says it: the Ancestors are twisting and calling our children. She has no plan, only the truth.
  3. Panic, and a crush at the gates: eleven dead. Theta has sold Nell to the First Accountant to save Tau, so a taster is ready. Nell tastes caraway in public, and her words turn from evidence into a curse. Hester stays silent.
  4. The Randall was right. The crowd demands the Great Tithe.
- **Twist:** the most loving rite in Ream is the doom engine, and saying so alone made things worse.
- **Hook:** the Great Tithe is set for twenty days' time, and Nell will be Passed first. In Minthome, Wells collapses.

**8 · "Pariah."** *Starved in a cell, Nell finds the way out hidden in three centuries of numbers.*
- **Cold open:** forty years ago, a young man walks out of the Old Graves shouting "Move the door!" His sister has him taken.
- **Acts:**
  1. The Randall confesses in Nell's cell.
  2. In Minthome, Wells is carried outside for the first time. There are ruins, a fence, a field of wild mint, signs that we can read and he can't ("CHIRAL HOLD · PANTRY 3"), and "the Mother": the station's own Lift, where Minthome gives its dead.
  3. Nell has Tau check the records. Every Pariah appeared at the Old Graves. Year 9 has forty days with no crossings. The plates' "Ancestors' hand" is an assembly count: 1 of 212.
  4. Plattner, smuggled in by Tau, lies down beside her.
- **Twist:** the Lift has been carried before. Minthome's door stands directly beneath Ream's Old Graves: a clean door, a kilometre from the dead.
- **Hook:** the needles spell MOVE. THE. DOOR., the Randall's forty-year-old sentence, arriving by the one channel nobody can fake. Then WELLS. Then seven single pulses: seven days.

**9 · "The Litany of Plates."** *To save one boy and a city, a hundred people must steal God in a night.*
- **Cold open:** the hardliners rescue the doctrine: "The Ancestors weight the coin as they will." The Randall: "We said fifty. A failed answer stays failed."
- **Acts:**
  1. At night the Randall writes the rate on the board in her own hand, frees Nell and opens her glasshouse. Nell eats.
  2. The Sundrum, with a daughter on the Great Tithe list, hears the assembly order in his own Litany of Plates. The two keys turn.
  3. Nell brings Hester a problem only an Accountant can solve: enough ballast to shorten Wells' time in the dark without falling short of the world. Hester works out her son's weight.
  4. The phone's gallery holds a twenty-second clip, *Day One*: starving people on these very hills, tasting mint and weeping. "Minthome. It tastes right."
  5. That night, Tallykeepers, Changers, families of the Called and the Sundrum's crew carry 212 plates and the sun-panels out through the North Gate. Theta watches the Lift's weight walk away on her needles and rings no bell. Nell tells Tau she won't stay.
- **Twist:** Minthome was Ream's first name. The founders were Turned exiles from the place Ream now sends its children: Earth, one world over.
- **Hook:** dawn. The bells of the Great Tithe ring over an empty plinth.

**10 · "For the Ending Only."** *Major reversal 3. Will a new answer become a new scripture?*
- **Cold open:** the crowd floods to the Old Graves. The Lift stands among the graves. The Tally board is nailed to the gate: PREDICTED, NEW DOOR: FEWER THAN 1 IN 20. IF MORE, I AM WRONG. N. ABBOTT.
- **Acts:**
  1. Cards go first. The last crate through the old door brought back 38 reversed cards in 100. Here, 4. Children sort the piles.
  2. The crowd calls it a miracle. They want the Great Tithe held here, and Nell as their Randall. She refuses and hands the cards to the children to work out the rate themselves.
  3. But 4 is twice Year One's rate of 2. Something small sits at this door too: Minthome's Givings at the Mother.
  4. The countdown comes in mass: two, three, five, seven. The twins cross from opposite worlds. In the black their lights touch, and she sees inside him: his heart, his empty stomach. She mouths "Eleven." He mouths "Thirteen." They don't hold hands.
  5. Wells lands among the graves. Someone shouts "Pariah!" The Randall answers: "Let him speak." The Jar tastes of mint: he is home. Nell lands in Minthome, where the mint tastes right, and Kit is waiting.
- **Twist:** the promised land has already begun the same rite.
- **Final scene:** a procession carries a body toward the Mother. Nell steps into its path. "Wait." Black.

## 7. The ending, and what it settles

- **Settled:** Wells is home and fed. Nell is fed where the food fits her body. The cause is named and repaired: the Giving has stopped, the door has moved, and the dash has become a number with a prediction beside it. The Passing is over as exile, and crossing becomes a choice. The Randall's past, the founders' origin and the meaning of the dots are all revealed.
- **The cost:** Mira, Nan, the eleven dead in the crush, and every Straight who starved. The Temple can never be used again: the Ancestors will hang at the old door as long as there is a Ream, and in the empty room the needle still registers them. The twins live in different worlds.
- **Left open:** Minthome's Givings, Earth beyond the fence, and whether Ream keeps counting once the fear fades.
- **The theme as the world's answer.** The world asks what becomes of a people who only do what used to work. What worked changes the world, and the rules can't see the change. One right person fails in secret, as the Randall's garden and Kit's sentence both did. What saves Ream is a practice: cards instead of children, a public board, and anyone willing to say "Wait."

## 8. The show's ledger

**Stipulated** (stated once, never added to):
1. The Lost stay close to where they were lost, and drift only slowly.
2. Mass hanging in the dark near a door twists crossers passing it and pulls them off course, more strongly the heavier and nearer it is and the longer they are exposed.
3. The First Leaf's memory survived three centuries.
4. Two crossers in the dark at the same moment can pass through each other's light without either one taking the other along.

**Taken from the Leaves as true in this world:** the seven Laws and their four stipulations, including that the neighbouring world's life has the opposite hand.

**In the Leaves but false in this world:**
- "One comes back mirrored" as a law. It is a scene from a story.
- "Field drift 50%." It is a slider's starting position.

**Derived by the show:**
- Lighter crossers stay out longer, so they are exposed longer.
- Moving the door about thirty times farther away cuts the pull about twenty-seven thousand times.
- The founders were Turned, so Ream writes in mirror image and only the Turned can read Earth's print.
- The Turned can live in Ream on wrong-handed crops.
