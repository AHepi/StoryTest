# Episode 2, "The Assayer's Wife": critique of the draft

Checked against `70-plan-v3.md` (all of it), `00-brief-2.md`, `05-owner-feedback-2.md` and `../the-catch-notes.md` ("The short answer", faults 1-4). No final episodes exist yet in `work/new`, so continuity is checked against the plan and, where the two touch, against the episode 1 draft (`80-episode-01-draft.md`) and its critique (`81-episode-01-critique.md`).

## Verdict

A warm, confident draft that hits every beat the plan gives episode 2, in order, with the right numbers. The Twitch, the ticket, 43.1 cheered, Peg's plan, the three-second limit, Danny's pitch and the tripled order book, seven hops with seven glances, Terry's line, the roll "joke", the cold, Con, the shed across the river, the plane, the clinic, the sign, the hand, the poster, Osei, Ray, the price and the van: all there. The dialogue is often very good, and the world feels ordinary and working. No banned word reaches the screen, and nothing from The Catch appears except one staging risk (finding 4).

The main problems:
- **The draft spends episode 3's reversal early**, in two ways. The family agrees aloud that the painting turned on Rosie's trip, which drops Peg's copy theory. And the camera and the lines keep pointing at Rosie, on top of an episode 1 draft that already does the same.
- **One lit display** breaks a world rule that episode 6 and the plan's main safeguard against The Catch depend on.
- **The Marlows handle hot goods carelessly**, and the hop sequence has no danger a first-time viewer can feel.

The episode does end higher than it began. Ray's "I come every spring. To see if it's come down." / "Not yet." shows Rosie her own future, and the next thing she does is go after a jeweller's van. That cause and effect is the best structural choice in the draft. Keep it.

**Protect these:**
- Terry's "Somebody next door's dropped the good china."
- Peg's "Bathroom scale's a liar. On the bridge." The bridge is the one that will lie.
- Peg's "Not on my bridge... Do it by the stove." It keeps the minus reading off screen until episode 5.
- Danny's "A wall's a tripwire... A floor's just there, being thick." And "Two floors to get through, not one. That's a long time to be nowhere": a fair, buried plant for episode 7.
- Liam praised for his head, with his ears going pink.
- "The black never gave me back." / "It's a picture, Tel." / "I know, love." One line means two things to two people.
- "Bloody thing's facing home better than we do."
- Kieran asking "Straight?" man to man.
- Mick's ticket shown the right way round. The camera stays on the straight side.
- Terry's "That's the list, love. That's all of it."
- Tess's "Nan tells it wrong. She says I was proud." / "I was hungry." It is followed at once by the Ahern shed cheering a thin boy.
- Ray's offered cracker, "So I hear", and "Not yet."
- Osei's "seven times in ten, he goes home and eats his tea."

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. Act one agrees the painting turned on Rosie's trip, which drops the copy theory the plan keeps until episode 3
**Scene:** 4.

**Problem:** Peg says "Copy, real, I don't care which", which is fine. But when Danny asks how you turn a picture round, she answers "Same way it went. Hop it." Tess adds "It went wrong on one trip." Both lines say the canvas turned during Rosie's Mayfair crossing. The episode 1 draft taught the audience, with the framed cuttings, the burned tissue and Danny's "Means we came back straight", that what a thief carries shows whether the thief turned. Once the family says on screen that the canvas turned on Rosie's trip, the audience has both halves of episode 3's answer.

The plan keeps two theories open until episode 3. In section 9, twist 3's "what the audience knew before" is "Peg's 'copy' against Ray's 'original'". The episode 1 gaps list "whether the painting is real". In the knowledge map, the audience learns "the painting turned, so its carrier did" in 3. (The plan's own phrase for this episode, "hop it until it turns back", invites the draft's reading. The twist table and the knowledge map should win.)

The new family belief, "A picture doesn't [face home]... You hold it loose, you let it roll about", is a good idea. It explains why a family that doesn't yet know the cargo rule would hop a picture without fearing for Rosie. But the action line calls it lore "as old as the rigs", and that clashes with the episode 1 draft's "Means we came back straight" about the wrapping. And Terry refusing to laugh at it (then humming about going home) marks it as wrong for everyone watching.

**Why it matters:** The first major reversal is the season's first big re-read. If the family agrees in act one that the painting turned on Rosie's trip, episode 3 plays as the family catching up, the exact fault version 3 of the plan was revised to remove (section 15, change 12).

**Fix:**
- Keep Peg on the copy theory. Danny: "How do you turn a picture round?" Peg: "Bank hung a copy the wrong way round. Buyer wants it the right way round. Hop it till it turns."
- Change Tess's line to one that doesn't say when it turned: "Could take a hundred goes."
- Keep the "nothing to face with / hold it loose" belief, but as Peg's own saying, made up at the table because she needs a sale. The table accepts it because Peg said it. Cut "as old as the rigs". Ray breaks the belief in episode 3 ("so did whoever carried it") and Tess corrects Danny with it in episode 4, as the draft's continuity note says.
- Let Terry laugh with the rest, a beat late, and cut the song (finding 11).
- Ask the episode 1 writer to change Danny's gloss on the cuttings from "Means we came back straight" to something that doesn't state the cargo rule, such as "Means we're tidy."
- Tell the plan's owner that the family now holds this belief, so the episode 3 and 4 writers honour it.

### 2. Rosie is pointed at far more than the plan's tells allow
**Scenes:** 9, 11, 14, 16, 17, 18.

**Problem:** The plan gives episode 2 these tells: the seven glances, the roll "joke", 43.1 cheered, the cure priced "for Uncle Terry", Terry's line, and the dog. The draft adds many more, several of them nearly confessions:
- On the step, Rosie asks "You ever come back wrong? When it was you." (14)
- To the painting: "Just you and me, then." (17)
- She re-pins the catalogue cutting "straighter". (17)
- "I'm a new woman every three seconds." "Straighter than you." "She likes it that way." (16)
- Terry is "not looking at anyone, least of all Rosie... Rosie looks at him. He doesn't look back." (9)
- She refuses the soup ("Making weight, me"), with the action line "hungry, and not for food". (11)
- She turns her head on the escalator to keep looking at IMMUNE. FED. (18)
- There are extra looks at the cutting in scenes 9 and 17, which blur the plan's "seven glances".

Worst of all, the clinic call is made alone, at night, in the dark office. The plan says the visit is covered by a promise "and we believe her: we heard her promise it." A secret call in the dark tells the audience it isn't for Terry.

**Why it matters:** Episodes 1-2 are meant to keep the audience "level with the family, with a few clues ahead for sharp viewers" (section 10). The episode 1 draft already over-points (see finding 1 of that episode's critique). Stacked on it, a first-time viewer finishes episode 2 sure that Rosie has turned, and the third episode's twist and hook lose their force.

**Fix:** Keep the plan's tells and cut the rest.
- **Step (14).** Rosie asks "Were you scared? First time?" Tess's "I was hungry" still lands, and becomes the scene's only turn.
- **Scene 17.** Cut "Just you and me, then" and the re-pinning. Move the call to daylight, made in front of Terry as the birthday joke: "Pricing your present, Uncle Terry." "Buy me two." The audience believes her, as the plan wants. On re-watch, Terry's silence after the joke is the tell.
- **Hops (16).** Cut "new woman every three seconds" and "Straighter than you". Keep "Come on. Come round." (it reads as care for the painting) and "She likes it that way", as one joke only.
- **Terry (9).** He says his line to the pan, and nobody exchanges looks.
- **Food.** Keep one refusal: Ray's cracker, which does two jobs. In scene 11 Kieran doesn't offer the soup, and "hungry, and not for food" goes.
- **Poster (18).** The camera sees it and Rosie doesn't linger (see finding 14 for where to put it).
- **Glances.** Exactly seven looks at the cutting, one per hop.

### 3. Liam watches the little bridge's display at night, which the world's rules forbid
**Scene:** 8, "Late Wednesday, the empty office".

**Problem:** "The display climbs. He watches it and steps off quickly, before the reading can settle." The plan (section 3.1): "at night their displays are dark and they only print." The episode 1 draft says so twice.

**Why it matters:** Two later things rest on that dark display.
- In episode 6, Danny's stakeout is "played on the wall clock, blind... there is nothing to watch but the clock."
- Section 14's rule against The Catch says: "No instrument showing an unseen visitor live, anywhere: every such reading is found on the roll afterwards." The 02:04 minus reading in episode 4 is found only as a ticket for the same reason.

Once viewers have seen the display lit at night, they will ask in episode 6 why Danny doesn't simply watch it.

**Fix:** Put Liam on the bathroom scale Rosie used in the cold open. It shows a number, prints nothing and leaves no roll. Two cousins on the same scale in the dark rhyme without anyone saying so. (Keep it as wanting to know his number, not dieting: see finding 8.) In scene 13, have Kieran tear off the ticket and read "Twenty point nought nought" from it, so the rule is kept in plain sight.

### 4. The cold open stages the Twitch as a detector going off in an empty room, live, in front of a watcher
**Scene:** 1.

**Problem:** A lone guard watches a monitor of an empty vault. The weigh-wall panel is in shot and the beads pour down. His line is "There's nobody in there. There is nobody in there." The plan allows the walls to flood (episode 2's cold open; section 15, change 26: "shown through the City's walls flooding... never as a live reading"). But this staging is The Catch's needle climbing in an empty room (the notes, fault 1, row 1): an instrument that detects unseen visitors, reacting live to something nobody can see, while a watcher insists no one is there. It also misleads, because the Twitch is a ripple from next door, not a visitor.

**Why it matters:** The owner's first rule is no echo of The Catch's devices. The scene is otherwise strong, so the risk comes only from the staging, and costs nothing to remove.

**Fix:**
- Play it as a City-wide event, not a haunted room. The guard hears the roar through the door, runs up the stairs into Cheapside, and in every doorway down the street stands another guard with a radio, all talking at once. The roar comes up through the pavement from under every building, and a night bus stops.
- Cut the monitor and the "nobody in there" line.
- Keep the hundred-basement montage as the aftermath: beads settling "like rain on a shed roof", grey dust in torchlight. The flood is then something the City suffered, not something one man watched live.

### 5. The Marlows handle a stolen painting like amateurs, and the hops carry no danger a viewer can feel
**Scenes:** 4, 6, 7-10.

**Problem:**
- **Witnesses.** At breakfast the stolen canvas lies on the table beside the catalogue cutting (headed STOLEN in episode 1). The family plans to sell it and to cross with it, with Mick and another driver at the counter. Mick "suddenly takes a great interest in the racing", which plays it as a joke. The episode 1 critique (finding 4) flagged the same fault.
- **No fear of Ray.** Three days earlier Ray proved to Peg's face that this canvas is the stolen original. Yet it lies on the dresser under a tea towel for three days, nobody watches the lane, and nobody mentions him as a danger.
- **Flat hops.** Rosie's danger is hidden by design, so for a first-time viewer act two is seven identical returns with nothing at stake but Friday.

**Why it matters:** The Crime lead promises a master pursuer and "deception on both sides". A family this careless with hot goods makes Ray look slow and the Marlows look lucky. The plan's formula for suspense (care x threat x uncertainty x time) needs a felt threat in the hop sequence that doesn't give Rosie away.

**Fix:**
- Hold scene 4 before the gate opens, like the hops at 05:30. Or let the hatch slide open for Mick, the table drop at once into talk of the Cheapside pour while the canvas goes under the tea towel, and the plan resume when the hatch shuts. That one beat shows the family's double life.
- Keep the painting in the safe between hops. Danny's hand fetches it through the door and posts it back each time. (Episode 1 already shows the sent hand; here it becomes routine, not a new plant.)
- Give one hop a false alarm. At 05:30, before the gate opens, the printer chatters in the middle of a hop: something has settled on the big bridge. Everyone freezes, Tess drops a tea towel over the empty place by the stove, Rosie lands, and it's Mick, early. It costs nothing, uses the self-printing scale, and makes the audience feel the police without learning anything about Rosie.

### 6. Nothing explains why there are only seven hops in three days
**Scenes:** 4, 7-16.

**Problem:** The family believes each hop is a coin toss ("Coin's got to come up sometime"). Tess says it "might take a hundred". Peg expects "forty tickets". The buyer leaves Friday, and a three-second hop costs a crosser with eleven seconds of budget nothing the family can see. Yet Rosie hops about twice a day, and nobody says why.

**Why it matters:** Any viewer will ask why they don't hop every ten minutes. The number seven is canonical, so the draft needs a brake the audience accepts.

**Fix:** Give Tess a second number to go with the first: "Three seconds. And a meal between every one." It spaces the hops at the times the draft already uses (05:30, 12:30, 22:00...), and it is Tess's character exactly: a tight number, lovingly meant. On re-watch it hurts, because the meals do nothing.

### 7. Osei puts a clinician in transit "the whole way", which the drift table makes impossible
**Scene:** 20.

**Problem:** "A clinician is with him in transit the whole way. Near him. Never touching." Restoration is a 10.0-second transit (episode 6). By the drift table, anyone who spends ten seconds in the black comes back turned seven times in ten, staff included. Obverse's clinicians would be turning at almost every treatment.

**Why it matters:** In episode 6 the audience learns the table. Sharp viewers will then see that Obverse staff would be turning constantly, and that such a clinic could never have kept the drift a secret. The plan's sign ("never touch staff in transit") already implies staff in the black, and the draft's line makes that explicit.

**Fix:** Osei: "We look in on him. A second or two at a time. Nobody touches him." A two-second look is safe (none ever recorded at two seconds). It explains the sign, since a patient could still grab a nurse who looks in, and it becomes a fair plant for episode 6: Obverse keeps its own crossings short because it knows. Tell the plan's owner, since the sign implies it.

### 8. Liam starts starving himself in episode 2, before Rosie teaches him to
**Scene:** 4 (and 8).

**Problem:** Sixpence is "waiting on Liam", and after Peg's joke "Sixpence gets his bacon". That is the plan's episode 4 beat ("At breakfast Liam slips his fry-up to Sixpence under the table"), used two episodes early. With the secret weighing it becomes a diet already under way.

**Why it matters:** The plan builds Rosie's guilt on her coaching Liam in episode 4: "Skip the fry-up. Sleep in your coat. She is passing on her own wound, and she knows it." If Liam started on his own, her guilt in episode 5 ("Rosie cannot look at Danny") is weaker. Episode 4 also loses its beat.

**Fix:** Keep the hurt and the want, and drop the diet. After Peg's "Not with that breakfast in you, love", Liam laughs a beat late and eats every bite, a little defiant. That also rhymes with the fry-up he eats in episode 5. The bathroom scale (finding 3) shows he wants to know his number. Sixpence waits on him because he's a soft touch, and gets nothing today.

### 9. "He's not fussed, Peg. He never is." gives away Ray's charity
**Scene:** 4.

**Problem:** Peg pays the Laundry short, and Dilys says the supplier doesn't mind and never has. The plan's Ray "charges the black-market price so that Peg won't smell charity." A dealer who never minds being paid late is charity, and Peg's "I'm fussed" shows she smells it.

**Why it matters:** It weakens Ray's cover and the logic of episode 8's twist. It also makes Peg look incurious for five years about a dealer who behaves like a friend.

**Fix:** Dilys: "He'll want it Tuesday, Peg. He always does." Peg: "He'll get it." The thin envelope carries the pressure, and the money he never touched stays episode 8's reveal.

### 10. The clinic consultation is a run of questions and answers
**Scene:** 20.

**Problem:** Rosie asks six questions in a row ("How does it work?", "And then what?", "But what do you do to him?", "And the other three?", "Can they have another go?", "How much?") and Osei answers each one. Osei says twice that the Register won't ask him anything. The episode 1 critique (finding 8) flagged the same pattern in Terry's lore scene.

**Why it matters:** The notes for episode writers ask dialogue to act, not explain. There is a real contest available here: Osei wants a starving man fed today, and Rosie wants a number without giving anything away.

**Fix:**
- Open on Osei's want. Her first line after "Your uncle" is "Is he on the Register?" and the push comes early.
- Cut three of Rosie's questions. Keep "But what do you do to him?" / "That's what the fee pays for", "Can they have another go?" / "At the same fee", and save "How much?" for last.
- Let Rosie deflect ("He's shy"; "We manage"), so the scene becomes a small fight she wins by giving nothing away.
- Keep one Register line, not two, and end on "Everybody says that. Until they're hungry."

### 11. "Show Me the Way to Go Home" again
**Scene:** 4.

**Problem:** Terry hums it straight after Peg's "You face home when you cross". The episode 1 critique (finding 7) flagged the song as a comment on Terry's ending, the song from *Jaws*, and still in copyright in the UK. The episode 2 draft lists it as "texture only".

**Why it matters:** Here it underlines the facing-home belief as wrong, which is the pointer finding 1 removes. It is also the owner's rule 5 in miniature: the theme said by a soundtrack.

**Fix:** Agree one replacement with the episode 1 writer: an out-of-copyright music-hall tune whose words say nothing about home or going, or a tune without words. Here, Terry turns the bacon in silence, and Peg's "Give it a rest, Tel" goes.

### 12. Rosie follows a van through central London on foot
**Scene:** 22.

**Problem:** "The grey van's brake lights at the corner, turning left. She pulls up her collar and starts to walk after it." A van in traffic will be gone in a minute. Episode 3 needs her to have "followed the Harley Street jeweller's van to its strongroom".

**Why it matters:** The Crime lead promises "each [crime] shows a particular Marlow mind at work". A hook in which the family's best thief sets off on foot after a van plays as a gesture, not as craft.

**Fix:** She photographs the van's plate and livery, and the jeweller's door card ("Collections Sat 10.25"), the way Tess would note a time, then turns and walks the other way. It is quick, competent and quietly ironic ("Marlows don't do paper"). Episode 3 can then show she followed it over several Saturdays.

### 13. Action lines that tell, and one line that recites a method
**Scenes:** 4, 7, 11, 13, 21.

**Problem:**
- "painted out centuries ago by someone who had changed his mind" (7) winks at the season's theme.
- "hungry, and not for food" (11) points at Rosie.
- "It's not a snack. It's his breakfast. It's what he eats." and "So, now, do we... It was. It always is." (21) explain the twist to the reader.
- "family lore, as old as the rigs" (4) is covered in finding 1.
- Kieran's "It's got to be the same thing every night or it's not data" (13) is a small maxim about method. The plan's Kieran asks "how do you know?" about his own graph, "never as a speech".

**Why it matters:** The plan keeps its scaffolding off the page, and the owner's rule 5 keeps ideas out of characters' mouths.

**Fix:**
- Keep the painted-out hand, cut the clause after "centuries ago".
- Cut "hungry, and not for food".
- Let the card and the lard do scene 21's work: cut "So, now, do we..." and the three short sentences before it.
- Kieran: "Same as every day. That's the point." Stop there.

### 14. Small fixes

- **Scene 18.** It does one job, the poster. Fold the poster into scene 22 as a bus-shelter ad behind Rosie while she watches the van. The hook then stands her, unknowing, between the crime she will commit and the life she will choose, with no look from her. The soldier on it should look well fed, not "thin"; the poster sells FED.
- **Scene 17.** "Terry's newspaper still lies folded at the advert he looked at on Kieran's birthday." In the episode 1 draft the advert was Wednesday and the birthday Thursday, and a week-old paper still lying there is convenient. Once the call is made openly (finding 2), she can simply read the number off her phone.
- **Scene 17.** Danny "clips a wrist unit on". In episode 1 he puts on the belt and thumbs the wrist unit. Keep the whole rig.
- **Scene 20.** Osei's "Then he's lucky. Most families can't" and "Everybody says that. Until they're hungry" are two neat sayings in one short scene. Keep the second.
