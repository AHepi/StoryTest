## Fix, from Fable's review

1. **Episode 4, scene 2: Tess's theory of how Rosie was turned is refuted by the grip rule set down later in the same scene.** This is Fable 1. Rosie has both hands, so by the family's own rule nobody grabbed her. The family's belief should be the one the season exists to break: that losing home in the black turns you.
   - Replace Tess's line "The copper said there's a way to kill a crosser without leaving a mark. Six months, and it looks like a diet. Turn her round in the black and send her home to her tea — that's the only way it could be done."
   - With: "The copper said there's a way to kill a crosser without leaving a mark. Sit heavy by her in the black till she loses home. She faces wrong, she comes home wrong, and six months later it looks like a diet."
   - Keep everything else in the scene exactly as it is, including "Nobody says anything about any of it."

2. **Episode 10, the epilogue, scene 16: Rosie crosses in a theatre, but nothing shows her training.** This is Fable 2. In episode 2 she says "Marlows don't do paper", and the plan says she is training with the licensed surgeons, but the page never shows it. Show the paper and the supervision; add no speech about it.
   - After the Sister's "Your file lives in our kitchen now, love. You eat where you work." add, from the Sister: "(beat) And you sit your exams in the spring, like anyone else."
   - In the theatre, after "The clock on the wall.", add: "At her shoulder, a LICENSED SURGEON, masked, watching the clock and not her. SURGEON: Her first. Count her."
   - The locker line becomes: "Her locker opens once, on the last shot of the scene: a water jug, the Fever Company card, brought with her from Whitechapel, and a trainee's logbook, open at its first page, one line filled in, in her own hand."

3. **Episode 7, scene 12: my round-7 fix was not carried through, and I missed it when checking.** Tess still says "It was six seconds, Danny, all her life." Episode 2 says the Marlows poured the bank's first floor in 2024, and Maggie now says "Four seconds, when she started. Six, since the bank had the floor done in twenty-four".
   - "It was six seconds, Danny, all her life. Then it grew a floor underneath." becomes "It was six seconds, Danny, from our floor in twenty-four. Then it grew another underneath."

4. **Episode 7 says its theme too many times in the same words.** This is Fable's quibble 1. I am settling it because the owner's first rule is that the message is shown and never stated, and it costs only cuts. Two of the phrases are my own round-7 wording.
   - Scene 9, Maggie: cut " — and she never asked why that was" so that the line ends "Six, since the bank had the floor done in twenty-four."
   - Scene 11, Tess: "Ours is Lee. Off his food all one winter, right as rain after Canary Wharf. Nine. Nobody ever asked why." becomes "Ours is Lee. Off his food all one winter, right as rain after Canary Wharf. Nine."
   - Scene 12, Tess: "and she went in again on her own and nobody counted her, and she's a reading on their fridge" becomes "and she went in again on her own, and she's a reading on their fridge".
   - Keep Terry's "Nobody ever asked why." in the loft (scene 4), Maggie's "Nobody counted her" lines on the ferry (scene 7), and her "Nobody of mine crosses till somebody's counted it."

5. **Episode 9, scene 11: the Trading Standards letter is dated the day after it arrived.** This is Fable's quibble 3, and it is a plain contradiction. In episode 4, scene 11, it arrives on the Friday after the Saturday 4 April quake, which is 10 April. "Dated the eleventh of April." becomes "Dated the ninth of April."

6. **Episode 8, scene 7: Terry's "Nobody's sending me in on my seconds, mind" still reads as a refusal to cross at all.** This is Fable's quibble 5. MiMo raised the same point in round 5, so under my rule (a point that keeps coming back and costs a clause gets settled) it is settled here. "I'll drive. (beat) Nobody's sending me in on my seconds, mind. But I'll drive." becomes "I'll drive. (beat) Nobody's sending me in on my seconds, mind. Twos I'll do. And I'll drive."
   - Keep episode 9's "Two seconds I can do, love. It was the long ones I said no to."

## Fix, from the audience test (slips the viewers caught; each a word, a line or a cut)

7. **"Aye" in Essex mouths.** All five viewers noticed it: "It read like a change of writer." It appears 11 times, from episode 7 on, spoken by Tess, Rosie, Shaun, Terry and Ray. None of these characters is northern; Ray is a London detective. Change every spoken "Aye" in the season to "Yeah" (or "Yes" where the moment is formal), and change nothing else in those lines.

8. **Episode 9, scene 1: "Then the birthday boy's turn, uninvited."** All five noticed it. It is June; the birthday was in episode 1. It becomes "Then Kieran's turn, uninvited."

9. **"Nobody low" is never made plain, so the finale's door is hard to follow.** All five viewers were lost at the flour tray in episode 10, scene 9. By the rounds' own definition this is a substantive finding: confusion that does not serve the story. The tray stays; each fix is one line.
   - *Episode 2* (Kieran with the 04:17 ticket): "It's them, Nan. Miss says you can't look at them, you can only weigh them. If I weigh the same thing every night for a year, I'll see their tide." becomes "It's them, Nan. The Twitch, the radio's calling it. Miss says you can't look at them, you can only weigh them. If I weigh the same thing every night for a year, I'll see their tide. *(beat)* The one the watermen call Nobody."
     - This also settles a second slip: four viewers stopped at "the Twitch" in episodes 4 and 8, because the name is never said before then.
   - *Episode 10, scene 9*, after Maggie tips the tray and the water lies along the ruler ("Nobody low."), and before the wet edge crosses the ruler, add one line from Rosie: "ROSIE: Anywhere else, you step off into their sea, or into their rock. There, it's a beach at our own height."

10. **Episode 5: the bite's "half your seconds" is used in episode 9 but never taught.** One viewer, the engineer, caught this. The plan says the bite is taught in episode 5 and fires "for half your seconds". Episode 9's "Half my seconds. Five point four." rests on a rule the audience never heard, which the owner's rules forbid (a secret new rule). In Tess's tab lesson, "Not in the black. Only once you've stood a minute on the wrong side." becomes "Not in the black. Only once you've stood a minute on the wrong side. Then it fires once, for half your seconds."

11. **Kieran "looks at his dad" (episode 5, last scene; episode 6, the scene ending "looking at his dad").** Kieran is Tess's son, and Danny is his uncle. One viewer lost "a good couple of minutes of trust" wondering about a secret-parentage twist. In both places, "his dad" becomes "his uncle".

12. **Episode 8, scene 10: "Two years of a family's one reason".** The nurse has just said "Not since 2019", and it is 2026. "Two years" becomes "Seven years".

13. **The pencil behind Tess's ear.** Two viewers counted its uses, as the show taught them to.
    - *Episode 10, scene 5:* Peg writes FRANK's line with "the pencil that has lived behind her daughter's ear for twenty years". Yet in scene 9 Tess takes that pencil down to hand to Kieran, and it has "come down once, for Rosie". In scene 5, Peg picks up her own pencil instead: "Peg picks up the hatch pencil — the one she marked his tickets with — and beside her own old marks she writes". Keep the rest of the sentence.
    - *Episode 10, scene 9:* "the one that has lived there for twenty years and come down once, for Rosie" becomes "the one that has lived there for twenty years and written once, for Rosie".

14. **Episode 7, scene 9: "and this is the first thing a Marlow has ever written on purpose."** Two viewers pointed out that Rosie wrote her name in episode 3, Peg chalks the board, and Kieran keeps a log. It becomes "and this is the first sum Tess has ever put on paper."

15. **Episode 10, scene 15: "Tess comes in last of all of them, first through the door."** Two viewers asked "Which is it?" It becomes "Tess comes in last of all of them."

16. **Narration that tells the reader what to feel.** All five viewers named some of these ("That's the writer talking to me"), and Fable's quibble 6 names the repeats.
    - *Episode 6, scene 6:* cut "Neither can we." so that the line is "She can't know that."
    - *Episode 7, scene 2:* "...has become ordinary in a fortnight, which is its own horror." ends at "...has become ordinary in a fortnight."
    - *Episode 8, scene 7:* "(She looks at Rosie, and her voice doesn't rise, which is the worst of it.)" becomes "(She looks at Rosie, and her voice doesn't rise.)"
    - *Episode 9, scene 10:* "When she speaks, her voice doesn't rise at all, which is the worst thing in the room." becomes "When she speaks, her voice doesn't rise at all."
    - Keep the first one, in episode 7, scene 12.
