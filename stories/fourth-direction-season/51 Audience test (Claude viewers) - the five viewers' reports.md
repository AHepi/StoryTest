# 51 Audience test (Claude viewers) - the five viewers' reports

*Five simulated viewers, each played by a Claude helper, read the whole finished season and reported, in their own voices, how it played for them. Spoilers throughout.*

---

## Ana, 34, nurse

### Seconds: panel report from Ana

*Ana, 34, nurse. I watch drama on my nights off, and I read all ten episodes straight through over two evenings.*

Quick summary first: I stayed for the family, and I'd have stayed for them even with half the plot. The kitchen table carried this show for me. The gravity, tides and molecules were the parts I put up with.

---

### 1. Where I was gripped, and where I drifted

**Gripped**

- **Ep 1, the cold open.** The black is written so you can feel it: her ears screaming that she's falling, seeing the guard's heart through him. Then the first "Straight?" / "Straight." I got the family's whole religion from those two words before anyone explained it.
- **Ep 1, the birthday.** The weigh-in instead of candles, then Terry's "Goes through me like water, love. Since I turned," and Peg's knife stopping in the cake. The fridge humming in the silence is the moment I knew this was my kind of show.
- **Ep 3, the hypo on the settle.** This is where the nurse in me sat up. I've done this scene for real: the finger prick, the reading, the gel on the gum, the second test. When it still read LO after two tubes of gel I had my hand over my mouth, because I'd guessed the meter couldn't *see* her. Then Terry says "Shift, Tess" and gives her the spoon from his pot. Best scene of the first half.
- **Ep 4, Terry and Con on the phone across the river.** Two old men who can't taste anything, telling each other about gravy and a sausage on Margate front. "You don't know where here is, Tel." I loved Con from a voice and about four scenes.
- **Ep 4, Liam and Rosie at the pit.** "They'll clap you." / "They never clap me." / "They will." She hears herself say it and looks at her own wrists. Rosie's tips (skip breakfast, give it to the dog, drink a pint of water when the hunger grabs you, sleep in your coat because you're always cold) are word for word what patients have told me. It was hard to read, and it's meant to be.
- **Ep 5, the river wall.** I knew as soon as Peg put the "lucky breakfast" down in front of him. "Everything in you counts, breakfast too" was back in Ep 1. I watched him eat every bite with my stomach in a knot. "He was two hundredths of a second from home" flattened me. Then Maggie holds up the printout: "He's on my wall."
- **Ep 6, "I gave you nine."** Tess at the dark table with the binder, doing the sum on herself. Rosie's lying grin starts and stops halfway. No music needed.
- **Ep 7, the ferry.** Two women who count, aground on the mud, filling in ticks and crosses on the back of a timetable. "Nobody counted her, Tess." This is the heart of the season for me. Then Danny rounds on Tess about Liam, and she says "I don't know," which she's never said before. Peg burns it all and throws her out.
- **Ep 8, nearly all of it.** Ray is the Laundry. The tomato plants he can't eat ("They need the watering"), then "How is she?" / "Hungry." Rosie on the Register ward re-dressing Den's burnt hands with the gloves on "for him, love, not for you." That's the first time all season she's where she belongs. And "Nine point four, Tess" at the gate, like a slap Tess has been owed.
- **Ep 10, Frank and the jam jar.** Once I saw the thin Frank turning the lid the wrong way and Peg laughing as she opened it, the whole season clicked shut. "He never saw this. I never let him." Then "FRANK. 10 ST 2. TURNED." in her sign-writer's hand.

**Drifted**

- **Ep 2, the seven hops.** I already knew Rosie was turned (see section 3), so hops two to seven played like waiting for the family to catch up with me: window on the right, eyes to the cutting, back. Hop four with the headlights was tense, and "She likes it that way" is a good line, but the middle of that episode sagged.
- **Every radio bulletin about next-door events.** Black holes with nothing in the sky, the Twitch, "forty-one events like a metronome." It felt like a second, colder show on the other channel. I tuned out every time the calm BBC voice came on, until "Come round for tea" in Ep 8, which is a lovely headline.
- **Ep 4, Kieran's test weight going to 19.78.** I believed Peg about the chip and let it go. That turned out to be the point (fair play), but I didn't care at the time.
- **Ep 9, act four.** The bag of flour weighing 4.95, the 1.1 per cent letter, then Liam's sum (63.9 plus the correction plus half a kilo of breakfast is 65.1, six point nine eight against seven). I followed about two thirds and trusted Tess for the rest. What brought me back was Peg's "I made him breakfast," not the arithmetic. The numbers were proof of something I'd already felt in Ep 5.
- **Ep 10, the flour-hill tide tray.** I drifted hardest here. I got the gist (the thing in the field came out of their sea when our tide went too low), but I didn't need the demonstration and I didn't follow it.
- **Ep 9, Rosie's rule-list before the Deep.** "If it's rock, I'm dead. If it's nothing, I fall for ever. If it's water, it's the minute, the tab, half my seconds." A lot of rules in one breath right before the most exciting thing she does. The sea itself (bubbles, weed, the crab in her hood) was brilliant.

### 2. What confused me, and whether it was worth it

**Worth it (deliberate, and it paid off):**

- "Forty-four dead at midnight. Eleven two, less the lady. Job's nine four." Pure gibberish on page one, and I loved that. Within a few scenes the painted rule (a fifth of a second a kilo under a hundred) made it make sense, and from then on I could half-follow Tess's sums. It made me feel like a guest in their kitchen, not a student.
- The tug-yard shed at the end of Ep 2. I had no idea who those people were: the lad on the steelyard, "Fifty-nine!", the woman who doesn't cheer, the baby crying upstairs. When they came back in Ep 4 as the Aherns, that was a real pleasure.
- Why the painting never turned back. It was obvious once the odds table arrived in Ep 6: three-second hops almost never flip you.
- "Something took hold of me." I spent Ep 3 to Ep 7 half-believing it. The payoff, that it was shame and nothing else, is one of the best things in the show.

**Just unclear, or it jarred:**

- **Kieran and "his dad."** Kieran is Tess's son and Danny is his uncle. But at the end of Ep 5, "Kieran follows his gaze, and looks at his dad," and in Ep 6, "he stands at the door a second too long, looking at his dad." Both times I stopped dead and wondered whether there was a secret-parentage twist coming (and a grim one, since Danny and Tess are brother and sister). Nothing came of it, so I think it's a mistake. It cost me a good couple of minutes of trust.
- **The Far.** "A kettle two doors down. Nobody's daft enough to find out." It's set up in Ep 1 and mentioned again, and it goes nowhere. Rosie gets the Deep and the Hill. I don't mind a mystery left for series two, but this one felt dropped rather than saved.
- **"Nobody low"** as a tide name. I didn't know until quite late whether it was a place, a thing, or a joke of Frank's.
- **Ray's backwards signature in Ep 10.** It's a small trick: everyone thinks he's still turned, then he laughs and the meter says 5.1. I had to read it twice. It's clever, but it's a puzzle beat in the middle of Terry saying goodbye, and I'd rather have had those thirty seconds with Terry.
- **What exactly next door is.** A mirror world with a sea eight metres above our lino, which sends sugar molecules by gravity. I accepted it the way you accept the magic in a fairy tale. I never understood why a long crossing flips you, and the show sensibly calls it a coin. That was fine for me. Where it lost me was when it tried to be exact about it (see the flour tray).

### 3. What I guessed before the show told me, and when

- **Rosie was turned:** from Ep 1. The dog growling at her like he growls at Terry made me wonder. The jar she can't open and Peg opens "the other way", then the birthday card in mirror script that "flows", made me sure by Ep 1, act four. Terry's pot being two fingers down that night settled it. The show told me at the end of Ep 3, so I was two and a half episodes ahead. Mostly that was dramatic irony rather than boredom: watching Peg draw bigger stars as Rosie got thinner was horrible precisely because I knew. The exception was the seven hops.
- **The white tubs are food for turned people, and Terry is turned:** Ep 1, the cake line.
- **Ray is turned:** Ep 1, the lard and the vodka at his desk. Confirmed in Ep 2 with the green card.
- **Frank turned, and the "fifteen stone" story is a family myth:** from the tea-coloured envelope to MR F. MARLOW in Ep 1 and Ray's "I met what was left of him." I was sure by Ep 7, when Tess puts box 02 back unopened. The flashback still hit me hard.
- **Tess burned Con's pills:** the moment the little white carton went into the stove in Ep 4, before Maggie rang.
- **Peg's breakfast would be what killed Liam:** the moment she put it in front of him in Ep 5. I didn't know it would come down to hundredths, or that the bridge had been lying as well.
- **The 59 kg in the dark was the Ahern boy:** Ep 5, when the plate printed −59 and Ray's notebook showed "2nd body 59.0." I remembered "Fifty-nine!" from the steelyard.
- **Maggie made the call about the police car:** Ep 4, from her scene with Joe at the bottle-making.
- **The Mynors floor:** half. Danny's "two floors now, long time to be nowhere" was repeated so pointedly in Ep 2 that I knew it mattered. I didn't connect it to Aoife until the ferry.
- **Didn't guess:** that Ray was the Laundry, the gravity change under the bridge, or that Kieran was making weight. Actually I clocked Kieran at his first "me" column (39.2) and spent every episode after that wanting to shout at the adults. So I did guess, but I didn't guess how long they'd take to notice.

### 4. Who I cared about, and who I didn't

**Cared about**

- **Terry**, most of all. The yellow gloves, his own blue pot, levelling the salt with a knife because he can't taste it, the washing-day song, the phone calls to Con, putting the butties back in Kieran's bag without a word. "Eleven goes, that was. I could've been eating at this table five year since." The die he won't show anyone. His gloves folded on top of his clothes on the gate rail. I cried at that.
- **Rosie.** A show-off and a liar, and the one who teaches Liam to starve, and the show lets her own all of it ("I'm the one taught him to be hungry for it"). The surgeon videos, "Marlows don't do paper," and then the epilogue in theatre at three seconds, "inside the limit she once called soft." That arc is earned.
- **Tess.** "So someone's counting." "I was hungry." Then the terrible "I don't know, Danny," and holding out her wrists at the police counter. She's the most interesting person in it.
- **Liam.** The big lad who's never had a star. His first star, the new hole punched in his belt with a nail, "He was so pleased about the star." Every scene of him was a bruise.
- **Maggie.** A weigher like Tess, and the ferry scene makes them sisters. "Nobody counted my girl." And she doesn't cheer when Siobhan is weighed in the very last episode. That tells you everything.
- **Kieran.** The quiet witness with his log. By the end he's the one holding the pencil, and I was frightened for him from Ep 5 on.
- **Ray.** He took a while. At first he's a clue (the lard, the crackers). By Ep 8 he was one of my favourites: counting a family in dinners, never touching the money, "People don't stop because you say."
- **Peg.** I didn't like her and I cared about her enormously, which is harder to pull off. She's the villain of the piece and every bad thing she does is love, or pride dressed up as love. "Bad luck," after Shaun turns, was the most awful line in the series. "I made him breakfast," and the Frank letter, earned her back just enough.

**Didn't care much**

- **Shaun.** He's a function more than a person: he loses a leg, he undoes knots, he turns, he lies "straight." I felt the leg as shock, not grief. The "I'm worth more without it" joke was the most personality he got.
- **Danny**, for the middle stretch, when he was mostly fists and "Blood pays blood." He came back to me at the end: "No. Give." And the appointment card he keeps turning over.
- **Declan**, **Joe**, **Siobhan**: thin. Declan is a cap and a grudge. Joe is a frightened lad with a bike, and I wanted one real scene with him.
- **Dr Osei**: she exists to explain the rules. Her "Everybody says that. In that chair. Right up until they're hungry" is good, though.
- **Mick, Pinnock, Dilys**: furniture, though nice furniture.

A nurse's grumble: Shaun's above-knee amputation looked after at home by a cash locum ("three weeks and a stick"), feverish on the settle, then crossing again a few weeks later? I rolled my eyes. The same goes, less so, for Danny's four fingers. The show is right about hunger and wrong about wound care. On the other hand, it got the hypo exactly right: LO, gel, retest, and "It's not low. It can't read her." The T-meter reading 2.8 for "a starving man" and 5.6 for "a dinner" are real numbers. That made me trust the rest.

### 5. Lines and moments I'll remember, and what jarred

**Remember:**

- "Straight?" / "Straight." (and every time after Ep 3, knowing it might be a lie)
- "Goes through me like water, love. Since I turned."
- "It was for Terry." / "No it wasn't, love."
- "So someone's counting."
- "They'll clap you." / "They never clap me." / "They will."
- "He was two hundredths of a second from home."
- "I gave you nine."
- "Nobody counted her, Tess."
- "They need the watering." / "How is she?" / "Hungry."
- "Nine point four, Tess."
- Terry eating the crab from next door, slowly, "because he is a cook", and "Nobody says what it tasted of."
- "I made him breakfast."
- "He never saw this. I never let him."
- "Six, I stay hungry. Anything else, I get my tea."
- Peg handing the painting over: "Her window's on the wrong side. She came back like that. She's staying like that." (It's also the show's thesis in one line.)
- Two images: the thin Frank and the jam jar, and Maggie not cheering over the bathroom scale at the end.

**Jarred:**

- Kieran looking at "his dad" (Danny), twice, as above.
- Ep 9: "Then the birthday boy's turn, uninvited." It isn't his birthday.
- **"Aye."** From about Ep 6, Tess, Rosie, Ray and Kieran all start saying "Aye." These are Grays and Tilbury people. Den from Leeds saying "nowt" is fine, but an Essex family drifting northern pulled me out every time.
- Some of the narration tells me how to feel. "Which is its own horror." "Which is the worst thing in the room" appears about three times for Peg's voice not rising. "She can't know that. Neither can we." It worked the first time and got noticeable after that.
- "The Twitch ticket" in Ep 4. Nobody had called it the Twitch before, and I wondered if I'd missed a scene.
- **Timing in Ep 10, act three.** Ray's crossing, Rosie on the Hill and Terry leaving are all intercut in one minute. Terry's goodbye deserved its own room. I wanted to stay on the field with him, not cut back to the office for a handwriting trick.
- Rosie's "Five in six I stay as I am" and "Five and a bit, Uncle" in Eps 9 and 10. I lost track of which odds applied to which crossing.

### 6. What it was about

A family that turned its love into rules ("face home," "make weight," "we feed our own," "paper talks") and then starved its own people rather than admit the rules were counting the wrong thing. It's about what shame does when a family decides some members don't get counted, and about honest counting, written down and shown to others, as the only real way to take care of anyone.

### 7. Score and recommendation

**8 / 10.**

I'd recommend it, with a warning. It's for anyone who loves a family drama where the kitchen table is the main set and the fights happen over breakfast. It's for people who like their science fiction to be about hunger and guilt rather than gadgets. And it's for anyone who's ever been in a family that "doesn't talk about" someone.

I'd tell friends who've been through an eating disorder, or nursed someone through one, to go carefully. The making-weight thread is accurate, and it's relentless.

I wouldn't push it on people who want a heist show (the jobs are short, and nearly all of them go wrong) or on people who want the physics to add up. The physics is the weakest part, and the show spends more time on it than it needs to. Cut a third of the gravity business and give Terry's goodbye more room, and I'd give it a 9.

## Tom, 52, engineer

### Seconds: panel report

**Tom, 52, engineer.** I watch every crime and science-fiction series going, and I check the sums.

I read all ten episodes in order and didn't skip anything. I kept a notebook as I went, with every weight, every count of seconds and every date. Before the rest, here's the headline: **the sums hold.** The rule on the wall is *a fifth of a second a kilo under a hundred*, and I checked it against every chalked weight. Rosie at 44.0 has 11.2 seconds, Shaun at 58.0 has 8.4, Danny at 89 has 2.2, Liam at 63.9 has 7.2, Rosie at 46.2 has 10.8, and so on through all of them. Every one comes out right. The stone conversions are right too: 12 st 4 is 78.0 kg and 10 st 2 is 64.4 kg. I looked the dates up and they're right as well. 4 April 2026 is a Saturday, 29 April a Wednesday, 21 May a Thursday and 12 June a Friday. Nobody gets that far into a drama without breaking their own rules. This one doesn't break them. Where the show seems to break one, it's lying to the family on purpose, and it gives you the evidence to catch the lie. I've waited years for a programme like this.

---

### 1. Where I was gripped, and where my attention drifted

**Gripped:**

- **Ep 1, cold open.** The black is well built. There's an arm's-length glow, and inside it you see the insides of things, like the guard's heart as "a slow fist". The crate stays banded and sealed while the canvas slides out through its side. That told me straight away it was a Flatland show made by someone who understood Flatland.
- **Ep 1, Sc 4.** The camera holds the catalogue cutting (window on the left) and the canvas (window on the right) in one frame, and nobody in the room looks. I sat up. Then in **Sc 18** Ray lays down the two linen photos: *"Linen has a hand, Mrs Marlow."* It's lovely forensic logic.
- **Ep 3, Sc 14–17.** Rosie goes the long way under the road. The **LM** handprint is set in the concrete. Danny says "Nine four", then Tess says "Ten dead". Rosie folds onto the plate, the meter reads LO three times, and Terry's pot comes out. *"It's not low. It can't read her."* That was the best run of the first half.
- **Ep 4, Sc 5–10.** In the reefer job, Danny's arm goes through the steel into the frost. Tess counts "nineteen for the arm", which works out if you allow the arm about five kilos, and he reaches for the box that rattles. Then the Aherns read the frost rings and the raked tracks (*"That's a hand."*). Then there are two old men on two banks of the river, on the phone, saying nothing. That was when the show stopped being about heists.
- **Ep 5, Sc 8–16.** Shaun takes the knots apart from the inside, the bowline "opening like a hand". The tugs drift off "like cows walking out of a gate", and then his leg comes back cut flat. After that Liam goes through the lock-up while I did the arithmetic alongside him, and he dies *"two hundredths of a second from home."* Then Maggie comes down the steps: *"It printed all night."* That episode is the one I'll tell people about.
- **Ep 6, cold open.** Danny waits in the black for six nights, steps in on the seventh and grabs, and the fingers are gone. **Sc 7:** the odds table is read aloud in the van. **Sc 10:** *"I gave you nine."*
- **Ep 7, Sc 9–11.** Two weighers build the ferry sheet out of tide books and memory. Two families, one page, and the crosses crowd above the eight. That scene is a spreadsheet, and I've never been so gripped by one on television. Then Mynors comes out of it.
- **Ep 9, Sc 11–12.** The flour bag reads **4.95** on the bridge. Tess works out Liam's number on the back of the council letter, and Peg says *"I made him breakfast."* Before that, in Sc 9: *"Two meters. Same blood. Both of them right."*
- **Ep 10, Sc 2–5.** Frank's tickets fall week on week from 78.0 to 64.4. The flashback has Frank twisting the jar the wrong way, and it cuts to Rosie wearing the same look. Peg writes **FRANK. 10 ST 2. TURNED.** Then comes the steelyard weigh-in, and then Ray's mirror-written signature, the room's faces falling, and a reading of **5.1**.

**Drifted:**

- **Ep 2, Act two (hops two to seven).** By then I knew Rosie had turned and that three seconds wouldn't turn anything back. The loop of "Straight?" / "Straight." / window on the right / eyes to the cutting became a metronome. The tipper's headlights in hop four woke me up again. The rest felt like watching an experiment when I already knew the result.
- **Ep 2, Sc 11.** A second surgeon video. We'd already had it in Ep 1, Sc 10.
- **Ep 7, Sc 4.** The roll call in the loft: Maureen, Gary, Vic, Lee. Only Lee carries weight. The others are names for the pile.
- **Ep 8, middle.** Den the fireman and his leeks is decent and sincere, but it signposts where Rosie's vocation is heading from a mile off. Also, by the "Carried" vote (Sc 7) Peg's *"We don't go to them"* had stopped escalating and started repeating (Ep 7 Sc 12, Ep 8 Sc 2, Ep 8 Sc 7). I was shouting at her, which may be the point, but I was shouting the same thing three times.
- **Ep 10, epilogue.** It's tidy, with a newspaper montage. *"REGISTER ASKS LEAVE TO OFFER CROSSING NEXT DOOR"* gets one line. That's a series' worth of consequence waved through in a caption.

### 2. What confused me, and whether it was deliberate

**Deliberate and rewarding:**

- **"Forty-four dead at midnight. Eleven two, less the lady. Job's nine four."** It's opaque jargon in the first minute. Then the painted rule over the board in Sc 3 lets you decode it yourself. That's the right way to do it.
- **"Nobody low" and the tide that "forgot to tell the moon."** It's used as if we already know it, and I didn't for nine episodes. It pays off through Maggie's tide book in Ep 10.
- **The tug-yard shed (Ep 2, Sc 15) and "Con" on Terry's phone (Ep 2 Sc 12, Ep 3 Sc 12).** These were two threads I couldn't place, and both land in Ep 4 at once. It's very satisfying.
- **Tess's tab lesson (Ep 5, Sc 13).** She says you can only bite it "once you've stood a minute on the wrong side", and "it'll fire in water." It puzzled me at the time, and it turned out to be the whole mechanism of Ep 9.
- **Kieran's 0.31 kg on an empty plate (Ep 2).** It pays off as the method behind the −59.0 and the −46.2.

**Just unclear:**

- **The flour-and-ruler tray (Ep 10, Sc 9).** I read it three times and drew it. I think their shoreline, at Nobody low, crosses our ground level at exactly one place, which is the field. On the page it's muddled. On screen it needs one clean overhead shot or it'll lose everybody.
- **Rosie at the field (Ep 10, Sc 12): "Seven in ten, it's Nan's fry-up for me."** I first read it as the odds on the crossing she'd just made. That was about five seconds, which is one in six, so it didn't fit. It's actually the ten-second cure she's turning down. One more line would fix it.
- **Terry: "Here, a five-second hop: one in six… There: five in six."** It took me a beat to see that "there" meant next door.
- **The minus readings.** In Ep 9, Sc 11 Tess calls −59.0 and −46.2 "the only true numbers it's printed since April." I can make that work: a crosser's pull isn't local gravity, so the 1.1% doesn't apply, and Ray's −58.6 on the plate matches his 58.6 on the steelyard. But the show only asserts it. One line of why would cost nothing. And the underlying rule, that a plate reads a man in the black as an upward pull equal to his full weight, is pure fiat. I let it go because it was laid down early (Ep 5) and used consistently afterwards.
- **The molecule (Ep 8, Sc 13).** Tremors "spell a molecule. A sugar, of the other hand." That's the biggest leap in the series, and it arrives by press conference. I believed it only because the crab and the T-meter prove the other side is the other hand. I'd like to see how 41 tremors become a sugar, even for ten seconds with Kieran and a ruler. Then there's the closing hook: a second molecule, *our* hand, "fainter". I don't know if that's Terry, or them offering to cook for us, or a goodbye. It's fine as a hook, but I can't check any of it.
- **Loose ends.** In Ep 3, Sc 16 Kieran tears off the ticket printed when Rosie fell, and I never saw it again. In Ep 9, Sc 5 Shaun sees Army cases and "nobody in his family knows that but him," and that isn't paid off either. Maybe both were cut.

### 3. What I guessed, and when

- **Rosie had turned: Ep 1, Sc 4.** The window was on the wrong side, she stared at the crisp packet in Sc 1, and Sixpence growled at her the way he growls at Terry in Sc 3. The jar (she twists the wrong way) and the flowing mirror-script card in Sc 13 made it certain, all before Ray's linen photos. The show gave the audience the answer first and let the family catch up over two episodes. That's the right way round.
- **Terry's "whites" are mirror food: Ep 1, Sc 3.** *"Fat and spirits. It's all that sticks."* Ethanol has no handedness, fat mostly gets by, and anyone who's read Clarke's *Technical Error* knows why a reversed man starves. "Since I turned" in Sc 13 named it.
- **Rosie was raiding Terry's pot: Ep 1, Sc 14**, the moment he looked up at the ceiling.
- **Time in the black decides turning: I was fairly sure by Ep 3.** The surgeons do three seconds and "four thousand, never turned". Terry did eleven two-second goes and nothing happened. Rosie turned on "the longest job a Marlow's ever done." Danny says "Every inch is a tenth of a second" (Ep 1) and "Every job just got a second longer" (Ep 2). The Obverse table in Ep 6 confirmed it. That also meant I knew Peg's three-second hops in Ep 2 could never turn the painting. It was a cruel, good irony.
- **Marlow concrete turned Aoife: Ep 4, Sc 8.** Declan says "their vans were outside it all week", and I laid that next to Danny's Mynors boast in Ep 2 (*"Two floors now. Long time to be nowhere"*). The show fairly plants that line twice. Confirmed in Ep 7.
- **The second body at Mayfair was the Ahern lad: suspected in Ep 3, certain in Ep 5, Sc 2.** The thin lad was at the Marrable Street bus stop and on the bike. Then Ray's notebook reads *MAYFAIR · 03:12 · 2ND BODY · 59.0*, and the steelyard had called "Fifty-nine!" in Ep 2.
- **The bridge was reading light: Ep 4, Sc 11.** Mick is "two hundred light" on a lorry, and Kieran's 20 kg reads 19.78. That's the same 1.1% on two different load cells. A chip the size of a fingernail is not 220 grams of cast iron. From then on every chalked number looked wrong to me. When Tess set Liam off with "two tenths" of margin (Ep 5, Sc 13), I went cold. When Maggie's wall read 65.1, that works out to 6.98 seconds against a 7.0 job. I blamed the whole 1.2 kg gap on the fry-up at first. Then in Ep 6 Osei says 61.7 against Tess's 61.0, which is 1.1% again, so I split it: about 0.7 was the bridge and about 0.5 the breakfast. Tess gets there in Ep 9. The show let me get there first, fairly, and then made it hurt more when she did. That's the best-engineered twist I've seen in a drama.
- **Danny's "two point four" was really 2.2: Ep 5, Sc 18**, when he reads 88 off the lying bridge.
- **Frank turned: the arithmetic smelled wrong from Ep 1.** Fifteen stone is about 95 kg, which gives under a second. Yet "in your dad's day two seconds did most jobs", and his last job was a street that takes ten. A fifteen-stone man can't do that job. Ray "met what was left of him". Ray's first case was Marrable Street. There's a tea-coloured *MR F. MARLOW* envelope in the drawer. In Ep 7 Terry calls it "the one thing everybody's sure of" and Tess puts the lid back on box 02. By then I was certain. Ray's "Sixty-four kilos" in Ep 9 sealed it.
- **Kieran making weight: Ep 5, Sc 18**, the column headed *me*. I dreaded it for three episodes, and I'm glad they didn't make me watch him cross.
- **Shaun turned:** I had him turned at Silvertown (8.4 seconds is one in two, and Peg says "He said straight" rather than meter him). I was wrong about which job but right about the man.
- **Didn't guess:** that Ray was the Laundry (I had him near the food, not supplying it, until Ep 8), that the Deep was a sea (the crab was a delight), or that food turns with whoever carries it. The painting should have told me that last one.

### 4. Characters I cared about, and ones I didn't

**Cared about:**

- **Tess.** She's the counter: *"Near enough isn't."* *"So someone's counting."* She's right about nearly everything and wrong about the one number she didn't check, and the show makes her say *"I don't know"*, twice, in Ep 7. There's *"I gave you nine"* in Ep 6, and she takes *"Nine point four, Tess"* in Ep 8 standing still, hands at her sides. Holding out her wrists at the station is earned.
- **Liam.** He's the big lad nobody claps. His ears go pink when he's praised for his head, and his handprint is in the concrete. There's the new belt hole punched with a nail, and the first star. Then there's Sixpence eating his breakfasts for three weeks, and the last one he eats because Nan put it in front of him. It's the best-built death I've seen on television, because every factor was on screen before it happened.
- **Terry.** The gloves are the kind of detail you can't un-see once the hand rules land. He levels the salt with a knife because he can't taste it. There's Con and the gravy, *"Cost someone."*, the crab eaten slowly "because he is a cook", and going on "two".
- **Kieran.** He's the only honest instrument in the house: the flat line, the step, the circled wobble. There's *FOOD TURNS TOO* written in the margin of a certified roll, and *CRAB. FROM NEXT DOOR.*
- **Ray.** Lard, vodka, hot water, tomatoes he can't eat but waters anyway. *"Dead men don't eat."* The passbook he never touched. The dry laugh after the backwards signature.
- **Maggie.** She's the other weigher. *"Nobody counted her."* The show doesn't let her off: she made the police call and sent the bullet.

**Cared about less:**

- **Danny.** He's understandable, but mostly a fist until *"Best job we ever did. And it's four fingers cheaper than the last one."* After that, *"Neither."* is one of the best single words in the series.
- **Peg.** I respected her more than I cared for her. She's the wall the whole season pushes against, and "I made him breakfast" flattened me. But in Ep 7 and Ep 8 she repeats herself instead of getting worse.
- **Rosie.** She's magnetic, with good tells: the laugh she uses when she's lying, her eyes going to the cutting. But for four episodes her lie ("something took hold of me") drives the war. She also coaches Liam to starve and tells Danny "Every time" about the pit. I held that against her. The swim into the Deep won me back, and the ending, with T on her badge on a fever ward, is right.
- **Shaun** is mostly a function: the knots, the leg, the turn. *"I knew before we were out the marsh. I said it anyhow."* deserved a scene of its own.
- **Declan** is one note, and **Joe** is a silhouette. **Osei, Dilys, the recruiter and Den** are mouthpieces or devices.
- **Liam and Rosie's mother** arrives as a framed photo in Ep 5, Sc 17, the morning her son dies. I'd no idea she existed. The line is good ("This house doesn't say her name"), but it needed seeding.

### 5. Lines and moments I'll remember, and what jarred

**I'll remember:**

- "Linen has a hand, Mrs Marlow."

- "I made him breakfast."

- "Eleven year I've wrote back to front so you lot could read me. Give us a fortnight."

**What jarred:**

- **The pencil.** In Ep 7, Sc 9 it has "never been sharpened", yet Rosie wrote her name twice with it in Ep 3. In Ep 10, Sc 5 Peg writes FRANK TURNED with it. Then in Ep 10, Sc 9 it has "come down once, for Rosie". By then it has come down three times. It's a small prop, but the show has taught me to count, so I counted.
- **"The first thing a Marlow has ever written on purpose" (Ep 7, Sc 9).** Peg chalks weights every episode, labels 26 boxes and keeps an order book. Kieran keeps a log. Rosie signs cards and a passport form. And in Ep 7, Sc 2, Peg's chalk replaces Tess's hand "where hers has been for twelve years", when Tess famously plans "with no paper on the table" and never writes. The show needs to pick one.
- **"Two years of a family's one reason" (Ep 8, Sc 10).** The police lost the list in 2019, so that's seven years. Danny says "Seven year" in Ep 9.
- **"Then the birthday boy's turn, uninvited" (Ep 9, Sc 1).** It's June, and his birthday was in March.
- **Maggie: "I sent a bullet to a boy's bed" (Ep 7).** The boy was only moved to the safe after the bullet was found.
- **Accent drift.** From Ep 7 on, Grays people say "Aye": Tess, Rosie, Shaun, Terry, Ray. Up to Ep 6 they said "yeah". Thurrock doesn't say "aye". It read like a change of writer.
- **Lampshades.** "Nobody asks why these were never burned" (Ep 10, Sc 2): I did ask. Tess putting the lid back on box 02 in Ep 7 is in character, because she's afraid, but you can see the story parking a reveal for the finale.
- **Tess's blind spot.** The family's calculator is the last to notice the bridge is off, after an earthquake, a driver complaining twice and her own son's graph growing a step. I just about bought it, because the roll is certified and Peg says it's the chip, so everybody leans on the paper. It's the thinnest ice in the plot.
- **Real-world regulation.** A certified public weighbridge running 1% light for two months would have hauliers at the hatch in a week, and Trading Standards wouldn't just post a letter. The drawer of unopened envelopes carries it, but only just.
- **A rule that arrived when it was needed.** In Ep 9, Rosie's tab return gives her "half my seconds. Five point four." That was the first I'd heard of the half-seconds rule. It's small, but it's the only rule in ten episodes that wasn't on the table before it was played.
- **Terry's die (Ep 10, Sc 8)** is rolled, covered and pocketed. I understand why: it's his choice either way. But I hate a die I'm not shown.
- **Prose tics.** These would vanish on screen but grate on the page: "which is the worst thing in the room" (three times, all for Peg's voice not rising), "flat as a ticket", "like a tray of glass", "the way every Marlow does". That last one is even used of Ray, which may be deliberate.

### 6. What I think it was about

A family that weighed everything and checked nothing. They lived on a story: Marlows come back straight, we feed our own, the black took Frank because he was heavy, paper talks so burn it. The show is about what it costs to trust your own certified numbers over anyone else's. The cure was never ten seconds. It was letting somebody else do the counting, and writing it down.

---

### 7. Score and recommendation

**9 / 10.**

I'd recommend it without hesitation to people who do the sums:

- Hard-SF readers will spot the family tree: Wells's *The Plattner Story* (the mirror writing), Clarke's *Technical Error* (the reversed man who can't digest his dinner), Flatland, and a straight-faced take on the real "mirror life" worry. They'll find it goes further than any of those.
- Fans of family crime and procedural drama (*Happy Valley*, *Line of Duty*, *Top Boy*) will get a proper tragedy.
- Puzzle viewers who liked *Dark* will get a mystery that is solvable from the evidence on screen, and solved fairly.

I wouldn't recommend it to anyone wanting a heist romp; the heists are the easy bits. It's also wrong for second-screen viewers, because miss one ticket and you've lost the thread. Or for anyone who can't face a twelve-year-old quietly starving himself over five episodes.

It gets a 9 and not a 10 because of the pencil and "first thing written" contradictions, the "two years" slip, the June "birthday boy", the "Aye" drift, the minus readings asserted rather than explained, and the molecule told by a newsreader instead of shown. There's also Ep 2's hop loop and Ep 8's slack middle to trim. All of that is cosmetic. I couldn't find a single cheat in the arithmetic.

### 1. Where I was gripped, and where I drifted

### Gripped

- **Ep 1, cold open.** One of the best openings I've read in a while. The numbers are in shorthand ("Forty-four dead at midnight. Eleven two, less the lady. Job's nine four."), and the next scene gives you the key: A FIFTH OF A SECOND A KILO UNDER A HUNDRED. Then you can do the sums yourself. The black is written so you can see it: the glow, the guard's heart, the beads falling in silence. Then Rosie looks at the crisp packet "a beat too long". That's a twist planted in the first three minutes, and nobody says a word about it.
- **Ep 1, Scene 4.** The printed catalogue photo on the wall and the canvas on the table, held in one frame, with the window on opposite sides, and "Nobody looks." That's pure visual storytelling. Then Ray's two linen photos in Scene 18 ("Linen has a hand, Mrs Marlow") and "I met what was left of him." That's a great act-out.
- **Ep 2, the seven hops.** In theory this should be the most repetitive stretch of the season, and it's one of the best. Every hop is the same: "Straight?" "Straight." The window is still on the right, and Rosie's eyes go to the cutting. Because I already suspected she'd turned, every repeat carried a charge. Hop four, when headlights arrive mid-count and everyone looks at the window except Rosie, is lovely. So is "She likes it that way," and "New roll's printed backwards, Nan. ... Joke."

It's the same minute, and the second time you see the wrists, the cords in Rosie's neck, Terry checking the pot, the dog growling under the applause. That's a smart structural move. The handwriting test that passes, the lard in the van, and Terry leaving the spoon standing upright in the pot, where anyone could reach it, all show you things without saying them. Rosie's collapse, the meter reading LO three times, and Tess's "It's not low. It can't read her" made the best end-of-episode run of the first half.
- **Ep 4.** The reefer raid (a hand through the steel, the baby at the lit window, Tess counting to seventeen) is tense in a quiet way. Then comes **"Other bank. Look across."** Con being an Ahern turns Terry's phone friend from comfort into collateral damage in one line, and it's the best reveal in the first half. "The gravy was nice, though" broke me.
- **Ep 5.** The bullet standing on the painted balance pan is a brilliant image, because it only reads if you can see inside the safe. Shaun's leg. Then **the lucky breakfast**. The camera stays at the table while Liam eats every bite and Sixpence's tail thumps, slows, and stops. I knew what that plate meant, and it's still the best-directed scene in the season on the page. "He was two hundredths of a second from home" is crushing, and so is Maggie holding up the thermal paper: "It printed all night... He's on my wall."
- **Ep 6.** Danny's cold open grab, the fingers, and the tourniquet done alone so the boy won't see. Then the midpoint turn: the £180,000 cure is just a ten-second crossing. It turns the whole season on its axis, from a revenge plot into a plot about the truth. Tess's memory montage ending in "I gave you nine" hit me hard.
- **Ep 7, the ferry saloon.** Two women from rival families build a graph on the back of a ferry timetable while the boat sits aground on the mud. It's the season's thesis done as a scene. The Mynors payoff ("Two floors now. Long time to be nowhere. He was pleased.") is a textbook plant and payoff.
- **Ep 9.** Rosie turns her back on home and sinks into the Deep. Then the sea, the crab in her hood, and "I went shopping." Ray's backwards signature turns out to be a *cure* ("Eleven year I've wrote back to front so you lot could read me"), which is one of my favourite reversals of the season. Terry stepping off the field on "Two."

### Where my attention drifted

- **Prose refrains that turn into tics.** I know ritual is part of the show's language, and some of it is the point ("Straight?" works every time). But "the printer chatters" appears about forty times. "Flexes his fingers once, the way every Marlow does" comes before every crossing, including Ray's, and he's not a Marlow. "Like a tipper shedding its load", "frogspawn in jelly", "like a tray of glass", "the clack like a knuckle", "lorries don't know", and "her voice doesn't rise, which is the worst thing in the room" (three times, all Peg). By Episode 7 I was skimming these lines. On screen a lot of them would just be sound design, but on the page they cost attention.
- **Exposition told twice.** Ep 7, Scene 12: Tess re-explains the ferry sheet to the family almost point for point, straight after we watched her and Maggie make it. The scene only comes alive when Rosie confesses and Danny turns. Ep 8 does the same: Ray lays out the Laundry in his flat, and then Tess repeats nearly all of it to the family in Scene 7 ("Turned in 2015. Seven seconds, carrying a kid..."). One of those should have been an ellipsis.

I understand she's the wall the season has to break. But from Ep 7 to Ep 9 her obstruction hits the same note each time, and I started waiting for her to lose rather than watching her.

### 2. What confused me, and whether it was worth it

**Deliberate and rewarding:**

### 7. Score and recommendation

**8 / 10.**

This is one of the most tightly engineered seasons I've read. Almost every number is a plant: the jar, the crisp packet, the dog, the "chip", Mick's two hundred grams, the key on orange baler twine, Mynors' second floor, "the tide went out and forgot to tell the moon". Almost every plant pays off, some of them nine episodes later. The central irony works on the first read: the revenge war in Episodes 3–6 is built on a lie told out of shame, and a lucky breakfast plus a scale nobody re-checked kills a boy by two hundredths of a second. It loses points for the prose refrains that become tics, for exposition delivered twice in Episodes 7–8, for Peg's refusals looping, and for a handful of dialect and continuity slips that a script editor should catch.

**Would I recommend it?** Yes. I'd recommend it to people who like rule-based sci-fi where the rules actually hold (*Dark*, *Primer*), to fans of British family crime and kitchen-table tragedy (*Happy Valley*, *Top Boy*), and to anyone who liked how *Chernobyl* made a number the villain. I wouldn't recommend it to anyone who wants the sci-fi on screen as spectacle (the neighbours never appear), or anyone who hates doing sums in their head. This show hands you the calculator and expects you to use it.

- **Ep 2, hops two to six.** I knew the painting wasn't going to come round long before they did, so seven goes of "Straight?" "Straight." "Window on the right" felt long. I know it's meant to build, but I was ahead of it.

- **Ep 7, the loft and the ferry.** Maureen, Gary, Vic, Lee, Brendan, Joe's dad, the grain job, the Mynors run. It's a list of people I'd never met and jobs I'd never seen, and I lost track of whose were whose. Cutting back to Shaun at Silvertown saved it. Two women going through their families' books by torchlight is a good idea, but it's a lot of numbers to follow by ear.
- **Ep 8, the Register ward.** Den the fireman and his leeks were nice enough, but it was a breather and I felt it slow down.
- **Ep 10, "the night of writing" and the flour tray.** The writing-it-all-down part was moving but went over a lot we already knew. The baking tray with the flour hill, the ruler and Maggie tipping the water lost me completely (see below).

---

### 2. What confused me, and whether it was worth it

**Confusing, but it paid off (and I liked it):**

- **The number talk at the start.** "Forty-four dead at midnight. Eleven two, less the lady. Job's nine four." I hadn't a clue. It came clear at the birthday weigh-in ("Seventy-two is five point six") and the painted rule on the board. Once I had it (the lighter you are, the longer you get) I enjoyed doing the sums along with Tess. Fair play, that one's earned.
- **What "turned" means.** It came together bit by bit: Terry's cake "goes through me like water", the linen twisting the other way, the mirror writing, the tubs. I never needed the chemistry. The glove on the wrong hand was enough.
- **Ray's line about "a way to kill a crosser that takes six months and looks like a diet."** I fell for it just like the family did. I thought someone was doing it to her on purpose. When it turned out to be nothing more than turning and starving quietly, I felt a bit had, but in a good way. I'd have liked Ray to say more than "There's no such person" when he owns up in Ep 8, though.
- **The bridge weighing light.** Peg's "it's chipped" fooled me, and I let Mick's "two hundred light" go past as a joke. Then Tess put the flour bag on the balance and on the plate and it read 4.95, and it all fell into place. That was the best "oh" of the season for me. Liam's sum coming out at 6.98 against a job of 7 with "Either one, he makes it. Both, he doesn't" is hard to watch, and it's meant to be.
- **Why Rosie kept looking at the cutting after every hop.** I only got this afterwards. She was reading the headline to see if *she'd* come back right. On the night I thought she was just checking the picture. It's clever and I respect it, but I needed Tess's run-through in Ep 6 to put it together.

**Just unclear:**

- **"Nobody low" / "the Nobody Tide".** It's used from Ep 1 as if I know what it is, and I never properly did. The flour-and-water tray in Ep 10 was supposed to explain it and the field. I read it twice and still couldn't tell you why the field is the one spot, or why 05:52 matters. I got the gist, that their sea meets our land at Peg's old farm, but only the gist.
- **The Hill, the Deep and the Far.** The Deep is a sea under Grays and the Hill is where Rosie gets sand on her feet. Fine. But the Far is set up by Terry in Ep 1 and never goes anywhere. I kept waiting for it.
- **The 0.31 kg ticket at 04:17 in Ep 2.** What was on the plate? I never found out. Later on it's called "the Twitch", in Ep 4 and again in Ep 8, as if the word had been said before. It hadn't, not that I noticed.
- **The Army cases and "FOOD TURNS TOO".** I followed it in the end (Shaun turned, so the case he carried turned too, and now the straight ones can eat it), but it went by fast in a busy scene.
- **Ray's backwards signature at the end.** Faces fall, then he laughs: "Eleven year I've wrote back to front so you lot could read me." I had to stop and think which way round he was. The meter reading 5.1 sorted it, but it was a clever-clever moment right when I wanted it plain.
- **The last scene of Ep 10** jumps between Ray in the office, Rosie in the black and Terry on the field inside a minute, and I kept losing who I was with.

### 3. What I guessed before the show told me, and when

- **The painting came back flipped: Ep 1**, when the camera held the cutting and the canvas in one shot. I didn't know what it *meant* until Ray's thread photos.
- **Rosie had come back wrong: end of Ep 1.** The dog growling at her the way he growls at Terry, then Terry's pot down two fingers after the birthday. I was sure in Ep 2 when she joked "New roll's printed backwards, Nan." The dog is a smashing device. A viewer like me can read a dog.
- **Ray was turned: Ep 1.** A mug of vodka, lard on crackers. It's Terry's gin and dripping again ("It's all that sticks"). The green card in Ep 2 confirmed it.
- **Rosie brought the diamond job for her own cure, not Terry's: Ep 2/3**, from the moment she followed the jeweller's van away from the clinic. Terry's "No it wasn't, love" was the payoff.
- **Con was an Ahern: late Ep 3**, from "You don't know where here is, Tel" and the voice "with a river in it". It was confirmed in Ep 4, and the call still got me.
- **Liam was going to die: Ep 4**, when Rosie taught him to feed his breakfast to the dog. After that I just waited for it, dreading it.
- **Frank hadn't died "too heavy": around Ep 7**, when Tess put the lid back on the 02 box, though Peg hiding the MR F. MARLOW letter in Ep 1 had been nagging me. When Frank couldn't open the jam jar in the Ep 10 flashback, just like Rosie at the birthday, I felt clever. That's the sort of clever I like, where the show lets *me* be the clever one.
- **Shaun had turned: Ep 7**, when Peg brushed off "Meter him." The show says it was Purfleet in Ep 9, so I was early, or wrong about which job.
- **Terry would go next door: Ep 9**, as soon as the crab moved his number.
- **What I didn't guess:** the Laundry being Ray, the bridge being out by a kilo, and Danny's own floor at Mynors being what turned Aoife. Nobody grabbing anybody, and the whole feud being about concrete, was a proper twist and a sad one.

### 4. Who I cared about, and who I didn't

**Cared about:**

- **Terry.** He's my favourite by a mile. He's a cook who can't taste, measuring salt with a teaspoon and levelling it with a knife, in his yellow gloves. There's his pot, "Kept man, me", the phone calls to Con, and the butties he fishes out of the bin and slips back into Kieran's bag. When he finally got a plate and a folded napkin for his crab, I was gone.
- **Liam.** The big lad nobody claps, who gets his first star and dies for it. "They never clap me." "They will." That one line did more than whole episodes.
- **Kieran.** The real detective in the house, with his test weight every night at nine. His column headed *me* going down all spring was frightening, because I could see it and none of the grown-ups could.
- **Tess.** She's the one who counts, and in the end she's the one who says "I don't know, Danny." Holding her wrists out at the police desk was right for her.
- **Rosie.** She's a show-off and a liar, and brave with it. "I went shopping" made me laugh out loud in a dark scene.
- **Maggie**, as it went on. "Nobody counted my girl" is the heart of Ep 7.
- **Ray.** He's a slow burn. Tomato plants he can't eat, "They need the watering", and a building-society book in Terry's name he never touched. I didn't like him at first and I did by the end.

**Didn't care about as much:**

- **Peg.** I understood her but I couldn't warm to her. Wiping the board, locking the rigs, throwing her own daughter out, the drawer of letters she won't open. She's the real villain for most of it, and she knows it by the time she writes "FRANK. 10 ST 2. TURNED." That moment did move me. I just didn't *like* her, and I don't think I'm meant to.
- **Danny.** He was mostly shouting "Aherns!" until he lost his fingers. He was better after, and "Two seconds is enough to grab someone" is a great bit of menace.
- **Shaun.** I never really got to know him. He's there to lose a leg and then turn.
- **Declan, Joe Ahern, Dr Osei.** Declan is a cap and a grudge. Joe is a lad on a bike (and I'll admit I noticed he shares my name). Osei is there to explain the rules.

### 5. Lines and moments I'll remember, and what jarred

**I'll remember:**

- "Straight?" / "Straight." The whole show is in that, and it goes sour by the end.
- "Never go back twice." / "Lightest goes longest!" / "Near enough isn't." / "Paper talks." All of them come back to bite.
- "I met what was left of him."
- Rosie with the painting that won't come round: "She likes it that way."
- Terry: "No it wasn't, love."
- Siobhan on the phone: "Are you a Marlow?" Terry: "Yes, love."
- "He was two hundredths of a second from home."
- Tess to Rosie in the dark: "I gave you nine."
- Danny to Tess: "You're the one that's never wrong," and then Tess saying "I don't know" for the first time.
- Terry: "You bought my dinners off a dead man's plate, Peg."
- "It's sea. Under us. All of it."
- Terry with the die: "Six, I stay hungry. Anything else, I get my tea." Then he pockets it without showing anyone. Lovely.
- Peg at the desk: "Mind, her window's on the wrong side. She came back like that. She's staying like that."
- The dog lying down quiet by the one case that's turned.

**Jarred:**

- **"The Twitch"** is used in Ep 4 and Ep 8 as if we'd been told the name. We hadn't.
- **Ep 9 calls Kieran "the birthday boy"** at the weigh-in. It isn't his birthday. That was Ep 1.
- **Ep 6, "She can't know that. Neither can we."** That's the writer talking to me, not something I'd see on screen.
- **Ep 10, "Tess comes in last of all of them, first through the door."** Which is it?
- **Dialect.** Tess, Peg and Maggie start saying "Aye", "wrote" and "come" halfway through. They're Essex and Kent river people, and "Aye" made them sound like somebody else. I'd let Ray have it, maybe.
- **The dead mother (Ep 5).** "This house doesn't say her name" comes out of nowhere at the worst possible moment, with Liam just gone. It felt dropped in rather than earned.
- **Timing.** The last two episodes pile reveal on reveal: the bridge, the letters, the 2019 rule, Liam's sum, Frank's weight, the Register people, the tide tray. I'd have spread a couple of those out.

---

### 6. What I think it was about

A family that would sooner keep a proud story than read its own paperwork: "Marlows come back straight", "we feed our own", "it's why we make weight". The price of that pride gets paid by the kids, starving themselves for a star on a chalk board. In the end the only brave thing left is to write down what really happened and hand it over.

---

### 7. Score and recommendation

**8 out of 10.**

The first five episodes are as good a heist-and-family thriller as I've had all year: proper jobs, proper stakes, proper cliffhangers. Episodes 6 and 7 turn into a numbers-and-records mystery, and some of it went over my head. The science and the tide business in 9 and 10 lost me in places. But I never stopped wanting to know what happened next, and the people carried me through where the cleverness didn't.

I'd recommend it to my mates who like a crime-family drama and a good heist, and to anyone who's watched a family lie to itself over the dinner table. I'd tell them two things: stick with it through Episode 7, and don't worry about the science, because it's the people that matter. It's not for anyone who wants car chases and shoot-outs, or who can't stand doing a bit of arithmetic in their head.

## Lena, 41, philosophy teacher

### Panel report: *Seconds* (ten episodes)

**Lena, 41. I teach philosophy at a sixth-form college and go to church on Sundays.**

I read all ten episodes over two evenings and one Saturday morning, in order, without skipping. I'll say it plainly first: this is the most morally serious drama I've been handed in a long time. It is also nearly unbearable in places, and a few seams show. Notes follow.

---

### 1. Where I was gripped, and where I drifted

**Gripped:**

- **Ep 1, the cold open.** "Straight?" / "Straight." I didn't yet know what the question meant, but I knew it was the question the family asks *before* it asks whether you're hurt, and that told me what this family values. Then Rosie looks at the crisp packet "a beat too long". I sat up.
- **Ep 1, the jar.** Peg laughs, "Our Rosie never could open a jar." Rosie watches her laugh and says nothing. Then the mirror-script birthday card goes up on the fridge beside the old wobbly one. That quiet sequence did more than any action scene.
- **Ep 2, the seven hops.** The repetition is the point. After every hop Rosie's eyes go to the cutting and back, and nobody sees her. When she whispers "Come on. Come round" in the black, I realised she wasn't talking to the painting. She was praying for herself. That was the moment the show had me.
- **Ep 3, Act five.** "LO." "LO." Then Terry kneels with the pot, and Tess says: *"It's not low. It can't read her."* In one line the season stopped being a heist show.
- **Ep 3, Terry's spoon standing upright in the pot,** left on the side "where anyone could reach it". It's an offering. It is the most Christian gesture in the series and nobody names it.
- **Ep 4, two old men on two banks** with the tide running out between them. "You don't know where here is, Tel." "No. I don't."
- **Ep 5, the whole of it.** Maggie making up the baby's bottle without looking at the chair where Con sits dead. Shaun's ninth knot ("Never go back twice", and he reaches anyway). And Liam's lucky breakfast, where the camera stays at the table and Sixpence's tail slows and stops. I had to put the pages down after "He was two hundredths of a second from home."
- **Ep 6, the odds table read aloud in the van,** then Tess at the dark table: *"I gave you nine."* Rosie's show-off grin starts and stops halfway. Very fine.
- **Ep 7, the ferry saloon.** This is the heart of the season for me. Two weighers from two feuding families sit on a grounded ferry and count their dead onto the back of a timetable. *"Nobody counted my girl." / "Then let's count her now."* The Mynors revelation lands like a dropped weight, and so does "Nobody spun anybody." / "Except Danny. And the only one Danny ever spun was himself." Intercutting it with Shaun's crossing at Silvertown, with Peg now doing the counting, was masterly.
- **Ep 9, the crab.** Two meters, one bead of blood, 5.6 and LO side by side: *"Both of them right."* Then Terry pushes his empty plate into the middle of the table "the way a man asks for more", and there is no more. And Tess weighs a five-kilo bag of flour and the bridge says 4.95.
- **Ep 10, the loft.** Kieran reads out Frank's weights week by week, and Terry says, "That's not a man going careful. That's a man that can't eat." Then comes the cutaway of thin Frank turning the jar the wrong way, and then Rosie in the hatch wearing exactly that look.

**Drifted:**

- **Ep 2, hops three and four.** The repetition works overall, but around the Wednesday-night hop I was reading for the next beat rather than living in this one.
- **Ep 5, Scene 2.** Ray explains "delivered", then the plate reading as an upward pull, then the arm's-length rule, all in one scene. It's necessary information delivered as a lecture.
- **Ep 6, Tess's memory montage** at the dark table. It re-plays lines I already had in my head. The show trusted me more elsewhere.
- **Ep 8, the Register ward.** Den and his leeks are lovely, but the pace dropped just as the family plot was coming to the boil at Purfleet.
- **Ep 10, Act four.** Tess's "objects" speech about how the rolls, the walls and the Register corroborate one another is the thesis of the season stated as a legal argument. I agreed with every word and still felt taught.

---

### 2. What confused me, and whether it paid off

- **The physics of the black** (the glow, the arm's length, beads flooding at three seconds, a posted hand being "still stood on the world") came to me gradually. That's fine. By Ep 2 I trusted it. **Deliberate and rewarding.**
- **The Nobody Tide** was used from Ep 1 with no gloss ("the tide went out and forgot to tell the moon"). I loved having it hang there. The payoff in Ep 10 is the flour hill on a baking tray, a ruler, water, and Maggie tilting it. I could not picture it on the page. I understood the *conclusion* (the object walked out of their sea onto Peg's field) but not the demonstration. It will probably work on screen. On paper it was **muddled rather than rewarding.**
- **The Hill, the Deep and the Far.** "The Hill's moved" in Ep 4, the 1.1% gravity letter, and the "strokes" of the message all seem connected. I'm still not sure whether the Hill moving *is* the first stroke or *causes* the scale change. That felt **half deliberate.** I didn't mind not knowing, but I'd have liked one clean sentence somewhere.
- **"The Twitch."** In Ep 4 Kieran tapes a ticket "under the Twitch ticket from March", and in Ep 8 Tess recognises Peg's face from "when the Twitch was on the radio". I don't remember anyone ever calling the March event "the Twitch" in dialogue. It reads like a writers' room nickname that leaked onto the page. **Just unclear.**
- **Ray writing his name in mirror script in Ep 10.** For one beat I thought he'd come back turned, and so did the room. Then: *"Eleven year I've wrote back to front so you lot could read me."* I had to reread it to follow the logic (a turned man who trained himself to write so that we could read him), and when it landed it was one of the best jokes in the series. **Deliberate, and worth it.**
- **The final radio hook**, "a sugar of our own hand… fainter". I read it as Terry, or whoever received him, answering in our hand, and "fainter" as either distance or frailty. Peg looking at Terry's ticket "a long time" tells me she reads it the same way. **Deliberately open, and good.** Leave it alone.

---

### 3. What I guessed, and when

- **Rosie had turned: end of Ep 1.** The crisp packet, the dog, the jar opened the other way, the flowing mirror-script card, and the painting's window changing sides. Ray's "Linen has a hand… This canvas came back the wrong way round" confirmed it. I also guessed from Terry's pot going down two fingers (Ep 1, Scene 14) that Rosie was eating his food at night.
- **Terry's tubs are other-handed food: Ep 1.** "Goes through me like water, love. Since I turned," and Peg's knife stopping in the cake.
- **Turning depends on the seconds, not on "facing home": Ep 1 into Ep 2.** The surgeon had done four thousand crossings at three seconds and never turned. Danny says it's "'cause she faces home", and Rosie doesn't answer. Terry went eleven times at two seconds and nothing changed. Rosie did 9.4 seconds and turned. The family creed was plainly a charm. The odds table in Ep 6 only put numbers on it.
- **"Something took hold of me" was a lie, or at best a misreading: Ep 3,** the moment she said it. The Ep 1 cold open had been careful to show a *pull* and nothing seen. The truth ("That's all it ever was. Shame. And you all clapped.") only came in Ep 7, and it was still a gut-punch.
- **The −59.0 on the empty plate was the skinny Ahern lad: Ep 5,** when Ray's notebook showed "MAYFAIR · 03:12 · 2ND BODY · 59.0". I remembered the old man calling "Fifty-nine!" in the tug-yard shed in Ep 2, which I'd taken for a stray cutaway. That was lovely plotting.
- **Liam would die: Ep 5,** the second Peg put down the lucky breakfast after Tess had given him "two tenths". I was right, and I wished I hadn't been.
- **The bridge was reading light:** I distrusted Peg's "it's chipped" in Ep 4. A fingernail chip is not 220 grams, and Mick had just been "two hundred light". But I didn't connect it to Liam's death until Tess weighed the flour in Ep 9.
- **Frank turned:** suspected in Ep 1 ("I met what was left of him") and more firmly in Ep 3, where "Fifteen stone" gets repeated a little too insistently. I was certain by Ep 7, when Terry calls it "the one thing in this family everybody's sure of". A show like this doesn't give you a line like that unless it means to break it.
- **Danny's Mynors floor:** in Ep 2 he mentions "the floor under the one we did in '24" twice in one episode. It felt planted. I didn't know what it would do until the ferry.
- **Ray as the Laundry: I did not guess this.** It surprised me in Ep 8 and was fully earned in retrospect: the lard, the Register card, the batch codes, the 2019 list. The detail that he *charged* Peg because "your mother would sooner starve than owe anybody" is the sharpest thing in the season about pride.

---

### 4. Characters I cared about, and why

- **Terry.** The man at the stove whom nobody looks at. He is the conscience of the house, in yellow gloves. He tops up the pot rather than accuse anyone. He leaves the spoon standing. He says "No it wasn't, love" when Rosie claims the job was for him. Peg washing him in the field before he goes is laid out like a burial and a baptism at once: the clothes folded, the gloves folded on top. I cared about him most, and I'm glad the show didn't tell me where he landed.
- **Tess.** "So someone's counting." For her, counting is a kind of love, then a way of hiding, and in the end a way of confessing. "I don't know, Danny" is the sentence she has never said, and she holds out her wrists at the police counter: "Every number's mine. Start with me." She is the one who takes responsibility for harms she caused without knowing the odds. That is exactly the moral-luck problem I teach, dramatised better than any textbook case.
- **Rosie.** Charismatic, funny, and a liar out of shame. Her confession in Ep 7 is the hinge of the whole feud. Choosing to *stay* turned in Ep 10 ("Can't catch anything. There's wards that need that") is a vocation. It isn't a defeat.
- **Liam.** I will think about Liam for a long time. He is "the big lad", never clapped and never starred. The star he finally gets costs him his life. "He was so pleased about the star."
- **Maggie.** She is Tess mirrored on the other bank, which is the show's title idea worked through people. She made the phone call that sent Rosie in the long way, and she sent the bullet, and she says so. "Nobody's square." I trusted her more than anyone.
- **Peg.** I didn't love her, but I understood her, and that's harder to write. Her creed ("We feed our own", "We don't go to them") is pride dressed as self-reliance. The show shows how that creed kills her husband's memory, her grandson, and very nearly all of them. Her opening Frank's letter and writing "FRANK. 10 ST 2. TURNED." is a genuine repentance, all the more so because it's done in pencil and plainly.
- **Ray.** He wrote everything down for twenty-four years and showed nobody. "That's my bit of it." A sin of omission, which is the kind we tend to forgive ourselves too easily. "They need the watering" about tomatoes he can't eat moved me. I did find him a little too *useful* to the plot: he has every missing fact, and in the last three episodes he hands them over on cue.
- **Kieran.** The column headed *me* in his exercise book is the most frightening image in the series, because nobody in a house full of scales noticed a boy getting lighter. I'd say, though, that he is occasionally a device. He always has exactly the right ticket in his book.
- **Danny.** I cared about him at "No. Give." and at "Best job we ever did. And it's four fingers cheaper than the last one." His vengeance plot in Eps 3–6 got repetitive.
- **Didn't much care for:** Shaun, until his lie "Straight" in Ep 9. Declan and Joe, who are sketches. Dr Osei, who is a very good idea ("That price fed forty thousand people") given about three scenes.

### 5. Lines and moments I'll remember, and what jarred

**I'll remember:**

- "Straight?" / "Straight." It starts as a catechism and ends up as a lie people tell out of love.
- "Near enough isn't."
- "So someone's counting." I think about prayer, and about the line that the hairs of our heads are all numbered. Being counted is being known.
- "It's not low. It can't read her."
- "Nobody counted my girl." / "Then let's count her now."
- "Two meters. Same blood. Both of them right."
- "We never fed our own."
- "Everybody says that. Until they're hungry."
- "That's not a man going careful. That's a man that can't eat."
- "FOOD TURNS TOO," pencilled by a twelve-year-old in the margin of the certified roll.
- Peg at the police counter: "Mind, her window's on the wrong side. She came back like that. She's staying like that."
- "Paper talks." It starts as the family's reason to burn everything and ends as Peg's reason to hand it all over. A whole moral arc in two words.

**Jarred:**

- **"Aye"** turns up in Essex mouths in the later episodes (Tess, Rosie, Peg: "ROSIE: Aye."). Ray might say it, and Den from Leeds says "nowt". But the dialect drifts north as the season goes on, and it pulled me out every time.
- **Ep 9, Scene 1:** "Then the birthday boy's turn, uninvited." It isn't Kieran's birthday. If that's an ironic echo of Ep 1 it doesn't read as one. It reads as a leftover.
- **Repeated stage-direction phrasing.** "Her voice doesn't rise at all, which is the worst thing in the room" appears in Ep 7 and again in Ep 9. There's also "which is its own horror", and in Ep 6: "She can't know that. Neither can we." (I liked that last one, but it's the writer speaking over the scene.)
- **Ep 7:** "the first thing a Marlow has ever written on purpose." That's not true. Rosie filled a leaflet with her own name in Ep 3. It's a lovely sentiment undercut by the show's own evidence.
- **Ep 10 epilogue:** Peg on bail "for putting the other hand down a Grays drain". I had to work out that it meant the sea water. It's clumsy.
- **Timing:** Ep 5 kills Con, takes Shaun's leg and kills Liam in one hour. Every one of those is earned, but stacked together they risk numbness. I'd move Shaun's leg to the end of Ep 4.

---

### 6. What I think it was about

It is about a family whose creed ("Marlows come back straight", "we make weight", "we feed our own") was built to avoid looking at its own dead. Pride and loyalty turn that love into harm, and the only way out is to count honestly, confess it on paper, and accept grace from a door with no lock on it. No one is the villain. Every death in it is the sum of small, loving untruths, down to the last two hundredths of a second.

---

### 7. Score and recommendation

**9 / 10.**

I'd recommend it to anyone who wants a drama that treats consequence as seriously as plot: my book group, colleagues who teach ethics, and people who say they don't like science fiction. The fourth-direction idea is only ever in service of the moral one. Be patient with the first two episodes and the dialect, and with some exposition in Eps 5 and 10.

Two cautions. It is relentlessly grim through the middle. And it depicts starving to "make weight" in detail, including in a nineteen-year-old and a twelve-year-old, with the family cheering it. I would not casually hand it to a teenager who struggles with food, and I'd want to watch it *with* my sixth-formers rather than set it as homework.
