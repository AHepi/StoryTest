# THE KEPT

*Season pitch, Angle C: a new world and new people, built from the same two documents.*

**Logline.** A hundred and thirty years ago, four hundred people escaped a plague by turning their bodies into their own mirror images, and since then not one of their descendants has been sick. Now a wasting illness they have no word for is killing their children. Their best midwife has to teach a people who outlawed questions how to ask one, and then how to ask it of her.

**Format.** Ten serialized episodes of about 55 minutes. Major reversals in episodes 4, 6–7 and 10.

**The trick at its heart.** For three and a half episodes the show is secretly printed mirror-reversed, so the valley looks normal and everything from outside looks backwards. When the heroine turns, the printing stops, and the audience learns it believed what the valley believed, for the valley's reasons.

---

## 1. The world

**The normal.** Holdfast: a high valley of two thousand people in Year 130 of its own calendar (about 2190 outside). Stone houses, terraced gardens, goats, bees, a Hall, and one machine, the Door. Every molecule in every Kept body is the mirror image of an ordinary human's. The Kept believe it is the rest of the world that is reversed.

**The founding.** In the 2060s a plague called the Wasting was killing the world. Dr Hester Kell, a physician who worked with crossing machines, knew that a body turned through the fourth direction comes back mirrored, and that no germ on Earth can touch a mirrored body. She crossed 412 healthy people again and again until each came back turned. Thirty-eight ran out of time in the dark. The sick were left behind. The founders carried turned seed, eggs, goat embryos and a sourdough, and in their own guts every microbe their new world would ever have. The terrified world outside signed the Glass Accord, sealing them in a valley behind a cordon, watched from the rim by Station Glass.

**Departures and their ripples.** One big departure, a whole people of the other hand. Everything else ripples out from it.

- **Nothing outside can feed them.** A wild blackberry passes through a Kept body unused: the Wild's food "turns to ash." Nothing wild will eat the Garden either, so there have been no pests and no blight in 130 years.
- **Nothing outside can make them sick.** They have no word for contagion. The founders' medical books were burned as relics of the Wasting. Everyone shares cups and greets with a kiss.
- **Nothing outside can rot them.** Their honoured dead lie uncorrupted in the Quiet, a cave of 130 years of faces, anointed weekly with mint oil by the Keepers. The ordinary dead are "given to the Garden."
- **The Leaven.** Only the Kept's own gut microbes can break down their dung, leaves and dead. Without their own night soil on the fields, the soil locks up. The Leaven carts at dawn are holy.
- **The Door and the Lanterns.** The Door lifts a body off the world for a few seconds. Lanterns are small women who fast and are weighed before every crossing ("fifty-one kilos: fourteen seconds"). In the Door's black a Lantern sees inside everything within arm's reach, and can reach through skin without a cut. They look inside the sick and the labouring. Every look is a coin toss nobody can feel: about three in a hundred bring her back turned again, *Fallen*, starving at a table full of food. After every look she eats a Garden leaf. Mint means Kept. Caraway means Fallen.
- **The Sweetleaf.** Once a week everyone eats a raw mint leaf from their own quarter's Mint Bed, to show they are still Kept.
- **The needles.** Every home keeps a founders' gravity needle as a shrine. Once a week it trembles in a long pattern: the Knock, Mother Kell calling from the Other Garden where she "went ahead" in Year 40. Sometimes it trembles off schedule, seventy kilos where nobody stands: a Heavy Guest.
- **The Release.** The Fallen, and the sick who won't recover, walk into the Wild with an old blessing: "Go to your bread." No body has ever been found. At night there are lights on the far ridge: "the Lanterns of the Dead."
- **Surplus, taken for granted.** Kept writing and clocks run the other way (on screen, until Episode 4, it is ours that look wrong). Mirrors are banned: "the glass turns the eye." Wild dogs growl at the Kept. The Holder eats every crumb "for the Thirty-Eight." The Leaven guild is feuding with the beekeepers over cart routes.

**What the Kept believe.** One creed is carved over the Hall door: THE KEPT DO NOT SICKEN. "The world turned; we were kept." The Pull, a slow wasting, is longing for the Wild, and it is cured by Keeping: the Pulled are gathered in the Keeping House, sung to and fed from a common bowl, and their Leaven, "the holiest," feeds the East Mint Bed. The pure do not fall. The Leaven is life. Questions killed the Doubters. When plague comes, we cross.

**Why nobody questions it.**
1. **It worked.** For more than a century every one of those sentences was true.
2. **The one time they questioned, people starved.** In the Schism of Year 71 the Doubters refused the Leaven as filth. The soil locked up and three hundred starved, among them the present Holder's mother; he was eight. "Questions kill" is a lesson from a real case.
3. **Every test they can run agrees with them.** No amount of looking says who is turned. Only a sample that never crossed can decide, and every sample in the valley is Kept. The Wild's food really is ash to them, and its writing really does look backwards.
4. **Every failure gets a smaller exception.** When something breaks the creed, a new clause is chiselled beneath it, each in smaller letters.
5. **The evidence walks away.** Doubting and falling earn the same Release, and nobody Released has come back to say otherwise.
6. **Mercy.** The Holder believes certainty is food, and he feeds it to them.

**The outside.** Station Glass: thirty underfunded staff the world has mostly forgotten. Its manual says *Observe. Never touch. Never tell.* Every week its crossers, in portable field packs, slip into the valley unseen to lift samples. Its Annex holds the Released for life, fed from a mirror kitchen at a fortune per meal: a body that can't be walled in can still be kept by its pantry. Both sides' rules come from one event. In Year 6 a Kept man, Tobias Marl, walked out to find the wife he'd left behind, and cordon guards shot him. Holdfast learned "the Wild kills." The Accord learned "never touch."

## 2. The doom engine, step by step

1. **Rules fitted to a past.** Immunity, incorruptibility, the Leaven and "questions kill" were all true for Holdfast's history. The immunity was never a law, only a fact about history: no germ had the Kept's hand *yet*.
2. **An old cure for an old illness.** The first "Pull" was the wasting of Lanterns who fell and lied about the leaf, starving on Kept food and longing, quite literally, for the Wild's. The founders' Release sent them where their bread was, and it cured them. "Go to your bread" meant exactly what it said.
3. **The practice changes the world.** After the Schism, the Holder's father opened the Keeping House for anyone who wasted, from grief or a mild stomach bug. Crowding, the common bowl, and the Keeping's Leaven feeding the East Bed's raw mint made a loop. Over nearly sixty years it bred a harmless microbe from the founders' own guts into a killer that also eats the dead. The culture grew its plague out of its own mercy.
4. **Surprise.** In Year 130 the rules meet a change outside their history. A saint rots and children die. The old name, the Pull, now covers a new cause: same outcome, different route.
5. **More of the same.** Erase the rotted saint's name. Carve an exception. Double the Sweetleaf. Keep the sick closer. Release the doubters. Then the Great Keeping, seven days of the whole valley gathered round the sick. Then the Exodus: "When plague came, Mother crossed us."
6. **Each re-tuning feeds it.** More Sweetleaf, more infected mint. More Keeping, more crowding. More looks, more Lanterns falling. The Exodus can't work even in principle: whatever is inside you crosses with you.
7. **The failure is structural.** Every response treats sickness as a condition of the soul, something you confess, fast away or leave behind. No re-tuning of that idea survives, because the cause is a living thing hidden inside the body that travels with it. What's needed is a new idea: a thing you can't see, of your own hand, that moves between people.
8. **One right person is not enough.** Carried in from outside, Ora's right answer is just something the Fallen say. It works only once the Kept build it themselves with a test they can watch: the East quarter eats West mint and stops sickening. Then the new answer meets a change of its own (Ora's hands), and only a people who have learned to ask catch it. The world always changes outside what your rules were fitted to. The only defence is a culture that keeps asking.

## 3. Main characters

**ORA HALE (34), Lantern and midwife.** Sixty-one looks, never fallen: the valley's living proof that the pure do not fall.
- *Want:* keep her daughter alive; underneath, stay the proof.
- *Wound:* six years ago her husband Jem told her his doubts, and she reported them to her father. Days later Jem fell on a look, and her testimony made the fall a sin. She believes she sent him to die.
- *Flaw:* she always thinks she's the exception, and believes whichever rule flatters her. "The pure do not fall" becomes, after her fall, "it can't touch me."
- *Levers:* competence (the cord lifted through skin), vulnerability (every look a coin toss), connection (Wren, her father), specificity (she counts seconds under her breath). Dry, sparing humour.
- *Arc:* proof, then Fallen, then outsider who knows the answer, then someone who learns that an answer handed over is only a claim, then one builder among many, then the vector. She submits to the question she once reported her husband for asking.

**WREN HALE (12), her daughter.** Counts everything: steps, crumbs, Knocks. She has known for years that a Heavy Guest comes on her birthday, and she says "hello" to it.
- *Want:* to know why, and to get her mother back.
- *Wound:* her father's Release when she was six ("he died of longing").
- *Flaw:* she hoards what she finds (a buried watch, a forbidden mirror, her tally). A question kept secret saves no one.
- *Levers:* specificity, humour, agency, vulnerability (she has the Pull).
- *Arc:* secret asker to the first public Asker, and the one who questions her own mother. In Episodes 1–4 her questions draw the rules out of adults, so she is the audience's guide.

**AUGUSTINE VALE (67), the Holder: Ora's father, Wren's grandfather.** Kind, funny with children, knows every name in the valley.
- *Want:* keep his people alive and together.
- *Wound:* his mother starved in the famine of 71.
- *Flaw:* he closes questions as an act of mercy.
- *Right about:* the Leaven really is life; unity really did keep two thousand people alive where nothing could feed them; the Wild really can't feed them.
- *Arc:* he re-tunes at every failure, even Releasing his own daughter, as she once gave up her husband. He hides his own Pull and leads the Exodus. In the Door's dark he sees inside his own body and understands. He goes neither on nor back, and becomes a weight that stays.

**JEM HALE (38), the Released husband, now Station Glass's best crosser.** Light, gentle, wry.
- *Want:* to go home. *Wound:* Ora's testimony.
- *Flaw:* punished once for asking, he traded one rulebook for another and obeys "never touch" to stay safe.
- *Arc:* he breaks the rule to save Wren, and his secret mercy becomes the Holder's proof. He learns that help nobody understands feeds the error it means to fix, and walks into the Hall in the flesh.

**LIS BARROW (19), Ora's apprentice, the new Lantern.** Devout and pregnant; her mother was Released three years ago.
- *Arc:* the Holder's new "proof" when her first looks don't fall. She protects Wren's tally, chooses the test over the Holder, trusts Ora with her birth, and dies of what Ora's hands carry. Her baby is raised by the mother Lis was told was dead.

**MARGIT SOLLE (58), Director of Station Glass.** The Holder's mirror outside. She filed the first report on the Pull eleven years ago, was told to observe, and closed the question.
- *Right about:* mirror life is genuinely dangerous, and contact has gone badly before.
- *Arc:* from "I filed it" to opening the Annex, at the cost of her post.

**Minor.** **Nan Tully (66)**, Leaven-master and child survivor of 71: "Don't stop it. Test it." **Brother Amos (50)**, head Keeper and true believer, who loses his son in the Great Keeping. **Caleb Rook (85)**, a Doubter of 71, fifty-nine years in the Annex: "We didn't lose because we asked. We lost because we answered before we'd looked." **Pell (9)**, Wren's friend, who names the Mint Bed bees.

## 4. The season

**1. "Sweetleaf"**
*A valley where nobody has been sick in 130 years watches its best midwife save a baby through its mother's skin. That night its oldest saint begins to rot.*
- **Cold open.** Ora is weighed: 51.2 kilos, fourteen seconds. She steps into the Door. In the black, by her own lantern, she sees inside the labouring mother: a cord twice round a baby's neck. She lifts it free through skin and is back at "thirteen." The leaf: mint. The Hall sings "Kept."
- **Beats.** Wren's questions draw out the world (why is the Wild's food ash? why don't the dead rot?). She digs up a watch that runs "widdershins." At the rim, a rusted sign in Wild letters, backwards on screen. The Sweetleaf. Pell goes to the Keeping House. Keepers walk bare-handed from the sick to anoint the saints. Ora looks inside an old man and writes "knot of longing" in the Book of Looks. On Wren's birthday her needle reads seventy-one kilos where nobody stands. She whispers "hello."
- **Twist.** A founding saint's anointed hands and face are rotting.
- **Hook.** The Holder draws a cloth over her and has the niche sealed. Cut to Wren pushing away her bread.

**2. "The Knot of Longing"**
*A child dies, and Ora looks inside him at something the colony has been writing down, and misreading, for twenty years.*
- **Cold open.** The council rules the saint hid the Pull in life. Her name is chiselled off, and under the creed goes the first small line: SAVE THE LONGING.
- **Beats.** Inside Pell: white threads in the gut wall. The Holder opens the Book of Looks: Lanterns have recorded these "knots" since Year 110, more every year. Pell dies, breaking the genre's promise that the child is safe. His mother is Released for "giving him the longing." Wren: "Nobody's ever found one." Nan Tully tells Wren about 71. Wren is sick; the Holder sends her to the Keeping House and Ora obeys. Wren smuggles in a forbidden hand-mirror.
- **Twist.** The evidence was always in the book, read as sin.
- **Hook.** Wren holds a Wild-lettered page from the archive to her mirror, and for her and for us the letters come right. The Founder's hand: *"Cross each of us until the mint tastes of caraway."* Why would Mother want them to fall?

**3. "Heavy Guest"**
*Ora hunts the source of the rot and the weight that visits her daughter, and both lead where she can't follow.*
- **Cold open.** Night in the Keeping House: the common bowl goes round, the needle twitches, Wren counts seconds.
- **Beats.** Ora unseals the saint. The rot began at the brow and the hands, where she was anointed, and Ora blames the mint oil. We see the Keepers' bare hands; she doesn't weigh them. The Holder won't stop the Sweetleaf and doubles it: "It shows who is Kept." Flashback: Jem's questions, Ora's report, his fall. Lis becomes a Lantern. Wren and Tam start the Children's Tally, scratched under their beds: who sickens, and when.
- **Twist.** Jem's weight at his last look was 71.4 kilos, the Heavy Guest's weight. Ora believes her husband's spirit walks.
- **Hook.** Wren stops eating. The Holder permits a look inside her. Ora will do it herself.

**4. "Widdershins" (Major Reversal 1)**
*Ora looks inside her daughter, sees what is killing her, and comes back the wrong way round. So does the show.*
- **Cold open.** 50.1 kilos, fifteen seconds. Her dog Bramble nuzzles her. She steps in.
- **Beats.** Inside Wren the threads are alive, eating the gut wall like mould on bread: a thing, not a longing. She comes back and Bramble growls. The leaf tastes of caraway. The picture flips: the creed over the Hall door reads backwards, and through the window the Wild's sign reads right. "It's alive. I saw it." Her father rules that she doubted and fell, word for word what she once testified about Jem. Wren watches from the Keeping House window. "Go to your bread."
- **Twist.** The Kept are the mirror people and the world outside is ordinary. The show, and Ora, had it backwards.
- **Hook.** The rim sign: GLASS ACCORD: MIRROR-LIFE CONTAINMENT ZONE. Starving, she eats a blackberry. It is food. Floodlights, hazard suits: "Don't touch anything."

**5. "Go to Your Bread"**
*Outside, Ora learns the world never ended, someone has been sampling her people for sixty years, and the Pull is a germ.*
- **Cold open.** A white room. A woman in a suit hands Ora a supermarket mint leaf. It tastes of mint.
- **Beats.** Quarantine. Solle tells the Accord's story plainly. Bread, apples, meat: all food. A truck; a city's glow. In the weighing room Watch crossers suit up for the valley: the Heavy Guests. Solle's lab keeps Holdfast samples by year, and under a microscope, the Pull's bacterium. No contact. In the valley Lis looks and doesn't fall ("proof"), and a second line is chiselled: SAVE THOSE WHO DOUBT. Wren's tally shows the sick are mostly East families. The Holder already has an explanation: "Longing runs in the East. That's where the Doubters lived."
- **Twist.** The ghosts are the Watch's crossers, and the Watch has had the Pull in its samples for eleven years.
- **Hook.** "And you did nothing?" "We did what the Accord says. We watched."

**6. "Lanterns of the Dead" (Major Reversal 2, part one)**
*Ora finds the Released alive in a comfortable prison, while her daughter is saved by a miracle that will kill dozens.*
- **Cold open.** The Kept on their doorsteps, gazing at the ridge lights. Reverse angle: the lights are windows, and at one stands Pell's mother.
- **Beats.** The Annex: 43 Released Kept, some held for decades, kept alive by a supply chain. Lis's mother is there. So is Caleb Rook, who explains that the first Release was a cure, sending the Fallen where their bread was. Annex patients who had the Pull recovered on mirror medicine: a sickness, not a sin. In the valley Wren is dying. The needle twitches, and by morning her fever has broken. The Holder proclaims a Grace of the Keeping and decrees the Great Keeping: every sick and every faithful soul in the Hall for seven days.
- **Twist.** The Released live.
- **Hook.** A crosser back from the valley pulls off his hood on the scale. It reads 71.4. It is Jem.

**7. "Delivered" (Major Reversal 2, part two)**
*Ora's dead husband is alive, and the medicine he secretly put inside their daughter is about to become the reason her people die.*
- **Cold open.** Six years back, through Jem's eyes: caraway, Release, blackberries, the Watch; then his first crossing home, unseen beside his sleeping daughter, watching her heart beat through her ribs.
- **Beats.** Rage, then grief: "You told them I asked." They eat together, the same hand now. Jem confesses he broke "never touch" and put a mirror-antibiotic tablet into Wren's stomach through her skin. Ora sees what the Holder will make of it. On Solle's screen the Hall's weight climbs as the valley gathers. The Knock is known outside as the Carvone Signal, a mint molecule of the Kept's hand sent from the neighbouring world, and the records show it began before Mother Kell ever left. It says *mint*. It never said *come*. Solle refuses medicine and refuses return. Caleb asks to go home and is refused.
- **Twist.** Jem is the Heavy Guest, the Grace was medicine, and the call is a molecule.
- **Hook.** Ora and Jem slip the cordon with enough stolen medicine for twelve. At dawn, bells: the Great Keeping has begun.

**8. "The Great Keeping"**
*Ora walks back into the Hall with the truth, and learns that a truth carried in from outside is just another thing someone says.*
- **Cold open.** Four hundred packed in, the sick on pallets, common bowls, a double Sweetleaf. The doors open on Ora, Fallen, eating an apple. Behind her, Jem. The dead walk.
- **Beats.** Ora lays it out: a sickness, the mint, the crowding, the Grace was medicine. The Holder: the Wild has turned her tongue. Most look away, and we see why: she only *says* so, and nobody can check. She smashes a bowl and Amos throws her out. She gives the twelve doses to children; the Holder calls it poison. In seven days 31 die in the Hall, Amos's son among them. Outside, Ora opens the dead to show the threads. Only she can do it safely ("It can't touch me"), and she has dropped the Watch's gloves, reasoning they only protect the wearer. Lis learns her mother lives, and joins Ora.
- **Twist.** Under the table the Holder slips his bread to his dog. The man who ate every crumb for the Thirty-Eight has the Pull.
- **Hook.** On day seven Wren walks out with her tally: the sick came from the East Bed. "Grandfather wouldn't look. Will you?"

**9. "The Book of Looks"**
*Children run the first experiment in Holdfast's history, the Holder's oldest law turns out to be younger than he is, and victory kills someone Ora loves.*
- **Cold open.** Nan Tully won't stop the Leaven ("that's what killed us in 71"), but she will rest it in the sun a season. East families eat West mint. The sick lie apart. Children count.
- **Beats.** The Askers form: Wren, Tam, Lis, Nan and a few parents. Within weeks their houses stop sickening while the Hall's keep dying. "East blood" or "East mint" is settled by a change anyone can watch. Wren and Lis read the Book of Looks from Year 1: falls run steady at about three in a hundred, saint or sinner. The founders wrote only *"Fell. Went to her bread."* "The pure do not fall" first appears in Year 72, in the hand of the Holder's own father. The Holder loses the Hall and proposes the Exodus, and the faithful start fasting to be light. Ora goes straight from the dissection table to deliver Lis's baby, then two more. The baby lives. Lis sickens.
- **Twist.** The eternal law was one man's word, fifty-eight years old.
- **Hook.** Lis dies. Wren adds three marks to her map and stares: all three are mothers Ora delivered.

**10. "Until We Know Better" (Major Reversal 3)**
*The Askers must question the woman who taught them to ask, and the Holder carries his certainty into the dark.*
- **Cold open.** Wren's map on the Hall floor. The Askers see it. Nobody says Ora's name. Ora does.
- **Beats.** She carried it on her hands from the dead to the births. Immune, she never felt it. In Episode 3 she blamed the oil and never looked at hands, the same way the Keepers carried it to the saint. The Askers ask her to stop, and she stops. Basins go up at every door, and nobody goes from the dead to the living. Solle opens the Annex and is sacked for it, and the Released walk home down the valley carrying the Watch's medicine. Lis's mother holds Lis's baby while a Kept neighbour nurses it. At the Door, the Holder faces ninety fasted faithful. Wren brings him the Book of Looks and the Carvone Signal. He will take no one who isn't sure, and only Amos is. The Holder goes first. In the black, by his own lantern, he sees inside himself: the threads in his own gut, which he would carry into Mother's Garden. He goes neither on nor back. The Door-house needle settles on a weight that never leaves, sixty-eight kilos, slowly spreading. Amos, watching it, stays.
- **Twist.** The heroine was the vector, and the Holder saw at last.
- **Ending.** See section 7.

## 6. Mysteries and wonders

**Mysteries (pressed hard, all answered in season):** why the dead rot (Eps 1→3, 5); what the Pull is (2→5); who the Heavy Guest is (1→6); why the Founder wanted caraway (2→4); where the Released go (2→6); why sickness runs in the East (5→9); why new mothers are dying (9→10); whether Ora gets home (4→8).

**Wonders (mentioned in passing, never chased):** whether Mother Kell reached the Other Garden; why the neighbours send mint; what the Holder felt in his last seconds (we see only what he saw); the world beyond the ridge.

**One declared exception.** The last shot opens a new mystery on purpose, promised to Season 2, not passed off as a wonder.

## 7. The ending, and what it settles

**Final scenes.** A table in Ora's house has two pantries. Ora and Jem eat Wild blackberries and a rabbit, and Wren eats Garden bread: the first table in Holdfast with two hands at it. In the Hall the creed stays, with its shrinking exceptions, unerased: a record, not a law. Over the new basin by the door, the Askers carve UNTIL WE KNOW BETTER. Then every needle in the valley trembles, and for the first time in a hundred and sixty years it isn't the Knock. It's a new pattern. People gather. Someone asks, "Is it Mother?" Wren: "I don't know yet. Write it down."

**What it settles.** Every mystery closes: the Kept are the mirror, the world and the Released live, and the Pull is a germ with three routes (mint, crowding, hands). The normal has moved from THE KEPT DO NOT SICKEN to UNTIL WE KNOW BETTER, from one pantry to two, and the cost is counted: Pell, Lis, the Great Keeping's 31, the Holder, Ora's hands. The world asks whether a people kept alive by certainty can survive a change their certainty never met. The season answers: not with more certainty, and not with one right woman, but only by becoming people who ask, including of their saviours. And they must keep asking, because the needle has already changed again.

## 8. The show's ledger

*Declared once. Nothing new will be added in secret.*

**Inherited physics (from "The Fourth Direction," used as-is, with its own stipulations):** worlds are stacked as leaves; a field lifts a body off its leaf for seconds, longer the lighter it is; walls don't hold, and the crosser's lantern sees inside everything nearby; a turn mirrors you, a slide doesn't, and nobody can feel which; mirrored bodies can't eat ordinary food, can't catch ordinary disease, and carry mirror microbes; only gravity crosses between leaves; the neighbouring world's life is the other hand.

**THE KEPT's own stipulations:**
- **S1.** The Door turns about 3 crossings in 100, and the rate never changes.
- **S2.** The Pull is a bacterium descended from the founders' own gut microbes, made deadly over nearly sixty years by crowding and the mint-and-Leaven loop. It can grow only in a mirror body, but it can ride on anyone's hands.
- **S3.** The Knock is a real signal from the neighbouring world: moving mass spelling a mint molecule of the Kept's hand, repeated weekly since before the Founding, until the last scene.
- **S4.** The Wasting, the plague of the 2060s, which the founders escaped by turning.

**Derived (no new purchase):** the Kept can't eat the Wild and the Wild can't sicken them; their dead rot only by their own microbes, so their fields need the Leaven; a Fallen Kept eats the Wild and can't catch the Pull; mirror versions of ordinary medicines work on it; dogs smell the difference; the Watch can weigh the Hall and the Quiet; a crosser sees inside their own body; a crosser who stays past their seconds becomes a weight, slowly spreading.

**Storytelling device, not physics:** Holdfast is shown mirror-printed until Ora's fall.
