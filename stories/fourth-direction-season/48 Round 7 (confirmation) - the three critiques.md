# 48 Round 7 (confirmation) - the three GLM critiques

*Three GLM critics, each through one lens, on the season after round 6. MiMo was dropped this round.*

---

## Critique: story

1. **Episode 9 (scenes 4–5) and episode 10 (scenes 11–12) — the bite is used twice on the same rig, against the rule as written.** Guess 5, rule 16 and the canonical table all say the brass disc "fires once." Rosie bites the tab of Tess's old rig (the gaffer-taped one) to leave the neighbours' sea in episode 9, then bites the same rig's tab again to leave their shore in episode 10. Either the disc is spent after episode 9 — in which case the finale's scouting method is impossible — or bites are reusable, in which case a rule the audience was taught in episode 5 ("Can I bite it if I'm short?") is silently reinterpreted at the exact moment it matters. A season whose currency is its own physics discipline cannot afford its one visible exception here. Fix: one beat at Mucking — Tess fits a fresh disc to the collar ("Tab's spent. New one.") before Rosie goes, which reloads the rule and shows the family now maintaining its own gear; or change episode 5's line to "one bite a crossing." **SUBSTANTIVE**

2. **Episode 6, cold open — the stakeout pays off by coincidence.** Danny's premise ("If the tow runs, the man comes again") is asserted, never evidenced: the audience knows the bullet was a single message, and nothing plants repeat visits. Six nights the black is empty; on the seventh Joe comes — to deliver Maggie's letter, an errand unconnected to the bullet and timed by the writer, not by anything Danny could know. The grab drives a major reversal (Danny's hand, the envelope, the ferry), and resting it on unowned luck weakens a set piece the show frames as grief-logic paying out. Fix: make the visits a pattern — let Kieran find a second −59.0 ticket in episode 5 or 6 (say, the night Liam died), so the watcher is established as a regular and Danny's bet is evidence-based; or give Danny a spoken limit ("Seven nights, then I'm done") and let someone call it a fool's errand, so the payoff is acknowledged as a gamble. **SUBSTANTIVE**

3. **Episode 5, scene 3 against episode 8, scene 6 — Ray's puzzlement contradicts what he knew.** On the A13 Ray is visibly puzzled that Rosie looks well ("You look well." / "I've eaten."), and the episode 5 plants explain it as "he knows the Laundry sends only enough for one." But his own ledger shows the order doubled on 7 April, and Ray says he knew there was a second mouth in the house "the week you did" — by 21 April he had been feeding two mouths for a fortnight, so her condition is exactly what his books predict. The two plant notes even give opposite rationales. A careful viewer replaying episode 5 after the Laundry reveal will find the beat inexplicable, denting the reveal's retro-fit. Fix: cut the exchange, or re-aim it — Ray's look follows his mention of the emptied Gravesend cold room, so the puzzle is whether the family has found a second source. **SUBSTANTIVE**

4. **Episode 8, scene 13 into episode 9 — Rosie's walk-out is triggered by the raid but aimed at the Deep, with no connective beat.** Kieran's text ("Nan's doing the depot tonight") is what puts her out of bed; she then makes no attempt to warn, join or stop the raid, and crosses to the neighbours' sea instead. The inference — no more raids if there is food — is available, and "a sum being done" gestures at it, but nothing on screen closes the gap between trigger and action for the season's boldest choice. Fix: one beat on the ward or the train — she rings the office and lets it ring out, or a single line placed near "I went shopping" ("There's food there, Kiz. Then there's no more depots"). **QUIBBLE**

5. **Episode 6, scene 4 — Maggie's "Thursday fortnight, then" plays as a decision taken after the grab, but the letter already named the date.** Joe delivered the envelope that night, and it reads "Thursday 21st"; spoken fresh after hearing about the grab, the line implies the meeting was arranged in reaction to it. Fix: "Thursday fortnight, like the letter says" — or make the delivered note undated and let a second, dated note follow. **QUIBBLE**

6. **Episode 8, scene 9 into episode 9 — nobody asks who rang the Register.** The chief says "somebody rang the night line," a crew takes Rosie at dawn, and a paranoid family in the middle of a blood feud never asks who called; the show never confirms on screen that it was Tess (Rosie's "Nine point four, Tess" only implies it). Fix: one line at the gate (Danny: "Was it her?" Peg: "Who else?"), or Tess owning it in episode 9 ("I rang them. You'd have buried her"). **QUIBBLE**

7. **Episode 9, plants note — "her two tickets on the plate read 45.7" cannot be true of the second ticket.** Rosie steps on the plate dry (45.7) but lands back soaked, with weed in her hair and a crab later weighed at 0.38 on the same bridge; the landing ticket must read heavy. Nothing breaks on screen (no landing number is spoken), but the note would mislead a later writer. Fix: correct the note, or use the gap — Kieran reads the landing ticket aloud as a kilo or two heavy and Rosie says the sea is still draining off her, which also underlines the biosecurity the epilogue charges her with. **QUIBBLE**

8. **Episode 7, act four — Rosie's vow states the season's method almost as a moral.** "And when it's been counted proper, and checked against somebody else's, I'm going first" — "checked against somebody else's" is the thesis in the show's own words. It is one line, in voice, but it is the nearest the season comes to saying its point aloud. Fix: trim to "when it's been counted proper, I'm going first"; the checking is shown everywhere else and needs saying nowhere. **QUIBBLE**

9. **Episode 3, scene 14 — the watcher on the bike is never followed up by the family.** A watcher at the mouth of the mews mid-job would be tomorrow's first conversation in any working firm; after Rosie's collapse swallows the scene, no Marlow mentions him again, though he is the Aherns' eyes and the audience knows it. Fix: one line in episode 4's war council (Danny: "There was a lad on a bike watched us out of the mews") — it tightens the Ahern thread and pays the plant. **QUIBBLE**

10. **Episode 1, scene 3 — the 7 a.m. radio already reports the 3 a.m. theft with forensic detail** ("crate was found still banded, knotted and under its lead seal, and empty") — a bulletin that fast, in the breakfast hour, stretches the world's own pace. Fix: move the item to scene 4 (mid-morning) or soften it to "police were called to a Mayfair bank overnight." **QUIBBLE**

11. **Episode 6, cold open — Danny's two-beat hover flirts with overrunning a count the season has made exact.** With a true 2.2-second budget the fizz starts at roughly 0.2; "for one beat, two beats, his hand hangs there" consumes one to two seconds, leaving almost nothing to wrench, orient and step. The plan itself cut Danny's "early tingle" line for exactly this reason; the staging still lets him appear to outstay the warning. Fix: one beat, not two, or begin the fizz as his hand is already closing. **QUIBBLE**

12. **Episode 10 — three endings in one hour, with Terry's exile the most at risk.** Frank's truth, the door, Ray's cure, Terry's step, the vote, the surrender and a four-scene epilogue all land inside 55 minutes. The plan is right that the rolls, not Terry, are the climax, but the compressed running order risks the audience under-feeling the season's biggest image — an old man counting himself off the world between a meter reading and a chalk argument. Fix: fold the epilogue's visiting-day scene into its front-page montage, and trim scene 6 (the investigators) to its threat and deadline, buying the 05:52 sequence two quiet minutes. **QUIBBLE**

13. **Episode 5, scene 17 — the second framed photograph is a beat the plants admit pays nothing.** Danny's glance at the young woman's frame and Peg's "This house doesn't say her name" is texture; the plants note says outright it "pays nothing this season." Surplus detail is welcome under the implied-world theory, but a planted-and-admitted-dead beat invites the fault. Fix: cut it, or give it one job — Liam's eyes on the frame during his last morning would make it about the family's silences rather than about nothing. **QUIBBLE**

## Critique: people

1. **Episode 10, the field (Scene 12): Rosie's final choice is announced, not dramatised.** After nine episodes of wanting the cure — the leaflet folded soft at its creases, "LISBON. HALF" in biro, the pitch to Peg, the walk to the ward — Rosie reverses the deepest want of the season in two flat lines at a fence ("Seven in ten, it's Nan's fry-up for me... I'm staying as I am. Can't catch anything. There's wards that need that."). The only seeds are "I went shopping" and the pocketed recruiter's card; the weighing itself happens off screen, so the season's biggest emotional transaction lands as information. It matters because she is the character the audience has held longest, and her choice is the price the whole season was buying. Fix: extend the beat the draft already blocks — she stands at the stove end "where Terry stands" during the night of writing — into a two-hander: the family's two turned people facing opposite exits (his one go, her seven-in-ten) before anyone goes anywhere. The fence then ratifies a choice we watched her make. SUBSTANTIVE

2. **Episodes 7–10 and the epilogue: every reckoning lands on Tess, and none on Peg.** Danny interrogates Tess twice ("And Liam? Did your sums do Liam too?", "You don't plan for this family") but when Peg confesses the lucky breakfast in episode 9 — the half-kilo that, with her bridge, killed his son — "Nobody breathes," and Danny never says a word to his mother, then or after. Peg also put Tess out of the house in the season's cruellest scene, and the breach is never mended: in December Tess has a letter from her son and Ray's signature in the visitors' book, and silence from her mother, while the world is cured by the records Tess assembled. It matters because the show risks teaching that the one who checked carries all the cost, and the woman whose plate, drawer and legend did the killing is spared every confrontation. Fix: two cheap beats. In episode 9, after "There's your number, love," Danny gets two lines ("You fed him, Mum." / "I did."). In episode 10 or the epilogue, Peg brings the box marked 02 down to Tess herself — "Your dad's year" handed over, not discovered — and her name appears on Tess's visiting order. SUBSTANTIVE

3. **Episode 10, Mucking Marsh and after: Terry leaves without Kieran, and the season's warmest bond gets no goodbye.** Terry and Kieran carry the show's gentlest thread — the pit and the lore, the soup, "I'll tell her you found your bubbles," the rewrapped sandwiches left in a school bag without a word. At the gate Kieran is miles away counting for Ray; his only response is silently taking Terry's spot at the stove, which a careful viewer may catch and most won't. The plan's rule against a farewell at the water is honoured, but restraint here tips into coldness precisely where the audience's care is highest. Fix: keep the no-farewell rule and spend one beat before it — Terry leaves one thing written on the roll in pencil, Con's gravy, the recipe of the two old men who couldn't taste: the man who kept nothing on paper leaving one paper thing to the boy who keeps everything. SUBSTANTIVE

4. **Episode 9, Scene 7: Peg's non-reaction to the Deep crossing.** Her granddaughter breaks the family's capital rule — did not face home — and nearly drowns, and Peg's whole response is "You went in the Deep" / "Same thing," followed by metering and business. This is the woman whose certainty runs the family, who said "No Marlow crosses till I say," whose fear for Terry comes out sideways as "Get rid of that jumper." Nothing comes out here at all, sideways or otherwise; the woman we most need to feel this doesn't. It matters because the audience's terror in the black water needs an answer in the room, and because a Peg who doesn't react reads as a Peg the writers protected. Fix: ten seconds in her own grammar — she wipes the salt crust off her bridge with her apron and can't stop wiping it, while Rosie watches her do it. Then business. SUBSTANTIVE

5. **Episodes 3–10: the cost of Rosie's lie is never laid at her door by anyone.** "Something took hold of me" launched the war that killed Con, took Shaun's leg and Danny's fingers; the retraction in episode 7 is met with silence; at the night of writing her confession of the lie and of coaching Liam is answered only by Danny's generous "And I said yes. Write that first." — shared guilt, no reckoning. Tess, who was deceived most and tested her, gets no scene with Rosie after the gate ("Nine point four, Tess"); their last charged exchange is a slap and a door. The fever wards become her sentence structurally, but the interpersonal debt — the thing the audience has been counting since episode 3 — is never paid between people. Fix: one beat. In the epilogue Rosie, the one free Marlow who owes her, is the sender of something to Tess in prison — a letter with the drift table copied out in her own hand, or simply her name on the visiting list. SUBSTANTIVE

6. **Episodes 6 and 7: Ray disappears as a pursuer for two full episodes, and his suspension happens off screen.** After taking the rig and reading the bullet (episode 5), the Crossing Unit detective vanishes while the family robs a clinic on Harley Street; we learn he was walked out of Victoria in a line of dialogue in episode 8. The Crime promise is a long fight between equals, and the duel goes dark through the exact stretch where the Marlows are most exposed. It matters because the middle third loses its outer pressure and becomes purely internal, and because Ray's fall is a beat of his arc the audience is told about rather than wounded by. Fix: thirty seconds in episode 6 or 7 — the audit walking him out of Victoria, box under his arm, the unit not looking up; or better, Ray staking out Obverse the same night Tess hits the records room, pursuer and thief missing each other by a staircase. SUBSTANTIVE

7. **Episode 8, Scene 13 (the hook): Rosie's walk-out is taken on a motive the audience cannot see.** A one-line text ("Nan's doing the depot tonight") and ten seconds of stillness, and she is out the unlocked door. The audience's likeliest read is that she is going to stop the raid; her real purpose — food — is named only retroactively in episode 9 ("I went shopping"). At the season's largest personal gamble, the want driving it is invisible in the moment, which blunts both suspense and care exactly when both are maximal. Fix: seed the want where the news plays: after "a sugar — of the other hand," one exchange with Den about his first ration box and the leeks that grow, her eyes on the T-meter; then the text lands as a trigger she has been waiting for. The card and the meter both going into the same pocket should read as a girl pocketing two answers. SUBSTANTIVE

8. **Episodes 9–10 and the epilogue: Kieran's foreclosed want is never mourned, and his recovery is withheld.** The boy's whole season is wanting to cross; Danny's "Not him. Not after Liam" closes it, and Kieran goes to bed "slowly, hugging the exercise book" — and that is the last the show feels of it. He converts to the pencil without a single beat of grief for the thing he lost. The epilogue then stages the perfect image — the fry-up eaten, "Everything in you counts," the ticket — and cuts before the number. It matters because his starvation was the audience's longest private dread, and the family's cure of its own child is the thesis in one person; withholding it is restraint in the wrong place. Fix: show the number (a derived, logged one, above 38.0), and let Peg's face do the work. SUBSTANTIVE

9. **Episode 7, the ferry: Maggie's "Nobody of mine crosses till somebody's counted it" is the season's moral spoken as a rule.** It is in her idiom and earned by Aoife, but it is the creed of the whole show — check, against someone else — delivered in one aphoristic line, the nearest the season comes to saying its theme. Fix: keep her grief, soften the creed: "Nobody of mine goes in till I've timed it myself," and let her tide books going onto the pile in episode 10 be the statement instead. QUIBBLE

10. **Episode 7: the loft and the ferry are the same device twice in one hour.** Two long scenes of reciting names and numbers off old paper, both ending in a charged silence (Gary, Vic, Lee; then the ticks and crosses). The loft's box-02 beat is essential and must stay, but the middle names could move to the night of writing in episode 10, where a litany is already the scene's rhythm, freeing episode 7's first half for something felt rather than filed. QUIBBLE

11. **Episode 5, Scene 17: Liam's mother is planted and abandoned.** The second frame beside Frank's photo, Peg's "This house doesn't say her name," Danny's long look — and nothing again all season. Danny grieves as a father-son pair with no wife, no in-laws, no funeral beat beyond the enemy's minute, and a careful viewer is left holding a question the show never picks up. Fix: either one line that makes the silence do work (Danny to Kieran at the night of writing, glancing at the frame, is enough), or cut the frame. QUIBBLE

12. **Episodes 9–10: Shaun's turn is absorbed as arithmetic.** "I never felt a thing... I said it anyhow" is a haunting line, and "I've given it a leg. I'll wait till someone counts it for me" is a bitter one — but between them Shaun gets no fear, no one at home, no cost beyond the queue. He risks becoming a data point in the culture's own bookkeeping. Fix: one humanising beat in episode 10 — a phone call he makes after the meter, or the first thing he asks to have written beside his name. QUIBBLE

13. **Season-long: Peg's diabetes is device-first.** The meter does magnificent plot work in three episodes, but Peg's own body never gets a fear beat; the disease exists for the instrument in the drawer. For a 72-year-old running a firm on certainty, one crack would deepen every scene the meter later detonates. Fix: the morning after Liam — the lancet in her shaking hand, no drop, Terry putting a sugar into her tea without a word. QUIBBLE

14. **Episodes 8 and 10: the Fever Company offer is never tempting enough to cost Rosie anything.** The recruiter is brisk, the card pocketed, the refusal implicit — but the offer never solves a live problem on screen (the family is broke, facing a warrant, three mouths unfed), so refusing it is free. A temptation that costs nothing is decoration. Fix: let the recruiter name, mildly, a signing bonus paid to family — at the exact week the Marlows need it — so the card in her locker in December is a road genuinely not taken. QUIBBLE

15. **Season-long: Kieran's father is a wound in the plan and nobody on screen.** "His dad left; the family is his whole world" is the stated root of the boy's hunger to belong, and no script ever references the man — not a Wednesday phone call that doesn't come, not a surname question, nothing. Tess's single-motherhood, which explains her arithmetic and her ferocity, floats unanchored. Fix: one line, anywhere in the first third — Kieran waiting on a call that doesn't come, and Terry filling the silence with soup. QUIBBLE

## Critique: continuity

1. **Terry's die contradicts the show's own arithmetic (Ep 10, sc. 8 and 12, against Ep 9, sc. 6).** In episode 9 Kieran states the rule on screen — "If you'd turned on the way back, you'd have come home straight. You'd be at Nan's fry-up" — and Rosie prices her own bite home on exactly that basis ("Five in six I stay as I am"). Terry's menu ("Six, I stay hungry. Anything else, I get my tea") treats the one-in-six turn on his outbound crossing as starving next door. But a turned crosser who turns arrives straight, and straight Terry still wears the bite: stand the minute on their shore, bite home on half his seconds, land at Grays cured, and eat everything. The "bad" roll is the cure this family has been denied for five years, and the open ending ("nobody knows whether Terry arrived straight or turned") quietly depends on the audience forgetting the escape the show taught them four days earlier; the plan itself frames the turn as "at worst one in six", so the error is systemic, not a typo. **Fix:** give the rule to its owner. Rosie, at the gate: "Turn on the way over, stand your minute, bite home — Nan fries you an egg." Terry: "I'm not biting, love. Whatever hand I land as, I've gone." The odds stay honest, and refusal rather than dice becomes the choice. SUBSTANTIVE

2. **Ray's spinner theory dies off-screen (Ep 3, sc. 5 → Ep 8, sc. 6).** In episode 3 Ray tells Tess "There's a way to kill a crosser that takes six months and looks like a diet... What it looks like, I know." That belief puts a car on the Marrable Street basement, sends Rosie in the long way at ten seconds, and arms the Aherns' mirror theory about Aoife. In episode 8 he says flatly, "There's no such person. There never was," and the knowledge map has him converted by episode 8 — but no scene shows or dates the change; the detective's engine simply switches off between episodes. It matters because Ray is the equal the Crime promise needs, and because Ray is the one person who held both halves for twenty-four years — his weight folder and the vaults' wall logs — so his conversion is where the theme lives in his hands. **Fix:** one beat in the flat. Ray, squaring the files: "Two thousand and two I watched a man run out under a road, and nobody did it to him. Twenty-four year of your weights in this folder, the banks' seconds in their logs. I had both halves and never laid them on one table. There's no spinner, Miss Marlow. There's time." SUBSTANTIVE

3. **Purfleet is west of Tilbury, not east (Ep 8, sc. 5).** "It turns east along the river road, and Tess follows it out of Tilbury, through Purfleet." On the real north bank the order is Purfleet–Grays–Tilbury; from Tilbury, Purfleet is upriver, west. The season's geography is otherwise exact (the A13 runs eastbound into Grays in Ep 5; the Gravesend–Tilbury ferry; Mucking east), so the one wrong compass word is exactly what an Essex audience will catch. **Fix:** "turns west along the river road," or move the bookmaker's parade to Grays. SUBSTANTIVE

4. **Terry is never mentioned between his step and the vote (Ep 10, sc. 13–15).** He steps off the world at about six in the morning; by afternoon the family is valuing the rolls as evidence, by evening Danny is opening the stove — and nobody names Terry, looks at the gloves folded on the rail, or counts his 70.6 ticket among the paper that would burn. The plan wants the going not to be the climax, and not-looking is in character, but twelve hours of total silence from Peg and Danny reads like a seam between writers rather than a family holding its breath. **Fix:** one touch — Danny glances at the rail before he says "Burn," or Kieran's "Give" lands a beat after his eyes find Terry's ticket in the pile. QUIBBLE

5. **The epilogue pays a line to the wrong character, in the wrong place (Ep 10, epilogue sc. 16).** "Three seconds. Inside the limit she once called soft." It was Danny who said "Three seconds. Soft" (Ep 1, sc. 10); Rosie answered him with "She's done four thousand. Never turned." And the correction sits in the action lines, where a camera cannot deliver it — a payoff no viewer receives. **Fix:** cut the clause, or pay it in dialogue (a scrub nurse: "You're quick." / Rosie: "Used to think this end was the soft one."). QUIBBLE

6. **Frank's weekly tickets on a job-day roll (Ep 10, sc. 2).** The loft rolls are introduced as "every job-day weigh-in," Terry says Farringdon was "the only one he did all year," yet Frank's tickets run "week on week" from 78.0 to 64.4. The curve needs its cause on screen, and the truest one is already in the house: Peg, who pricks her own finger every morning, weighing her husband weekly as he went off his food and reading it as "his sugar" — which makes the founding blindness worse and truer. **Fix:** one line in the loft — Kieran: "Why's he on here every week?" / Terry: "She weighed him. Fridays. For his sugar." / Peg, at the hatch, saying nothing. QUIBBLE

7. **"Aye" spreads through the Essex voices (Eps 5, 7, 8, 9, 10).** Tess says "Yeah" on the office step (Ep 7, sc. 14) and "Aye" in the same episode and ever after; Rosie answers Peg with "Aye" (Ep 8, sc. 9); Shaun ("Aye. Night, shrimp.") and Terry get it too. The Aherns and Den (Leeds) earn theirs; the Marlows' function words drift as episodes change hands, and function words are where a careful ear hears the writer change. **Fix:** fix one per character for the season — Tess and Rosie "yeah"; if "aye" is to be Terry's, give it to him once, early, as a personal tic. QUIBBLE

8. **Ray's month-up-front demand arrives after his suspension (Ep 7, sc. 2, against Ep 8, sc. 6).** On Thursday 21 May Terry reports "He wants a month up front or the van stops"; Ray was walked out of Victoria on the Tuesday, and the van stops for good on the Friday. A suspended man issuing commercial ultimatums through a launderette van only makes sense if the demand pre-dated the audit — Ray building cover for his own disappearance — and the episode never dates it. **Fix:** Terry: "He asked before Easter, mind — a month up front or it stops." (which also lets a sharp viewer catch the trick on re-watch). QUIBBLE

9. **Danny narrates the trap to the dark (Ep 6, cold open).** "Wait for the printer and the lad's gone. The plate prints after a load settles. I have to be in there first." The first and last fragments are a man talking himself into it (and rhyme with his solitary "Two seconds is enough" in Ep 5); the middle sentence is the rule, spoken for us, in a whisper that risks the boy it is meant to protect. **Fix:** keep the fragments, cut the mechanics; Danny stepping round the edge of the plate so the steel stays empty is the explanation. QUIBBLE

10. **The 7 a.m. radio knows too much (Ep 1, sc. 3).** The canvas left its crate at 3:15 in the morning, through a seal left intact, onto a shelf buried under beads when the wall tripped; by breakfast the BBC has police detail of "the crate... still banded, knotted and under its lead seal, and empty." Digging one crate out of a bead flood and opening it inside four hours, before the banks open, is brisk. The episode's notes fix the newspaper version (Peg prints the page mid-morning) but not the radio. **Fix:** give the bulletin less — "...a painting taken overnight from a bank in Mayfair; staff say the strongroom flooded" — and let the sealed-and-empty detail arrive with Aske on Saturday. QUIBBLE

11. **Peg never answers Rosie's taking of the rig (Ep 9, sc. 7 and 11).** "No Marlow crosses till I say" cost Tess her place at the table; Rosie takes a rig off the rack with the key still round Peg's neck, breaks the family's oldest rule, and Peg's whole response is "You went in the Deep... Same thing." By then Peg is broke and the sea is food, but one line would keep her scale of anger legible rather than merely absent. **Fix:** at the rack, not looking at her: "You took that down yourself, then." — and nothing more. QUIBBLE

12. **The ferry's midnight motion is unmotivated (Ep 7, sc. 1, 9, 11).** The deckhand says the last crossing has gone and the crew settle down with a telly; she grounds at 23:10, swims at midnight — then "noses out into the channel," lands Maggie on the far slip, and carries Tess round to "the Tilbury side" where her car already sits. A repositioning run would explain it, but nobody says so, and a viewer tracking the river will wonder what a done-for-the-night ferry is doing sailing a loop with two civilians aboard. **Fix:** one line from the deckhand — "She goes over to Gravesend for the morning; ride round if you like, it's warmer than my pontoon." QUIBBLE
