# 42 Round 5 - the four critiques

*Three GLM critics, each through one lens, and MiMo v2.6 Pro with fresh eyes, on the season after round 4.*

## Critique: story

1. **Episode 6, cold open (Danny's ambush at the safe).** The grab that turns Danny, costs him four fingers and drives everything to episode 7 rests on two unearned coincidences: Joe happens to come on the seventh night (for an envelope delivery Danny knows nothing about), and Joe happens to enter the black within Danny's 2.2-second field, even though the only evidence — the April ticket — pins Joe's arrival to a *minute* (02:04), not a second. The episode even names the correct causal cue and waves it away: "Wait for the printer and the lad's gone... I have to be in there first." A careful viewer will feel the dice being loaded at the second reversal's hinge. **Fix:** keep instruments out (as the plan demands) but give Danny a perceptible cue — the returning tow's thrum passing the wharf, then footsteps on the lane gravel, and Danny steps off the world when the footsteps stop — and plant one more unexplained −59.0 ticket in an earlier scene, so "he comes again" is a pattern Danny is staking out, not a hunch that happens to pay. **SUBSTANTIVE**

2. **Episode 10, scenes 13–14 (the vote).** The climax's stated logic is unsound. Tess says "Without the rolls, there's nothing to match Purfleet... No tickets, no trial," and Danny says "Burn them tonight... We stay free." But the depot's walls have already logged two body-masses to the hundred grams, cargo and all; the ward holds Rosie's 46.2; the big bridge printed the van going out heavy and coming back light; the state can simply weigh anyone it arrests. Burning the rolls destroys the drift, not the prosecution's path — so the family is voting on an escape that burning wouldn't buy, and a careful viewer will notice at the season's most important decision. **Fix:** one line from Ray or Maggie at the laying-out ("They can weigh the lot of us any day they like. It's not us those papers save — it's the count"), and soften Tess's absolutism ("no drift, and no ten-second cure for anyone, ever"), so the vote openly chooses the cure over leniency, which is what the scene already wants to be. **SUBSTANTIVE**

3. **Episode 9, scenes 4–6 (Rosie's crossing).** Rosie has the only rig in the house, sole access while the family raids Purfleet, and has known since the episode 7 table that ten seconds cures seven in ten — the very cure she begged Peg for two episodes earlier. She spends the rig on the Deep instead, and the episode acknowledges the choice ("I didn't go to be turned back, Kiz. I went shopping") but never the reasoning, which arrives only in episode 10 ("It's wrote down now. It'll keep"). At the moment of the season's biggest discovery, her motivation is opaque, and "why not your ten seconds first?" is the question every attentive viewer asks. **Fix:** one clause in scene 6 — Kieran asks it, and Rosie answers in her own idiom ("Three in ten I'm still this. This way Terry eats certain"), or have her restate her vow ("Not till it's counted proper. I said."). Episode 10's "It'll keep" then lands as confirmation. **SUBSTANTIVE**

4. **Episode 9, scenes 3 and 7 (the depot raid).** "Never go back twice" is chanted as family identity in episode 1, drives Mayfair and Marrable Street, and is even reasserted on the eve of the raid cycle (episode 7: "Never go back twice." / "It's a pantry, not a bank"). Then Terry goes in nine times in one night — the single safest tactic anyone in the family ever uses, and the exact inversion of the rule that helped kill Liam — and not one character notices, including Terry, whose eleven pointless hops make him the natural voice for it. A load-bearing plant reverses without a payoff, which blurs the season's own argument that the rules, not luck, did the damage. **Fix:** one sentence when the cases come home — Terry, counting them: "Nine goes in and out, and never a mark on me. All them years of never go back twice" — met with silence. **SUBSTANTIVE**

5. **Master plan §8 (episode 10, act one) against episode 10, scene 6.** The plan says the Purfleet walls "logged three bodies in the black"; the episode says two — one nine times, one twice — which is correct (Rosie crossed at Grays, never at Purfleet) and matches the eleven cases. The plan declares its numbers canonical and exact, so this is a breach of the show's own ledger. **Fix:** correct the plan to "two bodies — one nine times, one twice." **SUBSTANTIVE**

6. **Episode 7, scenes 4 and 12 (theme said aloud).** "Nobody ever asked why" is spoken twice in the loft, and Rosie's vow — "when it's been counted proper, and checked against somebody else's" — is the plan's thesis phrase almost verbatim. Neither uses a banned word, but together they brush rule 5 (the theme must live in events; no speech about asking). **Fix:** keep Terry's concrete answer ("He was off his food, love. Then he wasn't. That was enough for us") and cut the second "nobody ever asked why"; reword Rosie's vow into her own idiom ("counted on paper, and on their scales as well as ours"). **QUIBBLE**

7. **Episode 4, scene 2 (Tess's grip fine print).** Tess states the tightened rule with complete confidence — "Collar does nothing. It's skin, or it's nothing... a posted hand... slides off him like smoke" — but the show never shows where she learned the exclusions (cloth, pinches, sent hands). The clinic sign covers touch-in-transit; the ferry man covers the cost. **Fix:** attribute it in two beats — Rosie cites the clinic sign for "skin or nothing," and Terry or Shaun supplies the failed-attempt lore ("Fella on the ferry tried it through a cuff first, they say"). **QUIBBLE**

8. **Episode 7, scene 4 (the box marked 02).** Tess closes her father's year one beat after demonstrating on Lee's column that she can now read a hidden turning off a falling weight curve — and one night after running the whole season through the drift table. Denial is in-character and flagged, but it is unseeded: nothing earlier shows her turning away from Frank specifically. **Fix:** one flash in episode 6's montage — Ray's "I met what was left of him," and Tess turning from it — planting why she, of all people, cannot open 02 until the wall's 64.3 forces her. **QUIBBLE**

9. **Episode 9, scene 3 (the depot dog).** A dog at a state facility barks "level as a drip" for the length of an eleven-crossing raid and nobody comes; Peg's "Foxes" is the only acknowledgment. A Register site is also the one place a dog that alerts to the turned is doing its job, which makes the non-response stranger. **Fix:** one line placing it ("Gatehouse mutt. Barks at foxes all night") and let Peg's "Foxes" land as chosen self-deception with a beat on her face. **QUIBBLE**

10. **Episode 4, scene 8 (Aoife's fridge strip).** A month of weigh-wall readings is taped to the Aherns' fridge, but she went in "off our pontoon," and the only wall the family is shown to own is the lock-up's, up the lane. How the strip exists and reached them is unestablished, while Liam's equivalent strip (episode 5) is carefully sourced to their own kitchen printer. **Fix:** one line from Maggie or Con — she went in from the pontoon inside the lock-up wall's ten metres, so the kitchen printer kept her. **QUIBBLE**

11. **Episode 3, scene 16 (the pot revival).** One spoonful opens Rosie's eyes "after a beat," while Terry's meter takes an hour to climb from 2.8 to 5.6 on a whole crab. The timescales of recovery are inconsistent enough for a careful viewer to notice against the show's own instrument logic. **Fix:** stage it in two beats — the first spoon steadies her breathing; colour and coherence return over a minute of feeding, matching the meter's scale. **QUIBBLE**

12. **Episode 6, scene 4 (Joe's journey home).** After the grab Joe re-pins wherever he is in the black — next to the Marlow safe — yet arrives through the Ahern door at six in the morning "white to the lips, the river cold still on him." The tow is back by four; the gap and his exit point are unexamined. **Fix:** one line — "I came out in their yard and legged it for the tow" — or show him stepping out by the wharf. **QUIBBLE**

13. **Episode 2, act two (hops two to four).** Three consecutive hops each replay the same beat — out, "Straight?", window on the right, glance at the cutting — between the two scenes that carry new information (the false alarm and Terry's line to the pan). The middle of the act sags just where episode 1's briefing problem was supposed to be avoided. **Fix:** fold hops three and four into a single montage line each (the room emptying and refilling around one unchanged window), keeping the false alarm as the sequence's only full scene. **QUIBBLE**

## Critique: people

1. **Liam is a want without a person (Eps 1–5; see 1.5, 1.13, 2.8, 4.4, 5.11).** Liam gets a clear want — stop being "the big lad" — but no relationship that isn't instrumental: Rosie coaches him to her own end, Danny deflects him ("Best day of my life"), Tess rates him, even his dog-feeding is the diet. His one unguarded beat is the bathroom scale in the dark. Why it matters: he is the season's largest loss and the ratchet's hinge, and care is time plus access to the mind plus fear of loss; as written his death lands as horror and arithmetic (65.1, the minute, the printout) rather than as losing a boy we know. Fix: a recurring 30-second pairing in Eps 1–2 — Liam carrying Kieran's 20 kg test weight out to the plate at nine each night "till your arms are ready", with Kieran doing it alone afterwards — giving Liam a gentleness of his own and leaving residue in the log. **SUBSTANTIVE**

2. **Liam's mother does not exist (Ep 5, scenes 14–17; retroactively 1–5).** In ten episodes Danny's wife, Rosie and Liam's mother, is never mentioned, never established as dead or gone, and never present. Why it matters: at the birthday (1.13), at Rosie's collapse (3.15), and above all at Liam's running-out and the minute beside the wall, a careful viewer will ask where she is; in a family mapped to the cousin, her total silence at her son's death is a people-hole. Fix: one object and one line in Ep 1 (a second photo beside Frank's; Terry or Peg: "his mum would've had something to say about that"), and one empty gesture at the minute — Danny with his phone out and no one to ring. **SUBSTANTIVE**

3. **Episode 2's middle sags (scenes 7–9).** For a first-time audience the only threat attached to seven three-second hops is "will the painting flip back", which Ep 1's twist has drained to money; the Twitch is over and the false alarm is a single beat. Why it matters: suspense is care × threat × time, and the clock here is a sale, not a person — the season's softest stretch. Fix: make the Laundry debt the visible clock: the thin envelope from scene 4 called in mid-week (Dilys at the door Thursday, "He wants it Friday, Peg"), Terry's shelf counted down on screen, so every failed hop costs a day of Terry's food the audience can tally. **SUBSTANTIVE**

4. **Shaun is priced twice and never known (Eps 1–10).** He loses a leg (5.9) and then his hand of fate — he turns (9.7) — and he has no want, no wound and no scene of his own; his register is stoic one-liners ("Make it worth my leg"). Why it matters: he pays more flesh than anyone and we know him least; the plan gives Ray and Maggie scenes of their own and gives the twice-maimed cousin none, so the audience is asked to feel a price with no person under it. Fix: one office-step scene in Eps 1–3 of Shaun tying and splicing with Kieran (planting the knot knowledge he uses in 5.8) plus one line of small want of his own; then "I've given it a leg. I'll wait till someone counts it for me" (10.8) has a face and a person to wait with. **SUBSTANTIVE**

5. **Danny's week is missing (Ep 5 end → Ep 6 cold open).** Between the hand-clench "Two seconds is enough to grab someone" and the blind step at 02:04, his grief for Liam curdles into the ambush entirely off-screen; the cold open tells us he "has done the sum". Why it matters: the season's most premeditated violence arrives without our watching the man decide, and the displaced grief that later surfaces as an attack on Tess (7.12) reads colder than Danny is. Fix: a 20-second bridge — Danny at the yard fence at two in the morning watching the Ahern tow's lights pass upriver, opening and closing the good hand. **QUIBBLE**

6. **Danny learns Rosie coached Liam, and the beat is muffled (Ep 10, scene 7).** "I'm the one taught him to be hungry for it" is answered only by Danny's "And I said yes. Write that first" and "No reply". Why it matters: this is the collision wound since Ep 4 (the coaching was hidden from Danny specifically), the biggest deferred feeling-payoff left, and a careful viewer can miss that Danny registers it at all. Fix: hold the moment — Danny looks at her, Kieran's pencil stops, a breath — before he claims his own share first; or pay it silently at the fence in 11.12 with one look between them after Terry steps off. **QUIBBLE**

7. **Rosie goes opaque for three episodes (Eps 4–6).** Once the audience knows her secret, we watch her behave (Lisbon, the coaching, the casing) but are never inside her shame between 3.17 and 6.10. Why it matters: she is the inner circle's co-lead; four episodes of behaviour without one private beat risks her reading as a problem to be solved rather than a girl hiding. Fix: one dark-kitchen mirror of Terry's Ep 1 pot scene — 3 a.m., Rosie at the open fridge looking at bacon and eggs she will never taste, shutting it, eating from the pot standing up — placed in Ep 5 after the weigh-in. **QUIBBLE**

8. **Rosie's final choice is priced only in numbers (Ep 10, scene 12).** "Seven in ten, it's Nan's fry-up for me" is flat; nothing shows what food means to her before she refuses it for ever, and Terry carries the appetite-grief for the whole family. Why it matters: staying turned is the season's last free human choice, and without a sensory beat of what she's refusing it plays as arithmetic plus altruism. Fix: at the fence, Peg's bacon butties for the waiting party; Rosie holds one for the warmth the way she held the mug of tea in 2.14, and hands it untouched to Kieran before she speaks. **QUIBBLE**

9. **The pursuer vanishes from the last movement (Eps 8–10).** Ray is suspended off-screen between 7 and 8, the Ahern fight ends at the ferry, and the Monday threat has no face: two nameless investigators who appear once (10.6) and never return. Why it matters: the Crime promise is a long fight between equals and a pursuer who looks like less than he is; after Ep 8 the pressure is hunger plus a warrant, both abstract, and the final night's clock has no human on it. Fix: give the woman investigator one returning beat — a Sunday-morning phone call ("Nine o'clock tomorrow, Mrs Marlow, with the warrant") heard over the writing. An audience test would settle whether the finale feels under-pursued without it. **QUIBBLE**

10. **Terry's silence is never weighed (from 3.16).** He confesses it once — "Every night, Peg. Two fingers" — and no one ever asks the one turned man in the house why he said nothing for a fortnight while they cheered. Why it matters: his concealment is the same wound as Frank's and Rosie's, and the family's great audit in Ep 10 skips the member whose motive the audience most understands; it also short-changes the Peg–Terry reckoning. Fix: one line in the night of writing — Terry: "And I knew about our Rosie and said nothing. Put that under mine" — which sets up Peg's "Terry. Turned. 2021" an act later. **QUIBBLE**

11. **Maggie is off-stage for two episodes (8–9).** The season's best new relationship goes dark after the ferry, while her steelyard is cited in Ep 9 as the arbiter of truth, and her offer — "My half's in here. Whenever, Tess" — is never taken on screen. Why it matters: the two weighers are the proof that checking needs two people, and they vanish exactly when checking becomes the plot. Fix: one phone call in 9.11 — after the flour proves the plate lies, Tess rings Maggie and gets Joe's steelyard number read out to her — the alliance honoured in voice, her 2 a.m. arrival in Ep 10 unchanged. **QUIBBLE**

12. **Con is unmourned (after 5.6).** Terry's only friend, five years of nightly gravy calls, is covered in one table scene and dropped in the week Liam dies. Why it matters: Con is the death Terry owns a share of and the mirror of his whole condition; burying him wastes two episodes of friendship-building. Fix: one shot in Ep 6 — Terry cooking Con's gin gravy for the family's tea, tasting nothing, saying nothing, nobody noticing. Twenty seconds, no dialogue. **QUIBBLE**

13. **Tess and Kieran's warmth is by report (Eps 1–7).** Their bond is restraint and numbers, and the physical tenderness is asserted second-hand ("the way Tess does", via Rosie in 8.9) rather than shown. Why it matters: the pencil handoff in 10.9 is the season's closing image of inheritance and pays hardest if the audience has seen one direct warm beat first. Fix: in 2.13, after "Same as every day", let Tess — who never helps — hold the torch while he writes, or quietly pocket his first flat-line ticket, discovered in the prison scene of the epilogue. **QUIBBLE**

14. **Kieran's side of the exile is skipped (Ep 8).** His mother is put out of the house in 7.12 and lives in a car for a fortnight; his feelings are one strangled "Mum." and then nothing until he unbolts the door in 9.11. Why it matters: he is twelve and the mother–son thread is the season's spine; without him the exile is only Tess's story for two episodes. Fix: one beat — through the office window at night, headlights pause at the lane's end and pull away (Tess checking), and Kieran, at his graph, sees them go. **QUIBBLE**

15. **"Nobody ever asked why" twice in one episode (7.4 and 7.9).** Terry says it in the loft; Tess repeats it on the ferry. Why it matters: the loft exchange already dramatizes the theme perfectly — Tess asks "Why?" and Terry's answer is the diagnosis — and the repeat starts pointing at the meaning; one instance lives in events, two underlines. Fix: cut the ferry line; let Tess give Lee's seconds and let Maggie circle the cross in silence. **QUIBBLE**

16. **The cure is named aloud a little often (7.12, 10.7, 10.12, epilogue).** "When it's been counted proper, and checked against somebody else's"; "It's wrote down now. It'll keep"; "checked against Maggie's" — four spoken formulations of the family's new habit in the last two episodes. Why it matters: the habit is the theme made practice and is mostly shown (the night of writing, the steelyard, the two meters), but the spoken versions edge toward stating the lesson. Fix: keep Rosie's Ep 7 vow and Kieran's epilogue pencil note; mute the rest into action — Tess already lays the objects out in 10.13, so trim her lines to the evidential facts. **QUIBBLE**

## Critique: continuity

1. **Episode 9, scene 6 (Kieran sorts the night's tickets).** Rosie's two plate tickets from her crossing read 45.7 each (per the episode's own numbers), the minus ticket reads −46.2, and Rosie says the Whitechapel ward scale "had me at the same on Wednesday... to the gram." Three numbers for one body, half a kilo apart, all in the hands of the one character who reads every ticket to ten grams — and Kieran squares them and says nothing. Five scenes later, the flour-on-the-brass-balance beat then plays as the family's *first* discovery that the plate lies. This blunts the episode's key reveal, has Kieran act against his established self, and lets Rosie — who crossed on the plate precisely so it would be "written" — sit on a contradiction for thirty-six hours. Fix: have Kieran read the plate tickets aloud ("Forty-five seven... twice") and let Rosie explain it wrongly ("Wet clothes. Weed in me hood."), turning the gap into the plant the flour scene answers; or have her cross from beside the plate, as Danny does in episode 6, so only the minus prints. SUBSTANTIVE

2. **Episode 6, scene 5 against episode 2, scenes 18–21.** Rosie hands the family the Obverse records room — "I asked for the toilet" — keypad, steel door and wall panel included, all supposedly cased during her episode 2 visit. But episode 2 shows her reception → waiting room → Osei → Ray → out, with no detour and not one glance down a corridor. A whole act (Tess's dip, the protocol, the second reversal) turns on knowledge whose acquisition the audience never saw even hinted — the exact fault the owner's own Catch notes condemn. Fix: one shot in episode 2 scene 18 — Rosie asks the receptionist for the ladies, and the camera tracks her down the lower-ground corridor past RECORDS. STAFF ONLY and the plain steel panel, her eyes flicking, before she pulls the door to. SUBSTANTIVE

3. **Episode 8, scene 7 against episode 9, scene 3.** At the depot vote Terry says on camera, "Nobody's sending me in on my seconds, mind. But I'll drive." Two weeks later he is belted and running nine two-second legs, capped with "Two's all I've ever asked for" — a direct reversal of his stated refusal, arrived at off-screen with no shown cause. Terry is a principal; the reversal is the most poignant thing that happens to him (the two-second man finally useful), and a careful viewer will remember the refusal. Fix: bridge it — in episode 9 scene 1 Danny cannot pull crates and cross, Peg looks down the table, and Terry stands and takes a belt off the rack himself ("Two-second legs. That's me. Always has been."); or narrow the episode 8 refusal to long crossings ("Nobody's sending me in deep"). SUBSTANTIVE

4. **Episode 9, scene 11.** Tess says, "I gave you your raid. Then I came" — an admission that she knew the depot job was coming, knew eight seconds is one in two, and stayed away while it turned Shaun — and the room, Danny included, lets it pass in silence, though Danny tore into her for exactly this pattern ("And Liam? Did your sums do Liam too?") at the stove in episode 7. A confession the season has built as explosive gets no reaction. Fix: one flat line and no more — Danny, not looking up: "Aye. You let it run." QUIBBLE

5. **Epilogue, scene 18.** Beside Frank's photo hangs "Terry's last ticket — 70.6", annotated "71.4 on Maggie's" — but 70.6 was never printed or spoken on screen; the only Terry weighing we saw was the steelyard's 71.4. The framed object asks the audience to remember a moment it never had, and it is a moment the season owes: the man whom "nobody asks to step on the plate" (episode 1, scene 13) finally stepping on it. Fix: one shot in the night of writing — Terry, last of all, on the little bridge in his socks, the printer chattering, nobody laughing. QUIBBLE

6. **Episodes 4–7 (the cure question).** Peg settles it in episode 3 — "You'll go again. Short ones. It'll come up" — Terry's "Eleven times, Peg" disputes it, and then for four episodes nobody hops Rosie and nobody says why not, while the family chases £180,000 and Liam dies for it. The obvious question ("hop her — it's free") goes unasked until episode 8. Fix: one exchange in episode 4 scene 4 — Rosie: "Hop me, then." Peg: "And have you end like your uncle? Eleven goes and still hungry? We'll buy it proper." — which also seeds Peg's about-face when the money runs out. QUIBBLE

7. **Episode 5, scene 10 against episodes 6–7.** Declan, over the adrift tugs and the blood on the stairs: "Not enough." The Ahern side of the war then goes silent for a month — no retaliation, no gesture — until Maggie's letter, and nothing on screen shows Maggie holding him back. A promised escalation that never comes reads as a dropped thread rather than containment. Fix: one line of containment — episode 6 scene 4, Maggie to Joe: "Not a word to your grandad. He'd have us over there with torches" — or Joe on the ferry: "He wanted the yard burnt. Mum gave him the tide books instead." QUIBBLE

8. **Episode 3, scenes 2 and 5, against episode 7, scene 7.** Maggie's tip parks a car "outside an empty shop on Marrable Street" — the Marlows' exact planned entry — but the only Ahern surveillance shown is Joe at the bus stop and in the mews; nobody is shown clocking the TO LET, and the empty-shop plan is voiced only at the family table. The tip's precision exceeds the Aherns' demonstrated knowledge. Fix: one shot in episode 3 scene 2 — the hooded lad on the far corner while Rosie sits out the jeweller's van beside the empty shop. QUIBBLE

9. **Episode 7, scene 13 against episode 8, scene 1.** Terry counts the shelf on Friday morning — "Nine days. Less, there being three" — and on Sunday the family splits the last white tub. The arithmetic only closes if he means "nine days' worth at one mouth, therefore three at three," which the line doesn't say cleanly; read straight, there is food into June. Fix: have Terry state the division — "Three days, this. Nine days' worth, and there's three of us." QUIBBLE

10. **Episode 5, scene 2.** Ray pockets the delivered bullet in a handkerchief — "I'll keep this" — and it never returns: not in his files, not in any charge, though it is a firearm-sourced projectile removed from a scene he attended unofficially, and the Ahern war rests partly on it. Fix: one beat in episode 10 — the bullet in an evidence bag among Ray's surrendered papers, or the desk sergeant lifting it from the last box — or cut the pocketing and leave it standing on the painted pan. QUIBBLE

11. **Episode 4, scene 11 against episode 9, scene 11.** The Trading Standards letter drops through the door on the Friday-morning post (10 April, by scene order), but Tess reads it as "Dated the eleventh of April": mail dated the day after it arrives. Nothing rests on the day, though the date is read aloud. Fix: date the letter the tenth, or move the post beat to Saturday the eleventh. QUIBBLE

12. **Voice, episodes 9–10.** "Aye" begins as Ray's and Maggie's word (and old Terry's, once, in grief), but by the last two episodes Tess says it three or four times ("Aye", episodes 9 scenes 11 and 13; episode 10 scene 13), and Shaun and Terry pick it up as well. Tess's Essex register is "yeah", numbers and silence; "Aye" blurs her into the detective and the Gravesend weigher — exactly the three voices the season otherwise keeps distinct. Fix: give Tess "Yeah" or nothing, and ration "Aye" to Ray, Maggie and Terry. QUIBBLE

13. **The seam between episodes 3 and 4.** Episode 3's final image is Danny reaching for his coat under "Blood pays blood"; episode 4 opens ten minutes later with him coming back from a practice hop, coat over his arm, the violent errand already rerouted in the ellipsis. The recontextualization ("Seeing if I've still got it") is sound, but the swerve itself — who or what stopped him — is never played, and the season's hottest cliffhanger cools off-screen. Fix: half a scene — Peg's voice in the dark as his hand finds the coat ("And what does two seconds buy you over there, lad?"), Danny's hand coming off it, the pit instead; or let the quake catch him with the coat half on. QUIBBLE

## Critique: mimo

1. **Episode 4, Scene 2 — "Then you'd be eating it."** Tess's line asks the audience to chain three rules in one beat: the painting turned with its carrier (Ep 1–2 cutting), therefore stolen food would turn with whoever carries it, therefore if Danny turns during the crossing the food becomes the wrong hand for his new body. The nod at the catalogue cutting is a good visual shorthand and Terry eating his tub in the background seals it, but the inferential load is high for a first-time viewer, and the scene does not pause on it. Danny simply sits back and drops the plan, which can read as him giving up too easily rather than understanding a fatal flaw. **Fix:** give Danny one line of working-out before he sits back — something concrete and in his voice ("If I come back wrong, so does the grub — and I'm eating it") — so the logic is spoken once and the cutting is the confirmation, not the only route. **SUBSTANTIVE**

2. **Episode 10, Scene 2 — the audit-roll box marked "02" contains September 2001 data.** The scene opens with "September: FRANK 78.0," which the canonical numbers place after Farringdon in September 2001. Yet Terry calls the box "your dad's year" and it sits in a sequence labelled by calendar year. A viewer keeping track of the dates will wonder whether Frank's four-second job was 2001 or 2002, and the canonical numbers say 2001 while the box says 02. **Fix:** either label the scene "September '01" explicitly in the action line so the audience tracks the span, or have Terry say "that's the box that runs to the March" so the cross-year filing is named once. **QUIBBLE**

3. **Episode 1, Cold Open — "Something heavy passes in the dark."** Rosie feels a transient pull at about 5.0 seconds ("the way a lorry passing too close tugs at a car"), then it is gone. But the plan says Joe was six metres away throughout, and gravity at a constant distance would produce a constant pull, not a passing one. A viewer replaying the season for Joe's role may find the momentary tug inconsistent with "six metres off throughout." **Fix:** make the pull wax and wane as she moves toward and away from the shelf — "the pull thickens as she reaches the crate and thins as she turns" — so it reads as relative distance changing, not a body passing and vanishing. **QUIBBLE**

4. **Episode 2, Scene 4 — Peg's copy theory ("you hold it loose, you let it roll about").** Peg asserts the painting can turn independently of Rosie in the black. The established physics (cargo turns with its carrier) say otherwise, and the audience should already know this from the catalogue cutting in Episode 1. The scene is aware of this — Liam asks the right question and Peg waves it away — but for a first-time viewer the theory may sound like valid world-building rather than folk error. The seven failed hops then risk reading as bad luck rather than a wrong premise. **Fix:** one small beat after Liam's question, let Tess look at the cutting on the wall and say nothing, so the audience sees someone in the room notice the contradiction without anyone naming it. **QUIBBLE**

5. **Episode 8 — Tess calls the Register to report a "turned woman" without telling Rosie.** The scene plays as Tess's flaw (acting on her own sums again), and Rosie's "Nine point four, Tess" at the gate is the rebuke. But the outcome — Rosie is fed, walks out, finds the T-meter and the message — is largely positive, which risks teaching the audience that Tess's unilateral decision was fine. The plan says this is Tess "making things worse"; the episode shows it as accidentally productive. **Fix:** give Rosie one line at the gate that names the wrongness in her own terms — not just the number but the method ("You didn't ask me") — so the audience registers the flaw even though the outcome is mixed. **SUBSTANTIVE**

6. **Episode 7 — the graph on the ferry timetable.** Tess writes "eleven of ours" and Maggie writes "seven of ours," and they mark ticks and crosses. But the audience has not been shown most of these eighteen crossings; only a handful (Maureen, Lee, Aoife, Rosie, Terry) have names or scenes behind them. The graph's force depends on the audience feeling the weight of eighteen lives, and many of those names appear for the first time in this scene or in the loft scene earlier the same day. **Fix:** seed two or three of the unnamed turns earlier in the season — a framed cutting, a name on Peg's chalk board wiped long ago, a photo in a drawer — so the graph gathers ghosts the audience has already met. **QUIBBLE**
