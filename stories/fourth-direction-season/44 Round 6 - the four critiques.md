## Critique: story

1. **Episode 1, scene 18 and Episode 3, scene 5 — the twist's key plant is delivered twice, and once too early.** Ray's "If the painting turned, so did whoever carried it" closes episode 1 as the hook, then arrives again word-for-word at the top of the haul road in episode 3. The master plan's own revision (section 15, item 12) moved this line to episode 3 "not episode 1", precisely so an alert viewer would not hold the answer before episode 2 ended; the knowledge map still promises the audience learns it at 3, yet episode 3's plants section credits the episode 1 delivery ("heard only by Tess"). As written, the whole audience — Ray has just proved the canvas original, and they alone saw the catalogue cutting — is told Rosie turned before episode 2 begins, so the hops and the tests play as the family catching up: the exact failure the revision was made to prevent, with the plant then spent twice. **Fix:** cut the line from episode 1 (end the hook on "I met what was left of him", which plants Frank and the Laundry and nothing else) and keep the pit-scene delivery alone; if the hook needs Ray's menace, give him something oblique that does not state the inference. SUBSTANTIVE.

2. **Episodes 6–7 — the ferry hinge rests on luck.** Tess carries Maggie's sealed envelope for two weeks and slits it at 8 a.m. on the very morning of the date printed inside it ("Thursday 21st"); nothing on screen tells her it is the day. She is the family's planner, the pantry is emptying, the Laundry's bill has trebled, and the season's second reversal turns on this meeting. "Two weeks I've had that in my pocket" admits the delay without explaining the timing: opened Monday she has three days; opened Friday the ferry is gone. **Why it matters:** a coincidence this size at the reversal's hinge makes the character's passivity look like machinery. **Fix:** show her opening it the night Peg puts her out, reading the date, and spending the fortnight deciding; the car scene becomes a re-read, and the line becomes "two weeks deciding". SUBSTANTIVE.

3. **Episodes 5–7 — the enemy's war stops off-screen.** Declan's last hostile beat is "Not enough" on the quay (episode 5, scene 10) after the tugs go adrift. After that the Marlows strike again (the lock-up money, a boy dying in the Aherns' own wall), and for four weeks the Aherns mount nothing, say nothing and are absent until the envelope. "Blood pays blood" is established as both banks' law — Frank taught Declan — and the ceasefire that makes the ferry possible arrives by silence. The show also never establishes whether Declan knows a Marlow died in his wall: if he does, his inaction wants a cause; if he does not, Maggie is carrying the war alone and we are never told. **Fix:** one beat in episode 6's Ahern kitchen — Declan at the charts wanting to go over, Maggie stopping him ("They're burying a boy, Dad. The river's had its penny"), which also seeds her envelope. SUBSTANTIVE.

4. **Episode 4, scene 13 / Episode 5, scene 1 — the bullet's quiet ten days.** Joe delivers the bullet at 02:04 on 11 April; Peg finds it on the 21st. The scene that discovers it also establishes the routine that should have found it sooner: "Where's Danny? He's to put his hand in the safe for the money." The Laundry is paid weekly, the fence money is in the safe, and Danny's blind hand must fish that envelope past a bullet standing upright on the painting — the painting the Aherns later lay their letters on. A blind hand could miss it once; twice strains. **Fix:** one line — the fence money lived in the float tin until it settled, so the safe went unfished; or Danny's reaches are always for the cash box at the front, the painting flat at the back. QUIBBLE.

5. **Episode 9, scenes 4–6 — the minus ticket's mechanics.** Rule 14 lets a plate print a body's true mass as a minus when the body is in the black over it. Rosie spends roughly two seconds in the black over the plate and over a minute one leaf over, eight metres up in the neighbours' sea, where the show's own cube-falloff (a mountain's worth of rock next door is worth 1.1 per cent) should reduce her pull to almost nothing. As staged, the 00:45 ticket can be read either way, and two printer habits the show has established ("it prints whenever a load settles") go missing: a settle ticket when she steps out of the black into the sea, and any account of her return soaked — jumper, weed and a crab should put a kilo or two on her second plate ticket, in the one scene whose point is the gram. **Fix:** stage the printer chattering the instant she vanishes, pinning the ticket to her two black seconds, and let Kieran note her return reads heavier by "a crab and a jumper" — truer to the cargo rule anyway. QUIBBLE.

6. **Episode 1, scene 1 / Episode 9 — the rig's mass, and the lady's.** Every budget is computed from plate weights taken rigged (44.0, 41.8, 62.4), so the rig is inside the field's count; but episode 9's gram-match (ward 46.2 in a t-shirt, plate 45.7 with Tess's rig buckled on, minus 46.2) only closes if the rig weighs nothing. And "Eleven two, less the lady. Job's nine four" makes the painting cost 1.8 seconds — nine kilos of unframed canvas. Nothing on screen states either weight, so nothing breaks, but this season's pleasure is that its numbers check out. **Fix:** one canonical note in section 14 (a Marlow rig weighs so-many kilos and is weighed with the crosser), and give the canvas a stated weight that makes Tess's arithmetic honest. QUIBBLE.

7. **Episode 1, scene 16 — the expert is too sure.** Dr Farrant holds authentic canvas, old ground, correct cracking and correct varnish, photographs the tacking edge, and calls it a copy off a mis-projected slide without a flicker of "this shouldn't exist". A conservator facing original materials in a mirrored composition would hedge or demand lab days; her flat certainty is what the failed sale needs, and Aske's shoe is already moving. **Fix:** let her hedge and let Aske close it — "It's wrong, is what it is." / "Copy, then." — keeping the verdict, keeping the buyer's incentive, and letting Ray's later linen reading look like police doing the expert's job rather than the expert doing hers badly. QUIBBLE.

8. **Episode 8 onward — the pursuer switches off.** Ray is suspended in episode 8, and with him the Met: for the last third the family faces no police clock until they bring one on themselves, and the Crime promise's "long fight between equals" quietly becomes one scene with two polite Register investigators. The Marrable Street and Mayfair wall logs and the bullet all sit in Ray's unit, and his suspension is the only reason nobody is matching them to the family. **Fix:** one beat — a younger officer inheriting Ray's files, or Ray hearing that someone has — so the finale's choice is made against a police threat still ticking, not only against a Monday warrant from the Register. QUIBBLE.

9. **Episode 7, scene 12 — the method said aloud.** Rosie: "when it's been counted proper, and checked against somebody else's, I'm going first." The second half of that sentence is the season's thesis in a character's mouth; everywhere else the idea lives in flour on a balance, two meters on one drop of blood, a steelyard beside a lying plate, and a boy's pencil. One line is not a speech, but it is the closest the season comes. **Fix:** "when it's been counted proper, I'm going first" — the checking can stay behaviour, as it does in Maggie's "till somebody's counted it", which is hers and earned. QUIBBLE.

10. **Episode 10 — the finale's clock.** Inside about thirty-six story hours the episode carries Frank's reveal, the 2001 letter, the investigators' deadline, the night of writing, the steelyard, the baking-tray deduction, two crossings, the rolls' meaning, the vote, the police station and a four-scene epilogue. The vote is the season's true climax and Terry's step its strangest act, and both sit between a geography lesson and a delivery. **Fix:** fold scene 17 into two shots over scenes 16 and 18, and give the vote one more pass round the table; only timing could prove it plays long, but nothing else in the episode is loose enough to cut instead. QUIBBLE.

## Critique: people

## People and feeling: findings

1. **Ep 2 sc 14 → Eps 5–10 — the season plants its own wound-rhyme and never fires it.** Tess tells Rosie: "Fourteen, I was. Nan stood me on that plate and everybody clapped… I was hungry." Kieran then starves himself for six episodes under the same claps, and when Tess finds the *me* column (Ep 9 sc 11) she stops at "He's been making weight under your roof." She never connects it to her own fourteen, never owns that her code made her son do what was done to her, and no one ever apologises to the boy. The season's warmest bond is left with a graph and a handed-over pencil and no reckoning. *Fix:* in Ep 9, after "Since Easter," Tess sits on the camp bed: "I was fourteen when she stood me on that plate." Kieran: "You say it was a different week." Tess: "It was." Twenty seconds; the rhyme detonates.

2. **Ep 7 sc 12 — the cost of Rosie's Mayfair lie is never owned by anyone.** "Something took hold of me" (Ep 3) is the sentence Danny reached for his coat on; it produced the cold-store raid that killed Con and Shaun's reprisal that cost the leg. When Rosie retracts it at the table, the room absorbs the shame and moves straight to Mynors. Terry — whose only friend died on that story — says nothing. The first reversal's body count is left off the emotional ledger; a careful viewer does the sum the characters never do. *Fix:* after "That's all it ever was. Shame," Terry, not looking up from his gloves: "Con went to his chair on that story, love." Silence. Then Myners.

3. **Eps 6–10 — the pursuer's hunt ends off-screen.** The Crime promise is a long fight between equals; Ray's last act as a hunter is the rig seizure in Ep 5. His spinner theory — his engine since Ep 3 — dies in a reported line in the flat ("There's no such person"), and we never see him close the case, choose the family over it, or lay his Mayfair log, linen photos and bullet beside theirs. His turn from pursuer to accomplice happens to him (suspension), not through him. *Fix:* in the flat, Tess asks "When did you stop believing it?" and Ray opens the MARLOW folder to its last entry, dated the night of the grab, one line in his own hand. The hunt ends on screen, in the pursuer's handwriting.

4. **Ep 9 sc 12 — Danny's silence when Peg confesses the lucky breakfast.** Peg says "I made him breakfast," the room holds its breath, and the scene cuts around Danny to sums and chalk. This is the woman whose plate killed his son, and Danny — whose flaw is that every loss is an attack, who turned on Tess over exactly this two episodes earlier — gives nothing. That is against who he is, and the hole is visible. *Fix:* no words. Danny gets up, lifts the fry-up pan off the ring, and sets it in the sink — echoing Peg scraping the clean plate in Ep 5. Then the scene can move on.

5. **Ep 10 sc 15 — Tess's surrender meets logistics, not family.** The loyal daughter walks in first, claims twenty-five years, holds out her wrists, and her mother's only line is "They're in order." Nobody protests, nobody shares guilt, nobody touches her; the Peg–Tess exile of Ep 7 ("you won't plan for this family again") is never settled — Peg simply lets Tess plan again without a word. Care is social; the season's reckoning is paid by one woman alone. *Fix:* as Tess holds out her wrists, Peg steps up beside her and adds something of her own to the counter: "And the letter I kept off his dad. Put that in an' all." Peg shares the blame out loud; the repair happens in the same breath as the surrender.

6. **Eps 1–5 — Shaun loses his leg without a want or a wound.** He is "wiry cousin": jokes at the table, a mug on the stair, the war's enthusiasm. The knots job — his craft showcase and his maiming — lands on a cypher, and his Ep 9 turning lands on a stoic we've never been inside. We don't care before the threat, so the season's second-worst injury is the one we feel least. *Fix:* one plant. Ep 3: Shaun on the stairs teaching Kieran a mooring knot while the house worries upstairs — "Round turn and two half hitches. Only knot your great-grandad ever showed me." His craft is established; Ep 5's ninth line and the leg become the price of the thing he loved.

7. **Ep 8 sc 7 / Ep 9 sc 7 — the family's last crime is against the institution the season taught us to love, and nobody weighs it.** The Register fed Terry five years, fed Den, is feeding Rosie at Whitechapel while her family votes to rob its pantry; the Army cases are known to the audience and hidden from the family by prose. The Crime promise is criminals worth watching, and this job — stealing sick people's dinners — goes through without one character naming what it is. *Fix:* at the Ep 8 vote, Rosie: "That shed's what's been feeding me three weeks. Den's leeks get paid out of it." The family votes anyway, hungry — the line crossed, and felt.

8. **Eps 6–10 — the Tess–Rosie rupture is never repaired.** "I gave you nine" (Ep 6) is answered only by "Nine point four, Tess" at the gate (Ep 8), a slap that stands as their last word on it. In Ep 9 they exchange four lines; in Ep 10 they are on opposite riverbanks. The aunt who planned the job and the niece who hid the turn — the season's central guilt — part unreconciled. *Fix:* at the police station door, Rosie says it again, softly: "Nine point four, Tess." Tess: "I know it to the tenth." Same words, returned as a bond.

9. **Ep 7 sc 4 — "Nobody ever asked why" edges toward stating the theme.** Terry's line summarises the culture's failure one notch too neatly, and Tess's "Why?" right after does the work better. *Fix:* cut the line; keep "Why?" / "He was off his food, love. Then he wasn't. That was enough for us." The audience supplies the rest. QUIBBLE

10. **Ep 6 sc 1 — Danny's choice at Joe's heart isn't legible as a choice.** The plan intends "reaches for a boy's heart and chooses something slower," but the lethal option is never established, so the two-beat hover reads as menace, not mercy. *Fix:* before he closes on the wrist, Danny's eyes go to the camp bed — the sleeping boy three feet away. The refusal becomes visible. QUIBBLE

11. **Eps 6–9 — Declan vanishes.** The one character who never learns is last on screen in Ep 5 and returns only for the epilogue's final chill (chalk, cheers, unread front page). Four episodes away dulls the season's last image. *Fix:* one beat — Ep 8's radio intercut includes the Ahern kitchen: Declan switches the tea message off; Maggie writes the date down. QUIBBLE

12. **Ep 10 sc 14 — Danny's "Burn" to "Give" turns on a glance at the exercise book.** The reversal is earned by Ep 9's "Not him. Not after Liam," but the season's protecting father gives up the family's freedom in one look. *Fix:* he reads the column's heading aloud — "*Me*." — one word, then: "Give." QUIBBLE

13. **Eps 6–10 — Joe and Danny never share a word after the grab.** The boy who was wrenched round and the man who lost his fingers for it stand in the same room at the steelyard and part in silence. *Fix:* as Danny steps off the board, Joe puts out a hand to steady him. Danny takes it. No dialogue; the first touch since the black. QUIBBLE

14. **Ep 10 scs 2–5 — Peg never reacts to being deceived, only to her own drawer.** Frank let her believe weight killed him for twenty-four years — the mirror of Rosie — and Peg's feeling stops at "He never saw this. I never let him." *Fix:* one line at the ticket: "Twenty-four year he sat at this table and let me think it was his weight." QUIBBLE

15. **Ep 5 sc 17 — Liam's mother arrives only at his death.** The second frame and "This house doesn't say her name" introduce a grief at the exact moment it can do no work, and it is never touched again. *Fix:* put the frame on the office wall in Ep 1 with one early glance from Liam, or cut the beat. QUIBBLE

16. **Ep 8 sc 9 — Peg's oldest code breaks passively.** Twenty-five years of "we don't go to them" ends with Rosie self-authorising at the sound of a diesel and Peg managing four bleak words. The founder's surrender deserves one active beat of feeling. *Fix:* Peg hands Rosie the blue enamel pot to take. QUIBBLE

17. **Ep 10 scs 6–14 — the Monday warrant never flexes.** The finale's clock is announced at noon Saturday and never intrudes; the night of writing and the vote run under no pressure from it. Suspense wants the deadline to bite once. *Fix:* during the vote, headlights at the gate that turn out to be a lost lorry — a false alarm that stops every heart and makes "Give" urgent rather than noble. QUIBBLE

## Critique: continuity

1. **Episode 10, scene 2 (the loft) — Frank's falling year sits on the wrong roll. SUBSTANTIVE.** The scene unrolls "the 2002 roll" (the box marked 02, "where Tess left it in May") and reads straight down it: "September: FRANK 78.0", week-on-week decline, "The new year", then spring and the last ticket, 64.4. But the season's own chronology — Terry's "Farringdon. Four seconds" in September (this scene), Ray's "Marrable Street. Spring of two thousand and two" (scene 4), and ep 7's loft roll dated "Tuesday, March the ninth, '09" confirming calendar-year boxes — puts the turn in September **2001** and the death in spring **2002**. September's tickets belong to the box marked 01; no calendar-year roll can hold both the September and the following spring, and a dead man prints no ticket in September 2002. This is the third reversal's physical evidence, in the scene where the audience is being taught to read these rolls. *Fix:* pin rolls 01 and 02 side by side — 78.0 and the autumn on 01, the new year and 64.4 on 02, Peg's pencil marks straddling the join — which is also the better image: the decline crossing two boxes nobody opened. Terry's "that's your dad's year" (ep 7) still holds.

2. **Episode 7, scene 7 (the ferry) — Joe's Mayfair loiter breaks the drift the season is built on. SUBSTANTIVE.** Maggie says Joe "sat in the dark off your shelf with a stopwatch, timing what he could of your girl." To time Rosie's 9.4-second crossing he must have been unpinned for ten seconds or more; the table Tess steals in ep 6 — and the graph the two women draw in this very scene — puts that at about seven in ten of turning. Joe came home straight, the Aherns "time everything to the second" precisely to stay off long crossings, and nobody on either bank ever does this sum. It quietly contradicts "long crossings turn people" in the scene that proves it, and makes the careful Aherns reckless about their own son without cause. *Fix:* Maggie gives the duration — "Two goes, under three seconds each, one at either end of her; he counted her middle by heart" (the wall still logs two bodies at 3:12) — or Tess, running her sums on the ferry, says aloud what odds the boy ran and how lucky he was, and Maggie carries it.

3. **Episode 9, scene 7 — a misattributed line makes the exchange unreadable. SUBSTANTIVE.** As written: "ROSIE: You went in the Deep. / PEG: You went in the Deep. / ROSIE: I went in a sea, Nan." Rosie accuses her grandmother of the crossing Rosie herself made; Peg then echoes it back as an accusation. The exchange only parses if Peg speaks first, and it is the scene that first names the sea — any table read stops dead on it. *Fix:* delete the first line, or reassign it: "PEG: You went in the Deep. / ROSIE: I went in a sea, Nan. / PEG: Same thing."

4. **Episode 10, scene 16 (epilogue) against episode 8, scene 13 — the Fever Company card cannot be "still standing where she left it in June." SUBSTANTIVE.** Rosie stood the card on her locker shelf at the Whitechapel ward in June and walked out leaving it there. The epilogue opens her locker at the **Royal Free** — a different hospital, months later — and claims the card is "still standing where she left it in June." The season trains the audience to track objects exactly (tickets, tubs, keys, a die), and this closing image, the last note of Rosie's road-not-taken, is checkable against ep 8 and fails. *Fix:* set the beat at Whitechapel, or change the line to "stood the way she left it in June," or show two seconds of her re-standing it when she moves wards.

5. **Episode 10, epilogue (scene 18) — Terry's framed last ticket, 70.6, is never shown being printed. SUBSTANTIVE.** Episode 1 establishes "Terry doesn't step on the plate, and nobody asks him to"; every other meaningful weighing in the season is on screen; and the epilogue hangs Terry's ticket, annotated "71.4 on Maggie's. Went next door." in Peg's hand, from a printing we never saw, by a man the family never weighed, on the last weekend he was in the county. The beat being skipped — Terry choosing to be weighed the same night Peg finally writes "Terry. Turned. 2021" — is the payoff to five years of his erased name, and it arrives as an unplanted object. *Fix:* one shot in scene 8: after the steelyard, Terry steps onto the little bridge, 70.6, tears off his own ticket and puts it in Peg's hand.

6. **Episode 9, scenes 4 and 7 — Rosie's unauthorized crossing breaks a stated rule with no consequence beat. QUIBBLE.** Peg locked every rig two episodes earlier ("No Marlow crosses till I say," ep 7 scene 12) and wears the key on a bootlace; Rosie takes the leftover rig off the rack and crosses next door while the family is at Purfleet, and nobody — least of all Peg — ever mentions it. Grief covers a lot, and the episode's attention rightly shifts to the sea, but this season ratchets every stated rule to its breach, and this one breaks silently. *Fix:* one line — Peg: "You took that off my rack," and no answer — or Rosie: "You said no Marlow crosses. I weren't one last night."

7. **Episodes 7–10 — "Aye" migrates through the Marlow family. QUIBBLE.** For six episodes the Marlows say "yeah" (Terry, ep 6 scene 2); from the ferry on, Tess, Rosie, Terry and Shaun all say "aye" — Tess says both within ep 7 alone ("Aye," scene 5; "Yeah," scene 12) — while "aye" sits naturally on the Kent-bank Aherns and on Ray. The word is a river-bank marker the show itself draws, and the drift blurs the two families' voices exactly where the season is telling them apart. *Fix:* reserve "aye" for the Aherns and Ray; keep "yeah" for the Marlows, and if Tess's one "aye" stays in scene 5, play it as her mirroring Maggie across the table.

8. **Episode 9, scene 4 — the bite's half-duration is first stated at the moment of use. QUIBBLE.** Rosie whispers "Half my seconds. Five point four," the only on-screen statement that the bite fires for half the crosser's seconds. Episode 5's briefing (scene 13) teaches everything else about the tab — once only, after a minute, skin-tight, fires in water — and omits this one, in a season that puts every other rule on screen before it bites. *Fix:* four words in Tess's mouth in ep 5: "Half your seconds, once."

9. **Episode 6, scenes 5–8 — three numbers for one crossing. QUIBBLE.** Tess mandates "two and a half… not a tenth more"; her wrist unit reads 2.2 as she steps out with the binder; Osei's wall logs "Two point six seconds." Nobody reconciles them, and in a season where tenths are character — and where Tess later trusts that same wall over her own bridge (ep 9, "sixty-one seven") — the unremarked tenth reads as slack rather than meaning. *Fix:* match two of the three numbers, or let Tess hear "two point six," say nothing, and visibly file it the way she files the weight.

10. **Episode 10, scenes 2 and 4 — Frank's 64.4 against the wall's 64.3. QUIBBLE.** Peg's plate prints 64.4 the morning he dies (the plate is honest in 2002); the Marrable Street wall reads 64.3 — a hundred grams adrift, unexplained and unremarked, in a show that matches Liam to the gram (63.9 chalk against 65.1 wall) and has just declared walls and balances the instruments that don't lie. *Fix:* make the wall read 64.4 — or better, print the ticket 64.3 and let Peg's pencil round it up to "10 st 2": a woman adding a hundred grams to a starving man.

11. **Episode 4, scene 11 — "My weight's gone light too, Nan." QUIBBLE.** Kieran has kept no personal record before now; his nightly ritual weighs only the cast-iron test weight, and his own column begins in ep 5 (39.2, "a second column"). As spoken, the line claims a personal series the show hasn't started, and only Peg's reply — all about the dropped weight — makes the scene parse. *Fix:* "The twenty's gone light an' all, Nan."

12. **Episode 7, scene 7 against episode 10, scene 9 — Maggie's tide books are twenty-five years old, then thirty-one. QUIBBLE.** On the ferry they are "six of them, spines cracked, twenty-five years of them"; in the finale Maggie turns "her oldest tide book" back thirty-one years to 1995 to date the door. The job entries may span twenty-five years, but the line as spoken caps the books below the date the finale needs, and these books are half the season's evidence. *Fix:* in ep 7, "the job entries go back twenty-five year, the tide tables further, to her grandad's day" — or have her fetch the old pilot's almanac down in ep 10.

13. **Episode 8, scenes 8–9 — the anonymous call and the reachable next of kin. QUIBBLE.** Tess rings the night line and refuses her name ("No name of mine to give"); the next night the ward rings Tess directly, "the minute you walked." It is explicable — Rosie could have named her aunt at admission — but neither beat is planted, and the two facts sit in the same episode. *Fix:* one line at the minibus, the chief with a clipboard: "Next of kin?" — "Tess. Marlow Aggregates."

14. **Episode 10, scene 16 against episode 6, scene 8 — the licensed ten-second cure needs its law change said. QUIBBLE.** Osei establishes the three-second licence as hard law since 2014 — the fact that kept the drift hidden for fifteen years. The epilogue cures turned people "on licensed rigs" at ten seconds and prints Danny's card "LICENSED RESTORATION." The inference (the table went public, the limit lifted) is available but never spoken, in a montage that speaks everything else. *Fix:* one line of newsroom voice over the montage: "Ministers move to lift the three-second limit tonight."

15. **Episode 7, scene 12 against episode 10, scene 12 — Rosie's "I'm going first" is never answered. QUIBBLE.** At the stove Rosie vows: "when it's been counted proper, and checked against somebody else's, I'm going first." It is counted, Ray goes first, and Rosie declines her seven in ten with no one naming the promise; the wards speech carries her reason but not the reversal, which the audience is left to remember for itself. *Fix:* one line at the fence — Tess: "You said you'd go first." Rosie: "Wards first."

16. **The episode 3/4 seam — audited; one costume stitch. QUIBBLE.** Everything factual across the seam reconciles: the ten minutes ("Ten minutes after 'Blood pays blood'"), Danny's coat over his arm explaining the pit trip he set out for, the tray and Terry's pot on the table, Kieran asleep sideways in the armchair, the half-drawn 10.0 on the board. The only slip is costume: Peg is "apron over her nightie" at the weigh-in (ep 3 scene 1) and "in her dressing gown" at 05:40 (ep 4 scene 1), in a night she spent waiting up at the table. *Fix:* one word in either scene.

## Critique: mimo


**1. Danny's two-second turning contradicts the ferry count. — SUBSTANTIVE**
*Episode 7, scenes 9 and 12.* On the ferry, Tess lays out the sheet and says twice — once to Maggie, once to Peg at the stove, with Danny in the room — "Not one under three seconds, either side, not in twenty-five years." But Danny came back turned at 2.2 seconds two weeks earlier, and he is sitting at the table while she says it. Either he is one of the "eleven of ours," in which case the claim is false on screen, or he is excluded because the grip turned him and not the drift, in which case nobody says so and a careful viewer is left thinking the show has forgotten him. The "not one under three seconds" line is the spine of the second reversal; it cannot be wrong in the scene that delivers it.
*Fix:* one line, where Tess reads the sheet. Something like: "Eleven of ours. Not one under three seconds." / MAGGIE: "And Danny?" / TESS: "Danny was the hand, not the crossing. That doesn't go on the sheet." Then the claim holds, and the grip's difference from the drift is taught a second time for free.

**2. Rosie's and Danny's sentences do not follow the show's own stated law. — SUBSTANTIVE**
*Episode 5, scene 13 ("step out on purpose, it's twenty years"); Episode 10, Epilogue scenes 16–17.* The twenty-year penalty for stepping out onto another leaf is said aloud in episode 5 and again in the plan's background law. Rosie does it twice, brings back living matter, and Peg confesses both at the police station ("my girl stepped out on purpose. Twice. Put it with the rest"). Yet the epilogue shows Rosie gowned on a fever ward, training as a crossing surgeon, and calls the pantry arrangement "her sentence"; Danny gets a Register ward with an appointment card he has not used. Tess, who crossed only twice legally, is the one in prison. Nothing on screen explains why stepping out costs a job and a kitchen door. The season's whole method is that rules have consequences; here the rule is stated twice and pays nothing.
*Fix:* let the court's reasoning be visible in one line, or change the outcome. E.g. the desk sergeant, taking down Peg's confession: "Twenty years, that is. Unless the court takes the record in mitigation." Then show Tess inside and Rosie on the ward *as a suspended sentence, bound to the ward's kitchen*, with one word ("suspended") said or written on the file.

**3. Tess's pencil is "never been sharpened," but Rosie wrote with it. — SUBSTANTIVE**
*Episode 7, scene 9 against Episode 3, scene 10.* On the ferry, Tess's pencil "has never been sharpened," and Maggie swaps her own sharp one for it — a good beat, that Tess's instrument is decorative. But in episode 3 Tess hands that same pencil to Rosie, who writes "ROSIE MARLOW. Plain as a shop sign" on a torn ticket and then her mirror hand underneath. A pencil that has never been sharpened cannot write. The two scenes cannot both be true.
*Fix:* cut "It has never been sharpened" and let the beat live in use instead — Tess's hand goes up, she looks at the point, and puts it back: "Mine's blunt." / MAGGIE: "Mine's sharp." The meaning survives and episode 3 keeps its evidence.

**4. Episode 10 carries far more than one hour. — SUBSTANTIVE**
*Episode 10, whole episode plus the epilogue.* The hour has to hold: the 2002 cold open, the loft and Frank's falling tickets, the jam-jar replay, Ray's printout and "seven point one," the Ministry letter, Peg's first writing, the investigators and the warrant, the whole night of writing, the steelyard weighing, the odds on the roll, Terry's die, the baking-tray door, the intercut 05:52 crossings, Ray's mirror signature and the meter, Rosie's breath and her choice, Danny's choice, the rolls laid out, the vote, the police station — and then four epilogue scenes. That is sixteen-plus scenes in a script that elsewhere runs nine to eleven. The night of writing and the 05:52 minute are the two moments the season has been building to, and both will be cut to fit. Pacing sags where the show can least afford it.
*Fix:* move the police station and Peg's confession into the epilogue's first beat (the front page can carry "and the family handed its rolls over"), and give the 05:52 minute the full weight by letting it run uninterrupted. The vote can end the episode proper on the stove door closing cold.

**5. "Then you'd be eating it" does not say what it means. — SUBSTANTIVE**
*Episode 4, scene 2.* This is the line the plan assigns the job of teaching the cargo rule — that whatever rides in a field turns with its carrier — and it is the reason Danny's plan to turn the Aherns' tubs is refused. But Danny's plan is to flip *mirror* food into ordinary food so Con starves; if he carries it and turns, the flipped food is ordinary food, which a turned Danny cannot eat at all. So "you'd be eating it" is literally wrong for the case on the table, and the audience cannot derive the rule from it. The nod at the catalogue cutting and Terry eating his tub are asked to do work the sentence does not do.
*Fix:* make the collision explicit and keep it in Tess's voice: "You'd turn with it. Picture did. And that lot'd come back the wrong hand for you an' all — you'd be eating what Terry eats and wearing his gloves." Or simpler: "Turn their food and you turn with it. Then you're sat where Terry's sat."

**6. Danny's tingle timing does not match the rule. — QUIBBLE**
*Episode 6, scene 1.* The rule is that fingertips fizz "about two seconds before the field fails." Danny's true budget is 2.2 seconds (89.0 kg, and the family believes 2.4 off the lying plate), so on his budget the fizz starts about a fifth of a second in — effectively as he steps. The cold open instead lets him cross the room, read the sleeping boy, see the body appear at the safe, and only then has the fizz start under his nails. Elsewhere the show is exact (Liam at 4.9 against a 6.98 budget; Shaun at 6.9; Rosie at 9.0 and 9.2; Ray at 6.3). This one is the odd one out.
*Fix:* start the fizz as he lands and let it ride under the whole scene, so "the count dying in his nails" is what he has been living with since he stepped — which also makes his patience at the boy's bedhead costlier.

**7. "The first true number the board has ever held" is not true. — QUIBBLE**
*Episode 9, scene 12.* Peg writes 65.1 on the board and the action line calls it "the first true number the board has ever held." The board has held Frank's weights, Tess's, Rosie's 44.0 and 43.6 and 43.1 — all weighed before 4 April, all true. What 65.1 is, is the first true number the board has held *since the bridge started lying*, and the first true number about Liam. As written the line overstates and a viewer who has been tracking the April step will catch it.
*Fix:* "the first true number that board had held since April," or simply let Peg say "There's your number, love" and drop the claim.

**8. The Obverse leaflet vanishes between episodes. — QUIBBLE**
*Episode 10, scene 15 against Episode 3, scene 16 and Episode 4, scene 3.* The leaflet is a running object: found in Rosie's coat with her practised signature all over the back, priced with "LISBON. HALF." in biro, kept through the ward. At the police station Rosie folds the new property receipt "where the Obverse leaflet used to live" — a nice echo, but nothing has moved the leaflet. Either it stays, or it needs to go somewhere on screen.
*Fix:* one shot in episode 8 or 9 — the leaflet left face-up on the ward locker when she walks out, or folded into the exercise book beside the crab. Cheap, and it keeps the object honest.

**9. Peg says "Again tomorrow" and tomorrow never comes. — QUIBBLE**
*Episode 8, scene 3 against scenes 4–9.* Fourteen two-second hops on the Monday, meter LO, and Peg says "Again tomorrow." The next morning Tess is tailing the van and Rosie is at the kitchen table; there is no second session, and Rosie goes to the ward on the Wednesday. The ratchet the line sets up is left unwound.
*Fix:* either show one hop on Tuesday morning in half a line ("Again." / "Again."), or change it to "Again tonight," and let the second session be the thing Kieran's rewrapped sandwiches interrupt.

**10. Four hop scenes in episode 2 run at the same temperature. — QUIBBLE**
*Episode 2, scenes 7, 8, 13, 16.* Seven three-second hops, told across four separate scenes, each with the same shape: rig, "Straight?" "Straight.", the window still wrong, Rosie's eye to the cutting. The beats inside are good (the false alarm, Kieran asking "Straight?" man to man, the sweet) but the rhythm does not escalate, and the middle of the episode reads as a montage stretched into scenes — the fault the owner's own notes call "briefs instead of pressing."
*Fix:* compress hops two to five into one scene with a clock and a deadline (Peg's man's Friday flight), and give the saved minutes to the Harley Street visit, which is where the turn of the episode actually lives.

**11. Kieran writes on a certified legal record. — QUIBBLE**
*Episode 9, scene 7.* "FOOD TURNS TOO" in the margin of the audit roll is a lovely beat and it earns its place. But the roll is a Trading Standards record kept by law, and in episode 10 the family hands those same rolls to the police as evidence; a boy's pencil on it slightly undercuts the "these are certified, dated, crown-stamped, nobody can call it made up" argument Tess makes in scene 13.
*Fix:* put it on the printer's own ticket stub he keeps, or on the roll's paper wrapper — same image, and the certified surface stays clean.

**12. "Four hundred grams since Monday" is a day out. — QUIBBLE**
*Episode 1, scenes 1, 4 and 13.* Rosie is 44.0 at midnight before the Mayfair job, which the cold open puts at 3 a.m. on a Wednesday morning; the birthday weigh-in is Thursday evening. That is roughly forty hours, not "since Monday," and the board already carries 44.0 by Tuesday breakfast. Nothing turns on it, but it is the family's own arithmetic being said wrong in a show whose whole subject is arithmetic being said wrong.
*Fix:* "Four hundred grams since the job," or chalk 44.0 on the Monday and say it.
