# 40 Round 4 - the four critiques

*Three GLM critics, each through one lens, and MiMo v2.6 Pro with fresh eyes, on the season after round 3.*

## Critique: story

1. **Episode 6, cold open (scene 1) — the ambush breaks the show's own visibility rules.** Danny waits in the black beside the safe; Joe arrives "into the glow" and stands there while Danny's hand rises and hangs "for one beat, two beats" at his heart before the grab. By the season's established physics, visibility in the black is mutual and radial: an unpinned eye sees the inside of everything within its arm's-length bubble, and every rig's field glows. At the distance of a wrist-grab, Joe has Danny in his glow the instant he arrives — and Joe is the boy who "never came within three fathoms" of Rosie at Mayfair because he was frightened. As written, a frightened 17-year-old flies into a lit ambush and stands passive for two full beats while a man decides whether to kill him. *Why it matters:* this is the season's second-reversal hinge and Danny's crossed line; a careful viewer who has internalised the glow rules will ask why Joe didn't bolt. *Fix:* compress the choice into the reach — the hand is already moving as Joe resolves in the glow, passes the heart, closes on the wrist within a second (the choice plays on Danny's face, not in Joe's stillness) — and let Maggie's existing line ("He'd have finished that drop with his arm off. He's Ahern") plus one addition from Joe ("He was just *there*, lit up — my seconds were going") carry the freeze. **SUBSTANTIVE**

2. **Episode 10, scene 6 contradicts the master plan's canonical numbers.** The plan (section 8, episode 10, act one) says the Purfleet walls "logged three bodies in the black"; the episode says "two bodies... One of them nine times, three kilos each go. The other twice, the same" — which is arithmetically correct against the raid as filmed (Terry's nine 2-second legs plus Shaun's two 8-second legs = eleven cases), since Danny never crosses and Rosie was at Grays. *Why it matters:* the plan declares its numbers exact and binding on writers; a wrong body count in the inciting threat of the finale is exactly the kind of error the ledger system exists to prevent. *Fix:* correct the plan's line to two bodies (nine crossings and two), matching the episode; do not add a third crosser, because nothing on screen supports one. **SUBSTANTIVE**

3. **Episode 9, scene 11 — Tess's missing day and a half.** The ward rings her "Wednesday night, the minute you walked"; the raid she "gave" them ends by about 1.30 a.m.; she arrives Friday at nine in the morning, and all of Thursday (the crab, the meters, Terry's decision, the drain) is dramatized without her. "I gave you your raid. Then I came" covers roughly two hours, not thirty-one. *Why it matters:* Tess is the character who drives to a ferry within days of a note; unexplained stillness in her is against who she is, and the season dates every scene precisely, so the gap is checkable. *Fix:* either redraft the scene header to Thursday evening (after the drain), or give Tess one line — "I've been sat on the river road since your van came back; your mother kept the door bolted on me" — which also pays off Kieran unbolting it. **QUIBBLE**

4. **Episode 6, cold open — the seventh-night coincidence.** Joe's known deliveries are 11 April (the bullet) and this night (the envelope); Danny's vigil is seven nights, and Joe happens to come on the seventh. Nothing on screen explains why Joe waited a week or why that night. *Why it matters:* the stakeout's success currently rests on luck rather than on Danny's sum, and Danny's sum is the scene's stated engine. *Fix:* one line at the ferry or from Joe ("I'd been down that way twice since the bullet and lost my nerve") makes the visits frequent and Danny's patience the operative cause. **QUIBBLE**

5. **Episode 6, scenes 1–2 — the plate never reads Danny's fingers.** The four fingers are "left behind in the black" a foot from the little bridge, and the season's own rules say a dispersing mass reads on instruments for about a month and that the plate prints any black mass near it (−59.0, −46.2, −58.6, even the Twitch's 0.31). Kieran reads every ticket that morning and finds only Joe's. *Why it matters:* it is the one place the show's most-taught rules (clouds read; the plate prints; the boy checks) should have produced evidence of the fight, and their silence looks like oversight rather than choice. *Fix:* one prop — a −0.12 ticket Kieran tapes into his book under "GLITCH?" and never understands — turns the hole into another quiet piece of his collection. **QUIBBLE**

6. **Episode 8, scene 6 — the account in Terry's name.** Ray has banked five years of fortnightly cash (rising to £2,100-plus a week) in a building-society account opened in Terry Marlow's name, without Terry's knowledge, signature or presence. *Why it matters:* modern banking makes the account itself implausible, and the audit that caught Ray through batch codes would have found a standing structure like this first; it also parks criminal proceeds in an innocent man's name, which the ending never untangles. *Fix:* cash in a deposit box with Ray's own ledger as the record, or one line conceding Dilys fronts the account (only if the show wants her to be more than a courier). **QUIBBLE**

7. **Episode 7 — "Nobody ever asked why" lands twice in one episode** (the loft, then the ferry). Both instances are earned and in Terry's and Tess's voices, but doubling the phrase is the closest the season comes to saying its theme in words, and the owner's rules forbid the theme being spoken. *Fix:* cut the loft instance and keep Terry's "He was off his food, love. Then he wasn't. That was enough for us," which shows the habit without naming it. **QUIBBLE**

8. **Episode 9, act five — Peg's "the first true number the board has ever held" is literally false.** Every chalked weight before 4 April was true; the board has lied only since the dig. *Why it matters:* the season has taught the audience exactly when the scales went wrong, so the superlative is checkable and slightly cheapens the beat it wants to deepen. *Fix:* "the first honest number" or cut "ever." **QUIBBLE**

9. **Episode 4, scene 2 — Peg's folk-physics for the hops is never marked as hers.** "A picture doesn't [face home]... you hold it loose, you let it roll about" is Peg inventing a mechanism at the table because she needs a sale, and the episode's notes confirm it is meant to be her made-up idea — but nothing on screen distinguishes it from family lore, so a first-time viewer may take it as the show's rule and expect the hops to have a real chance. *Why it matters:* it blurs where the family's knowledge ends and its guesses begin, which is the season's engine. *Fix:* one beat of pushback already half-exists ("Could take a hundred goes"); add Terry's "The black never gave me back" landing on Peg rather than the pan, so the audience files her plan as hope, not physics. **QUIBBLE**

## Critique: people

1. **Episodes 1–2 — Rosie's inner life is withheld too completely before the first reversal. SUBSTANTIVE.** The script itself flags the missed hook and walks away: Ep 1 Scene 4, "Rosie isn't there. She has gone up to bed. We hear her door shut overhead." Apart from the bathroom scale in Ep 2 Scene 2 (which plays as diet pride), we get no private Rosie before the meter reads LO — no cost, no fear, only the show-off surface. The knowledge-map discipline ("level with the family") is respected, but care is built by access to the mind, and the season's emotional centre is sealed for three episodes, so the first reversal lands as brilliant plot rather than as a person we know falling. Fix without breaking the secret: one 20-second private beat in Ep 1 or 2 — alone in her room, Rosie practises writing her own name, plain, over and over, on toilet paper, and flushes it. First-watch viewers get a mystery; re-watchers get the plant Ep 3 currently reveals only after the fact (the leaflet's back). Add a second beat of her alone with a surgeon video, stopping it at the child's unbroken skin.

2. **Ep 3 Scene 5 → Ep 8 Scene 6 — Ray's method costs him nothing on screen. SUBSTANTIVE.** In Ep 3 he states his plan to Tess: "So I wait till she comes to me hungry." His founding wound (Ep 8: "I watched your dad's weight spread for a month. I wasn't watching it again") is exactly this: a Marlow disappearing while he watched. He is near-certain from the pit scene onward, the family is broke until the fence pays on the Friday of Ep 4, and he could have fronted tubs — he later demands a month up front. Nobody, including the script, ever confronts him with the three weeks he waited; the flat scene plays him as purely sympathetic. Why it matters: the pursuer's decency is the show's claim on us, and it has an unexamined hole the width of his wound. Fix: in Ep 8, after "I wasn't watching it again," Tess gets the line — "March. You told me you'd wait till she came to you hungry. She was four kilos lighter by the time we knew" — and Ray has no answer, his flaw (never laying what he knows beside anyone) made flesh.

3. **Ep 7 Scenes 7 and 12 → Ep 10 — Maggie's confession never reaches Rosie as feeling. SUBSTANTIVE.** Rosie is in the room when Tess tells the table that Maggie's phone call sent her the long way round, into ten seconds. She is given no reaction there, and none in Eps 8, 9 or 10 — by the finale the woman whose tip helped turn her is chalk-side at the family's table, weighing her, sliding brass weights, with the debt sealed over by the two mothers' peace. The reconciliation between families is wholesale; the one injury still open between two living principals is never transacted. Fix: one beat at the steelyard in Ep 10 Scene 8. Rosie, flat, while Maggie slides the weight: "You asked for ten." Maggie: "I did." Rosie: "Weigh me." Nothing forgiven, nothing stated twice — the family's own language carrying it.

4. **Ep 9 Scene 1 — Peg chalks KIERAN 38.0 and a star for a raid four episodes after Liam, with the connection unstaged. SUBSTANTIVE.** The doom-logic (more of the same, more lovingly) is the design, and Danny's refusal is right. But as shot, Peg's "yes" reads as reflex, not as a woman overriding what she knows: LIAM 62.4 and its star are still on that board, untouched, while she writes a starving twelve-year-old's number beside them for a job. A careful viewer reads forgetfulness rather than suppression, and Peg's credibility is the season's load-bearing wall. Fix: her chalk reaches the board, passes Liam's entry, stops a half-second — and she wipes Liam's number first "to make room." One action, no dialogue, and the horror becomes a choice.

5. **Episodes 6–7 — the pursuer disappears and his theory dies off-screen. SUBSTANTIVE.** Ray is in neither episode. His spinner theory — the engine of his want and of the Ahern war — is last heard in Ep 5 and is quietly dead by Ep 8 ("There's no such person. There never was."), with nothing on screen showing it die or what replaced it. The Crime promise of a long fight between equals goes dormant for two hours exactly when the detective plot resolves, and resolves to someone else. Fix: thirty seconds in the flat in Ep 6 or 7 — Ray with his notebook open at MAYFAIR · 03:12 · 2ND BODY · 59.0, ruling a line through "spinner," writing "59 = ?" and being unable to answer, then filing it. Keeps him present, dramatises his stated flaw (writing everything down and showing no one), and pays his Ep 8 line.

6. **Episodes 8–10 — the last third has no human opposition. SUBSTANTIVE.** After the ferry, the Ahern fight becomes alliance and the Ray fight becomes complicity; the pressures left are hunger, the dice and a calendar. The Register investigators — the only external agent with teeth — appear in a single scene (Ep 10 Scene 6) as a lanyard and a clipboard: no want, no face. The finale's conflict is properly internal (the vote), but a season that promised a long fight ends with three episodes of circumstances. Fix, either way cheap: give the lead investigator one humanizing line at the counter (she matches claim dates for a living; her own brother is on the Register), or let Declan — who never changes and is told nothing — be the tip that brings them, keeping the unchanged villain active to the last reel.

7. **Episodes 7–10 — the diagnosis is spoken a little too often. QUIBBLE.** No banned word is used and no one lectures, but the refrain accumulates: Terry's "Nobody ever asked why," Maggie's "Nobody counted her, Tess," Rosie's "And you all clapped," Peg's "And we clapped," Tess's "We never fed our own," Ray's "I wrote it all down in a book and showed nobody." Each is earned and in voice; six of them across four episodes starts to underline the thesis the events already carry. Fix: keep the idiom-plays ("Paper talks," "We feed our own" inverted) and cut one analytic instance — Terry's exchange lands just as hard reduced to "He was off his food, love. Then he wasn't. That was enough for us."

8. **Ep 6 Scene 3 → Ep 7 Scene 3 — Tess carries the enemy's envelope sealed for a fortnight. QUIBBLE.** Maggie's own line ("A thing in that safe gets read") makes the delay conspicuous, and Tess is the one person in Essex who cannot leave a number unread — she counts in her sleep. Danny says "Burn it," the war is live, Con is weeks dead, and the family's intelligence officer pockets it for two weeks while planning a burglary instead. A readable cause exists (opening it means going, alone), but the show never stages the refusal. Fix: one line — Danny: "You opening that?" Tess: "When there's something I can do about it." Or simply have her open it the morning after; the note's own date, "Thursday the 21st," does all the waiting.

9. **Episode 2, Act Two — the middle hops repeat their beat. QUIBBLE.** Hops two and three are the same scene twice (cross, "Straight?", window on the right, glance at the cutting), and the first-watch stakes are only Terry's year. The false alarm at hop four and the bathroom-scale beat vary it, but the act dips between them. Fix: put one small threat inside a hop — a bead drifting into her glow, the guard's cough, Tess's count faltering on a passing car — so the crossings themselves carry suspense and not only the concealment.

10. **Ep 5 Scene 3 vs Ep 8 Scene 6 — Ray's "puzzlement" contradicts his own ledger. QUIBBLE.** At the A13 he is "puzzled" that Rosie looks well; by Ep 8 we learn his ledger showed the doubled order from 7 April, so he knows exactly why she has colour. On rewatch the beat wobbles. Fix: replace puzzlement with cover — "You're eating, then," said flat, eyes on the rig — which reads as detective knowledge at the time and pays the ledger later.

11. **Ep 6 Scene 1 — Danny sits all night with the stump rather than wake Terry. QUIBBLE.** The choice ("He will not have the boy see it first") is characterful, but four quiet hours with four fingers gone strains belief, and Terry — the family's dresser of wounds — sleeps one floor up. Fix: one line of action admitting the choice: Danny looks up the dark stairs, tourniquet in his teeth, and doesn't call. The refusal becomes deliberate instead of macho by default.

12. **Ep 6 Scene 1 — the seven-night stakeout is compressed to a phrase. QUIBBLE.** "Seventh night" carries the whole gamble. Danny stepping blind into the black on a guess, night after night, at the family's own safe, with nobody in that house of insomniacs noticing, deserves ten seconds of one failed night — the tow's horn, Danny in, nothing there, the clock at 02:04, the crossing wasted. It sells both the wager and the household's blindness.

13. **Episodes 1–5 — Shaun is a body, not a person. QUIBBLE.** He loses a leg in Ep 5 and his hand of "Straight" in Ep 9, and his lines are all craft ("Never go back twice," "Make it worth my leg," "I'll wait till someone counts it for me"). Competence without want. Fix: one line in Ep 1 or 3 naming what the work is for — a girl in Gravesend, a barge of his own — so "I've given it a leg. I'll wait till someone counts it for me" comes from a man and not a casualty.

14. **Episodes 5–6 — Liam's death has no world around it. QUIBBLE.** The minute at the wall is the show's rite and it works, but afterwards there is no funeral, no wreath, no in-law, no driver taking a hat off; the Marlows' grief is sealed in the yard, and the boy's mother is not so much as mentioned in ten episodes. Fix: one cutaway — Mick and the drivers standing round an untouched bacon roll at the hatch, or a wreath lent against the gate — and one mention of where Liam's mother is. The world holding them, briefly, makes the table's closedness a fact instead of a production convenience.

## Critique: continuity

1. **Episode 8's calendar cannot be reconciled under any reading.** The anchors: Terry counts "Nine days. Less, there being three" of tubs on Friday morning (ep 7, sc 13); the last tub is split on the cold open's Sunday; Ray says "Tuesday week they walked me out of Victoria" at the flat (sc 6); Peg sets the raid for "Wednesday fortnight" (sc 7); the act is headed "Two weeks on" but scene 12 is captioned "Wednesday 10 June"; ep 9 fixes the following Friday as "the twelfth of June." If the last tub is Sunday 31 May (which is what Terry's nine days from 22 May gives), the reveal is Tuesday 2 June, Rosie is admitted Wednesday 3 June, and both "Wednesday fortnight" and "Two weeks on" point to 17 June — a week past the caption and the raid. If the last tub is Sunday 24 May, then "Tuesday week" (19 May), "Wednesday fortnight," "Two weeks on," the caption and "the twelfth of June" all click — but Terry's nine days should have run to 31 May, a week past the empty fridge. Exactly one anchor breaks either way, and the episode 8 plants note ("'Wednesday fortnight' = Wednesday 10 June", an eight-day fortnight) shows the writers lost the reckoning. It matters because this is a season that trains its audience to count days and tenths, and the raid date carries Rosie's ward stay, the Monday-warrant clock in ep 10, and Liam's posthumous arithmetic. **Fix:** adopt the 24 May chain and cut Terry's line to "Three days, if that" (or move the last Laundry delivery forward), and correct the plants note; or keep 31 May and change "Wednesday fortnight" to "Wednesday week," the act header to "One week on," and Ray's "Tuesday week" to "Tuesday fortnight." SUBSTANTIVE

2. **Ray's ledger dates the third mouth impossibly (ep 8, sc 6).** The scene's own narration: the figure doubles "against Tuesday the seventh of April" (correct — Peg learned about Rosie on 4 April), "then, against a fortnight Tuesday, it goes to three." A fortnight after 7 April is 21 April; Danny did not turn until Wednesday 6 May (ep 6 cold open, "a week since the river wall" of 29 April). Read instead as a fortnight before the scene, 19 May, it is still wrong: ep 7, sc 2 establishes Friday deliveries running from early May "since it went to three mouths," so the treble was in by Friday 8 May at the latest — Peg doubled within three days of learning about Rosie and would have trebled at once. It matters because the ledger is the episode's proof that Ray "counted us in dinners," and its one wrong number is checkable against two on-screen dates. **Fix:** date it plainly — "against Friday the eighth of May, it goes to three" — which also quietly confirms Ray clocked Danny before anyone told him. SUBSTANTIVE

3. **Ray's "puzzlement" at Rosie looking well contradicts what he knows (ep 5, sc 3 vs ep 8).** On the A13 he says "You look well," she answers "I've eaten," and the direction reads "That seems to puzzle him more than anything she has said." But Ray has known Rosie was turned since episode 1 (his own linen photographs prove the carrier turned), and from 7 April his ledger ran at two tubs — he tells Tess he knew there was a second mouth "the week you did." Her colour on 21 April is his own porridge working; there is nothing to puzzle over, and the ep 5 plants note ("he knows the Laundry sends only enough for one") flatly contradicts ep 8's doubled order. The beat wants to be a clue for the audience but is nonsense for the character who feeds her. **Fix:** cut the puzzlement and give Ray a line that plays both ways to the room and truthfully to him — "Someone's feeding you, then." — which sharpens the Laundry plant instead of blurring it. SUBSTANTIVE

4. **The plan's knowledge map (section 10) contradicts the episodes on two rows.** "Con is an Ahern" is dated Rosie/Peg/Danny episode 5, Tess episode 4 "(Maggie's call)" — but the episodes have Terry name "Con Ahern" to the whole family at the Thursday table (ep 4, sc 7), with Peg, Danny, Rosie, Shaun and Kieran all present, two days before Maggie rings. "The Aherns lost Aoife and blame a Marlow spinner" is dated to the family in episode 7 — but the war council of ep 4, sc 2 ("Something took hold of my girl... Then they meant her dead") already has the family believing the Aherns spun Rosie. This matters beyond tidiness: the map is the document a second season or a freelance episode writer will check, and stale rows will cause double reveals and wrong "who knows" blocking. **Fix:** redate both rows to episode 4 (Terry's call; the war council), and note that Tess's Maggie-call knowledge of Con arrives second, not first. SUBSTANTIVE

5. **The bite's "fires once" is leaned on twice without a re-arm shown or declared.** Rule 16 and guess 5 both say the bite "fires once, for half the seconds, only after a full minute standing on a leaf." Rosie bites home from the sea (ep 9, sc 5) and bites home from the shore (ep 10, sc 12). If "once" means per arming, the finale is legal but nothing on screen or in the bible says so; if it means once ever, episode 10 breaks the show's declared physics by a silent new rule — precisely what the plan forbids. **Fix:** one shot — the tab bent dead in ep 9, and Rosie clipping a fresh disc from the toolbox at the standpipe in ep 10 — or one clause added to guess 5 ("once per full minute's standing; the tab is single-use and replaced at service"). QUIBBLE

6. **"Aye" creeps into the Marlows' mouths in the back half.** Rosie ("Aye," ep 8 sc 9 and ep 9 sc 6), Tess ("Aye," ep 9 sc 11 and sc 13), Terry (ep 9, ep 10) — where the family's established assent is "yeah" and "all right" (Peg "Yeah," Tess "Yeah.," ep 7), and "aye" is marked twice over as belonging to Ray and to Den of Leeds. It slowly makes Grays sound like the north country and blurs the register that keeps the two banks and the Met distinct; it is also the clearest fingerprint of a writer change late in the season. **Fix:** a dialogue pass on episodes 8–10 returning "aye" to Ray and Den only. QUIBBLE

7. **Terry refuses to cross, then crosses (ep 8 sc 7 vs ep 9 sc 3).** At the vote he says "Nobody's sending me in on my seconds, mind. But I'll drive"; a fortnight later he runs nine legs into the depot. "Two's all I've ever asked for, love" half-covers it — his refusal was of long crossings — but the audience hears "not going in" and then watches him go in, unremarked. **Fix:** one clause at the vote ("Not on my five, mind — two-second milk runs, I'm your man") or let Terry propose the two-second legs himself, which would also sharpen his later arithmetic ("You can't buy nothing with two seconds"). QUIBBLE

8. **Shaun's tingle comes 2.6 seconds early (ep 7, sc 11).** The fizz starts at wrist-unit 6.9 against a true budget of about 9.5 (51.9 chalk, 52.5 on the steelyard later), where the show's own law is "about two seconds before the time runs out" and every other crossing lands on 2.0–2.3 (Rosie 9.0 of 11.2; Rosie 9.2 of 11.2; Liam 4.9 of 6.98; Danny; Ray 6.3 of 8.3; Shaun himself 6.9 of 8.9 in ep 9). This is exactly the kind of number the season teaches its viewers to check, and Shaun's ep 9 repeat shows the writers know the right value. **Fix:** move the ep 7 fizz to 7.5. QUIBBLE

9. **The Register depot's dog barks at turned smell (ep 9, sc 3).** "He can smell me. Sixpence's done the same five year" is a lovely beat, but Purfleet is the one building in England where the turned are daily callers, claimants and green-smocked staff; a dog that sets up at turned flesh would be barking at the post all day. **Fix:** keep the dog and let it bark at the crossing or the marsh, so Peg's "Foxes" stands and Terry's line becomes Danny's wrong guess — or establish it as a dog newly kennelled for the Army consignment. QUIBBLE

10. **Rosie's wardrobe breaks between episodes 8 and 9.** Ep 8, sc 13 sends her out of the ward in "the coat... over her pyjamas"; she comes straight from the last train to the office (ep 9, sc 2) and crosses within the hour, yet swims "kicking in her clothes" and later wrings out "Rosie's sea-soaked jumper" and sits "in the big jumper, dry now" — the Danny cast-off she wears all season, which she did not leave the ward in. **Fix:** one beat in ep 9, sc 4 — she pulls Danny's old jumper off the hook and drags it on over her pyjamas before crossing — which also makes cold-water sense, or dress her at the locker in ep 8. QUIBBLE

11. **The bullet Ray pockets in episode 5 never comes back.** It does its plot work (the radius proof, Kieran's bed, the stakeout), but the object dangles: by the handover it sits in a suspended detective's pocket, unmentioned in Peg's exhaustive confession at the counter ("their sea went down my drain... my girl stepped out on purpose. Twice"). A careful viewer will wonder what happened to the one piece of Ahern evidence in a policeman's pocket. **Fix:** one line at the station or in ep 10 — Peg adds "and someone stood a bullet on my painting; your lot can have that an' all" — or show Ray logging it with his crossing on the roll. QUIBBLE

12. **The Ahern side of the war ends off-screen.** After the ferry (ep 7) the Aherns take no further action on screen, and Declan — who "never changes," and who has lost his tugs, his brother and, as he believes, his granddaughter to the Marlows — is last seen in ep 5 ("Not enough") and then reappears in the epilogue merely chalk-in-hand. His acceptance of the women's truce is never shown, and he is the one Ahern the plan says does not change. **Fix:** one beat in ep 9 or 10 — Declan at the lock-up door refusing to look at Maggie's tide books when she carries them to Grays, or turning his back when she says "nobody spun anybody." QUIBBLE

13. **The season's hauls are never priced against its bills.** Everything else is numbered to the pound (£300 a day, £109,500 a year, £180,000 a cure, "thirty-eight days"), but the fence's take on the Marrable Street tray ("A third, if he's kind") and Corbett's "steel and fifties, call it three kilos" are never sized, and both vanish between episodes. The June starvation is properly carried by the supply cut, not the cash — but a viewer doing sums will ask why two big jobs left nothing, and the show never answers. **Fix:** one line — the fence holds the stones "for the heat" and pays a quarter, and Corbett's box comes out mostly documents and old tenners — so the destitution is armoured against the arithmetic it invites. QUIBBLE

14. **Kieran's voice ages up at the margins.** "The real tide's in the tables, but the tables don't say which moon it runs to. That's the bit Miss likes" (ep 7, sc 5) is seminar-Kieran, not the boy who asked "What's no rotation?" in episode 1; his register is otherwise superbly held (the log, "I eat some of it," "Nan says it's chipped"). **Fix:** break the line or attribute the analysis — "Miss says the tables don't know which moon it runs to. That's her favourite bit." QUIBBLE

## Critique: mimo


Having read the full season against the master plan, the Fourth Direction's laws, the owner's six rules, and The Catch exclusions, I find the work strong: the physics holds almost everywhere, the theme lives in events, and the characters carry real weight. Below are the genuine findings I can stand behind, most serious first.

---

**1. Rosie's odds at the fence contradict the drift table — and drain the choice of stakes.**
*Episode 10, Act Three ("05:52"), the field at Mucking Marsh, after Terry goes.*
Rosie says: "Seven in ten, it's Nan's fry-up for me on Friday. Three in ten, I'm still this." But her scouting step is three seconds (per the canonical numbers: "Rosie's step 3 seconds"). At three seconds the drift table reads one in five thousand — essentially certain she stays turned. "Seven in ten" is the ten-second Restoration figure. The line, as spoken, quotes the wrong row of the show's most important table, and it changes the emotional beat: instead of accepting near-certain exile-from-fry-up, Rosie reads as declining a real 70 % cure. Rule 7 of section 13 says the drift table is law and outcomes must match it.
*Fix:* Either rewrite the line to match three seconds ("One in five thousand it's the fry-up. I'm staying as I am") — which makes her choice about the fever wards, not about the dice — or have her explicitly price a ten-second Restoration she is declining ("Book me in for ten, it's seven in ten for the fry-up. I'm staying as I am"). The second option preserves the dramatic weight while matching the table.
**SUBSTANTIVE**

---

**2. Shaun takes two eight-second legs at the depot; the plan's canonical count is two eight-second legs for the whole season.**
*Episode 9, Cold open and Act One, the Purfleet depot raid.*
The plan's law ledger (Law 5) says "Shaun's two eight-second legs come up one each way" — one at Silvertown (ep 7, straight), one at the depot (turned). The canonical numbers table says "raid leg 8 seconds" (singular). The episode script sends Shaun into the black twice at the depot — Peg counts him home, then "Once more, son" — giving him three eight-second legs across the season. The brief instructs: "Where a number appears here, it is the number: do not round it, change it or add a new one." A viewer would not notice; the outcome (one turn out of three 50/50 tosses) is statistically unremarkable. But the plan's count is now wrong, and the family's oldest rule — "never go back twice" — is broken on screen without anyone noting the doubled risk.
*Fix:* Have Shaun make one trip to the far stack and Terry shuttle the second Army case on his two-second hops (the scene already shows Terry doing nine such carries), or revise the canonical numbers entry to "raid legs 8 seconds × 2" and the ledger to "three eight-second legs" and accept the change openly.
**SUBSTANTIVE**

---

**3. Rosie says "I never felt a thing" after the audience has watched her feel something.**
*Episode 7, Act Four ("The stove"), the family table.*
Rosie confesses: "Nothing took hold of me in the black. At Mayfair. There was never anybody near me. I never felt a thing." But Episode 1's cold open shows her flinch at "a pull in her gut, the way a lorry passing too close on the A13 tugs at a car" at 5.0 seconds — she builds her lie on that very sensation in Episode 3. And the Mayfair wall *did* log a second body (Joe, six metres away). "Nobody near me" is defensible (Joe was outside the glow's arm's-length radius); "I never felt a thing" is not. A viewer replaying Episode 1 after the confession may feel cheated.
*Fix:* Change "I never felt a thing" to "Nobody touched me. Nothing took hold of me" — which is true (the pull was gravity, not a grip) and still strips the lie down to shame.
**QUIBBLE**

**4. The Episode 10 cold-open heading "as Peg remembers it" may tip the third reversal before it lands.**
*Episode 10, Cold open, spring 2002.*
The cold open shows Frank as a big man in golden light. Act One replays the same morning "as it was" — a thin man who can't open a jam jar. The reversal works because the audience takes the first version at face value. If the words "as Peg remembers it" appear as an on-screen caption, they announce that the memory is subjective and prime the audience to distrust it, softening the twist. (If the heading is only a scene-direction for the director and never reaches the screen, there is no problem.)
*Fix:* If the caption must appear, use something neutral like "Grays, spring 2002" and let the replay's correction carry the weight.

**5. Peg's "I never let him" implies a knowledge she did not have.**
*Episode 10, Act One, Scene 5 (the drawer).*
Peg reads the 2001 Ministry letter aloud — contents she has never seen before (she put it in the drawer unopened) — and says "He never saw this. I never let him." The phrasing ("I never let him") sounds as though she knew what the letter said and chose to hide it. In fact she hid every official letter out of habit and never opened any of them. The guilt the line carries — *I could have saved him and chose not to* — is heavier than the character's actual failure, which is *I never looked*.
*Fix:* "He never saw this. I never opened it." This keeps the guilt (a habit of not-looking killed him) without implying she read and suppressed a cure.
