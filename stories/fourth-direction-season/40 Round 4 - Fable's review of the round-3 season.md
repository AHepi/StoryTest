# SECONDS, round 3: editorial review

*Read: the whole ten-episode revision in `round-3/full-season.md`, start to finish, against the owner's brief, the second feedback, the notes on The Catch, and the master plan (version 3). What follows is a reading of a scriptment (a written outline of each episode, scene by scene, with the key lines of dialogue). Nobody has filmed it, so anything I say about pace is judged from the page.*

*Plain-language key for a few words I cannot avoid. A **plant** is a small detail put in early so that a later surprise is fair rather than pulled from nowhere. A **payoff** is the moment a plant is cashed. A **cold open** is the short scene before the title. A **reversal** is a twist big enough to make you re-read everything before it. The **cargo rule** is the show's rule that anything a crosser carries turns or stays straight with the crosser. The **drift** is the show's word for the chance of coming back mirror-turned, which rises with every second spent in the black.*

---

## 1. Verdict

SECONDS is a ten-episode crime serial about an Essex family of thieves, the Marlows, who can step through walls for a few seconds at a time, and whose one boast is that they always come home straight. Over one spring the boast kills them: their best crosser starves at the table while the family cheers her weight loss, a nineteen-year-old is sent out two hundredths of a second short, and the family founder turns out to have died of the same hidden turning twenty-four years ago. The family that kept nothing on paper is undone, and then saved, by paper.

It achieves what the owner asked for. The core message (a culture that never questions anything dooms its people) is never spoken; it is built out of a chalk board, a drawer of unopened letters, a fry-up, and a scale that lies. The three big reversals land where the plan puts them and each one is planted well in advance. Every invented law of physics from the owner's world document is in the story and doing work; I found one action-line slip against one law, easily cut. Nothing echoes The Catch. No banned word reaches the screen. The wider world is ordinary and kind, not a dystopia.

Is it finished? As a season of scriptments, yes: every episode has its title, pitch, cold open, four or five acts, twist and hook, and the plants-and-payoffs lists at the end of each episode are honest and match the text. It is ready to go to a next stage (full scripts) once four substantive things are fixed, of which the first, an unexplained half-million pounds, is the only one that a careful viewer would carry out of the room. The rest of my notes are matters of a line or two.

## 2. How it keeps the owner's rules

### The core message: shown, never stated

I searched the whole text for the words the brief forbids as ideas (conjecture, criticism, error correction, refute, falsify, fallible, "good explanation", "hard to vary", growth of knowledge, static or dynamic society, tradition of criticism, authority, optimism, "nothing is settled", "question everything", "the problem of", infinity). None appears. Nobody makes a speech about asking questions. The questions on screen are ordinary ones: "Why's it a kilo out?" (ep 9), "What did my dad weigh?" (ep 9), "How do you know it's not the weight?" (ep 9), "Why nine?" (ep 7).

The culture's rules are said only as family banter, in a chorus ("Never go back twice." "Lightest goes longest!" "Face home." "And Marlows come back straight.", ep 1 sc 3), and each rule is then shown doing its harm through an event: one long Mayfair trip instead of two short ones turns Rosie; "make weight" hides her starvation as devotion; "we feed our own" costs a job every year and turns out never to have been true ("We never fed our own.", ep 8 sc 7); "never go to them" keeps a letter in a drawer that would have fed Frank; "face home" makes the one place a turned Marlow could eat unthinkable. The nearest the text comes to a thesis is Terry in the loft, about cousin Lee: "He was off his food, love. Then he wasn't. That was enough for us." (ep 7 sc 4). That is a man explaining his own family, not a writer explaining the show.

The doom grows from the family's own habits and success (Marlow Mix lengthens every job in London, including their rivals'), plus one change in the world they could not have foreseen (the neighbours dig under Grays and the scale starts to lie), which is exactly the pattern the brief asked for. "One right person is not enough" is shown twice: Tess is overruled and put out of the house (ep 7), and Maggie's Register form goes into Declan's stove (ep 4). Only when the whole family writes its crossings down and checks them against the Aherns' books does anything change (ep 10). Asking is not punished: Rosie's one rule-break finds the sea; Kieran's homework is the proof. And the heroine is not always right: Tess planned the 9.4-second trip that turned Rosie, gave Liam two tenths, and handed Rosie to the ward on her own sums. The last image is a girl cheered on a bathroom scale "Under the scale, folded, lies December's front page, unread." (ep 10 sc 18).

### Twists that are fair

Every major surprise has plants I could find in earlier text. The three reversals:

- **Rosie has been turned since Mayfair (ep 3).** Planted in ep 1 by the crisp packet ("She looks at it a beat too long."), the dog ("He's jealous. She's been away."), the jar ("Our Rosie never could open a jar."), the two mirror-script cards on the fridge, Terry's "Goes through me like water, love. Since I turned.", the pot that drops overnight; in ep 2 by seven glances at the catalogue cutting and "New roll's printed backwards, Nan." / "Joke."; in ep 3 by the lard "For the pit. Cold-water swimming." and the dodge from Tess's hand. The reveal is by an instrument (Peg's meter reads LO) and a man's pot, never by a look or a taste.
- **The cure is time, and so is the curse (ep 6); nobody spun anybody, a Marlow floor turned Aoife (ep 7).** Planted by the surgeon at three seconds, "She's done four thousand. Never turned." (ep 1); Terry's eleven two-second hops "Hungry every time after." (ep 1); seven failed three-second hops (ep 2); Danny's boast at the table, "Mynors had us back last autumn, for the floor under the one we did in '24. Two floors now. Long time to be nowhere." (ep 2 sc 6), replayed word for word on the ferry (ep 7 sc 11); the Marlow vans at Aoife's bank (ep 4 sc 8).
- **The scale has lied since April, and Frank was turned (eps 9 and 10).** Planted by Kieran's 19.78 and the "chipped" weight (ep 4), Mick "two hundred light" (ep 4), the envelope dropped unopened in the drawer (ep 4), the brass balance "Never told me a lie." (ep 1); and for Frank, "fifteen stone" said directly under the painted rule (ep 1 sc 6: "For a moment the camera holds the big laughing man and the rule in one frame."), so any viewer can work out that fifteen stone buys one second; Ray's "I met what was left of him." (ep 1); Rosie's weight curve, which is Frank's.

The smaller twists (Ray turned, the Laundry is Ray, Danny loses his fingers, the April quake is the first stroke of a message, Ray's mirror signature means he is cured) are all planted too; I list the one that is over-planted under Problems.

### Every invented law used and never broken

I checked the text against the plan's law ledger. Each law appears and does plot work, not decoration:

- **The stack; only gravity crosses.** Three tugs in the black ("the Hill", "the Deep", "the Far", ep 1 sc 8); a sea eight metres above the Marlows' lino (ep 9); a third, fainter pull for the second message (ep 10 epilogue).
- **Unpinning, timed by mass.** The painted rule on the board; the tingle two seconds before the end; Liam's fry-up counting ("You're not going over that water on nothing.", ep 5 sc 13); running out as a weight on a wall ("It printed all night.", ep 5 sc 16); a clean plane at Shaun's thigh and Danny's knuckles; a sent hand blind in a cold store; an amputee's "Eleven tenths, Nan." (ep 9 sc 1).
- **Free translation; every knot comes undone; sight inside everything at arm's length.** The Mayfair crate left banded and knotted and empty; Shaun taking the Ahern mooring knots apart from inside (ep 5 sc 8); a guard's heart seen through his ribs; Danny's hand hovering at Joe's heart (ep 6).
- **Rotation mirrors; a slide never does.** The painting's window on the wrong side; "Linen has a hand, Mrs Marlow." (ep 1); Ray's signature coming out backwards when he is cured (ep 10).
- **No up in the dark.** Blindfold in the flooded pit ("Breathe out a bit. Follow your bubbles."); the same trick keeping Rosie alive in black water; the grip, which turns one of two and takes a hand; the drift table read aloud from the Obverse binder (ep 6 sc 7).
- **Handedness is fate.** Fat and spirits; Peg's meter LO; looking-glass milk ("Her milk was wrong. He screamed three days before we understood.", ep 4); a dog that growls at the turned; HAND: T doesn't fly (ep 4 sc 3); the turned know at once and lie.
- **Neighbours weigh.** The Twitch flooding every City vault; a quake with no fault; a plate printing minus 59.0 for a boy standing in the black; the message in tremors; the flour on the brass balance against the little bridge, 5.000 against 4.95.
- **The other hand next door.** One crab, one turned man, two meters: "Two meters. Same blood. Both of them right." (ep 9 sc 9).

The seven theorems are all in play (walls became weights; privacy has a radius; the cure uses the same dice; the mirror is relative; custody by pantry; one honest channel; exile is a cure), and so are the corollaries (paper as witness, passports, lightest courier, the held breath, the pit as school, murder by diet, the dog, astronomy as neighbourhood watch). I found one slip against the rule of sight in an action line in ep 10 (Problem 4 below). Nothing else breaks a law. The drift table is obeyed: four eight-second crossings come up one turned, the two-second hops do nothing, the 9.4 turns, the 10.0 fails to cure at three-in-ten, Ray at 7.5 is cured at about four in ten.

### No echo of The Catch, or of Caraway

None of the listed devices appears: no lift or lift engineer, no gravity needle, no ledge, no oxygen, no dishes of germs, no flask of blood, no backwards signs seen through turned eyes, no hands on glass, no kind figure from next door, no child's drawing, no toy with a letter, no "the world didn't change, we did" reveal. The camera stays on the straight side of every mirror throughout. The word "flask" appears three times, all as a tea flask (see the last quibble). "Lift" appears only as an ordinary verb (a grate, a bag, a crab). No rite at a set age, no scripture, no heretics, no moving the machine. Religion is left out, as the plan says.

### Not a dystopia

The world works and is largely kind: Trading Standards certifies scales, a Register feeds the turned ("Nobody asks how you turned. Nobody asks anything.", Ray, ep 8 sc 6), a fireman goes home with his first ration box talking about leeks, Dr Osei keeps a cruel secret for a kind reason, the police take a rig rather than a person. Only two crossing families, across one river, never check. That is the brief exactly.

## 3. What works best

**The check-in that becomes a lie.** "Straight?" "Straight." is asked after every crossing from the first scene, and it dies on screen in ep 9 sc 7: Peg's meter reads LO on Shaun and he says, "I knew before we were out the marsh. I said it anyhow." In ep 10 nobody asks Ray; Kieran holds out the meter instead. A ritual the audience has heard forty times, turned into a wound. Protect it.

**The fry-up.** Ep 5 sc 13 is the season's best scene and it is silent: "The camera stays at the table. He eats every bite. Sixpence's tail slows, and stops." Then "That's my boy." The audience has known since ep 1 that "everything in you counts, breakfast too", so it watches a grandmother kill her grandson with love, and cannot say so. The answer, "Either one, he makes it. Both, he doesn't. By two hundredths." (ep 9 sc 12), is arithmetic anyone can follow.

**The ferry.** Ep 7 is the hinge and it is two women with a pencil. "Nobody counted her, Tess. That's what I've come to say." / "Then let's count her now." Then the first graph either family has ever drawn, on the back of a timetable, and the turn nobody saw coming: "Why nine?" ... "Yours, Tess. MARLOW on the side." Maggie's "My uncle died of a war about concrete." and "All these years I've had half of this in a drawer." are the season's theme in objects.

**The night of writing.** Ep 10 sc 7 and 8: the family writes its crimes onto the certified roll, knowing the Register comes Monday with a warrant. Peg says the word at last, "Terry. Turned. Twenty twenty-one." and Terry's whole answer is "Ta, Peg." Terry's die: "Six, I stay hungry. Anything else, I get my tea." and his refusal of the handshake: "I cook with these." Not a wasted line.

**The scale that lies and the boy who checks.** Kieran's homework (weigh the same twenty kilos every night) is the one honest instrument in the house, and the family laughs at it. "It's meant to be a flat line." / "Everything wobbles. Do your reading." (ep 6 sc 9). The flour on the brass balance, then "It was never the chip." (ep 9 sc 11), is the cleanest single proof in the season.

**Ray.** A detective who is the villain's secret feeder is an old idea; here it is done by ledger. "You counted us in dinners." / "It's what there was to count." And the tomatoes he cannot eat: "They need the watering." (ep 8 sc 6).

**Two lines that carry whole episodes.** "I gave you nine." (Tess, ep 6, after her own meter reads 5.4). "I went shopping." (Rosie, ep 9, dripping, with a crab in her hood).

**The cost, seen not said.** Danny punching a new hole in his own belt on raid night "rough at the edges, the way his son's was" (ep 9 sc 1). Peg re-chalking Liam's 62.4 as 65.1: "There's your number, love." (ep 9 sc 12), then writing beside her husband's ticket "FRANK. 10 ST 2. TURNED." / "There's your number, Frank." (ep 10 sc 5).

## 4. The problems, most serious first

### SUBSTANTIVE 1. Half a million pounds sits in Terry's name from episode 8 and nobody ever mentions using it

**Where.** Ep 8 sc 6 (Ray's flat) and sc 7 (the table); the plan's epilogue.

**What the text says.** Ray charged the family the going rate for five years and banked it: "There's a price for it. Three hundred a day, going rate. Your mother paid it." The passbook: "The name on it: T. MARLOW. The column of figures under it runs down the page and onto the next, deposits, fortnight by fortnight, five years of them. No withdrawals. Not one." Tess tells the family: "The money's in a building society in Grays. In Terry's name. He never spent a penny of it." The season's own arithmetic makes this sum: Tess in ep 1, "Hundred and nine thousand five hundred." a year for one mouth, times five years, is about £547,000, before the doubled and tripled orders since April.

**Why it matters.** Two scenes earlier in the season the family has said, of a £180,000 cure, "There's nothing in this country pays that but years." (Tess, ep 6 sc 5). Now they learn there is three times that in a legal account in Terry's own name, from a man who plainly wants them to have it. In the very same scene Peg answers hunger with a long-crossing raid on a Register depot for two weeks of porridge, and the family votes for it; nobody says "there's half a million in Terry's name". Obverse is a legal clinic that would take a bank transfer, and the Aherns buy mirror food off the Rotterdam boats for cash. The only response the text gives the money is Terry's: "Five year, and my name's on it. You bought my dinners off a dead man's plate, Peg." A careful viewer will spend the rest of episode 8 and all of episode 9 waiting for someone to mention it, and the pressure behind the depot raid ("I know what empty does. I've buried both.") leaks away. The plan carries the same gap ("the money sits untouched").

**Fix (one line, no change to the theme).** Let the audit have frozen it. Ray, in the flat, after "Never touched a penny": "And you can't touch it either. The audit found the book before it found me. It's exhibit now, same as the tubs." Then in ep 8 sc 7 Tess repeats it in one clause ("frozen with the rest"). If the owner would rather the family refuse it, that is on theme too, but it must happen on screen: Tess: "It's Terry's. It buys three of them." Peg: "Dead men's dinners. We'll not eat off it twice." Either way the epilogue's untouched account then reads as a choice or a lock, not a forgotten drawer.

### SUBSTANTIVE 2. Peg's plan to hop the painting until it "turns" contradicts what the family shows it already knows one episode earlier

**Where.** Ep 1 sc 3 (the tissue) against ep 2 sc 4 (the plan).

**What the text says.** In ep 1 Kieran asks why they burn the wrapping and reads the framed cutting: "Police forensics: wrapping shows 'clean translation, no rotation'." Danny: "Means we came back straight. Every time." Peg: "Police said so. In print. Paper talks. Burn it." So the family knows, as its trade, that a thing carried through the black comes back the same way round as its carrier; that is why the tissue is evidence. In ep 2 Peg proposes: "You face home when you cross, don't you? A picture doesn't. It's got nothing to face with. You hold it loose, you let it roll about, and one of these goes it comes back the right way." Tess's only objection is "Could take a hundred goes." Nobody at the table says the obvious thing: if the picture turns, so does the girl holding it.

**Why it matters.** The show needs the family not to make this connection, so that Rosie can use the hops as a secret cure and so that Ray's line in ep 3, "If the painting turned, so did whoever carried it.", lands as news. But because ep 1 has Danny state the rule almost in so many words, the family's blindness in ep 2 reads as the writers' convenience rather than the culture's habit, and Tess, who counts everything, looks briefly stupid. The episode's own plants-and-payoffs note admits it: "nobody in the family states the cargo rule before 3. Episode 1's 'Means we came back straight' about the burned tissue is as close as they come, and nobody puts the two together."

**Fix.** Make the family's mistake explicit and owned, so it is the rule at work. Give Liam, who is already the one who asks awkward questions ("Could've gone in twice.", ep 1), one line in ep 2 sc 4: "Won't she come back with it? Whichever way it goes?" and Peg the family's answer: "Not if she faces home. Picture's got nothing to face with." The table laughs; Rosie's face does one thing and stops. Now the plan is visibly a folk belief (facing home keeps *you* straight), Ray's line in ep 3 breaks a stated belief rather than an unspoken one, and the moment becomes another tell for sharp viewers. Alternatively soften Danny's ep 1 line to "Means the wrapping came back straight." and let Peg's "Paper talks" carry the rest, so the family never quite states the rule.

### SUBSTANTIVE 3. The episode 6 cold open does not say what Danny has been doing for six nights, and the hit looks like a coincidence

**Where.** Ep 6 sc 1.

**What the text says.** "Seventh night. If the tow runs, the man comes again." and "Wait for the printer and the lad's gone. The plate prints after a load settles. I have to be in there first." At 02:04 by the wall clock he steps into the black and Joe is there, having let the envelope go "a moment ago".

**Why it matters.** The display is dark, so there is nothing for Danny to watch on the other six nights but the clock. Either he stepped in blind at 02:04 on each of them and found nothing (six crossings spent, which the family, who count everything, would count), or he sat for six nights doing nothing, which makes no sense. The text does not say which. On top of that, hitting a crossing of a few seconds inside a sixty-second minute by wall clock is a lucky hit. The luck costs Danny four fingers and his hand, so it is the allowed kind, but the muddle about the stakeout makes a careful viewer ask "why tonight?" at the very moment the scene wants them holding their breath.

**Fix.** Two sentences in the cold open's narration: "Six nights he has stepped in at four past by the clock and found the black empty. Six crossings, two seconds each, and nobody told." That makes it a stakeout that finally pays, in character for Danny, and it keeps the blind step the plan wants. To widen the window honestly, let Joe still be at the safe rather than just done: Joe's budget is over eight seconds (59 kg), so change "let go a moment ago" to Joe's fingers still withdrawing from the steel, which also gives Danny's hovering hand its beat.

### SUBSTANTIVE 4. One action line breaks the rule of sight

**Where.** Ep 10 sc 12, Ray in the black.

**What the text says.** "The glow opens an arm's length round him, grey-gold, and in it the office stands open like a cutaway: the table's grain, the flue's old soot, and at the end of the plate the green safe."

**Why it matters.** The law (the plan's rule 3, and Law 3 on the owner's checklist) is that an unpinned eye sees only what lies inside the glow, an arm's length, and beyond that black. Ray is standing on the little bridge. The safe stands at the end of the plate, so that is fair. The long table and the stove flue are across the room. Every other crossing in the season keeps to the rule scrupulously (in ep 6 Danny sees the sleeping boy only because the camp bed's head is "a foot from the green safe"). This is the one place a director would be told to light the whole room. It is the smallest of the four substantives, but the owner's instruction is that no law is ever broken, and it is a two-word cut.

**Fix.** Delete "the table's grain, the flue's old soot, and" so the line reads: "in it the office stands open like a cutaway: at the end of the plate the green safe, its steel gone clear as water". If the steelyard hung under the flue needs to be in his glow for the scene, move the steelyard's hook to the wall bracket beside the safe in sc 8 instead.

### QUIBBLE 5. Ray's turn is given away in episode 1, not planted

**Where.** Ep 1 sc 11, thirty seconds at Ray's desk: "a mug of vodka, with no pretence that it's water, a packet of cream crackers, and a block of lard with a butter knife standing in it. He spreads lard on a cracker and eats it." It comes four scenes after Terry's "Fat and spirits. It's all that sticks."

**Why it might matter.** The plan means Ray's turn to be episode 2's twist (the Register card in the Obverse waiting room). A block of lard with a knife in it, right after Terry's line about fat and spirits, is not a plant; for anyone awake it is the reveal. The plan's own revision note says the desk scene "plants the vodka and lard", so this is intended, and only an audience can say whether it plays as a wink or a spoiler.

**Fix, if wanted.** Keep the vodka and the crackers at the desk and hide the lard: the colleague's line "Lunch, Ray? Or is that it?" still works over crackers and vodka alone. Save the lard for the waiting room, where the Register card sits beside it.

### QUIBBLE 6. "Nobody ever asked why" is said twice, and the second time it is the thesis

**Where.** Ep 7 sc 4, Terry in the loft: "Nobody ever asked why." (answered by "He was off his food, love. Then he wasn't. That was enough for us."). Ep 7 sc 11, Tess on the ferry, of the same cousin: "Nine. Nobody ever asked why."

**Why it might matter.** Terry's line is a man about his family and is at once answered by the family's habit, which is perfect. Tess repeating it to Maggie, without the answer, is the writer underlining. One is enough.

**Fix.** On the ferry, let Tess give the numbers only: "Ours is Lee. Off his food all one winter, right as rain after Canary Wharf. Nine." and stop.

### QUIBBLE 7. Rosie's "Still me" on the field has nothing to rest on

**Where.** Ep 10 sc 12, Rosie back on the grass: "Still me."

**Why it might matter.** The show's own rule is that the turned know at once because the world looks reversed, and that everyone else can only tell by an instrument; the plan wants no instrument at the field. Rosie's claim is therefore her own knowledge, but in fog on a field there is no print to reverse, and the audience has been trained all season to hear "Straight" as a hope or a lie. There is one asymmetric thing on that field: Danny's bandaged right hand, holding the gate.

**Fix.** Half a line of action before she speaks: "Her eyes go to Danny's hand on the gate, the bandaged one, where she expects it." Then "Still me." is her seeing, not her hoping, and no rule is bent.

### QUIBBLE 8. Maggie's arrival at two in the morning is not caused on screen

**Where.** Ep 10 sc 8: "Headlights swing across the blind. This time nobody tenses. The back door opens on MAGGIE AHERN with her tide books under her arm, and JOE behind her carrying the salvage steelyard".

**Why it might matter.** Nobody rang her in any scene we saw; ep 7 left the door open ("My half's in here. Whenever, Tess."). It is a small hole because the audience expects her, but one phone line pays the ep 7 plant and makes her crossing the river a thing Tess did.

**Fix.** In sc 7, between two names, Tess with the phone at her ear: "Whenever, you said." and puts it down.

### QUIBBLE 9. The dog does not growl at Shaun before the meter finds him

**Where.** Ep 9 sc 7. "The family comes in with the cold on them: Peg, Danny, Terry, Shaun on his stick — and stops, all together, at the sight of the salt." Then Peg meters Terry, Rosie, and Shaun (LO). Only afterwards: "Then Sixpence works along the stack, case by case, growling his low note at each one".

**Why it might matter.** The corollary the season has kept faithfully is that the dog knows before the lab does: Sixpence growls at Terry for five years, at Rosie from the first breakfast, at Danny the morning after the grab. Here the meter gets Shaun first and the dog only sorts the porridge. Letting the dog have him at the door keeps the run whole and makes Peg's "Straight?" "Straight." worse, because the audience already knows.

**Fix.** As Shaun crosses the threshold on his stick, Sixpence gets up and growls, "the low steady note", and Danny hears it before Peg reaches for the meter.

### QUIBBLE 10. Peg lets Rosie go to the Register on two words

**Where.** Ep 8 sc 9. The night before, Peg: "We don't go to them." and "I'm not starting on their word now." At the door next morning, to Rosie: "You'll be fed."

**Why it might matter.** The admission has already happened at the fridge (Peg putting the waiting spoon and tub away in the drawer is one of the season's best silent beats), so Peg's surrender is earned. But the door scene gives her nothing physical to do while she loses, and Peg is a woman who acts with her hands. This could go either way; the bareness may be the point.

**Fix, if wanted.** One object: Peg puts Rosie's coat round her shoulders and does the top button, the way she "takes the coat off her shoulders" in ep 3 sc 15. Then "You'll be fed."

### QUIBBLE 11. Ray's "that one I didn't need a ledger for" is not clear

**Where.** Ep 8 sc 6: "(His finger on the May line.) And that one I didn't need a ledger for."

**Why it might matter.** The line implies Ray knew about the third turned mouth (Danny) some other way, but nothing on screen shows him learning it; the plan says he learns Danny is turned in this episode. As written it is a small puzzle the scene does not answer.

**Fix.** Either "And that one you could hear from Victoria." (the tripled order was obvious) or cut it and let the finger on the May line speak.

### QUIBBLE 12. Peg's own pencil, "12 st 4", is read out in front of her and she is given no reaction

**Where.** Ep 10 sc 2. Kieran reads Frank's tickets down the roll with Peg in the loft: "September: FRANK 78.0. Beside it, in pencil, in Peg's own hand from twenty-four years ago: 12 st 4." The scene ends with the family not saying the word. Peg's admission comes two scenes later, to Ray: "Seven point one. That's my hand. I pencil'd it on his ticket."

**Why it might matter.** "Fifteen stone" is the family's founding number and Peg has said it three times in the season (ep 1 sc 6, ep 3 sc 3, ep 9 sc 12), the last time as "the one thing in this family that's never been wrong."; the ep 10 cold open then shows the fifteen-stone man as she remembers him. The loft is where her own handwriting proves the legend was never true even before he turned, and the page gives her no face. Her silence may be the choice; if so it should be a written silence.

**Fix, if wanted.** One action line after "12 st 4": "Peg looks at her own pencil for a long moment, and says nothing, and Kieran reads on." Or one line from her: "Twelve four. I wrote that."

### QUIBBLE 13. The rig strap "knotted short" invites the question the show's own signature raises

**Where.** Ep 7 sc 2 ("We'll put a knot in the tail."), sc 10; ep 9 sc 1 ("Shaun takes Danny's and knots the strap short"); ep 10 sc 10.

**Why it might matter.** The owner's Law 3 says "Every knot comes undone", and the show's proudest boast is "Didn't even touch the knot." (ep 1). The plan reads the law as "a crosser can undo any knot", so a knot inside the field stays tied, and the text is consistent with that. But a viewer who has absorbed the law may wonder why a knot in a rig strap survives the black, at the very moment (ep 10) the strap matters. It costs nothing to avoid.

**Fix.** Make it a punched hole, as with the belts: "Shaun works a new hole in the strap with the nail, the way Danny did his belt." That also rhymes with ep 9 sc 1.

### QUIBBLE 14. The word "flask"

**Where.** Ep 1 sc 5 ("Tea from a flask on the tailgate of the van"; "screws the lid back on the flask"), ep 2 sc 1 ("a flask of tea"), ep 7 sc 7 and sc 11 ("The flask sits between them"; "the flask goes cold between them").

**Why it might matter.** The plan's list of things never to echo from The Catch says "no flask". These are tea flasks, nothing like The Catch's flask of turned blood, and no reader would confuse them. But the rule is stated as a word, and the swap is free.

**Fix.** "Thermos" in all five places; ep 7 already uses "thermos" once.

## 5. What I am unsure of

- Whether Terry arrived straight or turned, what Rosie saw on the shore, and what went down the drain are all left open on purpose, and each reading has something on the page holding it (Terry's die in his pocket; the sand on Rosie's feet; Sixpence at the bucket). Those are working open questions, not holes. I have not counted them as problems, and a reviser should not close them.
