# SECONDS, full season (round 1 revision): editorial review

*Reviewer: Fable. What I read: the whole season, episode 1 to the December epilogue, start to finish, plus the brief, the second feedback, the notes on The Catch, and the master plan (version 3). I read a script on the page; nobody has filmed it, so anything I say about pace is a judgement from reading, not from watching. Line numbers below refer to the season file. Where I quote, the words are the season's.*

A few words I use, in plain terms. A **plant** is something shown early, quietly, so that a later surprise is fair rather than pulled out of a hat. A **cold open** is the short scene before the title. A **tell** is a small clue that a character is hiding something. The show's own words (the black, the rig, the bite, the little bridge, "make weight") I use as the show does.

---

## 1. Verdict

SECONDS is a crime family drama about an Essex firm of thieves who can step through walls for a few seconds at a time, whose pride is that "Marlows come back straight", and who are quietly killing themselves with the very habits they are proudest of: making weight, never going back twice, keeping nothing on paper, feeding their own. Across ten episodes it does what the owner asked. The message (a family that never checks its own numbers against anyone else's dooms its people) is never spoken; it is carried by a starving girl who is cheered for being thin, a boy sent out two hundredths of a second short, a founder's death that was a hidden turning all along, and a scale that has lied since the neighbours dug under the town. The three big reversals land where the plan says they should (episode 3, episodes 6 and 7, episodes 9 and 10), and every one of them is planted before it arrives. Every invented law of the world is used, and used for plot, and I found none broken. It is not a dystopia: the wider world is ordinary and works, and only two families on one river never check.

Is it finished? Nearly. It is a complete, coherent, very well-made season with a small number of fixable faults. Four are substantive (one is a plain continuity break at the hinge of the second reversal, one is a numbers contradiction in the family's own record, and two are moments where a character ignores a tool the show has taught us they have). None of them needs a scene rebuilt; each is a line or two. After those, what remains is a list of quibbles that an owner could settle by taste. I would call it one careful pass from done.

## 2. How it keeps the owner's rules

### The core message: shown, never said

Nobody in the season says anything like "we never questioned". The nearest anyone comes is a weigher counting: "Nobody counted her" (ep 7, sc 7) and "Nobody in this family crosses to the last of their count again" (ep 10, sc 10). Those are ordinary words about a job, which is what the plan asked for. The message is carried by events, and the events are strong because each one comes from a rule that was once right:

- "Make weight" hides Rosie's starvation as devotion: "Clears two plates and never puts a gram on" (ep 3, sc 6).
- "Never go back twice" turns Mayfair into one 9.4-second trip when two short ones would have done (ep 1, sc 3: "Could've gone in twice"), and the drift does the rest.
- "Nothing on paper" means the answer was in the loft the whole time: "It's on the paper, Terry. It was on the paper the whole time" (ep 7, sc 4).
- "We feed our own" turns out to have been Ray feeding them on dead men's rations: "We never fed our own" (ep 8, sc 7).
- "Never go to them" rests on a letter in a drawer that has been out of date since 2019: "Police don't get the list, love. Not since 2019" (ep 8, sc 10).
- "Face home" kept the turned from the one pantry they could eat from, one world over (ep 9).

"Always" is earned: Frank was killed by the same code twenty-four years earlier, and the Aherns across the river keep the code with one difference (Maggie opens her post) and lose Aoife and Con to it. The last image is a girl cheered on a bathroom scale with the answer folded under it, unread. And the season does not teach the opposite by accident: Tess, the one who counts, is wrong at Mayfair, wrong about Liam's margin, and wrong to hand Rosie to the ward; the boy who checks (Kieran) is the one who saves them; Rosie's one rule-break finds the food.

### The twists: fair, and hard to swap

I checked each reversal against its plants. All are planted before they land, and each is built from the world's own logic, so a different twist would break earlier scenes:

- **The "forgery" is the original** (ep 1): the catalogue cutting with the window on the other side is on the wall from act 2 ("In the cutting on the wall, the window is on the woman's left. On the canvas on the table, it's on her right. Nobody looks."), and the framed cuttings about "clean translation, no rotation" tell us why paper is burned.
- **Rosie turned at Mayfair** (ep 3): the crisp packet, the dog, the jar, the mirror-script card beside the wobbly old one, the pot dropping, the seven glances at the cutting, the lard. Fair. (Perhaps too fair; see Q1.)
- **Liam runs out though the numbers said he'd make it** (ep 5): 62.4, 1.5 of notes, 7.2 against 7.0 are all said aloud; the fry-up is eaten on camera; Mick's "two hundred light", the 19.78, and the council envelope in the drawer are all on screen in ep 4.
- **The cure is time** (ep 6): the TV surgeon "Never turned" at three seconds (ep 1), Terry's "Eleven times I went again. Two seconds a go. Hungry every time after" (ep 1), seven failed three-second hops (ep 2).
- **A Marlow floor turned Aoife** (ep 7): Danny names Mynors at the table in ep 2 ("Mynors had us back last autumn, for the floor under the one we did in '24"), and Declan says "Their vans were outside it all week" in ep 4.
- **The Laundry is Ray** (ep 8): the batch codes (ep 1), "what was left of him" (ep 1), the Register card (ep 2), "You look well." / "I've eaten." (ep 5).
- **The April quake was the first stroke of the message** (ep 8): "another next-door event" (ep 5), the "metronome" on the car radio (ep 7).
- **The scale killed Liam with Peg's plate** (ep 9): the whole of Kieran's log, the chip, the flour on the brass balance.
- **Frank was turned and starving** (ep 10): "Fifteen stone when the black took him" is said under the painted rule "A FIFTH OF A SECOND A KILO UNDER A HUNDRED" (ep 1, sc 6), a sum any viewer can do; the jam jar (ep 1) replayed as it was; Ray's "I met what was left of him" (ep 1).
- **The field is the door** (ep 10): "found wet, sand adhering" (ep 1), Frank's "the tide went out and forgot to tell the moon" (ep 1), the tide books (ep 7), the sea (ep 9).

### The laws: all used, none broken

I checked the arithmetic wherever a number is said aloud. The budget rule holds every time: 44 kg gives "Eleven two"; 72 gives "five point six"; 58 gives "Eight four"; 89 gives "Two two"; 61.0 gives "Seven point eight"; 46.2 gives "Ten point eight"; 38.0 gives "Twelve point four"; 63.9 gives 7.2 and 65.1 gives 6.98. The 1.1 per cent lie is exact everywhere it is used: 20.00 to 19.78; 5.000 to 4.95; 61.7 to 61.0; 89.0 to 88; 52.5 to 51.9; 71.4 to 70.6; 38.4 to 38.0; an 18-tonne lorry "two hundred light"; and Osei's wall reads Tess at "sixty-one kilos, seven hundred grams" against "point nought on my bridge" (ep 6, sc 8), which is the first outside instrument to catch the lie.

The odds table is respected and outcomes are believable at those odds: none of Terry's eleven two-second hops, Peg's fourteen, or Rosie's seven three-second hops does anything; Frank turns at four seconds (one in twelve); Rosie at 9.4 and Aoife at nine (one nine each way); Marrable Street's ten seconds "came up wrong" at three in ten; Shaun's three eight-second crossings come up straight, straight, turned; Ray comes home at 7.5 on "about four in ten". No long runs of luck either way.

The rules of sight, the wall, the grip, the bite and the mirror are kept: the sent hand is blind ("We stay on Danny's face, eyes shut", ep 4); the weigh-wall floods at three every time and logs bodies without flooding; Danny's grip is "on the bare wrist above the sleeve" and costs four fingers; the bite fires only after "a minute on the wrong side", blind, for half the seconds ("Five point four", ep 9); next door is never shown ("The screen goes black", ep 9 and ep 10); no look ever settles who flipped, only a meter, a balance, a steelyard, a catalogue, a dog, or a man's own blood ("Two meters. Same blood. Both of them right.", ep 9). The neighbours reach us only as weight: the quake, the Twitch, the Nobody Tide, the three tugs, the message. Exile is a real cure and someone takes it. Custody by pantry is built into the ward before anyone names it ("the only lock in the building, and it is hers to work", ep 8).

### The Catch

I found no echo of its premise, set pieces, devices, characters or ending. No mint, no taste test, no flask, dish or growth, no hands on glass, no reserve bar or spare with a light, no lift, no ship, no kind figure from next door, no backwards print from a turned character's point of view, no meal that can't be shared as a closing image. The words "spare", "reserve", "needle", "lift" occur only in ordinary senses ("a second and a bit to spare", "pins and needles", "lifts the crab out of the bowl"). Two brushes are worth watching, both slight (Q10, Q11 below).

### Banned words and off-screen names

I searched the whole file for every word on the plan's list (conjecture, criticism, error correction, refute, falsify, fallible, "good explanation", "hard to vary", growth of knowledge, static, dynamic, tradition, authority, optimism, "nothing is settled", "question everything", "the problem of", infinity) and for the background names (the Convention, the Looking Act, the Privacy Order, the seed bank, the Hand Company). None appears. Nobody makes a speech about asking questions. The questions on screen are the plain ones the plan asked for: "How much?", "Why's it a kilo out?", "What did my dad weigh?", "How do you know it's not the weight?"

### Not a dystopia; religion

The world is pleasant and works: Trading Standards hangs a certificate, a nurse hands out a meter, a fireman goes home with his ration box and talks about leeks, a scientist on a riverbank is "mild, astonished". No regime, no wall, no caste. Religion is absent, as the plan chose.

## 3. What works best

Protect these in any revision.

- **The check-in as ritual and plant at once.** "Then comes the question this family asks before it asks whether you're hurt. DANNY: Straight? ROSIE: Straight." (ep 1, sc 1). It runs through nine episodes, dies in ep 9 when Shaun says it and Peg's meter reads LO, and is replaced in ep 10 by a boy holding out a meter. That is structure doing the work of theme without a word of theme.
- **The cheering.** Every weigh-in is a party; on re-watch every party is a funeral. "Goes through me like water, love. Since I turned." Peg's knife stops in the cake (ep 1, sc 13). And the wound under it, said once: "Nan tells it wrong. She says I was proud." / "Weren't you?" / "I was hungry." (ep 2, sc 14).
- **Rosie passing on the disease.** "They'll clap you." / "They never clap me." / "They will." "She hears it as it comes out." (ep 4, sc 4). The theme's mechanism (a kind habit handed down) in four lines.
- **Liam's death and its two tickets.** "Tingle comes, you're back in the brick. He isn't back in the brick." (ep 5, sc 14). Then Peg with the van's going-out and coming-home tickets, "one in each hand", and "She sits down on the bottom stair." (ep 5, sc 17). The scale tells her before anyone can.
- **The dog ahead of the lab, and the line that ends the family's excuse.** "DANNY: Jealous old bugger. TERRY: (gently) No, love." (ep 6, sc 2).
- **The pencil.** "Tess's hand goes up behind her ear, to the pencil that has lived there for twenty years and never once come down. She holds it a second. It has never been sharpened. MAGGIE: Mine's sharp." (ep 7, sc 9). It comes down for good in ep 10 and goes into Kieran's pocket.
- **Peg stopped by a certificate, not a conscience.** "If the rolls burn, there's no licence. If there's no licence, there's no weighbridge. And the weighbridge, whatever else it is, feeds them." (ep 7, sc 12). The paper that saves the family is the paper the law made her keep.
- **Ray's tomatoes.** "TESS: Then why grow them? RAY: They need the watering. (beat) How is she? TESS: Hungry." (ep 8, sc 6).
- **Rosie crossing on the plate.** "(to the plate, almost a joke) Print me proper." (ep 9, sc 4), and later: "Wanted it written. (beat) If I'd drowned over there, somebody could read where I went." (ep 9, sc 6). The girl who hid everything leaves her number where it can't lie.
- **The proof of next door.** "Two meters. Same blood. Both of them right." (ep 9, sc 9). An instrument and a man's own hunger, never a taste, exactly as the plan demanded.
- **The confession that completes the sum.** "PEG: I made him breakfast." (ep 9, sc 12); "Either one, he makes it. Both, he doesn't. By two hundredths."
- **Frank.** "FRANK. 10 ST 2. TURNED." "PEG: There's your number, Frank." (ep 10, sc 5). The first thing the founder writes on paper is the founder's death.
- **Ray's signature.** "Eleven year I've wrote back to front so you lot could read me. Give us a fortnight." (ep 10, sc 12). The mirror is relative, played for a laugh at the one moment the family holds its breath, and settled by the meter.
- **The painting handed back.** "Mind, her window's on the wrong side. She came back like that. She's staying like that." (ep 10, sc 15).
- **The last image across the river.** "Under the scale, folded, lies December's front page, unread." (epilogue, sc 18).

## 4. The problems, most serious first

### SUBSTANTIVE

**S1. Episode 7, scenes 11 and 12: the ferry graph is in two places at once.**
On the ferry Maggie keeps the graph: "MAGGIE: *(folding the timetable in half along its crease, careful, like something she means to keep)*" (line 5421), then "*(She puts the folded sheet in her tide book, and the tide book in her bag.)*" (5425), then "*(She taps her bag.)* My half's in here. Whenever, Tess." (5439). Tess leaves "with the ticket in her coat and the folded shape of nothing at all, because everything worth carrying tonight is in her head" (5441). Twelve lines later, at the table: "She takes the ferry timetable out of her coat, unfolds it on the table, and turns it round to face them: the ticks, the crosses" (5453), and Peg burns it. *Why it matters:* this is the hinge of the second reversal and the cause of Tess being put out of the house; a viewer who has just watched Maggie bag the sheet will see Tess produce it from a coat we were told held nothing. The plan intends Tess to carry the graph home and Peg to burn it, and Maggie to keep only her tide books ("Maggie's drawer (10): 'My half's in here. Whenever.' Her tide books, her steelyard, Joe"). *Fix:* on the ferry, Maggie folds the timetable and pushes it across to Tess ("Yours. I've the books."), puts only the tide book in her bag, and "My half's in here" then means the books, as the plan says. Cut "the folded shape of nothing at all" and let Tess walk off with the timetable and the binder inside her coat, which is where scene 12 needs them.

**S2. Episode 7 against episodes 1 and 3: a job longer than "the longest job a Marlow's ever done".**
In ep 1 Peg toasts Mayfair's 9.4: "Longest job a Marlow's ever done." (line 125). In ep 3, at 9.4 on the stopwatch, "It's the longest a Marlow has ever been gone, and the count goes on past it." (2288). In ep 7's loft, Lee's Canary Wharf is "Nine and a half" (5049: "TESS: Nine and a half. *(beat)* Same as Mayfair, near enough."), and on the ferry "Nine and a half. Nobody ever asked why." (5375). Nine and a half is longer than nine point four, and Tess planned it, so she would remember. There is a second slip in the same exchange: Terry says "You planned it. You were, what, sixteen?" (5047), but Tess is reading the '09 roll ("Tuesday, March the ninth, '09", 4981), and Tess, 38 in 2026, was twenty-one in 2009. *Why it matters:* numbers are this show's currency, the audience has been taught to hold them, and "longest ever" is a toast, a record in chalk and the reason Marrable Street's ten is a milestone. *Fix:* make Canary Wharf "a nine" (still over eight, still "long", still a cure at three in five): "TESS: Nine. *(beat)* Same as Aoife's, near enough." And either drop the age or make it "twenty-one".

**S3. Episode 6, cold open: Danny ambushes on the wall clock, though the printer would tell him the second Joe arrived.**
"The scale's display is dark. There is nothing in this room to watch but the clock." (4277). But the show has taught us since ep 1 that "The scales print by themselves whenever a load settles on them" (89) and that the printer is an alarm you can hear: Ray's car ("the printer chatters out its weight. Nobody's due.", 773) and the false alarm in ep 2 ("Headlights sweep across the blind. The printer chatters. Every head in the room goes to the window.", 1203). It prints Joe that very minute: "he tore them off the printer the moment he saw the grown-ups' faces... 02:04 · LITTLE BRIDGE · −59.0 KG." (4379). Danny has 2.2 real seconds; stepping in blind on a guess when a till a yard away would chatter the instant Joe stood over the plate is a gamble the show has told us he needn't take. *Why it matters:* a careful viewer will ask "why not listen for the printer?", and the cold open's tension leaks. I know the plan forbids a live instrument reading (to keep clear of The Catch's needle), and the writers obeyed it; the answer is not to use the printer but to say why he can't. *Fix:* one line in the cold open, in Danny's head or aloud to the dark: "If he waits for the printer, the lad's gone. Two seconds, a drop, and back to the boat. The plate prints after a load settles. He has to be in there first." That keeps the ambush blind, keeps the ticket for the morning, and makes the gamble sharper, not weaker. (Ep 5's "RAY: It won't know till the morning. / PEG: Then I'll know in the morning." can stand as it is: nobody reads a ticket till morning.)

**S4. Episodes 6 and 7: Maggie sends the ferry invitation into the black beside the safe, by her son, a fortnight after she had Tess on the phone, and no one says why.**
In ep 4 Maggie rings the office and gets Tess: "Keep the rest. I'm not asking for the rest. The box I'll buy back. Name a price and I'll row it over tonight." (3293). In ep 6 the invitation comes as an envelope Joe posts through the safe in the black at 02:04, the same drop that had carried a bullet, and Danny is waiting: it costs him four fingers and turns him. Nothing on the page explains why a woman who has the phone number, and who had just been given the family's minute at the wall, chooses the one delivery that walks her seventeen-year-old into a Marlow's reach. Maggie's later "He'd have finished that drop with his arm off. He's Ahern." (5163) is pride, not a reason. *Why it matters:* the grab is the season's most physical cost and the cause of Danny's turning; the cause behind it should not be a question. *Fix:* the world already has the answer. Peg puts letters in a drawer unopened, and Maggie says "I know your mother's stove. It's famous on both banks." (5439). Give her the drawer too, in ep 6 sc 4 or on the ferry: "I rang that house once and got a dial tone. Post a letter there and it goes in a drawer. A thing in that safe gets read." Two sentences, and Joe's drop becomes the only way in.

### QUIBBLE

**Q1. Episodes 1 to 3: Rosie's turn may be too well planted.** By the end of ep 2 the audience has the crisp packet, the dog, the jar, the flowing mirror-script card beside the wobbly old one, Terry topping up the pot and looking "up at the ceiling" (677), seven glances at the cutting held "for exactly as long as it takes to read a line of print" (1155), the roll "joke", and the lard. In ep 3 sc 12, before the collapse, Terry tells Con "Pot's going down, Con. Every night since the boy's birthday. Two fingers. I top it up." (2160). By the meter in sc 16 most viewers will be certain, so the first reversal plays as confirmation. The re-read still works, and dramatic irony has its own pleasure; only an audience can settle this. *If you want more surprise:* cut Terry's look at the ceiling in ep 1 sc 14; show three of the seven glances, not seven; and move Terry's call to Con about the pot to after Rosie folds on the plate.

**Q2. Episode 7, scene 7: the nearest thing to a speech.** Maggie: "Nobody counted her." then "Nobody counted her, Tess. That's what I've come to say. Whatever else tonight — nobody counted my girl." (5187–5191), three times in a row; and "Nobody ever asked why" twice (Terry in the loft, 5029; Tess on the ferry, 5375). Each is in character and in plain words, but three in a row starts to sound like the show telling us its point. *Fix:* keep one "Nobody counted her" and one "Nobody ever asked why".

**Q3. Episode 7, scene 7: Joe timing Rosie from inside the black.** "Sat in the dark off your shelf with a stopwatch, timing your girl" (5151). Joe is 59 kilos, which the painted rule gives 8.2 seconds; Rosie was in for 9.4. He cannot have timed her whole crossing from inside, and Maggie, who now says nobody should cross uncounted, sent a boy in for a long stay to do it. Most viewers won't compute it; the ones who do will. *Fix:* "Two seconds, in and out, off your shelf, to feel what a Marlow's made of."

**Q4. Episode 7, scene 4: Tess closes the box marked 02 on Terry's say-so.** "You won't find anything in there, love. Your dad never turned... It's the one thing in this family everybody's sure of." and "the lid goes back on" (5041–5043), minutes after the same rolls have proved "Gary went to Spain", "Uncle Vic's heart" and "Maureen's nerves" wrong. It is defensible (it's her father, and the founding certainty is exactly the one nobody questions), and it holds the twist for ep 10. But her hand is on the lid. *If you want the beat to cost less:* let Terry put his gloved hand flat on the lid, so it is his refusal, not her lapse.

**Q5. Episode 9, scene 13: Tess rings Ray to ask what her father weighed, with the family's own rolls in the house.** "My dad. What did he weigh?" (6799). The '02 box is in the loft and she has just read Liam's ticket off a roll on the table. Asking someone else's paper is her arc, so the choice is right; only the cause is thin. *Fix:* a beat before she dials: she looks up at the loft hatch, and can't, and takes out her phone instead.

**Q6. Episode 10, scene 14: Tess does not vote.** Danny, Kieran, Rosie, Shaun, Maggie and Peg each answer "Round the table. Out loud"; Tess, who laid the case, says nothing (7305–7325). The plan wants the vote to be the family's, not Tess's, and that may be the point; on the page it reads as an omission. *Fix:* one line either way: Peg, "Not you. You've said your piece." or Tess, "Give."

**Q7. Episode 10, scene 13: a claim the world doesn't support.** "Every crosser that turned, and kept it hid, and kept on working — our rolls hold their year... The Register can put a name to every one of them." (7283). Those who "kept it hid" never claimed, so the Register has no date for them. The evidence still stands (the rolls' falling curves, the vaults' wall logs, the Aherns' seconds); only the line overreaches. *Fix:* "The Register can name the ones that went to them. The rolls name the rest."

**Q8. Episode 4, scene 5, against episode 5, scene 5: Con's pills change container.** In ep 4 Danny's hand brings out "a small white carton with a chemist's label in Dutch" and in the stove "a strip of foil blisters" (2945, 2979); in ep 5 "a small brown pill bottle with its lid off. It is empty." (3635). Props continuity. *Fix:* make the brown bottle the "few in my coat" he decanted, or make the bedside object the last empty foil strip.

**Q9. Episode 5, scene 7: "since Saturday".** "Their weigher rang me Saturday. I've known since Saturday." (3723). By the episodes' own dates the call was Saturday 11 April and this scene is Thursday 23 April; "Saturday" reads as five days, not twelve. *Fix:* "Saturday fortnight" or "twelve days".

**Q10. Episode 2, scene 10: the one line nearest The Catch.** "ROSIE: New roll's printed backwards, Nan." (1251). It is a slip she laughs off, the camera shows the ticket the right way round, and the plan approved it; but it is a turned character saying print is backwards, which is The Catch's image and the Fourth Direction seed The Catch used. *If you want no brush at all:* let the slip be about something the family can't check on the spot ("This roll's a different purple") and keep Tess's alarm about the certified roll.

**Q11. Episode 8, scenes 8 and 9: an event that rhymes with The Catch's question.** Tess reports Rosie to the night line without asking her ("I want to report a turned woman. Unclaimed. She's starving.", 6027), and Rosie's reproach is "Nine point four, Tess." (6051). The dialogue stays on the count, as the plan wants, and nothing needs changing. I flag it only so that no later line drifts into "you decided for me", which is The Catch's moral question and not this show's.

**Q12. Episode 7, scene 9: an action line that isn't true.** "this is the first thing a Marlow has ever written on purpose" (5225). Kieran has written his log every night since ep 1, Rosie practised her name down the back of a leaflet (ep 3), and Peg pencilled "7.1" on Frank's ticket in 2002. A camera can't film the line anyway. *Fix:* "the first number Tess has ever written down."

**Q13. Episode 6, cold open: the plate's exact reading.** The ticket reads "−59.0" again with Danny "just off the plate's end, an arm's length from where a man would come" (4281), and in ep 5 Joe printed the same "−59.0" standing "at the safe", which is also the plate's end. The plan's rule (a body in the black over the plate prints its true mass as a minus) is granted; the convenience is that Danny, equally close, prints nothing. Few will notice. *If wanted:* put Danny behind the dresser, not beside the safe.

**Q14. Episode 9, scene 5: Shaun's second Purfleet leg is through a shed the first leg has just flooded, and it happens off screen.** "PEG: Once more, son. Then we're away. DANNY: Never go back twice. PEG: It's a pantry, not a bank." (6389–6393), then "Shaun lands with the last case". Liam died in exactly those conditions. The irony (Peg breaking her own rule for a pantry, and that being the leg that may have turned him) is good; the second leg deserves one beat of beads. *Fix:* two lines in the black: beads to the plate above him, the case against his chest, the tingle.

**Q15. Episode 5, scenes 8 and 9: nobody meters Shaun after an eight-second job.** "LIAM: (from habit) Straight? / SHAUN: Straight." (3817) and the meter stays in the drawer, though the family used it on Rosie in ep 3. In ep 7 the refusal is made explicit ("DANNY: Meter him, when we're home. / PEG: He said straight.", 5357), and in ep 9 Peg meters everyone; so the silence in ep 5 is in keeping with the doom, but it reads as an oversight rather than a choice. *Fix:* let the meter be in someone's hand on the river stairs and go back into a pocket unused.

**Q16. Pace, judged from the page.** Episode 2's seven hops are the slowest stretch (the false alarm at hop four is the right kind of variation; one more would help). Episode 10 carries Frank, the investigators, the night of writing, the steelyard, the door on the baking tray, two crossings, Terry's going, Ray's cure, Rosie's choice, the case for the rolls, the vote, the police station and a four-scene epilogue: fourteen beats, which reads longer than 55 minutes. *If it must be cut to length:* end ep 9 on the loft (Frank's tickets, "That's not a man going careful. That's a man that can't eat.") and open ep 10 on Ray at the kitchen door with "Starving men are slow."

**Q17. Episode 6, scene 8: a phrase that contradicts the protocol just read.** Osei: "one of their clinicians took me ten seconds deep" (4699); the sheet says "Clinician in transit: less than two seconds. No contact." (4645). "Took me" sounds like company. *Fix:* "walked me in for ten seconds, from the doorway, a second at a time."

**Q18. The working notes quote two lines that are not on screen.** Ep 5's notes pay off "Danny's near miss, 'Nearly's nothing' (4)" (4228); the ep 4 text has only "DANNY: Nearly —" (2941). Ep 8's notes say the spinner is "cut here, as agreed — 'There's no such person. There never was.'" (6181); no such line is in ep 8. Not a viewer's problem, but a reviser trusting the notes could build on a line that isn't there. *Fix:* correct the notes.

## 5. What I am unsure of

- Whether Q1 is a fault or a design. The plan accepts that sharp viewers get ahead; my count of tells suggests most viewers will. Only a screening settles it.
- Running time. Ep 10 reads over-full and ep 2 reads slow, but I have read a script, not watched a cut.
- Osei's claim that Restoration's price "pays for the Register's kitchens" is never tested, and the epilogue has her repeat it while the Register still feeds Danny after the shares halve. I read that as a working open question (her belief against the world's evidence), not a hole; nothing needs adding.
- The tray scene (ep 10, sc 9) only works if Rosie's eight metres were measured near the neighbours' high water; Maggie's tipping implies she knows the tide state, and nobody says it. I would leave it.
