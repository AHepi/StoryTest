# Critique of plan v1, round 1: law audit and physics logic

Lens: every checklist item in 05-owner-feedback-2.md and every ledger row in 30-plan-v1.md. The questions: is each law true, does it do work, do the laws contradict each other or the plot now that they are all true together, are there secret rules, and are the obvious questions answered.

Owner's rules: I found no echo of The Catch or Caraway, no religion, no trace of the theory of explanation, no banned words on screen and no dystopia. The breaches of rule 4 (every law true and working) are findings 1-5, 8, 13 and 14.

Findings, most serious first.

---

**1. Two turned people can always cure one of themselves, for free. This breaks the world and episodes 6 to 10.**
- *Where:* guess 3 (section 5); ledger 4.5, last row; episode 6, act one ("A grip in the black turns one of the two, and Danny is the one"); the Obverse sign (episode 2).
- *Problem:* guess 3 says that when two crossers hold each other, "one of the two always comes back turned relative to the other." Put two turned people together and the grip must bring exactly one of them back straight. The cost is two seconds, a rig, and a 1-in-200 risk. Keep pairing whoever is still turned and the whole Register empties in a few rounds.
- *Why it matters:* it knocks down theorem 3, where the drift is the most valuable secret on Earth. It also knocks down Obverse's £180,000 cure and the 40,000 fed turned people. Inside the story it is worse. From episode 6 the family says the grip rule aloud, and Terry, Rosie and Danny are all turned under one roof. Later Shaun is too, and Ray is at the door. Tess, who counts everything, never says "hold hands." Dropping the word "always" does not fix it: any chance of a flip at all gives turned pairs a free cure they can repeat without risk.
- *Fix:* the grip needs a price, declared once as a sixth guess. The source points to one: "the handhold is what did it." When one field turns against another, whatever of you sits inside the other person's bubble is cut off at a clean plane, like a partial return. Whoever turns loses the gripping hand. The swap then exists, but it maims. In the world it is a back-street "hand swap," and that is why Obverse's sign exists. In the story it becomes a real choice the family can see and weigh. Terry could offer his hand for Rosie's cure in episode 10, a stronger scene than the vote. Danny's grab costs him the hand he used to send through walls, which is revenge paid in kind and Horror as a guest genre. If the author won't pay that price, section 5 has to say plainly why no turned pair ever uses the grip, and I can't find a reason that holds.

**2. Once Tess has the table in episode 6, the cure is at home, and nobody takes it or says why not.**
- *Where:* episodes 7-10; knowledge map (Rosie, Terry and Danny learn the drift in episode 7); the list of costs in section 12 ("Shaun turned").
- *Problem:* the protocol shows that time is the cure, and the Marlows own rigs. Rosie's budget is over ten seconds at 41.8 or 46.2 kg, so one ten-second hop in the office is a 70% cure and two hops are 91%. In episode 8 she knows this, yet she sits through Peg's fourteen two-second hops. Shaun turns on a nine-second leg in episode 9. At 52.5 kg he has more than nine seconds, so he could go again at dawn beside Ray at 3 in 5. The plan leaves him turned. Terry, at about 5.6 seconds, could take five-second hops at home at 1 in 6 each, about 72% after seven hops. He chooses exile, and nobody sets that option beside it. Section 10 also says Danny "could go too." At 88 kg his top budget is 2.4 seconds, less than either Rosie's three-second scouting step or Terry's five-second one.
- *Why it matters:* these are the most obvious questions in the back half. An audience that has just been taught "time is the cure" will ask them out loud.
- *Fix:* give each person a reason on screen. **Episode 8:** after the ferry Peg takes every rig into her safe ("No Marlow crosses till I say"), and Rosie, out of pride, won't believe a count that says Tess sent her in to turn. **Episode 10:** Tess lays out Terry's numbers. Staying means seven or more hops, each with half a second of margin, "Liam, seven times." Going means one step at 5 in 6. He chooses with the numbers in front of him. Shaun takes the dice at dawn with Ray, or refuses on screen and gives a reason. Danny's line becomes true only if he makes weight, and that is why "I'm done making weight" lands.

**3. The drift table doesn't fit the world or the family's own history.**
- *Where:* guess 1; section 3.2 ("licensed crossers... held to three seconds for safety"); the ferry count; Rosie's record before Mayfair.
- *Problem, short end:* the source's theorem 3 says the cure is repeated tries, "expected attempts one over p." At 1 in 50 for three seconds, sixty three-second hops give a 70% cure, as good as Restoration, for the price of an afternoon. A surgeon who crosses every working day would turn about five times a year and turn back just as often. The medical world would know the three-second odds to the decimal, and that repeating hops cures. The plan says "the short hops never cured anyone" and "only a drift that grows with time explains why short hops cure nothing." At its own odds, short hops cure slowly. They don't fail.
- *Problem, long end:* at 1 in 2 to 7 in 10 per long crossing, "eleven Marlows turned, most over eight seconds" means the family made only about a dozen long crossings in its history. Yet "jobs now take eight or nine," Rosie volunteers for everything, and Shaun is the long-leg man. If Rosie did four eight-second jobs before Mayfair, her odds of never turning were 1 in 16. Turned crossers who kept working would also have been cured without knowing it.
- *Fix:* push the short end down so the regulator's limit makes sense: two seconds, none ever recorded; three seconds, about 1 in 5,000. Leave four seconds and up unchanged, so every later scene still works. Say how often the Marlows dip (a few big dips a year; concrete pays the bills). Make Mayfair Rosie's first job over eight seconds. Let the loft rolls show "came back from it" cases, such as Gary's bad winter that mended after the next long job. That is a fair plant for episode 6.

**4. Episode 9 breaks rule 4, and the lore of the Deep contradicts the spare.**
- *Where:* episode 9, act four; episode 8, act one (hops "in the flooded pit"); guess 5; section 3.2 ("Nobody who followed the Deep has come back"); the reading of the second message in section 12.
- *Problem:* the bubble is arm's length, since that is how far a crosser sees, and everything inside it counts. Fire a field eight metres under the sea and the bubble holds about 1.4 tonnes of water. No budget survives that, yet Rosie fires her spare and lands "in a gush of sea." The same goes for Peg's fourteen hops in the flooded pit. The lore has the opposite problem. The spare exists "so a crosser who steps out in the wrong place can get back," and eight metres is an easy swim up. So why has nobody come back from the Deep in thirty-one years?
- *Why it matters:* the discovery that drives the finale sits on a broken rule, and the rule that could make it thrilling goes unused.
- *Fix:* declare, with no new guess, that a field fired in water fills with water and cannot lift you. That makes "the Deep keeps you" literally true: the lost step out eight metres down and their spare can't lift them. Rosie survives because the pit taught her to find up in black water, so "weightlessness is the crossers' school" finally does work. She swims up, takes one breath of the neighbours' air at the surface, and must get clear of the water to fire. Or revise guess 5 openly: the spare is a skin-tight field, which is why it lasts half as long and is blind. Either way she brings back only what clings to her: a crab in her hood, weed in her hair, a jumper Kieran wrings into a jar. Move Peg's hops onto the pit's pontoon.

**5. Mirror life is used as law, but what it does is never fixed, and the plot contradicts itself across the mirror.**
- *Where:* ledger 4.6, row 2; rules 10, 15 and 16; Terry "cooks the family's food"; the epilogue's fever ward; episodes 9 and 10.
- *Problem:* the Register bans the turned from food work because of their germs. Yet Terry, unregistered, has cooked for the whole family for five years, and nobody got ill. If his germs are harmless to straight Marlows, then by symmetry a straight Terry is no "danger to them" next door. The epilogue sends Rosie "unmasked between fever beds" and into a feverish child's body, which is the worst place for a carrier of germs nothing can fight. The plan also says a turned person is safe next door, but Rosie and Terry step onto their shore in our clothes with our air in their lungs, and that air carries our life. Last, the drain is presented as a new danger, but thirty years of unregistered turned Marlows and Aherns have used Grays drains.
- *Fix:* add one derived line. Mirror germs cannot be fought, but they can barely feed here either, only on fat, alcohol and a few foods with no hand. One person's germs are a slow, rare risk to wounds, newborns and the very ill, not a plague in a kitchen. A whole mirror food web is a different order of risk: sea water, weed that lives on light, a crab. That makes the drain a real residue. Then apply it. Terry has always cooked in gloves, a family habit nobody explains. Shaun's open stump, nursed at home among three turned people in episodes 5-9, is a risk nobody asks about. Rosie works gowned and gloved on the ward, for the patients' sake, not hers. Terry and Rosie step next door stripped, washed and on empty lungs, which puts "a crosser carries their own air" to work. Avoid breathing gear, which was The Catch's device.

**6. Liam's sum leaves out his cargo, so the season's cleanest reveal doesn't add up.**
- *Where:* episode 5, act four; episode 9, act five; canonical numbers.
- *Problem:* Marrable Street's sum counts the 2-kilo tray. Liam's leaves out the cash he is carrying out "for Rosie's cure," and £50 notes weigh about a kilo per £50,000. Even half a kilo of notes eats the 0.1-second margin on its own, so the lying scale is not what kills him. Two more obvious questions go unanswered. When the tingle came early, why didn't he drop back onto the world? And why didn't the spare that Tess checks on every rig save him?
- *Fix:* Tess's sum is 62.4 kg plus 1.5 kg of notes, 63.9 kg, which gives 7.2 seconds for a 7.1-second job. The real figure is 63.1 (the bridge reads 1.1% light) plus 0.5 kg of fry-up plus 1.5 kg of notes, 65.1 kg, which gives 6.98 seconds. That is a tenth short. The weigh-wall reads 65.1, and "why's it a kilo out?" still works (1.2 kg). Add two lines. The tingle came while he was inside the lock-up's bead floor, where stepping out means death. And the spare can't be fired from the black. It needs ground under you, as guess 5 should say.

**7. The lying-scale arithmetic breaks in places a viewer can check.**
- *Where:* episode 5 hook; episode 6 cold open; episode 4, act five and episode 7 (the 02:04 ticket); rule 12; the family weigh-ins.
- *Problems:*
  - Danny weighs 88 at home after April. By rule 12 that scale reads light, so he is really about 89 kg, with a 2.2-second budget. In episode 6 he crosses for 2.4 seconds and should run out.
  - The 02:04 ticket reads Joe's exact 59 kg on a bridge that reads 1.1% light.
  - It is never declared how a scale weighs a body that is neither on it nor on the leaf. That is a secret rule.
  - A lorry weighbridge weighs in steps of ten or twenty kilos. It can't give 62.4, or show Kieran's test weight to ten grams.
  - Rule 12's "Gravesend barely" is wrong. Gravesend is about as far from Mucking as Grays is, roughly eight kilometres each.
- *Fix:*
  - Weigh people and Kieran's test weight on the certified office platform scale that episode 9 already uses. The big bridge is for Mick.
  - Declare, from the Law 7 dial, that a load-cell platform feels a body hovering in the black over it the way a weigh-wall does.
  - Make the 02:04 ticket read 58, with Maggie saying "He's fifty-nine, my Joe." Then give Danny a planned 2.0-second hop with a tingle that comes early, which he blames on the grab. Those are two more fair plants for twist 11.
  - Change the Gravesend line to "about the same, but the Aherns weigh on a steelyard."

**8. The carrier rule has consequences the plan misses. One of them wrecks the depot raid.**
- *Where:* guess 3, second half; episode 9, act two ("three weeks of food"); the derived claim that "food can't be turned cheaply"; theorem 4's "sealed jars can be lifted" (ledger 4.9).
- *Problem:* Shaun turns on his nine-second leg while carrying the Register's mirror rations. By the plan's own rule, the rations turned with him. They are now ordinary food with the Register's seals still unbroken: useless to Terry and Danny, and useless to Shaun himself. Beyond that, anyone who knows the drift can make mirror food by carrying ordinary food on long hops, so "mirror food must be built by chemistry" is false once the table leaks. Meanwhile theorem 4's "sealed jars can be lifted" is spent on plain theft.
- *Fix:* use it. Peg counts three weeks of food. Sixpence growls at Terry's cases but not at Shaun's. That is a sealed jar that lies, which is theorem 4 working as the source meant, and the dog knowing before the lab. Terry eating from Shaun's cases and getting nothing is a quiet echo of the murder that looks like a diet. Add one line on mirror food: licensed rigs are capped at three seconds, so nobody lawful can turn food by crossing. What Obverse does is left open.

**9. Con dies of hunger in days, against the law that the turned live on water, spirits and fat for about six months.**
- *Where:* episode 4 hook ("hasn't eaten in four days"); episode 5, act two; ledger ("Con on lard in his last days").
- *Problem:* lard and gin are on every shelf. Losing a store of rations can't starve a man inside a fortnight.
- *Fix:* the stolen tubs held Con's mirror-made heart pills. Ordinary drugs don't fit him, and that half of the checklist item otherwise appears only as the glucose gel. Danny's crew killed him with a pharmacy, not a pantry, and the law does real work.

**10. The rolls aren't needed to make the drift public, because Tess already holds Obverse's table.**
- *Where:* episode 6, acts two and three; episode 10, act five; the headline in section 12.
- *Problem:* the stolen binder has the whole table, second by second. Eighteen remembered turnings can confirm a cluster but can't build a table. So why must the family hand over twenty-five years of rolls that incriminate them?
- *Fix:* in episode 7 Peg feeds the binder to the office stove along with the ferry timetable. Then the rolls are the only proof left, and giving them away costs everything. The epilogue headline stays true.

**11. The door at the field is worked out from 1995 evidence, but the Hill moved in April.**
- *Where:* guess 4; twist 13; episode 10, act two.
- *Problem:* if a mountain moved far enough to change Grays scales by 1.1%, its shoreline moved too. There is a second gap. Kieran's deduction needs to know how high and low the neighbours' tide runs at the field, and Maggie's tables give only its timing.
- *Fix:* make the move shift when the door opens, not where. Maggie already says it: "Your Hill moved our tide. Forty minutes, since April." The shore still crosses the old field, but at Nobody low water forty minutes later than the 1995 tables say. Kieran works out the time from her new tables, and Rosie's scouting step is the test.

**12. "Send a hand" relies on an undeclared rule. Does a sent hand turn?**
- *Where:* ledger 4.2 (claims episodes 4 and 6, but episode 6 is a whole-body crossing); episode 4, act two; the surgeons' three-second limit.
- *Problem:* Danny's hand stays in the black for a long time, lifting tub after tub. If time turns people, his hand could come back mirrored, and the tubs would turn with it. If an anchored hand can't turn, why do surgeons cross whole and accept a limit?
- *Fix:* declare, derived from Law 5, that a hand fixed to a body still on the leaf has an "up" and never turns. Add the reason surgeons cross whole: a sent hand is blind, because the eye has to be inside the bubble to see. So thieves send hands to lift heavy loads by feel, and surgeons must go in bodily. In episode 4 the camera sees the tubs; Danny works by touch.

**13. Custody by pantry isn't true in the ending as written.**
- *Where:* theorem 5; the epilogue (Danny in prison); episode 10, act five ("Rosie could lift her out of them").
- *Problem:* a turned Danny held behind walls shows the state holding a turned person, which contradicts the theorem. The cuffs beat needs Rosie to walk into a police station wearing a rig, when police take rigs as a rule.
- *Fix:* Danny serves his sentence on a Register secure ward whose only lock is the pantry door. That is the source's own image, "the quarantine is a kitchen." Rosie arrives without her rig, taken at the door. Tess's choice is shown by walking in, not by refusing a rescue.

**14. "Every knot comes undone" is never shown to be true.**
- *Where:* ledger 4.3, row 2.
- *Problem:* the Mayfair crate stays knotted. That shows walls and seals failing (theorem 1), not knots untying, and handcuffs aren't knots. The law is claimed but never seen working.
- *Fix:* add surplus detail. Crossers wear slip-ons and buckles, never laces ("knots don't hold in the black"). Then a plot beat: Shaun's stump is stitched, and on his nine-second leg in episode 9 the stitches come undone in the black. It is a horror beat, and it punishes "you're worth more without it." Terry holds his bundle in episode 10 rather than tying it.

**15. The plan says a turned person can't see their own turn. The source and the plan's own cold open say otherwise.**
- *Where:* rule 6; ledger 4.5, row 2 ("The family asks the one person who cannot know"); episode 1 cold open (the crisp packet).
- *Problem:* eyes and memory turn together, so every printed word looks reversed to a turned person at once. They can't prove who flipped, but they know something did, as Rosie does with the crisp packet. So "Straight?" is not put to the one person who can't know.
- *Fix:* reword rule 6: "A turn can't be felt. Afterwards the turned person sees the world reversed but can't prove which side flipped. Only chemistry settles it for anyone else." Then every "Straight." from a turned Marlow (Frank, Terry in 2021, Rosie) is a lie told on purpose. That makes the code sharper.

**16. Ray's "caseload" of Register claimants contradicts the 2019 Privacy Order.**
- *Where:* section 3.2; episode 8 cold open and act two.
- *Problem:* the police can't read the list, yet Ray draws the rations of seven dead claimants "on his caseload."
- *Fix:* one line. Since 2019 the Handedness Unit's welfare officers visit claimants who consent, and Ray is one of them. Or the dead claimants are pre-2019 files he never handed back.
