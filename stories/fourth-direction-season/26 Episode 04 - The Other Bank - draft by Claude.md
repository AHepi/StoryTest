# Episode 4: The Other Bank

*The war begins, and across the river the enemy is living the same nightmare.*

---

## Cold open

### Scene 1. The weighbridge office, Grays, Saturday 4 April, 05:40.

Ten minutes after "Blood pays blood". Grey light at the blinds. Nobody has been to bed.

The family is still round the long table where we left them. ROSIE (22) sits wrapped in a blanket with Terry's blue enamel pot in front of her, eating from it slowly, a spoonful at a time. The tray of stones from Marrable Street lies beside the pot, glittering on its velvet, and still nobody looks at it. TESS (38) holds a mug of tea gone cold. PEG (72), in her dressing gown, sits at the head under the chalk board, where Rosie's column ends in 41.8, the biggest star the board has ever had, and a 10.0 with its last nought half drawn. SHAUN (26) and LIAM (19) slump at the far end. KIERAN (12) is asleep sideways in the armchair under his school coat. TERRY (66), in his yellow gloves, pours the kettle into the teapot. SIXPENCE, the old lurcher, sleeps in his basket by the stove.

The back door opens. DANNY (41) comes in from the yard in his shirtsleeves with his coat over his arm and his rig still buckled round his waist, rubbing the cold out of his arms. Everything counts, so he hopped without the coat.

PEG: Where've you been?

DANNY: Pit.

PEG: At this hour.

DANNY: Seeing if I've still got it.

TERRY: And have you?

DANNY: Two seconds' worth.

He drops into his chair. Terry puts a mug in front of him.

The floor starts to hum.

It sounds like a loaded lorry rolling onto the big bridge outside, but there's no lorry. The hum turns into a grinding that comes up through the lino, through the steel plate of the little bridge, through the legs of the table. The cups on the dresser hooks begin to swing, and then to walk, one after another, off their hooks and onto the floor. Peg's brass weights chatter in their wooden box. The stones shiver on the velvet. Frank's photo swings on its nail. Rosie puts both hands round Terry's pot and holds it down. Sixpence is up, barking. Kieran jerks awake.

It stops.

A car alarm goes off somewhere on the estate. Outside, every gull on the gravel heaps goes up at once, screaming.

Terry turns up the radio on the counter. It's the same BBC voice that reported the silent merger in March.

RADIO: ...a tremor felt across Thurrock and Gravesham at twenty to six this morning. The British Geological Survey says there is no fault beneath the estuary that could account for it, and instruments in London recorded nothing at all. Scientists are calling it a next-door event.

Danny looks down at the floor, then out through the blinds, towards the marsh.

DANNY: The Hill's moved.

Peg gets up and straightens Frank's photo on its nail.

PEG: Hills don't move.

Kieran is already off the armchair. He hauls the cast-iron test weight out from under the counter with both hands and sets it down in the middle of the little bridge. The display is dark; the printer chatters. He tears off the ticket, reads it and frowns. He takes the weight off, lets the plate settle, and sets it down again. The printer chatters. The same.

He opens his exercise book on the counter. Under a long column of 20.00s, he writes: 19.78.

Nobody asks him what it says. Peg bends and picks a cup up off the lino. Its handle stays on the floor.

## Act one: Blood pays blood

### Scene 2. The weighbridge office, Saturday, 06:30.

The first lorries are queuing at the gate with their engines running. The hatch stays down and the door stays bolted. Terry passes bacon rolls out of the side window to the cabs, wrapped in foil, and comes back to the stove.

PEG: *(to Rosie)* Bed.

ROSIE: Nan —

PEG: Bed.

Rosie takes her pot and goes up the stairs. Halfway up, where the stairs turn and you can see the room through the banisters, she sits down.

Peg wets a dishcloth under the tap and goes to the chalk board. She wipes Rosie's column: 44.0, 43.6, 43.1, 41.8, the star, the unfinished 10.0. Three strokes, and it's a grey smear. Nobody at the table says anything. Terry has never had a column. Through the banisters, Rosie watches it go.

Peg rinses the cloth, sits down and pours the tea.

PEG: Go on, then.

Danny puts both fists on the table.

DANNY: Something took hold of my girl in the black. Two bodies in the wall's log, and the Aherns have worked Mayfair all winter.

On the stairs, Rosie's spoon stops.

TESS: *(quiet)* The copper said there's a way to kill a crosser without leaving a mark. Turn her round in the black and send her home to her tea. Six months, and it looks like a diet.

It's the first time she's told them. Danny stares at her.

DANNY: Then they meant her dead.

SHAUN: Blood pays blood.

DANNY: They've got turned over there. Everyone on the river knows. They buy their tubs off the Rotterdam boat and keep them in a reefer on their wharf. *(He taps the table: one, two.)* We take their tubs through the black and bring them back the wrong way round. Like the picture. Then their turned can starve at their table, like mine did at this one.

Tess doesn't answer. She nods at the wall by the stove, where the catalogue cutting is still pinned up: the woman, the balance, the window on the left.

TESS: Then you'd be eating it.

DANNY: Eh?

Tess keeps her eyes on the cutting. Danny follows them: the window on the left. In the safe at the end of the plate lies a painting with its window on the right. Then his eyes go to the stove end, where Terry has taken a white tub out of his fridge and is eating from it with a spoon, standing up, in his gloves.

Danny sits back.

DANNY: Then I'll turn one of them. Get hold of one in the black and wrench him round. I've got two seconds.

TESS: Grab someone in there and one of you turns. Coin says which.

DANNY: I'll have him by the collar.

TESS: Collar does nothing. It's skin, or it's nothing.

ROSIE *(from the stairs)*: And whoever turns leaves the hand in there.

Every head comes round.

ROSIE: Girl at the clinic's is plastic. Left one. Pours your water with the other.

KIERAN: Is that true?

SHAUN: You know the fella off the Tilbury ferry? Sleeve pinned up?

KIERAN: He said a dog had it.

SHAUN: Shook on it.

Kieran looks down at his own hand, opens it, and shuts it.

Peg puts the teapot down.

PEG: Nobody's wrenching anybody. *(to Danny)* You want to hurt them? Have the lot. Every tub. Let them find out what hungry is.

DANNY: Take it?

PEG: It's two mouths here now. *(She doesn't look at the stairs.)* Stones go to the fence Friday. A third, if he's kind.

TESS: That's Terry's year. Not two.

PEG: Then theirs'll do for this month.

TESS: A reefer's a tin box with a thin skin. You don't go in. *(to Danny)* You put your hand in. Same as the safe.

DANNY: Blind.

TESS: You've done the safe blind for years.

Danny flexes his right hand, and for the first time since the van came home, he nearly smiles.

TESS: Their tow goes out at half one, comes past ours just after two, and it's back for four. Declan takes her up. That leaves the women, the kids and an old fella.

SHAUN: Boat's Tuesdays.

PEG: Wednesday night, then. When it's full.

LIAM: I'll carry.

DANNY: You'll carry.

Liam goes pink to the ears.

KIERAN: I could carry.

PEG: You could eat your breakfast.

ROSIE: I'll go.

PEG: You'll stop in.

ROSIE: I'm lightest.

PEG: *(sliding an egg onto Shaun's plate)* You're poorly.

ROSIE: I'm turned, Nan.

The fish slice stops over Shaun's plate. Then it goes on. Peg doesn't turn round.

PEG: Shaun drives.

Rosie looks at the grey smear on the board where her name was. She takes her pot, and goes on up the stairs.

### Scene 3. Grays Post Office, Tuesday, 10 a.m.

A queue of pensioners and parcels. On the glass, a laminated sign: PASSPORTS. CHECK AND SEND.

Rosie is at the counter with a passport form. In her other hand, folded small, is a printout: a white clinic on a hill above a wide river, and one line in English under the Portuguese. RESTORATION. The price is half of Harley Street's.

The CLERK (60s, glasses on a chain) runs down the form with the end of her pen. Name. Date of birth. Place of birth. She stops at a box: HANDEDNESS: C (COMMON) / T (TURNED). Rosie has ticked C.

CLERK: Going anywhere nice?

ROSIE: Lisbon.

CLERK: Lovely. *(Her eyes go to the printout in Rosie's hand, then to Rosie's wrists.)* Only you'll want to be sure of this box, love. They strip-test you at the gate now.

ROSIE: Strip?

CLERK: Sugar strip on your finger, like my Alan does for his diabetes. Your photo can't tell them which hand you are. The strip can.

ROSIE: And if it comes up T?

CLERK: Then you don't fly. Portugal won't have a T. Most places won't. *(lower)* They're frightened what you'd bring in with you. Your germs are the wrong way round and all, aren't they? Nothing over there would know what to do with them.

ROSIE: And if I'd ticked C?

CLERK: Then you've told the Passport Office a lie, and you still don't fly. *(She turns the printout over with her pen, and sees the clinic.)* Had a lad in with that same bit of paper before Christmas. They turned him round at Stansted.

Rosie folds the form in half.

ROSIE: Southend, then.

The clerk laughs. Rosie laughs louder: the laugh the family knows, the one she does when she's lying.

CLERK: Next!

Outside, in the Post Office window, the poster from the bus shelter: IMMUNE. FED. NEEDED. Join the Fever Company. Rosie looks at it the way you look at a bus you're not getting on. She drops the printout in the bin by the door.

### Scene 4. The flooded gravel pit, Tuesday, dusk.

Liam stands on the pontoon in a wetsuit that won't zip the last hand's width up his back, with the black-painted goggles pushed up on his forehead. Rosie sits on the upturned drum at the end, in Danny's old jumper under her coat, with the stopwatch open on her phone.

LIAM: So?

ROSIE: T doesn't fly. *(beat)* So it's a hundred and eighty thousand. And it's here.

LIAM: Then I'll get it you.

ROSIE: Carrying tubs?

LIAM: Crossing. A proper job. *(He slaps his thigh; it wobbles.)* Not like this. I'll get under.

ROSIE: You're the big lad.

LIAM: Not for ever.

Rosie looks at him: the broad white shoulders, the wetsuit gaping at his back.

ROSIE: Goggles.

He pulls them down and drops off the pontoon. She starts the watch. Her lips move as she counts, exactly as Tess's do.

He comes up gasping and shoves the goggles back.

LIAM: How long?

ROSIE: You kicked down.

LIAM: Everyone kicks down.

ROSIE: Breathe out a bit. Follow your bubbles.

LIAM: *(grinning)* That's what Tess says to Kieran.

He hangs on the pontoon edge, dripping.

LIAM: How d'you keep it off, Rosie? Really.

The last time he asked her that, she said "willpower". She looks at the water.

ROSIE: Skip the fry-up. Nan never looks under the table, and the dog'll have it. When it grabs you, drink a pint of water, and it goes off. *(beat)* And sleep in your coat.

LIAM: What for?

ROSIE: You get cold. You're cold all the time, making weight. *(beat)* After a bit you stop noticing.

LIAM: *(delighted)* Every morning, then. I'll do it every morning.

ROSIE: They'll clap you.

LIAM: They never clap me.

ROSIE: They will.

She hears it as it comes out. Her eyes drop to her own wrists, all bone in Danny's sleeves. When she looks up he's on the edge again with his goggles down, waiting for her, happy.

ROSIE: Again.

He drops in. She starts the watch. Her lips move.

## Act two: The hand

### Scene 5. An alley behind the Ahern yard, Gravesend, Thursday, 02:10.

Over the river by the QEII bridge, lit up like a fairground, and down into Gravesend.

The alley runs along the back of a tug yard on the south bank. Wet cobbles, one broken lamp, chain-link with a strand of barbed wire along the top. Hard against the wire on the inside, filling the frame, stands a forty-foot refrigerated container, white, streaked with rust, with ROTTERDAM stencilled along its side. A fat cable runs from it to the tug shed. It hums like a fridge in a quiet kitchen.

Beyond it lie the slipway and an empty berth. The mooring lines lie looped round the bollards on the quay, waiting for the tow to come back, their big knots furred green with weed. Above the shed are the windows of the Ahern rooms, all dark.

The van waits at the end of the alley with its lights off. Shaun stands at the corner, keeping watch. His eyes keep going back to the bollards and the knots.

Liam stands by an empty crate in his coat, shivering, though the night isn't that cold.

TESS: No wall on it. Nobody weighs a pantry.

Danny takes off his jacket and hands it to Liam. He rolls his right sleeve up past the elbow, buckles his rig on over his shirt and thumbs the wrist unit. He lays his palm flat on the chain-link, where the container's steel side presses against it, and pushes.

His arm goes into the wire and the steel together, like a hand going into water, up to the elbow, and stops there in a clean edge. Liam has watched that hand go into the safe a hundred times. He has never seen it go into the dark in somebody else's yard. He swallows.

TESS: Arm weighs next to nothing. It'll last longer than your nerve.

The camera goes to Danny's face, and stays there. His eyes are shut, his cheek against the cold wire. The container hums.

DANNY: Shelf. *(beat)* Something. *(His breath hisses through his teeth as his fingers close.)* Tub. Frozen solid.

He draws his arm back out of the steel. In his fingers is a blue plastic tub, white with frost, with a label in Dutch. There's frost on his fingertips and across his palm. Liam takes the tub in both hands as if it were hot, and lays it in the crate.

DANNY: One.

He goes in again. Tess counts each reach under her breath. Danny says what his fingers find: tub; two stacked; a loose lid; tub. The crate fills.

LIAM: *(a whisper)* Nan says never go back twice.

DANNY: *(eyes shut)* It's only my hand.

At the corner, Shaun snorts.

By the ninth tub Danny's fingers come out white and stiff, and he fumbles the handover. Liam catches the tub an inch off the cobbles.

TESS: Can you feel your tingle?

DANNY: Can't feel owt. It's like grabbing ice.

TESS: Then I'll count.

From then on she counts aloud, low and even, and when she says "Out" he comes out, whether he's found anything or not.

Above the shed, a window lights up.

Everyone stops. Danny's arm is inside the container up to the elbow.

A WOMAN comes to the window with a baby on her shoulder, walking it: up to the glass, away, up to the glass. She stops and looks out over the yard, at the river and the empty berth, the way you look for a boat that isn't due yet.

TESS: *(not raising her eyes)* ...seven... eight...

DANNY: *(barely a breath)* Tess.

TESS: Nobody moves. ...nine... ten...

The woman turns away from the glass, still walking the baby. The window stays lit.

TESS: Out.

He comes out with a tub.

In again. Danny's face changes.

DANNY: Something little.

TESS: ...four... five...

DANNY: Box. Card. It's light. *(beat)* It rattles.

TESS: Leave it.

DANNY: Mum said the lot.

He leans his cheek harder into the wire, reaching.

TESS: ...eleven... twelve... thirteen...

DANNY: It's gone down the back. I've got a corner —

TESS: Fourteen. Danny. Fifteen. Out.

He pulls. His hand comes out of the steel, and pinched between two numb fingers is a small white carton with a chemist's label in Dutch. He drops it in the crate among the tubs, shakes out his hand and blows on it.

DANNY: Never felt it come.

TESS: It came.

Two more reaches. Bare shelf. Bare shelf. His hand sweeps the container's floor and finds nothing.

DANNY: That's it.

SHAUN *(from the corner)*: That's all? For a whole family?

Danny opens his eyes and draws his arm out, empty. Two crates, and the second isn't full.

DANNY: They're shorter than us.

Tess looks up at the lit window.

TESS: Then that's all of it.

They carry the crates to the van. Behind them the window stays lit.

### Scene 6. The weighbridge office, Thursday, 04:30.

The van rolls onto the big bridge and the printer chatters. Peg has waited up with the kettle on. Terry sits at the stove end in his dressing gown. Rosie sits on the stairs.

Liam carries the crates in. Sixpence gets up in his basket and growls at them, low and steady: the growl he keeps for Terry.

PEG: Hark at him. Smells the Laundry on them.

She takes a table knife and starts on the first tub.

PEG: Labels. Paper talks.

Tess takes the knife from her, and Peg lets her, because Tess is quicker. Tess scrapes, Terry stacks, and Liam flattens the cardboard sleeves the tubs were packed in. Tess feeds labels and cardboard into the stove a handful at a time. The little white carton goes in folded inside a sleeve. For a second, in the stove's mouth, a strip of foil blisters and a line of Dutch print curls up in the flame. Then Tess shuts the door. Nobody saw it.

Terry stacks the blue tubs on the bottom shelf of his fridge, under the white ones from the Laundry. Rosie comes down the stairs and counts them without meaning to.

PEG: Two mouths, the best part of a fortnight, and it never cost us a penny.

TERRY: Cost someone.

PEG: Them.

Terry shuts the fridge.

At the sink, Danny runs the hot tap over his right hand. The fingers are red and white and he can't close them. Rosie comes and takes the hand in both of hers and rubs it between her palms, the way he held her feet on the settle on Saturday. He lets her. Neither of them says anything.

Across the room, Tess wipes the soot off her hands onto her jeans.

## Act three: Across the river

### Scene 7. The Ahern yard, Gravesend, Thursday, 7 a.m.

Daylight on the south bank. The tow is back at its berth. JOE AHERN (17), the skinny lad from the steelyard, in the hoodie we last saw on a bike at the mouth of the mews behind Marrable Street, snips the numbered seal on the container's doors and hauls them open. Cold smoke rolls out over his boots.

Every shelf inside is furred with frost, and in the frost are clean round rings where the tubs stood. The frost on the floor is smooth as a pond. There isn't a bootprint anywhere.

DECLAN AHERN (70, cap), the old man who called "Fifty-nine!", stands at the doors. Behind him is MAGGIE AHERN (44), the woman who didn't cheer, with her tide book under her arm.

DECLAN: Seal was on.

JOE: Padlock and all.

MAGGIE: *(her lips moving over the rings)* Twenty-odd tubs, one at a time. Nobody hops twenty times. *(beat)* That's a hand.

DECLAN: One man on this river sends a hand like that. Frank Marlow's boy.

MAGGIE: All gone on the ebb.

She looks up. At a window above the shed an old man in a cardigan, thin as a coat hanger, is looking down at the open doors: CON.

### Scene 8. The Ahern rooms over the shed, Thursday, 6 p.m.

Up a wooden stair from the shed. Through the floorboards you can hear the tide working at the slip. A long kitchen with one window over the yard, a stove, and waterman's tide books in a row along the dresser. Through the door at the head of the stair you can see down into the shed, where the old salvage steelyard hangs from its beam.

By the door hangs a chalk board: JOE 59, with a star; a few other names; and one column headed AOIFE, with nothing under it.

On the fridge is a photo of a young woman laughing at the wheel of a tug with a baby in a sling on her chest. Beside it, taped at the top and curling at the bottom, hangs a yard of printer paper from a weigh-wall: the same line printed again and again down the strip, a date, a time and a weight, and the weight a little smaller every line. Written across the top in biro: *Aoife.*

SIOBHAN AHERN (13), the girl with the saucepan, is laying the table. CIAN, nine months old, bangs a spoon on the tray of his high chair. Declan sits at the head. At the foot sits CON AHERN (70s) in the cardigan, with a jar of dripping, a bottle of gin and a spoon, and nothing else in front of him. Joe sits by his grandad.

Maggie has the day's post and a butter knife, and she opens every envelope and reads every one: harbour dues; the Port's new tide tables; a catalogue. Then she takes a form off the dresser, filled in in her square capitals, and lays it beside Con's jar with a pen on top.

MAGGIE: Register. They'll feed you from Monday. They don't ask how you turned, or who you work for. I've done it all. You sign.

Con looks up the table at his brother.

Declan reaches down the table, picks up the form, opens the stove door and posts it in.

DECLAN: We feed our own.

MAGGIE: With what, Dad? Boat was short Tuesday. Boat's been short since Aoife. And now there's none.

DECLAN: Then we'll have it back off them.

SIOBHAN: Why'd they take it?

DECLAN: 'Cause they're Marlows. First they spin our Aoife. Now they starve our Con.

SIOBHAN: Did they, though? Spin her?

DECLAN: Aherns face home. Nine seconds she was in that bank, and she'd done nine once before and come back right. Their vans were outside it all week, MARLOW on the side. And she comes home to us the wrong way round. *(He looks at the fridge.)* Somebody had hold of her in there.

Nobody answers. Cian bangs his spoon. Maggie looks at the photo.

SIOBHAN: Grandad. Do me.

She fetches a bathroom scale from the landing, sets it on the lino and steps on in her socks. Declan leans over it, squints, and goes to the board with the chalk. He writes her number under Joe's and draws a star beside it.

DECLAN: Light as Aoife was, at your age.

Siobhan glows. Joe claps. Con raps his spoon on the dripping jar.

Maggie gets up, cuts two more slices off the loaf, butters them thick, and puts them on Siobhan's plate. Declan doesn't notice.

### Scene 9. The Ahern kitchen, Friday, 3 a.m.

One lamp. Maggie is at the stove making up Cian's bottle: water just off the boil, a scoop of powder levelled with a knife, another, another. Her tide book lies open on the table at the Nobody columns, a pencil in the spine. Overhead, the baby grizzles.

Con sits at the table in his cardigan, wide awake, with his gin, his dripping and his spoon.

CON: You spoil him.

MAGGIE: Her milk was wrong. He screamed three days before we understood. *(She screws the teat on.)* He can have it warm.

CON: She went again for him.

MAGGIE: So she could feed him. *(She shakes the bottle.)* The wall read her for a month. Less every day. We stood our minute on the pavement outside that bank, with the buses going by.

Con rubs his chest under the cardigan, slow circles with the flat of his hand.

MAGGIE: Where's your tablets?

CON: Few in my coat.

MAGGIE: And the rest?

CON: In the cold. With the rest.

Maggie stops shaking the bottle.

MAGGIE: How many's a few?

CON: Enough till the boat.

MAGGIE: Boat's Tuesday.

CON: Then Tuesday. *(He pushes himself up and takes the cordless phone off its hook on the wall.)* Can't sleep. I'll ring my pal.

He goes down the stair to the yard with it.

A creak on the other stair. Joe, in the hoodie, woken by the baby. He runs the tap and drinks from his cupped hands. He looks at the fridge, at the curl of paper.

JOE: *(low)* All winter you've had me sat outside their jobs. *(beat)* Was it worth it, Mum? Your phone call. They still came out with the stones.

MAGGIE: Did the police sit on their basement?

JOE: All week. They went in the long way.

MAGGIE: Good.

She tests the bottle on the inside of her wrist.

MAGGIE: Ten seconds under a road, Joe. Let them count that. *(beat)* One of these nights it'll be more than they've got.

Joe looks at the paper on the fridge: the weights, smaller every line.

MAGGIE: Not a word to your grandad.

JOE: He'd kill you.

MAGGIE: He'd put the phone in the stove.

She goes up with the bottle. Overhead, the grizzling stops.

### Scene 10. Two yards, either side of the river, Friday, 3:10 a.m.

**Gravesend.** Con sits on a bollard at the edge of the empty berth, beside the humming container, with the phone to his ear. Across the black water are the lights of Tilbury and, further up, the orange glow over Grays.

**Grays.** Terry sits on his upturned crate by the bins with one glove off and the phone to his ear. Beyond the fence the gravel heaps stand pale in the dark.

TERRY: Con. You're late tonight.

CON: Your lot took my dinner, Tel.

Silence. A lorry goes by on the A13.

TERRY: My lot.

CON: Every tub, and never broke the seal. Dec says there's only one arm on this river goes through a tin like that.

Terry turns his head and looks through the lit office window, at his fridge.

TERRY: Where are you, Con?

CON: Other bank. Look across.

Terry looks out past the gravel heaps, at the river, and at the lights on the far side.

CON: Con Ahern. Declan's my brother.

TERRY: You knew who I was.

CON: Everyone knows a Marlow. *(beat)* Gravy was nice, though.

TERRY: I'll bring it back. Every tub.

CON: You won't. They'd not let you. Mine wouldn't. *(beat)* I'd not have, neither.

Neither of them rings off. Two old men on two banks, with the tide running out between them, each holding a phone to his ear and saying nothing.

CON: Can't sleep, Tel.

TERRY: No. Me neither.

**Later.** The office, dark but for the printer's small green light. Terry opens his fridge. White tubs on the top shelf. Blue on the bottom, with their labels scraped off. He looks at the blue ones for a long time. Then he shuts the door.

## Act four: Light

### Scene 11. The weighbridge office, Friday, 06:30.

The hatch is up and the day has begun. Lorries roll onto the big bridge and the printer chatters. Terry, who hasn't slept, turns bacon in his gloves and says nothing. Peg works the hatch.

MICK (50s, hi-vis) leans in for his ticket and a bacon roll. He turns the ticket over and frowns at it.

MICK: Your bridge has had me two hundred light, Peg.

PEG: Then you've had a good day.

Mick shrugs, takes his roll, and goes. The printer chatters behind him.

At the end of the counter Kieran has his exercise book and the night's tickets. His graph is taped to the wall above him.

KIERAN: My weight's gone light too, Nan.

PEG: You dropped that on the step at Christmas. It's chipped. Stop wasting roll.

Kieran gets down on his knees and tips the test weight over on its side under the counter. There's a chip on the rim of its base: grey paint gone, bright iron under it. He runs his thumb over it. He nods, satisfied, and sets it back.

The post comes through the door. Peg sorts it on the counter: a seed catalogue, a feed bill, and a brown window envelope, THURROCK COUNCIL · TRADING STANDARDS · IMPORTANT. She opens the bill. She drops the brown envelope into the dresser drawer, on top of years of others, unopened. The drawer won't shut. She shuts it with her hip.

### Scene 12. The weighbridge office, Friday, 07:30.

Breakfast, with the hatch down for twenty minutes. The family at the long table: Peg, Danny (his right hand wrapped round his mug, still stiff), Tess, Shaun, Liam, Kieran, and Rosie, down for breakfast for the first time since Saturday. Terry at the stove. On the board, the grey smear where Rosie's column was.

Peg puts a full fry-up in front of Liam: two eggs, bacon, sausage, beans and fried bread.

PEG: Get that down you. You're carrying for your dad now.

LIAM: Ta, Nan.

Rosie gets up, goes to Terry's fridge and takes a blue tub off the bottom shelf without looking. At the stove, Terry watches her peel the lid off. He says nothing.

Tess watches Rosie eat, every spoonful, as she has since Saturday.

Under the table, Liam's hand comes down with a rasher. Sixpence's nose finds it. The rasher's gone. Then a sausage. Across the table, Rosie sees the dog's tail thump against the table leg. She looks at Liam. He looks back and waits for her to say something.

She doesn't. Peg turns from the stove with the teapot, heading for Liam's end of the table.

ROSIE: *(loud)* Nan, who's had the brown sauce?

Peg turns to look for it. Under the table, the second rasher goes.

At the end of the table, Kieran has seen the whole thing. He looks at Liam's plate, and at the dog, and at his nan and his mum, who saw none of it.

Peg comes back and pours.

PEG: Clean plate, Li. That's a Marlow.

Liam glows. Nobody has ever said it to him before.

Rosie looks down into her tub. Tess is still watching Rosie.

## Act five: 02:04

### Scene 13. The weighbridge office, Saturday, 08:00.

Kieran is at the counter sorting the night's tickets for his log. There are the lorries that weighed after hours on the big bridge, and his own, 21:00, the little bridge, 19.78. He copies it in under the others.

And there's one more. 02:04. LITTLE BRIDGE. −59.0 KG.

He reads it twice. He looks at the plate: a square of steel set in the lino, empty, as it was all night. He looks at the green safe at its end, then at the bolted door, the stove, the settle. At four minutes past two, everyone in this house was in bed.

He turns to call his nan. She's at the hatch with a driver. He remembers "Stop wasting roll", and shuts his mouth.

A scale can't weigh less than nothing. He tapes the ticket into his book under the Twitch ticket from March, and writes beside it in pencil: GLITCH. He looks at it. Then he adds a question mark.

The camera stays on the safe.

### Scene 14. The weighbridge office, continuous.

The phone rings on the wall by the dresser. Peg is at the hatch. Terry is at the stove. Tess, coming down the stairs, picks it up.

TESS: Marlow Aggregates.

MAGGIE *(on the phone)*: Is that the weigher?

TESS: Who wants her?

MAGGIE: The other one.

Tess goes still.

MAGGIE: Maggie Ahern.

At the stove, Terry's spoon stops. He doesn't turn round.

Tess turns her back to the room and drops her voice.

TESS: What do you want?

MAGGIE: Little white box. Chemist's label, in Dutch. It was in with the tubs.

Tess's eyes go to Terry's fridge. Then, slowly, to the stove. Behind its little window the fire is going, the same fire she fed on Thursday, a handful at a time.

MAGGIE: Con's heart pills were in them tubs. Made to his hand. There's no more.

The line clicks and goes to the dial tone. Tess stands with the phone against her chest.

The stove ticks.

**Cut to black.**

**END OF EPISODE 4**

## Plants and payoffs

**Paid off here, from episodes 1 to 3**
- **"Blood pays blood" (3).** The cold open is ten minutes later; Danny has been to the pit to see if he's "still got it". The war council follows at 06:30.
- **"Something took hold of me" and the two bodies at 3:12 (3).** Danny makes them a war, and Tess at last passes on Ray's "six months, and it looks like a diet", so the family reads it as murder. Rosie's spoon stops on the stairs. Across the river Declan says the same of Aoife: "Somebody had hold of her in there."
- **The cargo rule (Ray's line at the car in 3).** Tess has learned it: a nod at the catalogue cutting, "Then you'd be eating it", and Terry eating his tub finishes the thought.
- **The clinic sign and the receptionist's hand (2); the one-handed man on the ferry (1).** "Girl at the clinic's is plastic." Kieran learns the grip rule, and that the ferry man told him a dog had his hand.
- **The leaflet (3).** The Post Office closes Lisbon (the strip at the gate; HAND: T doesn't fly), so it has to be £180,000, here. That is why Liam wants to cross.
- **The shed nobody could place (2)** is named: Declan, Joe, Siobhan, Maggie, and Cian crying overhead. **The thin lad at the bus stop and on the bike (3)** is Joe, in the same hoodie. **"Had a call" (3)** was Maggie, and Declan doesn't know. Closed.
- **Con (2, 3).** "Ours do that" becomes Siobhan chalked with a star; "'We feed our own,' my lot say" becomes the form in the stove; "Boat's late again" becomes "Boat's been short since Aoife"; "You don't know where here is, Tel" becomes "Other bank. Look across." Closed for the audience and for Terry.
- **Danny's vault in '24 and "the floor underneath" last autumn (2).** Declan: "Their vans were outside it all week, MARLOW on the side." Planted, not pressed; Maggie names the bank in 7.
- **Liam's "How d'you keep it off?" (3)** gets the true answer, and Peg's "That's a Marlow" (3) goes to Liam, for a plate he fed to the dog. The chalked 10.0 (3) is wiped with Rosie's column.
- **Kieran's nightly test weight and his sorting of the tickets (1, 2)** catch the 4 April step and the 02:04 ticket, taped under the Twitch ticket. **The drawer (1)** takes the April envelope.

**Planted here, for later**
- **The quake, "The Hill's moved", "Hills don't move" (8, 9).** The first stroke of the message; Danny's guess is wrong and Peg, for once, is right. "Instruments in London recorded nothing" is the one hint that the pull comes from under the estuary.

- **02:04, −59.0, "GLITCH?" (5, 7, 9).** Sharp viewers can match 59 with "Fifty-nine!" (2) and JOE 59 on the Ahern board. Nobody is shown standing by the safe; the bullet (5) was delivered that night, off screen. The camera rests on the safe.
