# Episode 3, "Making Weight": critique of the draft

Checked against `70-plan-v3.md` (all of it), `00-brief-2.md`, `05-owner-feedback-2.md` and `../the-catch-notes.md` ("The short answer", faults 1-4). No final episodes exist yet in `work/new`, so continuity is checked against the plan and against the drafts of episodes 1 and 2 (`80-episode-01-draft.md`, `80-episode-02-draft.md`), with their critiques in mind.

## Verdict

This is a strong, well-built draft. Every beat the plan gives episode 3 is here, in order, and the sums are right: 41.8 plus the 2-kilo tray gives 11.2 seconds for a 10.0-second job. The crime is a pleasure to watch:
- Liam's memory of the floor decides the route ("thick on the mews side"; "one skin of Mix" on the party wall).
- Ray's car, parked to be seen, adds the seconds the logline promises.
- Tess paces the mews and it all comes down to one word: "Ten."

No banned word or named idea reaches the screen. The family's code does visible harm without anyone saying so: Peg won't call an ambulance because of what's lying on the floor, and orders tea with four sugars instead.

The main problems:
- **The draft gives Rosie away** before Ray has said a word, on top of episode 1 and 2 drafts that already do the same. By the meter scene the audience has nothing left to learn.
- **One image in the set piece echoes The Catch's last image.**
- **Rosie's lie describes a grip**, and the family's own rule about grips disproves it.
- **Three things are said aloud that the plan keeps for events:** Ray states the reversal, Kieran asks the adults' question, and Terry's look is written to settle a twist that belongs to episode 7.

The episode does end higher than it began. It opens on a cheer and closes on a girl found starving, a detective's log turned into a war, and "Blood pays blood."

**Protect these:**
- The float tin, and "Concrete money goes in the bank, where the taxman can see it... Concrete doesn't pay the Laundry." It answers why a family with a tripled order book is short of cash.
- Peg's Old Street story. It gives "never go back twice" the reason it was once right.
- The pit scene: Ray breaks Tess's count, and then "How long was I?" / "Long. Good."
- Tess opening her mouth to ask Ray the next thing and not asking it. It plants episode 9's "What did my dad weigh?" without a word.
- Ray's "A wall gives you a weight, not a name." / "What weight?" / "I'll keep that."
- Liam's "Can she hear you?" / "No." / "Then why d'you do it?" / "So someone's counting."
- The lad on the bike under the lamp, and Tess's "Nobody moves."
- The handprint and "LM" inside the wall. Keep them, but see finding 2.
- The ticket the printer makes as Rosie folds, which nobody tears off.
- "Shift, Tess." Also Terry's "No it wasn't, love," and Rosie's "She did. I passed."
- Con's "Ours do that." and "You don't know where here is, Tel."
- Peg's "Second and a bit to spare. Your dad never had a second to spare in his life."
- The idea that Tess's watching is what stopped Rosie feeding before the job. It is the best new cause in the draft. Finding 1 moves it to where it lands hardest.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The audience learns Rosie is turned before Ray says a word, so the first major reversal plays as the family catching up
**Scenes:** 2, 8, 9, 12 and 13.

**Problem:** The plan wants the audience "level with Tess from Ray's line in act two, suspecting as she does", with only the dodge letting sharp viewers edge ahead, and then level at the meter (section 10). Version 3 moved tells out of the early episodes because "an alert viewer had the answer before episode 2 ended" (section 15, change 12). On top of the plan's tells, the draft adds five beats that come close to confessions:
- **Scene 2, act one, before anyone suspects:** Rosie, alone, practises writing her own name "the right way round", slowly, then faster "until it ... looks careless." Anyone who saw the flowing mirror-script card in episode 1 can think of only one reason a 22-year-old would drill her own name.
- **Scene 8:** she looks at the fridge, where the pot lives at night, and drinks water instead, while Terry's face watches from his door.
- **Scene 9:** in the replay, Terry checks that the pot's level hasn't moved.
- **Scene 12:** Terry tells Con the pot went down every night, then stopped.
- **Scene 13:** Rosie stops at the pot, "her hand moves", Tess walks in, and she takes a sandwich instead.

The episode 1 and 2 critiques found the same fault in those drafts. With all three together, the meter has nothing left to reveal.

**Why it matters:** This is the season's first major reversal and its first big re-read. If the audience is sure by scene 2, and watches Rosie reach for the turned man's food in act three, then the meter confirms what they know instead of turning it over. The "tests that pass" act also loses its question, because we already know why she passes the writing test.

**Fix:**
- **Scene 2.** Cut the windscreen practice. Give the stakeout another job: Rosie times the guards with her lips moving, the way Tess counts. It shows her want to be the best, and what she has learned from Tess. If the practice is worth keeping, show it after the reveal as a trace. In episode 4 the van's windscreen fogs, and ROSIE ROSIE ROSIE comes up on the inside, each one faster than the last.
- **Scene 8.** Cut the look at the fridge and Terry's face in the door. She just drinks water, which reads as making weight.
- **Scene 9.** Cut Terry checking the pot. Keep Tess's eyes on Rosie's wrists, and the growl nobody hears.
- **Scene 12.** Keep the plan's beat, "Pot's going down". Cut "Last night it never moved. Nor today." and "Is it?"
- **Scene 13.** Keep Terry's clean spoon standing in the pot. Let Rosie walk past it with the sandwich without stopping.
- **Scene 16.** Pay the chain off after the meter, where it hurts Tess most. Terry: "She's been at my pot since the party. Every night. Till Wednesday." Wednesday is when Tess started watching. In one go the audience re-reads the liver and onions, the glass of water and the sandwich "for Liam". Tess learns that her own watching starved Rosie before a ten-second job.

### 2. Rosie puts her turned hand inside her brother's handprint, which is The Catch's last image set in concrete
**Scene:** 14.

**Problem:** Inside the wall Rosie finds Liam's handprint, and "puts her hand flat inside her brother's, through it". The Catch ends with a turned woman who can no longer share food with her husband and brother: hands meet on glass as mirror images. Here a turned woman's hand, now a mirror image of what it was, meets her brother's across a barrier. The plan bans hands meeting on glass (section 14). This is the same picture on a different surface.

**Why it matters:** The owner's first and loudest objection to the rejected attempt was that it ripped off The Catch. An image this close, in the episode's main set piece, is the one the owner will spot first.

**Fix:**
- Keep the handprint and the "LM" beside it. They plant Liam running out inside a bead floor in episode 5, and the image is strong.
- Let the glow slide over them while the count runs, and let the print slide past again on the way home, as the draft already has it. Rosie doesn't touch it.
- A season rule for later writers: no turned character's hand meets or matches anyone else's hand, print or reflection across a surface.
- Scene 17 opens on Rosie eating food the rest of the family can't share. The scene ends on "Blood pays blood", which is safe. Keep it that way in later drafts: section 14 says no episode ends on a meal that can't be shared.

### 3. Rosie's lie describes a grip, and the family's own rule says a grip would have taken part of her
**Scene:** 17.

**Problem:** Rosie says, "It had hold of me, Dad. It pulled me round." Under the show's sixth guess, a grip in the black turns one of the two crossers, and the one who changes leaves the held part behind, cut clean. Rosie changed, so she would have lost whatever was held. She is whole.

The family has always known this. The knowledge map marks the grip as known "always", everyone in Grays knows a one-handed man who "shook on it", and episode 1 put one on the ferry. In the very next episode Rosie herself says, "whoever turns leaves the hand in there." And the episode 1 cold open showed the audience what she actually felt: a pull in her gut, with nothing touching her.

**Why it matters:** Danny goes to war on a story his own rule disproves, and nobody at a table of experts notices. That makes the family look stupid rather than blinded by their beliefs. It also gives sharp viewers a reason to throw out the spinner theory at once, and the plan needs it alive until episode 6 (twist 8).

**Fix:** Build the lie on the family's own wrong belief, that facing home keeps you straight.
- ROSIE: "Something heavy came right up by me. Pulled me off home. I lost it, Dad. When I found it again..." DANNY: "Pulled you off home."
- Now Peg's "You faced home" sets the lie up, the two bodies in the log back it, and Ray's spinner fits it. Episode 7's confession, "I thought I'd faced wrong, and I was ashamed", becomes its exact truth.
- Keep the plan's line, "Something took hold of me in the black," as the opener. It now means a pull, not a hand.

### 4. Ray says the reversal out loud in act two
**Scene:** 5.

**Problem:** The plan gives Ray the spinner theory: "a murder that takes six months and looks like a diet." The draft adds two more things:
- "And her family cheered every kilo." This is the twist's own re-read, "the thinness they cheered was starvation" (section 8), handed over two acts early.
- "I've watched your family's weight since 2002, Miss Marlow. Your niece gets lighter every time I see her." This gives away the folder that episode 8 turns on ("Twenty-four years of our weights in your hand, and you never showed a soul"). It also has a man whose flaw is hoarding what he knows hand it over for nothing.

**Why it matters:** The owner's rule is that the point is earned by the plot, never said. Said here, it robs the meter of its re-read. And Peg's "And we clapped" in scene 17 then says the same thing a second time.

**Fix:**
- Stop Ray at "Six months later she's dead, and it looked like a diet."
- Replace the 2002 lines with the plan's leaner one: "When a crosser comes back turned, and there was somebody else in the black beside her, I stop calling it luck."
- Keep "I'll keep that", which shows his hoarding.
- Peg's "And we clapped" is then the only time the point is spoken: after the meter, by the person who led the cheering. It can stay.

### 5. Kieran asks the question the adults need
**Scene:** 16.

**Problem:** Kieran asks, "in a small clear voice", "Is Rosie turned?" The child says what the adults won't. Section 14, under "Never echo Caraway", says: "Kieran never asks the question the adults need; he writes down what they remember."

**Why it matters:** It repeats a device from the rejected attempt, and a stock one: truth from the mouths of babes. It also takes the naming of the truth away from Tess, who has been the detective all episode, at the very moment her count fails her.

**Fix:** Give the word to Tess, at the end of her own reasoning: "It's not low. It can't read her." A beat. "She's turned." Peg flinches at the word, as written. Kieran stays on the armchair cushion, watching, and says nothing.

### 6. Terry's look is meant to tell the audience that "took hold" is a lie, four episodes early
**Scene:** 17.

**Problem:** The action lines say Terry "knows exactly what she has just done, and why". The draft's notes say "Terry's silent look shows the audience that it's a lie." But the twist schedule keeps Rosie's story alive for the audience. Twist 8's "what the audience knew before" lists "Rosie's 'something took hold of me'", and says the audience has guessed otherwise only "since 6". The war in episodes 4 to 6 needs viewers who half-believe in a spinner. The line also can't be filmed as written: a camera can show a look, not what a man knows.

**Why it matters:** If the audience is sure in episode 3 that nobody spun Rosie, episodes 4 to 6 play as a war they know is pointless. Episode 7's "Nobody spun anybody" then loses its turn for the audience.

**Fix:** Play Terry's look as grief at the family's silence, not as knowledge of the lie. He looks at Peg, who has never said the word about him, and puts the lid back on his pot. Cut "Five years ago he came back ... He knows exactly what she has just done, and why." Cut the matching plant note too.

### 7. Danny's charge makes Tess's fault a secret kept for Rosie's sake, which is The Catch's question, instead of her number
**Scene:** 17, with 8.

**Problem:** Tess keeps Ray's visit to herself (scene 8). At the end Danny says, "Wednesday. And you sent her tonight." That frames Tess's fault as deciding Rosie's fate and hiding it from the family. Section 14: "Nobody's moral question is whether you may decide for someone else; the season's question is whether you wrote it down and checked it against someone else's." Change 41 cut "You decided. Again." for exactly this reason, and Danny's line is the same kind of line.

**Why it matters:** It drifts into The Catch's moral core, and it misplaces Tess's flaw. Her fault is trusting the number in her head over the evidence in front of her: the lard, the dodge, the dog, Ray's line. The draft shows that flaw well in scene 11: "The number hasn't changed. Eleven two."

**Fix:** Aim Danny at the number.
- DANNY: "Wednesday. And tonight you still said eleven two."
- TESS: "It was eleven two."
- Keep "I tested her." / ROSIE: "She did. I passed."

The exchange is now about a sum that was right and a test that was beaten, which is the season's question. Scene 8 can stand.

### 8. Tess's pencil comes down four episodes early, and Rosie spells out what it means
**Scene:** 10.

**Problem:** The draft says of the pencil, "It's the first time we've ever seen it come down." Then Rosie: "Don't lose that. You've never used it." Tess's arc turns on the pencil she never uses. She first writes on the back of a ferry timetable in episode 7, and hands the pencil to Kieran in episode 10 (section 7). The draft spends the pencil's first use in a scene about Rosie's writing, and has a character say what it stands for.

**Why it matters:** Tess's first written number, in episode 7, is her turning point, and it needs the pencil untouched until then. A line that points at an object's meaning turns the object into a symbol.

**Fix:** Tess grabs the stub of chalk from the board's ledge, or the biro on its string by the hatch, and holds it out with the blank ticket. The pencil stays behind her ear. Cut "Don't lose that. You've never used it." and the tuck behind the ear. Rosie can still post the ball of paper into the stove and say "Paper talks", which is good.

### 9. The draft does the audience's re-reading for it, and the chalk board it shows is wrong
**Scene:** 16.

**Problem:** "Tess's eyes go round the room, and the room is full of it": the old and new cards, the catalogue cutting, the board, the lard, the dog. Then the leaflet slides out of Rosie's coat. The camera lists the clues for the viewer. The board also shows "four stars". The drafts of episodes 1 and 2 give Rosie one small star, at 43.6, and none at 44.0 or 43.1. The big star is tonight's.

**Why it matters:** The pleasure of a reversal is the re-read the audience does for itself (section 9). Walking viewers through it turns it into an explanation. The star count is a slip viewers will notice, because the board is on screen in every episode.

**Fix:**
- Keep one image: the new card on the fridge, its backwards letters running "as easily as breathing", held long enough to register. Let the leaflet do the rest.
- Change "four stars" to "a little star at 43.6 and tonight's big one", or have the drafts of episodes 1 and 2 give Rosie stars.

### 10. A few lines state the point or wink at the title
**Scenes:** 3, 12 and 17.

**Problem:**
- **Con:** "'We feed our own.' Fed on what, I say." A character names the flaw in the code.
- **Peg:** "Your dad's mate went back for seconds on the Old Street job." The plan says the title's meanings, second helpings among them, are never spoken. This line packs two of them into one pun.
- **Peg:** "And we clapped." This is fine on its own. Alongside Ray's line (finding 4), it says the reversal twice.

**Why it matters:** Each is small. But the owner's rule is that the theme lives in events, and every episode has to keep that discipline.

**Fix:**
- Con: "'We feed our own.' Boat's late again." His hunger says the rest.
- Peg: "went back a second time".
- Keep "And we clapped" once Ray's line is cut.

### 11. Several action lines tell the reader what a camera can't show
**Scenes:** 3, 5, 6, 11 and 17.

**Problem:** These lines tell the reader what a character knows or remembers, and nothing on screen can carry it. Line by line, with a fix for each:
- **Scene 3:** "She was fourteen that week. It was the week her own name went at the top of the board." Show Tess looking at the top of the board, or cut the line: episode 2 already established it.
- **Scene 5:** "For the first time in her life, she doesn't know." Show it instead: Tess opens her mouth, and no number comes.
- **Scene 5:** "Tess has heard Terry say those exact words at the stove every morning for five years." Cut it. The audience heard Terry say them in episode 1, and Tess's pause is enough.
- **Scene 6:** "Something Ray put in Tess's head goes quiet." Cut the clause. She picks up her fork, as written.
- **Scene 11:** "She saw a knife spread that same white fat on a cracker two days ago." Cut it. The audience saw it, and Tess's stare carries it.
- **Scene 17:** "Five years ago he came back from the black turned, and told this table so, and nobody has said the word since." Cut it. It is also untrue on screen: Terry said "Since I turned" at the birthday, and the word has just been spoken at the settle.

**Why it matters:** Nothing on screen carries these lines, so the audience never gets what they say. A few, like Tess's count failing at the pit, are real beats and need a picture. The rest repeat what the audience has already seen.

### 12. "Eats like a horse" contradicts the Rosie of episodes 1 and 2
**Scene:** 6, and a line in 16.

**Problem:** Peg says, "Look at her. Eats like a horse and never puts a gram on." In the drafts of episodes 1 and 2, Rosie gives her fry-up to Liam ("Big lad needs it"), turns down Kieran's soup and Ray's cracker ("Making weight, me"), and is cheered for not eating. Peg's line makes clearing her plate sound like her habit. Danny's "She had a fry-up yesterday. I watched her." in scene 16 has the same problem, more mildly.

**Why it matters:** The scene is a good idea. Rosie eats two plates to quiet Tess, and a sharp viewer remembers from Terry that food goes through the turned like water. It works best as a change in her behaviour that Tess notices, not as her normal.

**Fix:** Peg: "Look at her. First time she's cleared a plate since Christmas." Danny: "Burns it off in the black." Then the second plate. Tess sees a change, and the viewer sees a performance. In scene 16, Danny: "She had two plates Wednesday. I watched her."

### 13. Small fixes and continuity with the drafts of episodes 1 and 2
No final episodes exist yet, so these are checked against the drafts.
- **Timeline.** On Wednesday at 6 p.m. Rosie says, "I've had it three nights running." The stakeout only shows Monday and Tuesday, and the shop shuts at six on Wednesday. Make it "two nights running", or add a Wednesday-morning beat.
- **Sight radius.** In the diamond room Rosie sees the guard's heart "through a steel door". Her glow reaches only an arm's length. Put the guard's chair hard against the far side of the door she passes, and have her pass close to it.
- **Staging.** At the end of the pot beat in scene 13, Terry is "at the stove" without having come in. Put him there from the start of the beat.
- **A beat used twice.** The episode 1 draft has Danny: "She can't hear you, Tess." / "I know." Scene 14's "Can she hear you?" / "No." / "Then why d'you do it?" / "So someone's counting." is the better version. Ask the episode 1 writer to cut or change theirs, so this one lands fresh.
- **Fingertips.** Section 14's crossing grammar keeps the close-up on fingertips for the tingle. In the cold open and scene 9, have Rosie steady herself on the dresser with the flat of her hand, so the weakness can't be read as a tingle.
