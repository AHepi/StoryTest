# Episode 6: Ten Seconds

*Danny gambles his hand to turn an enemy, and Tess breaks into a clinic for a cure and finds a clock.*

---

## Cold open

### Scene 1. The weighbridge office, Grays, Wednesday, ten past one in the morning.

A week since the river wall. The office is dark except for the small green light of the ticket printer and the grey square of the blind where the yard lights lie. The house above is asleep. On the camp bed at the end of the little bridge, a foot from the green safe, KIERAN (12) sleeps the sleep of a boy who has been allowed to stay up too many nights, his face turned to the safe, the way he has slept since Peg moved him down here.

At the table, in the dark, sits DANNY MARLOW (41), and he is not asleep. His coat is folded over the chair back, his boots under it, because everything counts. He is in his vest and trousers and socks, and buckled over all of it is his rig: the padded belt, the collar with its brass disc at the throat, the wrist unit reading zero. His right sleeve is rolled to the elbow.

He has done the sum, and the sum is all in his head, because that is how this family keeps things. The Ahern tow goes out at half one. It passes the Marlow wharf just gone two. Ten days ago a man came off that tow, stood in the black beside this safe, and left a bullet standing on a painted balance pan, a foot from where a boy sleeps. If the tow runs tonight, the man comes again.

The scale's display is dark. There is nothing in this room to watch but the clock.

At half one, far off on the river, a tow's horn, twice. Danny's eyes stay on the clock. The fridge hums. The clock ticks.

02:03. He stands up without a sound. He flexes the fingers of his right hand once, the way every Marlow does.

02:04. He steps off the world.

**The black.** Sound drops to his heartbeat and his breath. His glow reaches an arm's length round him, grey-gold, and past it there is nothing at all. His ears scream that he is falling. He knows that voice. There is no down to fall to.

The plate lies under him seen from inside, steel and rust. The safe's wall goes up past him like a cliff seen through water. And then, into the glow, out of the black — a body.

Slight. Young ribs, narrow as staves. A heart going fast, a bird's heart, close enough to close his hand on. And inside a jacket, white in the glow, a letter.

Danny's hand goes up. Hovers. The heart is two fingers from his fingertips, beating like a sparrow's, and for one beat, two beats, his hand hangs there.

Then he closes it on the bare wrist above the sleeve, and wrenches.

Close on his fingertips: the fizz starts under the nails — before the turn is half done, while he has still got the boy swinging.

Home. The one pull in the black. He faces it, and steps.

**The office.** A gasp. The smell of the room: bacon fat gone cold, printer ink, dog. Under the counter, the printer chatters once, and stops. On the camp bed the boy sleeps on, face to the safe.

DANNY: Got him.

He raises his right hand into the green light.

The four fingers are gone. Off clean across the knuckles: a flat, dry plane, white bone and dark meat and yellow fat, all perfectly smooth, bloodless.

For a second.

Then not.

He closes his left hand round his own wrist above the plane and holds it hard, up against his chest. His face doesn't change. He looks at the wall clock to see how long he was gone, and looks at it a beat too long, and takes nothing from it.

He gets up, his hand held out before him like a candle, and takes a clean tea towel off the rail, and wraps it. He sits down at the long table under the chalk board, where LIAM 62.4 and its star are still chalked, and nobody has wiped them, and he waits for morning without waking a soul.

The clock ticks over to 02:05.

## Act one: breakfast

### Scene 2. The weighbridge office, Wednesday, half past six.

TERRY (66) comes down first, in his dressing gown and his yellow gloves, and puts the kettle on out of habit before he sees his nephew at the table with the towel gone rust-brown around the knuckles. The kettle goes on anyway.

PEG (72) follows, in her dressing gown. Then ROSIE (22) on the stairs, and TESS (38) behind her, and Kieran sitting up on the camp bed with his hair on end.

Danny crosses the room to the table, and SIXPENCE gets up out of his basket and growls at him. The low, steady note. The one he has given Terry for five years.

The kitchen goes very still, because they all know that note now.

PEG: Danny.

DANNY: Put the kettle on, Mum.

Peg goes to the dresser and takes her glucose meter out of the cutlery drawer, from among the spoons. Danny sits and holds out his hand, the good one, and she pricks his finger, and squeezes the bead of blood onto the strip, and waits for the beep the way she has waited for it every morning of her own life.

The screen reads **LO**.

Nobody speaks. Peg looks at the meter, and at her son, and her hand goes out, not to him but to the fridge — to the shelf of white tubs that came in the Laundry van, the only food in this house her son can now eat — and stops on the door handle.

PEG: *(to Terry, quietly)* How much of yours is there?

TERRY: It's ours, Peg.

She opens the fridge anyway, takes out a tub and a spoon, and puts them down in front of Danny. Then she stands there with her hand flat on the table, because there is nothing else her hands know how to do.

TESS: You went alone.

DANNY: You'd have had sums against it.

TESS: I told you. Skin, or it's nothing. I told you what it costs.

DANNY: He stood over Kieran's bed.

Tess has no number for that. Her lips move, and nothing comes off them.

TESS: How long had you?

DANNY: Two point four. *(beat)* Fizz started before I'd even got hold of him. Still reaching.

TESS: Reaching's a tenth.

DANNY: Tingle came early. That's the grab.

Her lips go on moving a moment longer, the way they do when a number won't settle, and then they stop, because there is no number here this morning.

TESS: Who was it?

DANNY: A lad. Light build. Wrist like a birch twig.

On the camp bed, Kieran has the night's tickets in his hand — he tore them off the printer the moment he saw the grown-ups' faces. He holds one out. 02:04 · LITTLE BRIDGE · −59.0 KG.

KIERAN: It printed him again.

TESS: Fifty-nine. *(beat)* Maggie's boy. The one at the lock-up door.

PEG: Seventeen years old.

DANNY: He'll be home for his breakfast.

Nobody laughs.

ROSIE: Coin says which.

TERRY: Aye.

Terry fetches the bowl, the warm water, the clean towels. He dresses the hand on the table, working in his gloves, and nobody asks why the gloves. The plane across the knuckles is smooth as a chisel cut. The thumb he keeps. Four fingers, gone, and no wound anywhere else on him, and no fingers anywhere in the room.

TERRY: Locum at nine. Cash.

Peg nods. Terry ties off the dressing, tucks the ends in neat as a butcher's parcel — and then, without a word, he reaches over and slides his blue enamel pot across the table to Danny.

Danny looks at it a long moment. Then he takes the lid off, and eats, a spoonful at a time, the way Rosie eats, the way Terry eats.

ROSIE: *(quietly, across the table)* Little and often, Dad.

DANNY: I know how to eat.

ROSIE: You know how to make weight. This is different.

He looks at her. She holds his eyes, because she is the only person at this table who knows what she is talking about. He takes another spoonful.

Sixpence growls again, low, from the basket.

DANNY: Jealous old bugger.

TERRY: *(gently)* No, love.

Danny stops chewing for one beat. Then he goes on.

Tess, at the counter, does the only thing she can do with any of it. She counts.

TESS: One mouth was three hundred a day. Then two. *(beat)* Three's nine hundred. Six thousand three hundred a week, and he'll want it front.

DANNY: How much is in the house?

TESS: Thirty-eight days.

She doesn't say the money. She has never not said a number before.

DANNY: The lock-up job was the cure. That box under the beads.

Nobody answers that either. Peg looks round the kitchen — the fridge, the pot, her son eating with one hand — and says the family's own words back to it, flat as a ticket.

PEG: We feed our own.

Out in the yard the first lorry sounds at the gate. Kieran is dressed already, school bag on, and he stands at the door a second too long, looking at his dad.

DANNY: Bus.

KIERAN: Dad —

DANNY: *Bus.*

He goes. Through the window we see him stop in the yard and look back once.

### Scene 3. The weighbridge office, Wednesday, nine.

The locum, a tired man with a bag, meets Danny at the yard door and examines the dressing under the awning.

LOCUM: Guillotine. Who did the first dressing?

TERRY: Me.

LOCUM: It's good work. *(He repacks his bag.)* You people are going to run out of parts.

Peg pays him in notes from the cash box, and he goes. Then she takes her key ring to the safe — the long brass key, and the small padlock key on its loop of orange baler twine gone pale — and kneels down on the steel plate to open it. Under the counter the printer chatters out her weight. Nobody tears it off.

The lock gives with its clack like a knuckle.

The cash box. The brown envelope. And lying flat on *The Assayer's Wife*, face up on the painted woman's balance, on the very pan where the bullet stood, a white envelope.

In a round, schoolteacher's hand: **TESS MARLOW**.

Peg lifts it off with two fingers and holds it out, unopened.

PEG: From them.

DANNY: Burn it.

PEG: It's got her name on.

Tess takes it. She turns it over once. The back is blank. She puts it in the inside pocket of her coat, which hangs on the chair, and looks at nobody while she does it.

### Scene 4. The Ahern kitchen, Gravesend, Wednesday, six in the morning.

The tide book lies open on the oilcloth at the Nobody columns. Two mugs of tea, one gone cold. CIAN grizzles overhead, thin, and stops. MAGGIE AHERN (44) sits with her coat on over her nightclothes, as she has sat since half one.

The door opens. JOE (17), white to the lips, the river cold still on him.

She is up before the door shuts. She takes his wrist — the wrist — and turns it to the lamp. Whole. Unmarked. She turns it over anyway.

JOE: He was waiting in there. In the black. At the safe. *(beat)* He had hold of me, Mum, and then he was holding nothing.

MAGGIE: And the letter?

JOE: Went in. I heard it land.

Maggie lets his wrist go. She looks down at the tide book, and her finger finds the Nobody column and runs down it, the way other people say a prayer.

MAGGIE: Thursday fortnight, then.

JOE: They know the tow now. They know it's me.

MAGGIE: I know. *(She closes the book.)* Half one. You're on her.

JOE: Grandad says —

MAGGIE: Your grandad says a lot. *(not unkindly)* Bed.

He goes up. Maggie stands at the window, across the black water, at the orange glow over Grays, and does not move for a long time.

## Act two: the records room

### Scene 5. The weighbridge office, Wednesday evening.

The lorries are parked up and the hatch is down. On the long table: the last of the money in thin piles, and the Obverse leaflet, soft at its creases, with £180,000 printed on it and LISBON. HALF in biro underneath.

DANNY: Aherns keep their money in that lock-up. What's left of it. I'll —

TESS: You're not crossing.

DANNY: I've still got a hand.

TESS: You've got two point four seconds and one hand. You're not crossing.

DANNY: Then what do we do? Starve politely?

TESS: *(tapping the leaflet)* Two of them to cure is three hundred and sixty thousand pounds. There's nothing in this country pays that but years. *(beat)* So we don't pay it.

PEG: We don't go to them.

TESS: We're not going to them for help.

Peg looks at her daughter across the kitchen.

TESS: The method's written down somewhere. Price lists, deliveries, how it's done. Whatever a hundred and eighty thousand buys, there's a paper for it under the price.

PEG: *(a long beat; then, because it is her own line, from the wall, in a frame)* Paper talks.

ROSIE: *(quietly, from the stairs)* There's a room marked records.

Every head comes round.

ROSIE: At the clinic. I asked for the toilet.

TESS: You asked for the toilet.

ROSIE: Course I did. *(counting it off on her fingers)* Black door, three steps. Reception desk. Corridor past her room — Osei's. Stairs down, lower ground. Toilet's under the stairs. And past it a steel door. RECORDS. STAFF ONLY. There's a keypad on it.

DANNY: Keypad doesn't matter.

ROSIE: I know it doesn't matter. I'm telling you where it is.

TESS: The cabinet?

ROSIE: Didn't see in. Door was shut.

DANNY: Strongroom furniture. It'll be steel, and it'll be bolted through the slab. Tall as Terry.

TERRY: Flattered.

Tess is already walking it, lips moving: pavement, down a floor, half left, first door.

TESS: Cabinet on the left going in.

ROSIE: The wall you face coming out.

TESS: Say it back.

Rosie says it back, word perfect.

Peg goes to the steel rack by the back door, where the rigs hang on hooks like coats. Rosie's is gone into a police evidence store in Chelmsford. Shaun's went under the river wall with Liam. Danny's belt is on Danny. Two left: Terry's, and a third, older, its strap gone cracked.

Tess takes down her own rig.

PEG: Not you.

TESS: Rosie's got no rig. Danny's got one hand. Shaun's got one leg. *(buckling it on)* Terry's turned, and I'm not sending anything else in the black for this family. Not this month.

TERRY: *(going over the buckles with his gloved hands, thorough)* Charge is sound. Collar's sound. You want the strap seen to after.

TESS: After.

She steps onto the little bridge in her jersey and socks. The display is dark after hours; the printer chatters. Terry tears off the ticket. 61.0.

TESS: Seven point eight. *(beat)* I'll take two and a half.

TERRY: That's five seconds of change.

TESS: First go in twelve years. I'll have the change.

PEG: Face home.

TESS: Face home.

From the camp bed, where he has been pretending to read, Kieran puts his book down.

KIERAN: I'm not forty kilos any more.

TESS: Bed.

He goes, and takes his book, and doesn't argue, which is not like him, and nobody notices.

### Scene 6. Harley Street, Thursday, ten to two in the morning.

Night. The black door with its brass plate, OBVERSE, and the fanlight dark above it. A minicab rank, one car idling. A man walks a terrier along the railings.

Round the corner, in a side street, the white van sits with its lights off. Terry at the wheel. The window down an inch.

TERRY: Ten minutes and I'm coming through the shopfront.

TESS: You'll count to a hundred and fifty. I'll be out at ninety.

She gets out, crosses, and stands on the pavement by the black door like a woman waiting to be late for an appointment. She flexes her fingers once, and steps off the world.

**The black.** It comes back to her body like an old song. Her ears scream falling; she greets the scream like a bus she's caught a thousand times. The glow opens round her, an arm's length of grey-gold, and the wrist unit counts tenths, and her lips move, and she drops by count, the way you drop a floor into a vault.

0.4. Stone steps from the inside. 0.9. A coal chute, a mouse run in old lath. The glow slides her down through the dark of the house. 1.3. A void, warm, and the shapes in it: a desk from the inside, its lock, a lamp's wiring lying in the wood like veins. On the wall a plain steel panel the size of a door, with nothing on it to read.

1.7. The cabinet. Steel, floor-bolted, tall as Terry, exactly as Danny said, and through its door the spines stand clear as through water: LEDGER A–F. LEDGER G–M. SUPPLIERS. KITCHENS. PROTOCOL.

2.1. She draws the PROTOCOL binder out through the steel, the way Rosie drew the canvas out of its crate, and holds it flat against her chest.

No fizz in her nails. She has five seconds of change, and she spends none of it. Home is one pull, the strongest thing in the world. She faces it and steps out onto the pavement.

A gasp. Exhaust, cold, the hiss of a night bus. The door of the van, and Terry's window down.

TERRY: Straight?

TESS: Straight.

She can't know that. Neither can we.

## Act three: the protocol

### Scene 7. The van, a side street off Harley Street, continuous.

A streetlamp over the bonnet. Terry sits with his hands on the wheel. Tess opens the binder on her knees. Cloth-bound, OBVERSE stamped in the corner.

The first sheet is in a plastic sleeve, and it is typed like a recipe.

TRANSIT 10.0 s.
EXPECTED RESTORATION 0.70.
CLINICIAN IN TRANSIT: ≤ 2.0 s. NO CONTACT.

Tess reads it twice. Then she turns pages, and stops.

An annex: two columns, hand-ruled, filled in ink. Seconds down one side. Odds down the other. She reads it out loud, flat, the way she reads a shopping list.

TESS: Two seconds: none ever recorded. *(beat)* Three: one in five thousand. Four: one in twelve. Five: one in six. Six: one in four. Seven: one in three. Eight: one in two. Nine: three in five. Ten: seven in ten.

Silence in the van. A taxi goes by outside, lit up yellow.

TERRY: Eleven times I went in. On two seconds.

TESS: I know, Tel.

TERRY: *(looking at the road)* You can't buy nothing with two seconds.

TESS: *(reading the sleeve again)* Her clinician comes in for a second at a time. A second or two. *(beat)* That's not bedside manner. That's the odds.

She turns the sheet over. It's blank. She turns it back.

TESS: It's a job, Terry. That's the whole of it. They put them under and walk them through, ten seconds deep. *(beat)* The cure's a crossing.

Terry looks at her then, properly, and then away, out of the window at the dark clinic down the street, and something goes out of his face that has been in it for five years.

TERRY: All them goes I made.

TESS: Aye.

He starts the engine. As they pull out he starts, very quietly, half-singing the old washing-day song, the one he sings when there is nothing to say, and Tess lets him sing it. She holds the binder flat on her knees with both hands, all the way over the river.

## Act four: the sums

### Scene 8. Dr Osei's consulting room, Harley Street, Thursday, nine in the morning.

The waiting room, the orchids, the hush. The RECEPTIONIST looks up, her right hand moving on the keyboard, her left hand very still beside it.

TESS: No appointment. Tell her it's about her records room.

The receptionist's eyes go, for half a second, to the tall window. Then she picks up a phone, and speaks into it, and puts it down.

RECEPTIONIST: Dr Osei will see you.

The consulting room, as before: the tall window, the desk with nothing on it. DR HELEN OSEI comes in and closes the door, and stands, and waits.

Tess lays the binder on the desk.

Osei looks at it. She doesn't touch it for a moment. Then she opens it and squares the pages, unhurried, a woman tidying her own kitchen.

OSEI: Two point six seconds. Sixty-one kilos, seven hundred grams.

TESS: Point nought on my bridge.

Osei's eyes come up at that, and stay a half-second longer than the words are worth. She files it. We watch her file it.

TESS: How long have you known?

OSEI: Since 2011. It cured me. *(She sits, slowly, like a woman who has stood in courts.)* I turned on a rescue, in the years before licences. Nine seconds, two men and a door frame on top of me. A man in Whitechapel walked me back through, on a borrowed rig, before any of this was a business. It cost him a favour and me a career. *(beat)* Then I made it a business, so it would cost money instead.

TESS: Seventy per cent. You could put that in plain numbers on your door.

OSEI: We do. Inside. *(beat)* Your two and a half seconds. Who taught you that?

TESS: Nobody. It's just sense.

OSEI: In 2013 a surgeon ran past three seconds with a child's tumour in her hand. The licence came in the year after, at three, as a margin against running out. *(beat)* Nobody counted what else it did.

TESS: *(flat)* I know what else it did.

The window, the street, the taxis. Neither woman speaks for a moment.

OSEI: That price pays for the Register's kitchens. The Register buys its tubs from us at cost because Restoration pays for the rest. That secret feeds forty thousand people, every day. *(beat)* You take the price out of the world, they go back to gin and dripping. Put it back.

TESS: It's feeding three at my table.

OSEI: Then bring them to the Register. Nobody asks them anything.

TESS: We feed our own.

OSEI: (not unkindly) Everybody says that. In that chair. Right up until they're hungry.

Tess picks up the binder.

OSEI: If I call someone, this becomes an exhibit, and exhibits walk. That's the only reason you're standing up. *(beat)* Put it back, or keep it dark. There's no third thing.

Tess goes. Osei sits still a while after the door has shut. Then she opens her diary and calls in the next patient, and it is nine-fifteen, and the day goes on.

### Scene 9. The weighbridge office, Thursday, nine at night into the small hours.

**Nine o'clock.** Kieran hauls the cast-iron test weight out from under the counter with both hands and a lot of breath, and stands it in the middle of the little bridge. The printer chatters. 19.78. He tears off the ticket and opens his exercise book.

Down the column, since the weekend: 19.79. 19.77. 19.78. 19.78. Tonight makes the fifth, and the flat line on his graph has grown a blur. He takes his pencil and circles the five of them, all in one ring.

KIERAN: It wobbles now.

PEG: Scales wobble, love. That's why they certify them.

KIERAN: It's meant to be a flat line.

TESS: *(at the table, her lips moving on something else, her eyes not on him)* Everything wobbles. Do your reading.

He looks at her — the one person in this family who counts — and gets no more out of her than that. He writes 19.78 in the book. On the settle, Danny eats from the pot with his left hand and watches nobody.

**Later.** The house asleep. Kieran comes down in his socks, stands on the plate in his pyjamas by the printer's green light, and tears off the ticket. 38.9. In the second column, the one headed *me*, under 39.2, he writes it. Book under the pillow. Back up to bed.

**Later still.** Tess comes down alone. She doesn't put a light on. She sits at the long table with the binder open under the green glow, and the pencil behind her ear where it has lived for twenty years, and she does not take it down.

Her lips move. The season runs through the table in her head, and we go with it.

— The office telly, the masked surgeon over the sleeping child, three seconds; and Rosie, chin on her fists: *"She's done four thousand. Never turned."*

— Terry at the stove: *"Eleven times I went again. Two seconds a go. Hungry every time after."*

— Her own voice, in the office at dawn: *"Three. Face home. Out."*

— The whole table, the old chorus, mugs raised: *"Never go back twice."*

— Liam, grinning over a tube of drawings in a flooded bank: *"Could've gone in twice."*

— The chalk board: 10.0, the second nought half drawn, and the biggest star the board ever had.

— Danny alone in the dark, a week ago, opening and closing his hand: *"Two seconds is enough to grab someone."*

Her lips go down the column and stop between two rows.

Nine point four. About three in five.

There is no number on her lips now. She closes the binder, puts her hands flat on top of it, and sits in the dark with the fridge humming and the green light blinking, for a long time.

### Scene 10. The weighbridge office, Friday, two in the morning.

Rosie comes down the stairs in the big jumper, barefoot, and goes to the fridge, and takes out the pot, and eats standing at the counter, a spoonful at a time, in the dark. She doesn't switch the light on either.

Tess is still at the table.

For a while, neither of them says anything. Then Rosie nods at the coat on the chair, where the corner of a white envelope shows above the pocket.

ROSIE: From them?

TESS: From them.

Neither of them touches it.

Tess gets up. She opens the cutlery drawer and takes out Peg's meter, and pricks her own finger, matter-of-fact, the way she does everything, and squeezes the bead onto the strip, and waits.

The beep. The screen: **5.4**.

She lets a breath go, slow, and it comes all the way out.

TESS: Straight.

Rosie watches her. She knows exactly what that waiting was, and how long it is.

Tess puts the meter back among the forks and shuts the drawer. Then she looks across the dark kitchen at her niece, and says it the way she says numbers, once, and plainly, like something she has carried all day and can put down nowhere else.

TESS: I gave you nine.

A beat. Rosie looks at the binder under Tess's flat hands — at the shape of it, the thickness of it, not inside it — and then up at her aunt. And there it is, the grin, the show-off's grin she does when she is lying.

Only this once, she isn't.

ROSIE: Give me ten.

Cut to black.

## Plants and payoffs

**Paid off here, from earlier episodes**
- **"Two seconds is enough to grab someone" (5), said alone under the clock: ** the stakeout and the grab. The grip rule (4: "It's skin, or it's nothing"; "whoever turns leaves the hand"; "Girl at the clinic's is plastic") is paid in full: the coin takes Danny, Joe comes home whole, four fingers stay in the black. Terry, who dressed Shaun's leg, dresses the hand, in the gloves.
- **The tow times and the 02:04 ticket (4, 5): ** Danny's sum is made on screen — out at half one, past the wharf just gone two, four past by the clock — and Kieran's repeat ticket (−59.0, again) corroborates it. # Episode 6 draft — findings, most serious first

**1. Scene 10 — the closing line gives Rosie a number she cannot know.**
*Problem:* Rosie's last line is "Give me ten." Only Tess and Terry have seen the binder; the stage direction itself says Rosie looks at "the shape of it, not inside it." Nothing in her experience points to ten — her own lived data (seven three-second hops that changed nothing) points the opposite way.
*Why it matters:* It breaks the knowledge map (Rosie learns the drift at 7), and the audience can only read it as unearned knowledge or coincidence. It also collides with two planted beats: episode 7's "Ten seconds. Seven in ten. We can cure them here" (the family's discovery) and episode 8's "Rosie asks Peg for ten seconds", which the plan makes her first ask.
*Fix:* Make the number Tess's — ROSIE: "How long?" / beat / TESS: "Ten." / ROSIE: "Give me ten." — and move Rosie's line on the map to late 6; or, most faithful to the plan, end on "I gave you nine" and the grin, no words.

**2. Scene 5 — Kieran announces his weight loss to the whole family.**
*Problem:* "I'm not forty kilos any more" is said aloud in a room containing Danny, Peg, Tess, Rosie and Terry. "Nobody notices" covers his not arguing, not the line.
*Why it matters:* The map has every adult learning Kieran is starving himself at 9, and episode 9's beat is Danny's realisation ("he was 40 at his birthday… You've been making weight"). If Danny has heard his son announce the loss, that discovery is dead three episodes early.
*Fix:* Cut the spoken line. He starts — "Mum—" — and gets "Bed" before anything lands; or, better, wordless: on his way past he steps on the plate, the printer chatters, nobody tears the ticket off (the roll now holds his 38-odd, which is exactly where episode 9's proof lives). Keep scene 9's private 38.9.

**3. Scene 1 — Danny crosses standing over the plate.**
*Problem:* "The plate lies under him." The plate prints the true mass of any body in the black over it (rule 14; that is how episode 4's −59.0 came).
*Why it matters:* Danny's print would read −89.0, not the 88 the family believes — episode 9's reveal (the lying scale, his true 2.2 seconds) spent three episodes early, on a roll the investigators will read in episode 10. And with Joe over the plate too, the overlap should disturb the reading; the single clean −59.0 is unexplained.
*Fix:* Stage the step-off beside the safe, just off the plate's end (the safe stands at the plate's end — say so), so only Joe, over the plate as in episode 4, prints. One clause at breakfast — Kieran: "Just the one" — closes the question. Nothing may ever print Danny.

**4. Scene 1 — the printer chatters live during the ambush.**
*Problem:* "Under the counter, the printer chatters once, and stops" plays an unseen visitor's reading in real time, inside the blind stakeout.
*Why it matters:* The plan's rule is explicit: no instrument ever shows an unseen visitor live; every such reading is found on the roll afterwards, and "the episode 6 ambush is played on the wall clock, blind." A machine registering the body as it stands there is the beat The Catch built its needle on.
*Fix:* Cut the line. The ticket's only appearance is Kieran's hand at breakfast, corroborating what the meter has already said — which is its job in the plan.

*Why it matters:* The episode contradicts itself about its most-watched beat. *Fix:* The fizz begins as he reaches, before contact; the hover plays with it already running; Danny's line becomes "It was fizzing before I ever touched him." "Tingle came early. That's the grab" stays as his wrong explanation, feeding episode 9 exactly as planned.

**6. Scene 8 — Osei's cure contradicts what she said on screen in episode 2.**
*Problem:* The draft has her cured in 2011 by "a man in Whitechapel… on a borrowed rig, before any of this was a business."
*Why it matters:* The plan says she was "cured by Restoration herself", and in the finished episode 2 she says "I sat in that chair once. Hungry" — under the draft's origin she was never Obverse's patient, so a line already broadcast stops being true. The scene is also the episode's second consecutive explanation scene, carried by her longest speeches.

I sat in that chair hungry, and a clinician took me ten seconds deep, on a rig they'd borrowed off a rescue crew. It cost me everything I had. So now it costs money." Cut "Nobody counted what else it did" from her mouth and leave it where the plan has it, in Tess's silent run-through. **7. Scene 1 — "Ten days ago" is wrong.**
*Problem:* The bullet was delivered at 02:04 in the early hours of Saturday 11 April (episode 4's ticket; Peg found it on Tuesday 21 April in episode 5); this episode is about Wednesday 6 May. That is twenty-five days.
*Why it matters:* The dates are canon in the finished episodes and the owner tracks them.

*Problem:* The run-through never states that Marrable Street's ten seconds was a seven-in-ten chance of curing Rosie that came up wrong — the job she pitched to pay for her cure *was* the cure. The half-drawn 10.0 alone doesn't say it.

**11. Scene 3 — the envelope is in the wrong hand.**
*Problem:* "In a round, schoolteacher's hand: TESS MARLOW."
*Why it matters:* Maggie fills forms in square capitals (episode 4's Register form, "filled in in her square capitals"); the envelope is hers, and episode 7's recognition depends on small things matching.
*Fix:* "In square capitals: TESS MARLOW."

**12. Scene 3 — the cash box is paid out before the safe is opened.**
*Problem:* Peg pays the locum "in notes from the cash box", then opens the safe in which the cash box is sitting on the shelf.
*Why it matters:* The box cannot be in her hand and in the safe; it is the scene that finds the envelope, and the moment should not wobble.
*Fix:* Pay from the float tin in the dresser (established in episode 3), or have the box already out on the counter and show her putting it back when the envelope catches her eye.

**13. Scenes 1–2 — Danny's four untreated hours.**
*Problem:* He wraps the hand in a tea towel at ten past two and sits undiscovered until half six. A clean-plane wound bleeds — Shaun's leg needed a belt and a spanner — and no reason is given for waking nobody.
*Why it matters:* The audience will do the arithmetic of the clock and the blood.
*Fix:* Two touches: his eyes on the camp bed a foot away — he will not have the boy see it first — and his belt off and cinched round his forearm, Terry's own trick from the river stairs, done one-handed and badly. It buys the hours and rhymes.

**14. Scene 9 against plan act one — the wobble's place, and Peg's line.**
*Problem:* The plan puts Kieran's circled pattern (19.78, 19.79, 19.77, 19.78) at breakfast; the draft moves it to Thursday night. Peg's "Scales wobble, love. That's why they certify them" reassures with the opposite of what certification promises.
*Why it matters:* The move is defensible — the dismissal lands harder once we know what Tess knows — but it is a deviation from the plan's act structure and should be declared, not slipped. The Peg line is a false note in a family that lives by the certificate on the wall.
*Fix:* Keep the night scene (it is better), declare the move, and sharpen Peg — "Then Trading Standards can come and certify the wobble" — which also tips its hat to Pinnock and the drawer.

**15. Scene 6 — the hop meets no threat.**
*Problem:* Tess's first crossing in twelve years finds the cabinet exactly where Danny predicted, takes the binder, and leaves with five seconds of change. The weigh-wall she passes is described and never presses.
*Why it matters:* The show's own suspense grammar: show the rule, then let it press. Obverse is the one place in the season that knows exactly what a crossing costs, and the scene never lets us feel that it knows.
*Fix:* Arm it in scene 5 — Terry, at the collar: "Their wall in there floods at three." / TESS: "I'm two and a half. Not a tenth more." — and in the black, one half-second of near-lingering: a spine she can't read at the cabinet's edge, the count at 2.2, then out. Same numbers, same result, a held breath.

A gasp. Exhaust, cold, the hiss of a night bus. The door of the van, and Terry's window down.

TERRY: Straight?

TESS: Straight.

She can't know that. Neither can we.

## Act three: the protocol

### Scene 7. The van, a side street off Harley Street, continuous.

A streetlamp over the bonnet. Terry drives them round the corner and parks under it, and kills the engine.

TERRY: I'll be at the corner. Watching the street.

He gets out and walks to the corner, and stands there, a big shape under the next lamp, and the terrier man passes him going the other way.

Tess opens the binder on her knees. Cloth-bound, OBVERSE stamped in the corner.

The first sheet is in a plastic sleeve, typed like a recipe. She reads it aloud, low, to nobody, the way she counts, so someone's counting.

TESS: Transit ten point nought seconds. Expected restoration nought point seven nought. *(beat)* Clinician in transit: less than two seconds. No contact.

She turns pages, and stops. An annex: two columns, hand-ruled, filled in ink. Seconds down one side. Odds down the other. She reads it out loud, flat, the way she reads a shopping list.

TESS: Two seconds: none ever recorded. *(beat)* Three: one in five thousand. Four: one in twelve. Five: one in six. Six: one in four. Seven: one in three. Eight: one in two. Nine: three in five. Ten: seven in ten.

Silence in the van. A taxi goes by outside, lit up yellow. At the corner, Terry doesn't turn round.

She sits with it a moment. Then she squares the pages, and shuts the binder, and holds it flat on her knees with both hands.

Terry comes back. He opens the door and gets in, and looks at her face, and then straight ahead, and doesn't ask.

TERRY: Well?

TESS: It's a crossing. Ten seconds in the black, under their roof. That's the whole of it. That's what the money buys.

A long quiet. Terry looks down the street at the dark clinic, and something goes out of his face that has been in it for five years.

TERRY: Eleven times I went in. On two seconds.

TESS: I know, Tel.

TERRY: You can't buy nothing with two seconds.

He starts the engine. As they pull out he starts, very quietly, half-singing the old washing-day song, the one he sings when there is nothing to say, and Tess lets him sing it. She holds the binder flat on her knees with both hands, all the way over the river.

## Act four: the sums

### Scene 8. Dr Osei's consulting room, Harley Street, Thursday, nine in the morning.

The waiting room, the orchids, the hush. The RECEPTIONIST looks up, her right hand moving on the keyboard, her left hand very still beside it.

TESS: No appointment. Tell her it's about her records room.

The receptionist's eyes go, for half a second, to the tall window. Then she picks up a phone, and speaks into it, and puts it down.

RECEPTIONIST: Dr Osei will see you.

The consulting room, as before: the tall window, the desk with nothing on it. DR HELEN OSEI comes in and closes the door, and stands, and waits.

Tess lays the binder on the desk.

Osei looks at it. She doesn't touch it for a moment. Then she opens it and squares the pages, unhurried, a woman tidying her own kitchen.

OSEI: Two point six seconds. Sixty-one kilos, seven hundred grams.

TESS: Point nought on my bridge.

Osei's eyes come up at that, and stay a half-second longer than the words are worth. She files it. We watch her file it.

TESS: How long have you known?

OSEI: Since 2011. It cured me. *(She sits, slowly, like a woman who has stood in courts.)* I turned on a rescue, before the licences. Nine seconds, two men and a door frame on top of me. I sat in that chair hungry, and one of their clinicians took me ten seconds deep, on a rig this house had borrowed off a rescue crew. I was one of the first they ever walked through. It cost me everything I had. *(beat)* So now it costs money.

TESS: Seventy per cent. You could put that in plain numbers on your door.

OSEI: We do. Inside. *(beat)* Your two and a half seconds. Who taught you that?

TESS: Nobody. It's just sense.

OSEI: In 2013 a surgeon ran past three seconds with a child's tumour in her hand. The licence came in the year after, at three, as a margin against running out.

She leaves it there. The window, the street, the taxis. Neither woman finishes the thought.

OSEI: That price pays for the Register's kitchens. Forty thousand people eat off it, every day. You take the price out of the world, they go back to gin and dripping. *(beat)* Put it back.

TESS: It's feeding three at my table.

OSEI: Then bring them to the Register. Nobody asks them anything.

TESS: We feed our own.

OSEI: *(not unkindly)* Everybody says that. In that chair. Right up until they're hungry.

Tess picks up the binder.

OSEI: If I call someone, this becomes an exhibit, and exhibits walk. That's the only reason you're standing up. *(beat)* Put it back, or keep it dark. There's no third thing.

Tess goes. Osei sits still a while after the door has shut. Then she opens her diary and calls in the next patient, and it is nine-fifteen, and the day goes on.

### Scene 9. The weighbridge office, Thursday, nine at night into the small hours.

**Nine o'clock.** Kieran hauls the cast-iron test weight out from under the counter with both hands and a lot of breath, and stands it in the middle of the little bridge. The printer chatters. 19.78. He tears off the ticket and opens his exercise book.

Down the column, since the weekend: 19.79. 19.77. 19.78. 19.78. Tonight makes the fifth, and the flat line on his graph has grown a blur. He takes his pencil and circles the five of them, all in one ring.

KIERAN: It wobbles now.

PEG: Then Trading Standards can come and certify the wobble.

KIERAN: It's meant to be a flat line.

TESS: *(at the table, her lips moving on something else, her eyes not on him)* Everything wobbles. Do your reading.

He looks at her — the one person in this family who counts — and gets no more out of her than that. He writes 19.78 in the book. On the settle, Danny eats from the pot with his left hand and watches nobody.

**Later.** The house asleep. Kieran comes down in his socks, stands on the plate in his pyjamas by the printer's green light, and tears off the ticket. 38.9. In the second column, the one headed *me*, under 39.2, he writes it. Book under the pillow. Back up to bed.

**Later still.** Tess comes down alone. She doesn't put a light on. She sits at the long table with the binder open under the green glow, and the pencil behind her ear where it has lived for twenty years, and she does not take it down.

Her lips move. The season runs through the table in her head, and we go with it.

— The office telly, the masked surgeon over the sleeping child, three seconds; and Rosie, chin on her fists: *"She's done four thousand. Never turned."*

— Osei by the tall window that morning: *"The licence came in the year after, at three, as a margin against running out."*

— Terry at the stove: *"Eleven times I went again. Two seconds a go. Hungry every time after."*

— The catalogue cutting pinned by the stove, the window on the left; Rosie's eyes sliding to it after every hop, seven times, and every time the woman at her wrong window.

— Her own voice at dawn, over a plate of dinner: *"Three. Face home. Out."*

— The whole table, the old chorus, mugs raised: *"Never go back twice."* And Liam over a tube of drawings: *"Could've gone in twice."* Her lips over both: *Twice. Under three.*

— The chalk board as it was: 10.0, the second nought half drawn, the biggest star the board ever had. Then Rosie folding onto the plate, the tray skidding to the safe, and the meter among the mugs: LO. Her lips: *Seven in ten.*

— Danny alone in the dark, a week ago, opening and closing his hand: *"Two seconds is enough to grab someone."*

Her lips go down the column and stop between two rows.

Nine point four. About three in five.

There is no number on her lips now. She closes the binder, puts her hands flat on top of it, and sits in the dark with the fridge humming and the green light blinking, for a long time.

### Scene 10. The weighbridge office, Friday, two in the morning.

Rosie comes down the stairs in the big jumper, barefoot, and goes to the fridge, and takes out the pot, and eats standing at the counter, a spoonful at a time, in the dark. She doesn't switch the light on either.

Tess is still at the table.

For a while, neither of them says anything. Then Rosie nods at the coat on the chair, where the corner of a white envelope shows above the pocket.

ROSIE: From them?

TESS: From them.

Neither of them touches it.

Tess gets up. She opens the cutlery drawer and takes out Peg's meter, and pricks her own finger, matter-of-fact, the way she does everything, and squeezes the bead onto the strip, and waits.

The beep. The screen: **5.4**.

She lets a breath go, slow, and it comes all the way out.

TESS: Straight.

Rosie watches her. She knows exactly what that waiting was, and how long it is.

Tess puts the meter back among the forks and shuts the drawer. Then she looks across the dark kitchen at her niece, and says it the way she says numbers, once, and plainly, like something she has carried all day and can put down nowhere else.

TESS: I gave you nine.

A long beat. Rosie looks at the binder under Tess's flat hands — at the shape of it, the thickness of it, not inside it — and then up at her aunt. And the grin starts to come, the show-off's grin, the one she does when she is lying.

It stops halfway. For once in her life, she hasn't got anything.

She picks up her pot, and the spoon, and goes up the stairs without a word. At the top, her door shuts, very softly.

Tess sits on. Under her flat hands the binder; in her coat pocket the envelope. The fridge hums. The green light blinks.

**Cut to black.**

**END OF EPISODE 6**
