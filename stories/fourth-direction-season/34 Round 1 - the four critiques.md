# 34 Round 1 - the four critiques of the season

*Three GLM critics, each through one lens, and MiMo v2.6 Pro reading with fresh eyes. Each finding is marked SUBSTANTIVE or QUIBBLE.*

## Critique: story

1. **Episode 6, cold open (Danny's ambush at the safe).** Danny's whole stakeout rests on one ticket from 4 April: a man came off the Ahern tow once, so he will come again, tonight, at 02:04, into the arm's length of black Danny has chosen — and on a true budget of 2.2 seconds the gamble pays first try. Maggie sent Joe that night for her own reason (the ferry letter), independently of Danny's guess, so the episode 6 climax lands on authorial coincidence, and the show even has Tess flag the hole ("If he came off the boat") without closing it. It matters because this is a major reversal scene, and the owner's rules say twists should be earned by cause, not luck. The "again" in Kieran's line ("It printed him again") hints at repeat visits nobody ever reported, which is worse: if more −59.0 tickets existed, Kieran — the boy who keeps every ticket — sat on them. **Fix:** either give Kieran two or three dated −59.0 tickets in the episode's first act (which also justifies Danny's certainty and the bed move), or make Danny's stakeout a persistence — one shot of Kieran waking to find him at the table "fourth night running." Either converts luck into pattern. SUBSTANTIVE

2. **Episodes 9 and 10 (the sea, the tide and the door).** Three on-screen facts never reconcile: Rosie counts the neighbours' sea surface eight metres above our ground at Grays; the baking tray requires that at Nobody low water the sea's edge sits *at* our ground level (the wet edge crosses the ruler); and the finale fixes low water at 05:52 on the Sunday. For the first two to coexist the tide range must exceed eight metres, which nobody says; and by any ordinary tide arithmetic, back-computing from the Sunday 05:52 low, Rosie's ~00:45 crossing on the Thursday falls mid-ebb, not at the top — she should swim up through roughly half the range. It matters because the door's tide logic is the finale's hinge and the season trades openly on exact numbers; a tide-literate viewer can catch it, and "the Deep keeps you" silently becomes tide-dependent into the bargain. **Fix:** one line and one shot. Have Rosie check Kieran's graph on the office wall before she steps (her finger on the tide curve — "top of the Nobody tonight"), and let Maggie say at the tray, "She went at the top. At low, over your yard, it's a puddle." That states the range and anchors her crossing. SUBSTANTIVE

3. **Episodes 3, 4 and 6 (what the Marrable Street tray was worth).** Rosie sizes the haul at "two kilo, tray and all" of diamond-room content; the family values it at "Terry's year" (£109,500), and the money arithmetic afterwards (the float tin empty, "thirty-eight days" in the house at £900 a day, a month up front demanded for the Laundry) implies the fence paid far less even than that. Nothing on screen explains why two kilos of stones fences so cheap, and it is load-bearing: the family's being broke after their best-ever job is what sends Liam into the river wall. **Fix:** one line — the stones are laser-inscribed and registered ("Every stone in that room's got a number cut in it. Hot as a kettle. He'll pay for the settings and no more"), or trim the tray to "under a kilo, papers and settings." SUBSTANTIVE

4. **Episode 6, scene 8 into episode 7, scene 12 (Tess and the 61.7).** Osei's wall reads Tess at sixty-one kilos seven hundred grams; her bridge said 61.0. The scene gives the noticing to Osei and gives Tess only a flat correction ("Point nought on my bridge") — she hears an outside instrument contradict her bridge by exactly the 1.1 per cent that killed Liam, and never circles back: not at the ferry, not when Danny demands "And Liam? Did your sums do Liam too?" and she says "I don't know." The plant is fair for the audience, but the character is shown filing the one clue that cracks the case and then keeping it filed for three episodes while her nephew's death goes unexplained, which reads as the writer's hand rather than her flaw. **Fix:** one beat of wrong dismissal in character — "Their walls read heavy. Everyone knows that" — or a confession at the stove: "Their wall had me at sixty-one seven. I told myself their wall was wrong." SUBSTANTIVE

5. **Episode 9, scenes 4–5 (the bite in water).** Rosie bets her life that the tab fires from inside their sea, but nobody before this has said on screen that the bite works wet — only that the main field doesn't (which she has to derive mid-drowning). Her inference from Tess's "only once you've stood a minute on the wrong side" is traceable, so it plays, but the fair-play plant is one line short of airtight for the season's most dangerous act. **Fix:** at the episode 5 tab-tug, Terry or Tess adds: "It'll fire in water. Only thing on this rig that will." QUIBBLE

6. **Episodes 8–9 (Rosie's two weeks of silence about the list).** The nurse tells her on day one that the police cannot read the Register, and the episode marks that she "doesn't say a word about it either" — while her father and uncle starve across the river and the fact dissolves half of Peg's refusal. The silence is in character (she inherited Frank's hiding), but two weeks of it, unwitnessed, costs her sympathy at exactly the moment the audience needs to keep faith with her. **Fix:** one shot — she starts a text to Kieran, thumb over the keys, and deletes it. QUIBBLE

7. **Episode 9, scene 11 ("I gave you your raid").** Tess claims foreknowledge of the depot raid — a raid she believes turns people — and did nothing: no warning, no vigil, no call, while Shaun goes and turns. A reading exists (Peg's choice to make; Rosie's rescue her priority), but the line asserts a knowledge the staging never shows her having or using. **Fix:** either cut the claim ("By the time the ward rang, you were already in the marsh") or stage her choice — Tess in the car outside the gate at midnight, lips moving, not going in. QUIBBLE

8. **Episode 10, scene 9 (the tide called "tomorrow").** At four in the morning Kieran says "Next Nobody low's tomorrow morning. Five fifty-two," and the door scenes play at 05:52 that same morning, under two hours later; Ray's "before Monday" confirms it is Sunday. A straight continuity slip in the scene the numbers matter most in. **Fix:** "Next Nobody low's five fifty-two. Two hours." QUIBBLE

9. **Episode 1, scene 16 (the expert's macro lens).** Dr Farrant photographs the tacking edge up close — the exact evidence of the mirrored linen — and still declares a copy on the light alone. Ray's lampshade ("Their expert looked at the paint. I look at the cloth") covers the verdict, not the lens in her hand. **Fix:** have her shoot the tacking edge only in the noon session after the Unit asks for it, or let her murmur "odd weave — continental" and move on, so the miss is a near thing rather than a blind spot. QUIBBLE

10. **Episode 4, scene 3 (Lisbon priced off-screen).** Rosie's leaflet already carries "LISBON. HALF." in biro, but nothing shows when or how she found the half-price clinic; the clerk's line about "cut-price jobs" arrives afterwards, for the audience's benefit only. **Fix:** one beat in episode 3 — a foreign number on her phone at the wheel, or a printed page folded into the leaflet. QUIBBLE

11. **Episode 6, scene 3 into episode 7, scene 3 (the envelope carried unopened).** Tess pockets the Ahern envelope on the Thursday and is shown slitting it — "one slit, clean" — a week later, on the morning of the ferry it names. Waiting eight days on an enemy's letter, then opening it on the one day it could still be actioned, is luck dressed as control. **Fix:** a line to motive it ("Nine days I carried it. Weren't giving them the pleasure") or open it the night she is put out and let her sit on its contents instead. QUIBBLE

## Critique: people

1. **Episode 4 (scenes 7–9) and episode 7 (scene 7) — Aoife Ahern.** The war's dead centre — the turning, the wrong milk, the second crossing alone, the month on the fridge — exists only as other people's loss: a photo, a printout, a locker snapshot, secondhand testimony. She has no voice, no hand, no scene; even her crossings live in her family's books, not hers. Why it matters: "no fridging" is an explicit rule of the brief, the Aherns' half of the season rests on her, and Maggie's 3 a.m. bottles are left carrying a grief the audience never gets to share in a person. Fix: give Aoife handwriting. On the ferry, let Maggie open the tide book to a column in a younger, different hand — "Hers. She kept the seconds since she was fifteen" — so the first graph either family ever draws is drawn over Aoife's own figures, and her two nines are read out in her own numerals. That does it in the season's own language (mirror script, Peg's pencil, Kieran's log) and makes her present at her own count. **SUBSTANTIVE**

2. **Episode 4 (scene 4, the pit; scene 12, breakfast) and episode 9 (scene 12) — Rosie's second secret never surfaces.** The season runs on secrets coming out: Rosie's turn, Terry's pot, Danny's yes, Maggie's tip, Ray's ledger, Peg's breakfast, Kieran's column. The one concealment never discharged is the heroine's second one — she taught Liam to make weight ("Skip the fry-up... the dog'll have it"; "They'll clap you"), covered for him at the table, and then sat silent while Peg confessed the lucky breakfast as the whole human half of the sum ("Either one, he makes it. Both, he doesn't" names the scale and the breakfast, never the coaching). Why it matters: Rosie's guilt is planted beautifully (she can't look at Danny in 5; "He was so pleased about the star" in 9) but never paid, and the confessional pattern that carries the family to the vote breaks at its lead — the best-loved crosser keeps the one thing that would hurt most to say. Fix: one beat in episode 10's night of writing. When Liam's line goes onto the roll, Rosie says her half out loud — "Nan put the plate in front of him. I'm the one taught him to be hungry for it." Danny's grief gets a partner, and her choice to stay turned is made clean-handed. **SUBSTANTIVE**

3. **Episode 9 (scene 11) and episode 10 (scene 8) — Tess and Kieran, the season's central wound, blinked at.** The woman stood on the plate at fourteen in the week her father died discovers her own son has been starving under her roof to be chosen the same way — and it is discovered and processed in four lines ("Since Easter?" / "He's been making weight under your roof" / back to the graph). Mother and son never get a scene alone; and in episode 10 her surrender ("Every number's mine. Start with me") costs him his mother as his father left, a price never weighed with him either. Why it matters: Tess is inner circle; her wound is the pilot's engine; the payoff of that wound is this boy, and the season steps over it to get back to the flour. Fix: one collapse in her own grammar before she is "Tess again" — "I can't remember what he had for his tea," from the woman who has counted every mouthful since she was fourteen. Then in episode 10, let Kieran ask the pit question again ("How old were you?") and this time let her answer what the different week was. **SUBSTANTIVE**

4. **Episode 8 (scene 6) and episodes 9–10 — Ray's fall is narrated, and the long fight between equals ends without the equal.** The Crime profile promises a plain-looking master pursuer and a long duel. Ray's pursuit is excellent through episode 5 (contracts, stakeout, the rig), but his fall — the biggest event of his arc, and the show's own line-crossing — happens offscreen and is delivered in one speech at the flat; after it the family faces no active pursuer until polite Register investigators ask for weights. Why it matters: a promised beat isn't twisted, it's dropped, and the audience is told the collapse of the duel instead of watching it; the station handover in episode 10 should end a fight, not a formality. Fix: thirty seconds of the audit — the episode 1 colleague ("Lunch, Ray?") standing silent while Ray is walked out of Victoria, which shows the fall and plants that colleague as the unit's new Marlow man; then one line in episode 9's phone call ("My gaffer's got your file now") keeps the hunt breathing in the back half. **SUBSTANTIVE**

5. **Episode 9 (scenes 3, 6–7) against episode 8 (scene 10) — the climax crime's cost to people we love is never felt.** The last job is robbed from the institution the audience has been taught to cherish: the unlocked ward, the nurse, Den going home to his leeks on Purfleet's boxes. Episode 4 gave Terry the exact line for this ("Cost someone") about the Ahern tubs, and it has no echo at Purfleet; the only horror that lands is Shaun's turned case. Why it matters: the "line crossed" promise and the reckoning paid in kind need the family to cross against the sympathetic world, not the abstract state; without the beat, giving the rolls reads as the Marlows saving themselves, not answering anyone they robbed. Fix: the callback is nearly free — Terry, stacking crates in the reeds: "Cost someone." And at the vote, one line from Rosie that fuses her fortnight with the debt: "I ate off that depot a fortnight. Den's eating off it now." **SUBSTANTIVE**

6. **Episode 5 (scene 6) through episode 6 (scene 4) — Declan never grieves the brother he killed.** Declan is the nearest thing the season has to a villain, and his defining act — putting Con's Register form in the stove — kills his own brother. He is then absent from the season until the epilogue's chalk. Why it matters: villains need care; as written he is the theme wearing a cap (the culture that never changes) rather than a man who buried the brother he starved, and the epilogue's chilling return — cheering Siobhan's hunger — lands far harder if we have once watched him lose to his own rule. Fix: thirty seconds in episode 6. Night, the Ahern kitchen: Declan alone with Con's dripping jar and the dead phone hook; he lifts the receiver, and puts it down. Grief with the door shut — the unchanging shown as a choice renewed, not an absence. **SUBSTANTIVE**

7. **Episode 9 (scene 1) — the war council doesn't know what it knows.** After episode 7 the whole family has heard "most over eight" and watched Peg burn the proof, yet the cold open chalks SHAUN 51.9 + 3.0 = 9.0 in pre-episode-7 innocence. Defensible — the one person who says such things has been put out of the house — but the design (Tess's absence causing the silence) is easier to admire than to feel, and a careful viewer may read forgetting where the show means suppression. Fix: one flicker in Shaun's own voice ("Nine and a bit. Coin, they say"), waved off by Peg ("Everything's a coin. Eat your butty"), so the knowledge is seen to be chosen away. **QUIBBLE**

8. **Episode 7 (scenes 4 and 7) — the two nearest approaches to saying the thesis aloud.** "Nobody ever asked why." / "He was off his food, love. Then he wasn't. That was enough for us." and "Nobody counted my girl... That's what I've come to say." Both are grief lines in character about specific dead people, not speeches, and Maggie's is a purpose rather than a moral. But Terry's second sentence ("That was enough for us") does the culture's fault out loud, and the two piles of stubs — straight and turned — already say it without him. Fix: test whether "Nobody ever asked why" can go and let the acts carry it; keep Maggie's. **QUIBBLE**

9. **Episodes 8–10 — Peg and Rosie never close.** "You're poorly." / "I'm turned, Nan" (episode 4) is their sharpest exchange, and every "turned" moment Peg gives afterwards goes to Terry (said aloud), to Frank (written) and to the board — never back to Rosie. Between the gate in episode 8 ("You'll be fed") and the epilogue the two women get no look at each other, though Rosie's starvation at Peg's cheering table is where the season's guarantee broke. Fix: one wordless gesture at the vote or the station door — Peg fixing the collar of Rosie's coat the way she tugged Liam's bite tab. **QUIBBLE**

10. **Episodes 1–5 — Shaun's care arrives too thin for his leg.** He is the least-seen crosser of the principals (eating standing up, the bottom stair, a long look at mooring lines), so when the flat plane arrives the horror is exact but the loss is thin: we lose an effect before we have lost a person. Fix: one beat in the episode 5 plan scene that is his alone — why he loves the boats, a joke with the dog, anything wanted — so the leg is his and not the plot's. **QUIBBLE**

11. **Episode 10 (acts four and five) — the clock stops ticking after Terry's step.** The emotional peak lands at 05:52 in act three; the vote is rightly quieter, but the Monday warrant — the only pressure left — is never felt in the room, and the deliberation can read as aftermath rather than decision. Fix: put the clock in the frame. Peg glancing at it during the vote, or the first lorry of Monday's queue already idling at the gate, keeps the choice tense after the goodbye. **QUIBBLE**

12. **Episode 10 (scene 8) — Danny and Maggie share a kitchen all night without a word.** The man whose floor turned her daughter and the woman whose family's war took his son stand in the same room through the night of writing, and the truce is carried silently by the tide books going onto the pile. Why it matters: the Gangster guest's rival-family thread is resolved by objects between the two weighers; the two people who actually owe each other never acknowledge it, and a careful viewer feels the gap. Fix: one line while she reads the steelyard for him — "Your girl's floor. I poured it." / "I know. Write it down." **QUIBBLE**

13. **Episode 8 (scene 7) — the source of Peg's "them" is one she never read.** Her intensity at the vote ("They wrote to this house once before... I'm not starting on their word now") draws its explanation from the 2001 letter's police-interview line — a letter Peg never opened, so the audience cannot source her vehemence to her wound; the farm grievance covers the Ministry, not the Register, and the leap from "they took my field" to "their food is a trap" is plot-shaped. Fix: one remembered betrayal in her voice, rooted in the family's actual history — Maureen, say: "Last Marlow that signed their book, the police had him by Friday." **QUIBBLE**

14. **Episode 6 (scene 9) — the one stop in the season's fastest episode.** Tess alone replaying six clips the audience has already seen is the craft notes' own warning (teaching without a clock) in miniature, at the hour the drift table has just detonated. Fix: run it under pressure — dawn coming up the blind, footsteps on the stairs, Rosie due down for the pot — or cut the chorus and the surgeon and keep the four clips that do new work (Terry's eleven, Mayfair's nine four, "never go back twice", the LO). **QUIBBLE**

## Critique: continuity

1. **Episode 7, scene 12 (the confession at the table).** Rosie says, "Their lad was six metres off with a stopwatch, timing me, and I never knew him from a pull in my gut." The stopwatch and the distance exist only in Maggie's ferry account (scene 7: "He never came within three fathoms of her... with a stopwatch"), told to Tess an hour earlier across the river. Tess walks straight from the pontoon into this scene and never relays it on screen; Rosie was not at the lock-up conversation, Joe never spoke, and no Ahern scene has reached her. The season's spine is its knowledge map, and here the central confession asserts facts the character cannot have. **Fix:** either give Tess one line earlier in the scene ("Their lad sat six metres off her with a stopwatch — Maggie's word for it"), or strip the specifics from Rosie ("There was never anybody near me. I never felt him at all"), which is stronger anyway. **SUBSTANTIVE**

2. **Episode 8, scenes 7 and 12 (the vote and "Two weeks on").** Peg says "Wednesday fortnight" on Tuesday 2 June (the day Tess follows the van and visits Ray). Act five is headed "Two weeks on" and scene 12 is dated "Wednesday 10 June". Episode 9, scene 11 then pins the raid for ever: Tess reads the April letter and says "It's the twelfth of June," making the raid Wednesday 10 June — one week after Rosie's admission on 3 June, not a fortnight. Two of the three dating elements are wrong, and a viewer who has been given exact dates all season (4 April, the twelfth of June) will do this sum. **Fix:** Peg says "Wednesday week" and the act header becomes "A week on"; nothing else moves. **SUBSTANTIVE**

3. **Episode 10, scenes 11–12 (Rosie at the fence).** She crosses "on empty lungs": "one long out, and Rosie stands empty, holding nothing, her chest still." On the shore the script then gives her "Her held breath in her chest," and after landing she "lets her last breath out — hers, only hers, into our air." She cannot be both empty and holding a breath; and if she never breathed over there, the released air is stale Grays air, which guts the epilogue's declared wonder ("what her last breath smelled of — released alone... into our air"). Episode 9 set the honest precedent: one breath of theirs, held home, spent alone. **Fix:** one beat in the black on the shore — she takes one breath of their air and holds it (the protocol bars arriving with ours, not taking theirs); everything else stands. **SUBSTANTIVE**

4. **Episode 10, scene 2 (the loft).** Tess unrolls "the 2002 roll" and the first ticket on it is "September: FRANK 78.0," followed by the decline, then "The new year," then spring. Frank turned at Farringdon in September 2001 (canon; six months starving, dead spring 2002), and the boxes are labelled "01, 02... year by year" (episode 7, where the box marked 02 is already "your dad's year"). A September 2001 print cannot sit on a calendar-year 2002 roll, and a roll spanning autumn-to-spring breaks the year-by-year boxes Pinnock's line established. **Fix:** open box 01 first (the September ticket, 78.0, "12 st 4"), then box 02 for the decline to 64.4 and the "7.1" — two beats, and Peg handling her own pencil twice is stronger. **SUBSTANTIVE**

5. **Episodes 8 and 9 (Rosie's weight, "to the gram").** The ward weighs her in a t-shirt: 46.2 (episode 8, scene 12). She crosses at night rigged — belt, collar, brass disc, wrist unit, clothes, socks — and the minus ticket reads −46.2, which she proclaims: "Me. To the gram. Ward scale had me at the same on Wednesday." Since episode 1 the family has weighed crossers rigged ("in what she'll cross in"), and every kilo is a fifth of a second; the rig cannot be massless in this world, so the gram-match is impossible. This is exactly the arithmetic the season trains its audience to do (Liam's 65.1, Kieran's two columns). **Fix:** give the rig a canonical mass once (say 2.5 kg): the ticket reads −48.7, Rosie says "Forty-six two of me and the rest is the rig — Ward had the same Wednesday," and her bite becomes 4.9 seconds; "Five and a bit, Uncle" still works. **SUBSTANTIVE**

6. **Episode 9, scene 11 (the April letter) — a loaded gun never fired.** The re-verification order is read aloud, dated the eleventh of April, 28 days, and Tess underlines the lapse: "It's the twelfth of June." Trading Standards never comes, and worse, the lapse quietly undercuts the finale's stated stake ("without the rolls, no licence, no weighbridge"): by the show's own letter the certification is already compromised regardless of the vote. **Fix:** one beat — Pinnock re-verifies against the steelyard in the epilogue (a new certificate beside Kieran's "checked against Maggie's"), or the investigators note the lapse and the family's surrendered rolls and steelyard become the re-verification. **SUBSTANTIVE**

7. **Episode 10, scene 6 (the investigators' summary) against episode 9, scene 5.** The depot wall "logged... One of them nine times. The other twice — the second time carrying three kilos." But on screen Shaun lands with a case on *both* legs ("He lands with a gasp, the case in his arms," then "Shaun lands with the last case"), and Terry's nine crossings each carried a case too. A season that taught its audience to audit wall logs (Liam's 65.1) has its own auditor misdescribe the raid the audience watched last week. **Fix:** "One of them nine times, three kilos each go; the other twice, the same," or restage Shaun's first leg as the empty run to the far stack. **SUBSTANTIVE**

8. **Episode 5, scene 13 against episodes 9 and 10 — the twenty-year law.** Tess tells Liam "step out on purpose, it's twenty years." Rosie then does it (documented on the surrendered rolls: two 45.7 tickets around a minute-long minus), Terry does it, and no one ever mentions the offence again; Peg's bail lists the depot and the drain only. In a season where every other legal thread (the Register list, custody by pantry) pays, this plant hangs. **Fix:** one line at the station — the sergeant reads the charge and Peg says "And the girl went next door. Write that an' all," owning it as she owns the drain. **QUIBBLE**

9. **Episode 6, cold open (Danny's ambush).** Danny's budget is 2.4 seconds (2.2 true). He steps off at 02:04:00 because the ticket says 02:04, and Joe happens to be at the safe within that window ("a white envelope... let go a moment ago"). A minute has sixty seconds; nothing on screen says Joe's arrival is precise to the second — though the show's own material says it is (the Aherns "time everything to the second"). The guess is owned, the odds are not. **Fix:** one line from Danny at the stove — "Declan times his farts to the second. Tide, tow, the lot. If the boy's off at four past once, he's off at four past every night." **QUIBBLE**

10. **Episode 6, scene 9 (Tess's night montage).** The montage includes Danny's "Two seconds is enough to grab someone" — words he said alone, in the dark, at a whisper, with the house asleep or pretending (episode 5, scene 18). Tess was never shown or implied to hear it. As filmic montage it passes; against this season's own knowledge rigour it leaks. **Fix:** swap in a line Tess actually heard — Danny at the episode 4 table: "Then I'll turn one of them. I've got two seconds" — which is the better plant anyway. **QUIBBLE**

11. **Episode 7, scenes 7 and 11 (Maggie's image repeated).** On the ferry, an hour apart in one continuous conversation, Maggie says "my girl's a reading on a fridge" and later "my girl's a fridge door." The same grief described twice in nearly the same words reads like a seam between drafts rather than a deliberate refrain. **Fix:** keep the first (the more exact, colder phrase) and let the second land differently: "and my girl's a month of print in my kitchen." **QUIBBLE**

12. **Episode 6, scene 5 (Peg's line direction).** Peg's "Paper talks" is directed "because it is her own line, from the wall, in a frame." Episode 1 established it as her spoken line ("Police said so. In print. Paper talks. Burn it"); the frames on the wall hold the forensic cuttings, not those words. The direction invents a framed version nobody hung. **Fix:** reword to "her own line, at last believed against her," or have her nod at the framed cuttings as she says it. **QUIBBLE**

13. **Episode 6, scene 5 (the clinic casing).** The whole records-room heist map rests on Rosie's "I asked for the toilet" — a moment in her episode 2 visit that was never shown or hinted at. Her casing instinct is well planted (the jeweller's van), but the layout itself is retrofitted; a sharp viewer may feel the plan was waiting off screen. **Fix:** one planted beat in episode 2 — Rosie, waiting, asking the receptionist "Where's your toilet?", and a two-second walk down the basement corridor past a steel door. **QUIBBLE**

14. **Episode 9, scene 11 (next of kin).** The ward rings Tess "the minute you walked. I'm down as next of kin." Danny — Rosie's father, alive, local, and turned like her — is the obvious next of kin, and Rosie's choice of the aunt who has just been put out of the house is a decision the show needs but never explains. **Fix:** one line — Rosie at admission, "Put my auntie down. Not the house" — which also characterises the rift. **QUIBBLE**

15. **Episode 8, scene 7 (the envelope of fifties).** The month-up-front money is squared on the counter "untouched since Friday," Ray confirms "It's still there," and then it vanishes from the story — in a season that counts every pound (the float tin, thirty-eight days, £6,300 a week). The family's cash is tracked too carefully for a brick of fifties to walk off unremarked. **Fix:** Ray pushes it back across the table ("That's yours. The price died with the round"), or Peg banks it back into the float tin on screen. **QUIBBLE**

16. **Episode 9, scene 10 (the weed frond).** The same night the family treats the jumper as contamination — wrung down the drain, Peg's "Get rid of that jumper before they come" — Kieran tapes a flattened frond of the neighbours' weed into his exercise book, and nobody, including the one turned man who understands what it is, ever notices that this is the same "other-hand matter" Peg is later charged over. **Fix:** make it a choice — Kieran asks Terry "Can I keep it?" and Terry, of all people, says yes; the double standard becomes a character beat instead of an oversight. **QUIBBLE**

17. **Episodes 8 and 9 (the chalk board).** Peg writes "REGISTER DEPOT — PURFLEET" (episode 8, scene 7); the same board the next night reads "PURFLEET — DEPOT" (episode 9, cold open), with no rewrite shown. Trivial in itself, but this is the one object the show polices down to the star and the smear. **Fix:** keep one wording across both episodes. **QUIBBLE**

## Critique: mimo


I read the season cold against the master plan, the brief, the plan's physics and the bans. The season is strong: the physics are used as plot rather than décor, the numbers mostly hang together to the gram, and the theme is earned rather than said. What follows is what a careful viewer would still trip on, most serious first.

---

**1. Terry steps off the world at the fence with no rig on him.**
*Episode 10, act 3, scene 11–12 (Mucking Marsh, 05:52).* The scene strips and washes him ("His clothes go folded onto the rail. The yellow gloves go on top") and then has him count "One. Two." and go. Four beats earlier the same scene says Rosie is "rigged in her aunt's old belt with the gaffer-taped strap"; every other crossing in the season is buckled in on screen (belt, collar, wrist unit). The rig *is* the field: without one there is no unpinning, and the season's hardest scene plays as a naked man vanishing.
*Why it matters:* it breaks the show's central mechanic in the emotional climax, and it is exactly the kind of thing the owner's rules call a breach of the plan's physics.
*Fix:* one silent beat at the standpipe — Peg buckles his own belt over his bare shoulders and clips the collar after she washes his back — and, if you want the arithmetic to sing, have the steelyard weighing include the rig. Alternatively write "Terry, in his rig, washed around it."

---

**2. The fortnight between episodes 6 and 7 is carried by one word, and the wrong reading puts Tess in two places at once.**
*Episode 6, scenes 3–10, and episode 7, cold open and acts 1–4.* Episode 6 closes with Tess at the table Thursday 9 p.m. "into the small hours", Rosie finding her at 2 a.m. Friday with the unopened envelope's corner still showing in her coat. Episode 7 opens "Thursday night, quarter past ten" on the ferry and brings her home with the timetable at 1 a.m. Friday. The only cue that these are different Thursdays is Maggie's "Thursday fortnight" in episode 6, scene 4. On the natural reading (the same week) the two episodes collide: Tess is simultaneously at the table and on the water, and the envelope is unopened after she has opened it.
*Why it matters:* the gap is real and internally consistent (Maggie's "fortnight" fixes the ferry at 21 May and all of episode 8's dates fall out correctly from it) — but nothing on screen marks it, so a careful viewer reads a contradiction where the story means a two-week silence in which Tess sits on the enemy's letter.
*Fix:* caption episode 7's cold open ("The ferry, Thursday fortnight"), or give the note a date ("THURSDAY 21ST"), and add one line in the car — "Two weeks I've had that in my pocket" — which also explains why the woman who checks everything let it sit.

**3. "Twenty years, stepping out" is planted twice and charged to nobody.**
*Episodes 5 (act 4, Tess to Liam) and 10 (act 3, "It's twenty year, stepping out" / "So's starving"); compare the epilogue.* Rosie steps onto another leaf on purpose twice, Terry once. The rule of the world is explicit and is said aloud by characters twice. In the reckoning, the family is processed for the dips (Tess, "Every number's mine"), for the depot and for "release of other-hand matter" (Peg's bail) — and the crossings themselves never appear on a charge sheet. Rosie is at the Royal Free and Danny on a ward, but nothing ties those sentences to the twenty-year law; the audience is told the crime is catastrophic and shown nobody answer for it.
*Why it matters:* it is a stated law of the world not paid in the plot, and the finale is the season's confession scene — the one place where the family writes everything down.
*Fix:* in scene 15, after "And their sea went down my drain. Write that an' all", let Peg add one line — "And my girl stepped out on purpose. Twice. Put it with the rest." — so the ward is plainly the sentence the world gives, and custody by pantry is shown as the law's own answer.

**4. The sea is written as staged action where the plan demands black.**
*Episode 9, act 1, scene 5 (the Deep).* The step-out says "The screen goes black", but the following beats are written as visible action: "her head breaks surface", "a wave slaps her face", "weed slides across her cheek", "something small walks into her hood". Rule 2 of the plan is emphatic — next door is never shown, not through her eyes, not with any lamp; we see only what comes back — and the season's best wonder ("what did she see?") depends on it.
*Why it matters:* as drafted, a director will light and shoot the surface, and the audience will have seen next door; the residue ("what her last breath smelled of") and the epilogue's restraint both collapse.
*Fix:* hold the black and write the minute as sound and breath only — her counting, the slap of water, the tickle of bubbles — and let the weed and the crab be *discovered* on her body when she lands, as the crab already is.

**5. "Rosie did it in three. You'll take longer." has no referent where it sits.**
*Episode 10, act 2, scene 8 (the night of writing, 2 a.m.).* The line falls between Terry reading his odds off the roll and Peg writing "Terry. Turned. 2021" — inside the season's densest information scene. At that moment the audience has just been told the cure is ten seconds in the black. "Did it in three" therefore reads as a three-second cure, which contradicts everything on the page; what it means is that her step to the shore took three seconds because she knew the pull, and Terry's will take longer because he has never followed that one — a rule never stated on screen.
*Why it matters:* confusion in the middle of the reveal that re-reads the whole season, and it makes Tess (the numbers woman) sound vague for the one time she must be exact.
*Fix:* "Rosie found that pull in three seconds. You've never followed it." Or move the line to scene 12, where his seconds are set ("Five and a bit, Uncle"), and let the 2 a.m. scene stay about the odds and the name.

**6. The tingle's two-second lead is a rule stated on screen and leaned on four times, but it is not in the show's declared guesses.**
*Episode 5, act 4 ("It comes two seconds before you're done"); compare episodes 1, 3, 6, 7 and 10.* The whole "lying scale" plant depends on its exact length: Liam's tingle at 4.9 against a true 6.98, Danny's "fizzing before I ever touched him" against a true 2.2, Shaun's 6.9 against 8.9, Ray's 6.3 against 8.3. Section 5 of the plan says no writer may add a guess and nothing may be added "on screen or off" without being declared; the ledger's Law 2 row says only "tingles as time runs out".
*Why it matters:* the owner's process treats an undeclared rule as a breach, and this one is not decorative — it is the fair plant for the episode 9 reveal.
*Fix:* declare it once, in the ledger's Law 2 row or in the derived list: "the tingle begins about two seconds before the field fails." Nothing else changes.

**7. A "cylinder" stands behind the scientist in two shots about next door.**
*Episode 8, scene 13 and episode 10, scene 19 ("on a riverbank with a cylinder behind him").* The plan bans, in as many words, "no oxygen cylinder or breathing gear". A bare cylinder doing no job in a riverbank shot about the neighbours is precisely the echo the ban is written against, and it is the plan's own staging that puts it there.
*Why it matters:* the owner has already rejected a draft for echoing *The Catch*; this is the one surviving brush against that list.
*Fix:* cut the word and hold the shot on the man and the water; or name the instrument in one word ("behind the gravimeter housing"), so it cannot be read as breathing gear.

**8. Rosie's crossing is clocked in two places at once.**

**9. The Trading Standards letter arrives the day before it is dated.**
*Episode 4, scene 11 (Friday 10 April, the post); episode 9, scene 11 reads it as "Dated the eleventh of April".* The envelope comes in with the seed catalogue on the Friday morning of the episode's last full day, and the letter is dated the next day.
*Why it matters:* small, but the letter is the hinge of the doom — it is the 28-day warning Liam dies inside — and its dates are read aloud twice.

**10. Joe's distance off Rosie is changed from the plan's canonical figure.**
*Episode 7, act 2 (Maggie: "He never came within three fathoms of her") and act 3 ("six metres" is what the plan fixes, in three places).* Three fathoms is about five and a half metres. The plan says the canonical numbers may not be rounded or changed; this one is quietly re-expressed in a waterman's idiom and then logged in the episode's own notes as new.
*Why it matters:* nothing in the plot turns on the half-metre, but the number discipline is one of the things holding ten scripts together, and the season has already had to re-cut a draft for drifting.
*Fix:* keep the idiom and make it land on the plan's figure — "He never came within four fathoms of her" is too far; simplest is "six metres — three fathoms and a bit of it", or "He never came near her. Six metres, and a frightened boy keeps more."

**11. Peg's plan for the painting contradicts the family's own trade lore, and the contradiction is never shrugged at.**
*Episode 2, act 1 (the table) and act 2 (the seven hops).* Peg decides to "hop it till it turns", on her own folk theory that a picture can roll about in the black and come back the right way. But the family's own framed cuttings say the police read a thief's turn off the wrapping paper ("clean translation, no rotation"), and the family burns tissue for exactly that reason. Their practice is the cargo rule; their plan assumes the opposite.
*Why it matters:* the season means the audience to see a family with a bad idea that nobody checks — but as written it reads less like untested habit than like a family forgetting its own trade, which is a different (and weaker) thing.
*Fix:* one exchange at the table — Danny: "Tissue'd have come back with her, Mum." Peg: "Tissue's paper. That's paint." — and the blindness becomes deliberate, which is the theme sharpened rather than blunted. (This is likely the one place I am arguing with a deliberate choice; if so, the line still earns its keep.)

**12. The grip takes four fingers and spares the thumb, and nothing on screen explains the anatomy.**
*Episode 6, cold open ("The thumb he keeps. Four fingers, gone, cut clean across the knuckles").* The grip's price is "whatever of them was holding or being held"; he closed his hand on Joe's wrist, and the plane runs across the knuckles. A viewer counting will ask why the thumb and the palm survive.
*Why it matters:* the image is the season's most physical horror and it should be exact — this world's violence is always exactly costed.
*Fix:* one beat of staging — he grabs with four fingers, thumb braced behind the wrist — or let the plane run through the whole hand and let the prosthetic be his epilogue. (The plan's canonical figure is four fingers; the fix is in the blocking, not the number.)
