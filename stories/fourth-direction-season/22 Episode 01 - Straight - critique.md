# Episode 1, "Straight": critique of the draft

Checked against `70-plan-v3.md` (all of it), `00-brief-2.md`, `05-owner-feedback-2.md` and `../the-catch-notes.md` ("The short answer", faults 1-4). No final episodes exist yet in `work/new`, so continuity is checked against the plan. The episode 2 draft was skimmed only where the two drafts touch.

## Verdict

This is a strong, warm, well-built first draft. Every plan beat for episode 1 is there, every number checks out (see "Checks run" at the end), no banned word or named idea reaches the screen, and nothing from The Catch appears. The problems are with emphasis and believability, not structure:
- The draft points at Rosie so often, and so hard in the last scene, that an ordinary viewer finishes episode 1 knowing what episode 3 is meant to reveal.
- One crossing detail, the tingle, sets a rule that later episodes can't live with.
- Two scenes make the Marlows and Ray look careless in ways the Crime lead can't afford.

The episode does end higher than it began. Ray's hook, and Tess counting in the yard, lift a modest money twist into a real question.

**Protect these:**
- Tess's "Eleven two, less the lady."
- Danny posting the painting "like a letter through a letterbox", and the clean edge at his wrist (it plants his fingers in episode 6).
- The tissue burned under the "clean translation" cuttings.
- The careful old mirror card beside the new one that flows.
- Farrant's theory of the slide put in the projector the wrong way round.
- Liam's "What needs seven?" / "Most things, now."
- Tess burning Liam's ticket while the audit roll keeps its own copy.
- The van sign, HARGREAVES & SON, WEIGHTMEN, over the painted-out LOCKSMITHS.
- Terry quietly topping up his pot.
- The printer weighing Ray's car as he leaves.
- Tess in the yard, counting, unable to make it come out.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. Rosie is pointed at so often that episode 3's reversal is spent in episode 1
**Scenes:** 17, 18 and 19 above all; also 3, 10, 14 and 15.

**Problem:** The plan gives episode 1 about eight quiet tells for Rosie: the crisp packet, the dog, the two cards, the jar, the cheered weight, Terry's pot, the price promise, and Terry's "goes through me like water". It wants the audience level with the family, with only sharp viewers suspecting (section 10). Change 12 in version 3 moved tells out of the early episodes for exactly this reason: "an alert viewer had the answer before episode 2 ended".

The draft roughly doubles the tells, and stacks the heaviest at the moment the audience learns the painting turned:
- At the Ferry House, "the camera finds Rosie... gone very still... Nobody looks at her."
- On the ferry road she "stares until the lights change" at a one-handed man she has just heard "shook on it".
- At "It's the original", her hands stop under the tap.
- At the same moment Terry "looks down into his pot", the pot we have just watched drop.
- Ray stares at ROSIE 43.6 and its star "for a long moment".

Ray's added lesson on flax ("Flax curls one way... This curls the other") also makes his case airtight. That kills Peg's copy theory on arrival, though the plan keeps it alive as a rival until episode 3.

**Why it matters:** The painting turned, everyone in the room reacts, Terry looks at his pot, and a detective stares at the thinnest name on the board. Any viewer can add that up: Rosie has turned. The first major reversal then plays as the family catching up, which is the exact fault version 3 fixed. The camera is putting weight on an answer the plan promises for later.

**Fix:** Keep the plan's tells and cut the camera's pointing.
- **Ferry House.** Give the stillness to Tess, whose hundred and nine thousand five hundred has just gone to nothing, and keep Rosie busy wrapping the canvas. Cut "Nobody looks at her".
- **Ferry road.** Let the one-handed man be Peg's aside and Tess's "Don't stare", with no reaction shot on Rosie.
- **The hook.** Keep one reaction at most. Cut Terry's look into the pot, because it links the pot to the painting for everyone. Then either cut Rosie's hands at the tap, or play the tap running with her back to us and no close-up.
- **Ray's long look.** Send it to Frank's photo instead of the star. That sets up "I met your dad once" and saves Ray's suspicion of Rosie for episode 3.
- **Ray's lines.** Trim him to the plan's words ("Linen has a hand, Mrs Marlow. This canvas came back the wrong way round. It's the original."), so the audience weighs a policeman's photo against an expert's verdict.

### 2. The tingle comes three and a half seconds early, and later episodes can't survive that
**Scene:** 1.

**Problem:** Rosie's tingle starts at 7.3. Her budget is about eleven seconds (44.0 kg, less the painting), and she takes another 2.1 seconds to get home. The source says crossers read the tingle "as the budget running out". The plan uses its timing as a clue: Liam's tingle "comes early" in episode 5, and in episode 6 Danny says "Tingle came early. That's the grab".

**Why it matters:** Episode 1 is where the audience learns how much warning the tingle gives. If the warning is three and a half seconds, Danny, with a 2.2-second budget, would feel it before he starts, and "came early" would mean nothing. Liam's early tingle would stop being a fair clue to the lying scale.

**Fix:** Re-block the crossing:
- Rosie leaves by choice, on Tess's plan. She flinches at about 5.0 and faces home soon after.
- The tingle comes in the last stretch, within the last two seconds of her real budget, at about 9.0. By then she is already coming home.
- Close on her fingertips, then the landing at 9.4.

The audience learns that the tingle means the seconds are nearly gone. If the plan's owner wants a fixed warning time for all writers, it belongs in section 14 as a note for writers, not on screen.

### 3. Ray proves the Marlows had the stolen original, with it in front of him, and leaves
**Scene:** 19.

**Problem:** The painting lies "on the dresser, face up". Ray has the insurer's photo from noon, says "somebody borrowed your van", declares the canvas the original, and drives off. The plan's Ray is a working detective on exactly these crimes: in episode 3 he reads pour contracts and stakes out the Marlows' next job.

**Why it matters:** The Crime lead promises a plain-looking master pursuer. A detective who walks past stolen art he has just proved stolen reads as a plot hole or a bent copper, and nothing on screen explains it until episode 8. It also cheapens the hook, because the family is safe only because the writer let them be.

**Fix:**
- Put the painting out of sight before Ray reaches the counter. The big bridge prints his car's weight as he rolls on. In the moment that buys, Tess drops a tea towel over the canvas and slides it face down under the Saturday post. (Or Peg has it back in the safe from the start of the scene.)
- Make Ray's second photo one the insurer passed on as "offered to my client by persons unknown".
- Cut "Then somebody borrowed your van".

His visit then reads as a warning from a man playing a longer game, which is what he is.

### 4. The family cheers its own burglary in front of customers
**Scene:** 3.

**Problem:** Two lorry drivers eat bacon rolls at the counter all through the scene. In front of them the family toasts "Nine point four", chants the crossing rules, lays out the stolen canvas, prices it as "Terry's year", and cheers the radio report of the Mayfair theft.

**Why it matters:** In the same scene they burn the tissue because "paper talks". A family that careful with evidence would never confess in front of witnesses. The Crime lead needs their craft to look real, and as written Ray could solve the case by buying a bacon roll.

**Fix:** Set the celebration before the office opens, or serve the drivers only through the hatch. Better, use them. The hatch slides open for Mick, and the table drops at once into talk about the Moorgate pour. The canvas goes under a tea towel. The hatch shuts, and the cheering starts again. That single beat shows the double life the Gangster guest genre promises, and it costs nothing.

### 5. "We know where the thin bits are" points the concrete the wrong way
**Scene:** 5.

**Problem:** Liam's "Us included" plants the first link of the doom: the family's own concrete lengthens its own jobs. Danny's reply suggests that Marlow floors have weak spots only the family knows about, a back door.

**Why it matters:** The irony of Marlow Mix is that it is sold by day and slows the family by night. If the Marlows build in thin bits, that irony is gone and their long jobs make no sense. The Aherns' nine-second job in a Marlow floor (episode 7) also becomes Marlow sabotage rather than honest concrete.

**Fix:** Let Danny answer the way this family answers everything, with more of the same: "Then we'll get lighter." It plays as a builder's joke, it plants "make weight" as the family's answer to thicker floors, and it is the doom exactly. While you're there, paint MARLOW MIX on the mixer drum so the name reaches the screen.

### 6. Peg's "every job he ever did" contradicts Frank's record
**Scene:** 3.

**Problem:** Peg says "Your dad did two seconds, three at a push, every job he ever did." The plan fixes Frank's Farringdon job in 2001 at 4 seconds, and his last job at 7 seconds, which Peg herself planned and pencilled "7.1".

**Why it matters:** In episode 10 the loft shows those jobs. The line will read as a continuity slip, not as Peg's memory turning, unless episode 10 pays it off, and episode 10 isn't built to. It also blurs the plan's plain statement that "most jobs took two seconds in Frank's day".

**Fix:** "In your dad's day two seconds did most jobs. Now it's eight, nine. And we still come home with it." This keeps the pride and the plant, and drops the false claim.

### 7. "Show Me the Way to Go Home" is on the nose, borrowed, and used three times
**Scenes:** 3, 8 and 15. The episode 2 draft uses it too.

**Problem:** Terry's ending is going "home" one world over, and he hums a song with that title, with its words quoted. It is also the song the three men sing on the boat in *Jaws* just before the shark attacks. And it is still under copyright in the UK.

**Why it matters:** The song comments on Terry's fate, and the owner's rule 5 keeps that kind of comment off the screen. A famous film already owns it, and three uses make it a motif the plan never asked for.

**Fix:** Give Terry a song whose words say nothing about home or going: an old music-hall number long out of copyright, or a tune with no words. Agree it with the episode 2 writer.

### 8. Terry's lore scene is all question and answer
**Scene:** 8.

**Problem:** Kieran asks five questions in a row ("What if you stay in too long?" "And then what?" "What about the Hill?" "What if you followed one?" "And the Far?"), and Terry answers each one. The lines are good (the crust with no dish, the kettle two doors down), but nobody in the scene wants anything until Peg's last line.

**Why it matters:** This is the episode's longest block of rules, and the craft notes ask dialogue to act, not explain. A scene with a push in it could carry the same lore and deepen Terry at the same time.

**Fix:** Give Kieran a goal: he wants Terry on his side ("Tell Mum I'm ready"). Terry answers each question to put him off, getting darker as he goes, and the Far is the one question he won't answer properly. Keep the minute by the reading: it sets up Maggie's gift to the family in episode 5.

### 9. The TV surgery rigs the child and brings back a dish
**Scene:** 10.

**Problem:** The child is "rigged with a hospital-white collar", and the surgeon comes back "holding something small and dark in a steel dish".

**Why it matters:** A rigged child suggests the child crosses too, and so could turn, which muddles why crossing surgery is safe. A dish carried through the black is cargo inside her field. The plan's image is a surgeon reaching into a child who never leaves the table.

**Fix:** Put no rig on the child. The surgeon comes back with the tumour in her gloved palm and drops it into a dish a nurse holds out. The child's skin is unbroken.

### 10. A crown envelope arrives seconds after Pinnock says "you'll hear from us"
**Scene:** 9.

**Problem:** The timing makes the envelope read as Trading Standards writing already, in March, before anything has changed in April. The plan's drawer holds three letters that matter: 2001, 2019 and April 2026. This fourth one is never explained, and it turns episode 4's drawer beat into a repeat.

**Why it matters:** The April letter has to be the one that orders the scales re-verified, with no rival for that job.

**Fix:** Keep Peg's habit and change the envelope: make it a council rates demand, or something else plainly not from Trading Standards. Better still, as the drawer opens, let the camera catch one yellowed envelope near the top addressed to "Mr F. Marlow". That is a fair, silent plant for episode 10 that nobody can decode yet.

### 11. Tess's flaw is underplayed, and her line has gone to Peg
**Scenes:** 7, 13 and 14.

**Problem:** The plan's Tess trusts the numbers in her head and never checks them. Her line is "Near enough isn't." In the draft she says "When I say" to Kieran three times, plus "Lightest goes when I say", which reads as control, not as trust in her own sums. And Peg gets the line about precision at the balance ("This doesn't do near enough. This does right"), though in episode 7 Peg is the one who plans "near enough".

**Why it matters:** Version 3 took "deciding for others" away from Tess because it is The Catch's moral question, and "When I say" nudges her back toward it. It also blurs the contrast between Tess and Peg that drives episode 7.

**Fix:**
- Turn one "When I say" into a test Tess can measure: "When you can find up blind, first time, every time."
- Give Tess "Near enough isn't" somewhere it bites: to Liam at the printer, when he asks what needs seven.
- At the balance, give Peg a reason that is about trust, not precision: "My mother's scales. Never been wrong in fifty years."

### 12. Two short scenes do one job each, and the meter never shows a reading
**Scenes:** 12 and 13.

**Problem:** Scene 12 (the finger-prick) and scene 13 (flour on the brass balance) carry one plant each. The meter beeps, but we never see what it reads.

**Why it matters:** In episode 3 the meter reads LO. That lands harder if the audience has already seen it give Peg an ordinary reading.

**Fix:** Merge the two scenes. On Thursday morning Peg pricks her finger at the table while she spoons flour onto the brass balance, and Kieran licks the spoon. That puts two plants and the start of the birthday into one scene. Let the camera read the meter's screen. The plan has no number for Peg's reading and forbids new ones, so ask for one to be added to section 14.

### 13. Kieran's nightly weighing happens at three different times
**Scenes:** 6 (4 p.m.) and 15 (after midnight). The episode 2 draft says "his own ticket from nine last night".

**Problem:** The routine matters. The 04:17 and 02:04 tickets are found when he sorts the night's tickets each morning, and the plan calls the weighing nightly.

**Why it matters:** A loose routine makes the later tickets look convenient.

**Fix:** Set it at nine, before bed. In scene 6, after school, he sorts last night's tickets and copies out the 20.00. In scene 15, cut his after-midnight visit and give the scene to Terry alone. The dark display and the printer's light are already planted at the birthday weigh-in.

### 14. Small fixes
- **Scene 1.** "He looks at his little sister" comes straight after Rosie lands. Write "at Tess", since Rosie is his daughter.
- **Scene 1.** "Then a room" and "Racks of crates" suggest she can see beyond arm's length. Show only what the glow reaches: a stool leg, a man's ribs, the bands on one crate.
- **Scene 18.** The man holds "a newspaper under his arm with a stump", but his sleeve "is pinned". Pick one.
- **Scene 13.** In episode 9, Peg's five-kilo bag of flour is weighed against the brass. Give the set weights that add up to five kilos.
- **Scenes 3 and 4.** Cut the action lines that speak the theme ("Nobody at this table has ever asked where they come from before that"; "where pride goes"). The plan keeps its scaffolding off the page.

### 15. Continuity
No final episodes exist yet, so continuity is with the plan only. The draft is faithful to it apart from the points above. Where the two drafts touch:
- Both put the painting into Peg's safe with Danny's sent hand (episode 1, scene 4). The episode 2 draft stages it again and lists it among its own plants as if it were new.
- Both use the same song (finding 7).
- They disagree on when Kieran weighs his test weight (finding 13).

I suggest episode 1 keeps the safe beat and episode 2 only refers to it.
