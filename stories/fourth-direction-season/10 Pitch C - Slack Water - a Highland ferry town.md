# SLACK WATER

*Pitch C. Ten episodes of about 55 minutes. Inverfarrach, a town on Loch Farrach in the West Highlands of Scotland, in the present day, over one autumn and winter.*

## Logline

For twenty-five years the ferrymen of Inverfarrach have cured the mirrored, seven times in ten, by crossing with them at slack water on a loch whose tide matches nothing in the sky, and they never let go of the pilgrim's hand. When Jess Rennie's brother vanishes mid-crossing and the pilgrim who "let go" comes back alone, Jess, who keeps the town's tide table and has never crossed, starts counting what everyone can see and nobody talks about. She finds that the town's kindest custom is turning its own sons and daughters, and that its famous tide is somebody else's sea.

**Where the culture is found.** Not a cult or a regime: the most admired small town in Britain, whose miracle is real and audited by Lloyd's. It never asked why the miracle works, because it works. Its doom comes the day the reasons change.

## Lead genre and guests

**Lead: Detective**, turning Thriller from episode 7 (Detective and Thriller profile).
- **Promise.** The expected mystery, *what happened to Callum Rennie, and who is to blame?*, is pressed in every episode and answered in the last, with an answer that shocks and still fits. Fair play: every fact the answer uses is on screen first (the fading trace, the watch, the dog, the weigh-plate log, the memorial's brass traces). A battle of accounts: Jess's account against the town's at the town meeting (episode 9). A payment that fits.
- **Guarantee broken.** *The investigator's ally is safe.* Ewan Fraser, Jess's ally and old love, dies in episode 5 in a crossing the town ordered out of love. From then on every crossing we care about is a real threat.
- **Question.** *How is the truth found, and who carries the blame?* The truth is found by counting what everyone saw and nobody asked about. The blame lands on a whole town's kindness, including the detective's own silence: the trail leads home. The ending answers with a decision, not only a discovery.
- **Beats twisted.** The killer's plan is the victim's own plan (Callum chose to go). The main suspect (Del, the pilgrim) is the witness keeping a promise. The rival investigator (Aditi, the insurer's actuary) has a pet theory, fraud or sabotage, and is nearly right: the published cure rate *was* being quietly rounded up, out of love.

**Guest: Science Fiction, as first contact** (whole season). The hinge technology is the Cure; the traffic jam is the hotel's feeding custom; the fork is the Night of Leaves (episode 10). Promise: a world built in depth, today pushed further. Question: *which habit does everyone use without thinking, and what will she do once she sees it?* First contact comes as weight, never as a face.

**Guest: Thriller** (episodes 7 to 10). Can Jess trust the two people closest to the truth, her grandmother Isa and the pariah Del? The evidence swings on both.

**Visiting: Love Story.** Callum and Del, told only in fragments. A farewell the audience accepts, then an answer nobody expected.

## The world

**The normal.** Inverfarrach: three thousand people, twenty thousand pilgrims a year. A Victorian boathouse turned clinic, the **Ferry House**, with its **Pool**, a flooded slipway where the crossing frame hums over dark water. The Loch Farrach Hotel. A distillery. A pier with a brass memorial. A clock tower with two faces, one showing Greenwich time and one **Loch Time**.

Outside, the world is ordinary and working. Crossing frames are built under licence from the Otterburn object (found on a Northumberland moor in 1994, no crater, no trail, already unpinned). They do incisionless surgery, pipe repair and security work. Banks pour their gold into concrete. The mirrored can eat almost nothing on a supermarket shelf; a company called Chirality makes their food, at a price.

**Three departures, with deep ripples.**

*1. The loch has a tide.* Loch Farrach rises and falls half a metre on a cycle of twenty-six hours and fourteen minutes, which matches nothing in the sky. The town lives by it.
- Slack water, the still moment between tides, comes two hours and fourteen minutes later every day, so Inverfarrach's calendar drifts round the clock. Weddings are held on the flood for luck, and the pub stays open until slack.
- Jess Rennie keeps the **Table**: each year's slack-water times, worked out from the formula her grandmother drew from twenty-five years of notebooks, and printed on a card every household pins by the door.

*2. The Cure.* In 2001 Isa Rennie, a North Sea diver turned crosser, found that crossings made at slack water on the loch turned the mirrored back seven times in ten. Everywhere else the odds are a coin toss.
- **The Hand.** Nobody crosses alone. A ferryman holds the pilgrim's hand through the whole crossing. Isa made that rule after four people crossed alone in the first year and never came back: the **Lost**, among them her husband Alasdair. Since the Hand, nobody has been Lost.
- **The weigh-plate.** Everyone stands on it before crossing, because the lighter the pair, the longer the frame can hold them. It sets their **allowance**, a few seconds, and logs every weight.
- **The Strengthening.** Since 2023 the hotel's new mirror kitchen (eight hundred pounds a meal, free for the poor) has fed pilgrims for two weeks before they cross, "to build their strength for the Tide".
- **The Ferryman's Breakfast.** Ferrymen eat a huge breakfast before a crossing day. Nobody remembers why.
- Pilgrims bring a sealed jar of mint from their own garden to taste when they come back. Mint that tastes of mint means you are home. Mint that tastes of caraway means you are still mirrored, and you try again next slack.

*3. Weight crosses.* Only gravity passes between the leaves of the world. The Pool has a gravimeter, which is how the town knows when someone is Lost: their weight stays in the empty water, spreading and fading for weeks. On the pier memorial each of the four Lost of 2001 has a name and a small brass copy of their trace. Children run their fingers along the grooves.

**What its people take for granted.** The Tide works. Nobody crosses alone. A ferryman eats well. The sick must be strengthened. You don't ask a pilgrim how they turned. A ferryman the Tide turns never crosses again, and nobody speaks of it: the hotel feeds him quietly at the back door. The Table is right.

**The camera's rule.** The camera never leaves our leaf. The neighbours are only ever weight: a line on a screen, a tremor, a breath.

## The culture that never questions, and how it grows its own doom

**Which culture.** The town of Inverfarrach and its Cure: ferrymen, the council, the hotel, the families who pin the Table by the door. Kind, proud, funny, generous, rich. Nobody is forbidden to ask anything. It simply never occurs to anyone, because the answer is seven in ten and has been for a generation.

**Why its ways were once right.** Three things made the Cure work, and the town knew only one of them.
- *Slack water* (the town knows this one). The odds of a turn at Farrach peak at slack. The town thinks the loch does it. The truth (episode 6) is that the neighbours' sea lies over the town, one leaf away, and its pull tilts the odds.
- *The Hand* (the town knows the rule, not the reason). It kept pilgrims from wandering off the way the Lost did. But a held pair turns at most once between them, and it is always the lighter body that turns.
- *Weight* (nobody knows). In 2001 Isa crossed in her old diving weight belt, out of habit. Later ferrymen were big fishermen who ate the Ferryman's Breakfast. Pilgrims arrived starving, because a mirrored body cannot use ordinary food. So the pilgrim was always the lighter one, and the turn went to them: a cure. A photograph of Isa in her belt hangs by the Pool, captioned "The First Slack".

**How the habits grow the doom, step by step.**
1. **Success.** The Cure makes Inverfarrach famous and rich. In 2023 the hotel opens a mirror kitchen and begins the Strengthening, because it is cruel to send the starving across.
2. **The change the town made itself.** Well-fed pilgrims now often outweigh their ferrymen. In a held pair the turn goes to the lighter body, so it goes to the ferryman. The pilgrim stays mirrored and the ferryman comes back mirrored. The cure rate slides from seven in ten towards four. Twelve ferrymen turn in three years; in the twenty-two years before, three did.
3. **Kindness hides it.** A turned ferryman "never crosses again", which was a custom of respect. So the town hides its turned sons as "Pensioners", fed by the Convener's kitchen at the back door (custody by pantry). The one thing that could turn them back, crossing again as the lighter partner, is the one thing custom forbids. Fraser quietly rounds up the published rate so pilgrims won't lose hope.
4. **More of the same, more lovingly.** The council's answers to falling cures: strengthen pilgrims for longer; the **Long Crossing** (stay until the fingertips tingle, "give the Tide time"), though the outcome is fixed the instant you return, so the extra seconds buy only the risk of running out; the **Double Hand** (two ferrymen per pilgrim, so the lightest of three turns, usually a young ferryman); and recruiting lighter, younger ferrymen because the lightest go furthest.
5. **The world moves anyway.** In episode 6 the neighbours move a mountain for their own reasons. The loch's slack shifts forty-one minutes for good. The council calls the gauge broken and keeps the Table.
6. **Doom.** Crossings at the wrong time cure almost no one. Pilgrims stop coming, the insurer pulls out and the hotel goes broke. The Convener locks the mirror-food store, and the Pensioners, twelve of the town's own children, begin to starve on whisky and dripping. The council answers with the Great Slack: forty pairs crossing at once, Double Hand, Long Crossing, at the old time. It would kill dozens.

Every step comes from a habit that was right, comfortable, admired or kind. There is no villain. The world changed twice, once by the town's own success and once by someone else's business, and a town that could not change its mind was only waiting.

**The question the culture has no place for.** Not "is the Tide real?" (it is), but *why does it work, and would it still work if we let go?* Letting go is how Alasdair was Lost. Asking it sounds like asking the town to abandon the sick.

## Main characters

**JESS RENNIE (34), Keeper of the Table.** Reads the loch gauge, sets every allowance, keeps the logs. Dry, precise, loved, discreet to the bone. Has never crossed.
- *Want:* first, to prove her brother's death was not his fault. Then, to save the Cure.
- *Wound:* at eight she watched the weigh-plate needle when her grandfather Alasdair was Lost.
- *Flaw:* she tends the numbers and never asks what they mean. She would rather be kind than know.
- *Secret:* she knew Callum had turned. The dog growled at him and he couldn't open a jar, and she opened his jars for months and said nothing.
- *Levers:* competence, specificity (her watch set to Loch Time; she says the tide aloud without noticing), connection, fear of crossing, humour.
- *Arc:* from keeper to asker. She learns she cannot make a town believe; she can only make it count. She ends as one voice among many.

**DEL MARR (39), the pariah.** A former vault thief, famous for the "Geneva lift": gold bars rising off a shelf on CCTV. Forensics read the wrapping paper and knew she had turned. She lost her lower left leg in a partial return, which gives her eleven extra seconds of allowance. She has crossed at Farrach eleven times, and her passport's handedness line has been amended eleven times. She asks ferrymen rude questions and was banned in 2019 for letting go.
- *Want:* to stop being mirrored, or to stop needing to be.
- *Wound:* she no longer knows which hand is hers.
- *Flaw:* she runs, and she lies by habit.
- *Secret:* Callum let go of *her*, on purpose, and asked her to let his family think he was dead.
- *Levers:* wit, competence, vulnerability, a hidden decency.
- *Arc:* from someone who always leaves to someone who goes somewhere on purpose, carrying something for the people she leaves.

**ISA RENNIE (83), founder of the Cure.** Jess's grandmother, the town's living saint. Pilgrims touch her sleeve.
- *Want:* the Cure to outlive her, and the Hand never to be broken.
- *Wound:* Alasdair, who crossed alone.
- *Flaw:* she made her grief into a law.
- *Secret:* Alasdair's trace, printed out and framed in her kitchen. It fades too fast and drifts north-east. She has looked at it every day for twenty-five years and never asked why.
- *Arc:* she keeps the Hand and puts back the thing she forgot. In the finale she crosses again at eighty-three, in her old belt.

**RORY FRASER (61), Convener of the council and owner of the hotel.** He feeds the town. The Strengthening is his, and so is the back-door kitchen.
- *Want:* to keep everyone fed and the town alive.
- *Flaw:* generosity as control.
- *Secret:* he has been quietly rounding up the published cure rate, and his son Ewan is turning.
- *Levers:* decency, connection, an opponent's grip on the plot without malice.
- *Arc:* his own kindness kills his son. He is the last to ask, and the first to pay.

**EWAN FRASER (29).** Ferryman, Callum's best friend, Jess's old love. Turns in episode 3 and hides it for his father's sake; dies in episode 5 by his own choice, crossing as the lighter partner to be cured, obeying his father's Long Crossing to the last second.

**CALLUM RENNIE (30).** Ferryman, singer, the town's favourite; turned eight months before episode 1, wasting and hiding. After episode 1, only a line of weight.

**ADITI RAO (37).** The Cure's insurer's actuary, who once computed Del's eleven seconds. She asks everything, has no manners, and is wrong for most of the season.

**DUNCAN MUIR (64),** Skipper and keeper of customs. **SHONA MACLEAN (26),** ferrywoman and nursing mother. **DR KOFI ANSAH (50),** gravitational physicist. **BRAN,** Callum's collie.

## The ten episodes

**1. "Slack Water."** *A ferryman vanishes holding a pilgrim's hand, and she comes back alone.*
Cold open: Callum paces three steps and turns, three steps and turns, waiting for slack under the photograph of Isa in her belt. He steps onto the weigh-plate beside Del (Duncan: "You're wasting, lad." Callum: "Breakfast's on the Convener"). The frame hums, the Pool empties, the bell rings, and Del surfaces alone. The screen shows 76 kilograms where nobody stands, fading. Acts: the town's normal through its grief (Loch Time, the Table, pilgrims tasting their own mint on the pier); the verdict, "the Tide took him, and she let go"; the police reacher looks inside Del and sees a watch strapped in the socket of her false leg, but not why; the fiscal rules the look an unlawful search; Jess clears Callum's cottage, where Bran growls at his jacket and a jar Jess once opened sits on the shelf. Jess notes that the trace faded in forty minutes, and files it.
*Twist:* the weigh-plate log shows Callum at 90 kg in January, 76 on the day. *Hook:* Del, released, to Jess: "I never let go of him."

**2. "The Ferryman's Breakfast."** *Aditi comes to audit a miracle; Jess audits her brother.*
Flu sweeps the town, and the ferrymen who never catch it are noticed only by the audience. Aditi arrives: claims say four in ten, the brochure says seven. Her theory: fraud, or Chirality sabotaging a cure that costs it customers. She gives Jess Del's file (the Geneva lift, the paper that proved she turned, the leg, the eleven seconds). Jess finds Callum's tab at the hotel's back kitchen, 214 meals, and Fraser says kindly that it is none of her business. Del hands Jess the watch.
*Twist:* its hands run anticlockwise. It turned with Callum months ago. *Hook:* Bran growls at Ewan.

**3. "The Pensioners."** *The healers are the sick.*
Jess follows Ewan to the hotel's back room: eleven of the town's young ferrymen eating mirror food at a long table, fed "on the Convener" and hidden to protect the Cure's name. Ewan turned last week. Del, pressed at last: in the bubble she could see inside Callum, and his heart was on the right. Jess realises what she knew and never said. Duncan: "The Tide takes some. We don't speak of it."
*Twist:* twelve ferrymen turned in three years, three in the twenty-two before. *Hook:* Jess stands at the Pool at slack with a crate of sealed mint jars.

**4. "Seven in Ten"** (major reversal 1). *The Tide is fine. It's us.*
All night Jess crosses jars of Isa's mint alone, no hand, the way a crew sends a hand instead of a body, and counts. Seven in ten turn at slack. Then the weigh-plate logs, which nobody ever read for anything but allowances: every cure, the pilgrim was lighter; every turned ferryman, the ferryman was lighter. She sees the photograph of Isa in her diving belt. She takes it to the council. Fraser: "You want us to starve the sick?" Isa: "Nobody crosses alone."
*Twist:* the town's own kindness, the Strengthening, is turning its children. The council votes for the Long Crossing and the Double Hand. *Hook:* Ewan volunteers to cross. He has done the arithmetic: as the lighter one, he might turn back.

**5. "The Long Crossing."** *The town tries harder.*
Ewan crosses with Ingrid, a strengthened Norwegian pilgrim, under the new order to hold on to the tingle. On another pair the field collapses early and a ferryman comes back with two fingers ending in a clean, bloodless plane. The operator holds Ewan's field to the last second, and it runs out. The screen shows 147 kilograms, centred, spreading slowly and evenly. That trace will fade for weeks. Fraser sits by the Pool all night.
*Twist:* Ewan and Ingrid are gone, and the shape is nothing like Callum's. *Hook:* at 03:12 every gravimeter in the Ferry House twitches once.

**6. "No Fault"** (major reversal 2, first half). *The loch answers to somebody else.*
Every gravimeter on Earth twitched at 03:12. Farrach has a tremor with no fault, and the loch's slack moves forty-one minutes, for good. The council calls Jess's gauge broken and keeps the Table. The Double Hand runs at the old slack; Shona, the lightest of three, turns. Dr Ansah arrives, chasing sixty years of dishes pointed the wrong way. He and Jess lay the loch's rhythm over the tremor's timing.
*Twist:* the tide is the pull of a moving sea at the town's exact coordinates, one leaf over. Farrach's weddings, calendar and miracle run on another world's ocean. *Hook:* Del: "I know. I've been in it."

**7. "Held Breath"** (major reversal 2, second half). *The pariah's story, and the brother's choice.*
Del tells it whole. In 2019 she let go and followed the wrong pull, and stepped out for one heartbeat into black water under a light that was not the moon. She came back holding her breath and let it out on the Pool floor, and nobody believes a thief. Then her secret: Callum, wasting and ashamed, asked her how to let go. He let go of her on purpose, gave her the watch for Jess, and asked her to let them believe he was dead. Jess confesses her own silence to Del, not to the town. Then she crosses for the first time, alone at the old slack, where her gauge says the odds of turning are now low. She lets go the way Del taught her, steps over for a heartbeat and comes back with her cheeks full. Aditi holds the bag.
*Twist:* the air is breathable and carries a molecule of the wrong hand. For a mirrored body, the food over there might fit. Callum chose exile. *Hook:* Fraser padlocks the mirror-food store. The hotel is broke.

**8. "Custody by Pantry."** *The leash was always the kitchen.*
The Pensioners live on Farrach whisky and dripping. Shona's milk cannot feed her son, and her mother feeds him formula while Shona watches. The oldest Pensioner dies thin in his bed. Aditi finds that Fraser has rounded up the published cure rate for three years, so she was half right, and the insurer pulls cover. The council plans the Great Slack to win the pilgrims back. At night the Pool screen shows a weight that moves. The town says Callum's cloud haunts the Pool; Aditi says it is a spy standing beside the frame. In Isa's kitchen, Jess sees Alasdair's framed trace: it fades fast and drifts north-east, like Callum's.
*Twist:* Fraser's numbers were false, and he faked them out of love. *Hook:* the ghost weighs three kilograms more than last week.

**9. "The Town Meeting."** *One right person is not enough.*
Jess lays out everything in the church hall: the leaves, the logs, the belt, the gauge, the breath. The town hears her and says no. The Hand is sacred. Starving pilgrims is cruel. The gauge is broken. She is grieving. Isa stands and backs her granddaughter, and even Isa is not enough. Duncan refuses the belt; the Great Slack is set for tomorrow's old slack. So Jess stops arguing. She gives every household a slot on the frame tonight and asks them to bring a jar of their own mint. Fraser says it is a stunt. Isa unlocks the frame with the founder's key.
*Twist:* Isa chooses her granddaughter over her own Table in public, and it still changes nothing. *Hook:* on the Pool screen the ghost paces three steps and turns, the way Callum always paced while he waited for slack.

**10. "Nobody Crosses Alone"** (major reversal 3). *A town counts, and a brother answers.*
The Night of Leaves. Household after household crosses its own jar and chalks the result on the board: at the old slack, two in ten; at the gauge's slack, seven. Duncan counts his own jar twice. At dawn the Great Slack's first pairs stand on the plate at the old time. Duncan, not Jess, stops them. The first crossing the new way: Isa, eighty-three and forty-eight kilograms, buckles on her old diving belt, which makes her the heavier of the pair, takes Shona's hand at the gauge's slack and crosses short. Shona tastes her mother's mint and weeps. The town counts every crossing after.
Then the Pool. The ghost now weighs 94 kilograms. A cloud of the dead can only spread and fade; this one has gained weight for a month.
*Twist:* Callum is alive one leaf over, eating food that fits him. The four brass traces on the memorial have his shape; the first Lost may have stepped over too, and the Hand was built on a misread line. Del chooses to go: her twelfth crossing, letting go on purpose, carrying Ansah's smallest gravimeter and a jar of her own mint.
*Final image and hook:* the next night the Pool shows two weights, pacing. Jess steps on the weigh-plate, three steps and a turn. A moment later the two weights answer. Nobody can fake that. The last shot is the new sign on the Ferry House, chalk on a board: "Today at slack: seven in ten. Counted by Inverfarrach."

## Mysteries and wonders

**Mysteries, all answered this season:** what happened to Callum; why the Cure is failing; why ferrymen are turning; why slack water works; why Del "let go"; what moved the loch; what the ghost is.

**Wonders, mentioned in passing and never pressed:** who the neighbours are and what they were building; who made the Otterburn object; whether Alasdair and the first Lost live; what Callum has found, and who, if anyone, feeds him.

## The ending: what it settles and what it costs

**Settled.** Callum was not killed. The town's customs pushed him out, and he chose to go. The blame falls on no one person and on everyone, Jess included. The Cure works for reasons the town now knows and checks every day. It keeps the Hand, adds weight to the ferryman and takes slack from the live loch, not the card. The town's normal has changed: it counts. Children cross leaves after school and chalk the results.

**Cost.** Ewan, Ingrid and an old Pensioner are dead. Shona missed her son's first months at the breast. Fraser is ruined and has lost his son to his own kindness. The Cure as a brand is gone, along with most of the pilgrims. Isa's Table is retired, and so is her grief's law. Jess's brother and her only friend outside the town are one leaf away for good.

**Residue.** Somewhere under the loch, two people are pacing on a shore we will never see, and the town's luck belongs to strangers who could move again tomorrow.

## The show's ledger of new guesses

Everything in *The Fourth Direction* holds as written. The show adds only these, declared once:
1. **Stipulated.** The odds of a turn at a place are set by mass on the neighbouring leaf nearly overhead. At Farrach that mass is a sea, so the odds rise and fall with its tide.
2. **Stipulated.** In a held pair, at most one body turns, and it is the lighter one.
3. **Stipulated.** At Farrach the neighbours' pull is strong enough that a crosser who lets go can reach the next leaf within the allowance and come back. Whoever is still there when the allowance ends stays there. The air there is breathable.
4. **The book's own ending stipulation, used here.** The neighbours' life is the other hand.

*Derived, not new:* a mirrored person can eat and gain weight over there, and a dissolved cloud cannot gain weight; a longer crossing changes nothing but the risk of running out; a ferryman heavier than his pilgrim keeps the Hand safe. Nothing else is added in secret.

## Check against the owner's six rules

1. **Not The Catch.** No lift, shaft, trapped family, kind figure or ship, child's drawing, toy, needle kicking at a vanishing object, "nothing has happened to the mint", backwards signs, germ dishes, flask, oxygen, charge bar, ledge or hands on glass. The heroine never turns, and no couple ends mismatched.
