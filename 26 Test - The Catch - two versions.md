# 26 Test - The Catch - two versions

The first real test of the workshop: your screenplay *The Catch*, in two versions.
- **Version A**: the undated file.
- **Version B**: "Final4", headed "Revised screenplay — 22 September 2026".

I don't know which came later, so I call them A and B. Line numbers are A:n and B:n.

**How it was run.** Your `story-critique` skill led, as the craft skills say it should for a whole script. Four agents each applied one craft skill to both versions: `plot`, `character`, `story-world` with `genre`, and `dialogue`. I did the scene-by-scene pass and put it together. No complaint came with the scripts, so each check assumed a first-time film audience.

## Correction first

The worked example in `story-critique` (and the case in hard-to-vary's `by-domain.md`, section 5) comes from an earlier draft of this script. That draft had fifty-one rungs, a trolley drop, and Iona hiding the warning tag. Neither version on the table matches it: here Jude reads the tag aloud and hangs it back up. Notes from that session are history, not a description of these drafts.

## The short answer

**The two versions share the same strong spine. Almost everywhere they differ, A does the job better.** A version keeps showing the rules before they are needed and lets Iona work things out. B mostly cuts those set-ups and has Saye or Eli explain instead. B has a handful of real gains worth carrying into A (listed below). B's new last image is a strong feeling, but it breaks the story's own rules as written.

All five checks (my own and the four skills) reached this verdict separately, from different angles. They are not independent readers, though: they all read the same page, and none of them has watched a film of it.

## What works in both, and must be protected

- **The climb earns the catch.** On the way up, her arm "settles into the shape" of the bright sill. At the catch, "her arm knows it before she does." Remove the rest on the sill and the catch becomes luck.
- **The mint.** Backwards signs, the car and the clock let the audience build the wrong theory (the world changed). "Nothing has happened to the mint" breaks it: they changed.
- **The puck reveal, spread over three scenes:** the hidden hand in the fall, the empty clip, the recording. It is fair to the camera, and it makes "Drive." land as something Eli does to her.
- **The cylinder chain.** Her fear cracks the figure's cover, her tape mends it, and "only one of them can be mended with tape" ties it together.
- **The figure turning out kind.** It was planted fairly: the cup slid within Jude's reach, the arm lifted "as carefully as a nurse", the air tank with a hand-cut fitting.
- **The toy carriage with the F.** One object shows both things a turn does, and is used four times, ending with Iona asking the animal.
- **The one question every character answers differently:** may you decide someone's fate without telling them? Eli hides his hand. Saye waits, then burns. The figure takes people, then tends them. Iona shows the animal the carriage before she turns it.
- **Detail that does no plot job and makes the world real:** "Goods only. No persons.", OSTREL, Saye's empty kitchen, the black thumbprint. And two questions left open on purpose: the engine in a field, and "partway isn't a place." Never explain them.

## The faults that matter most, ranked

**1. B cuts set-ups whose payoffs it keeps (B).** Each of these is shown in A and missing or told in B:

| Set-up in A | Payoff | What B does |
|---|---|---|
| The needle kicks when the toy carriage vanishes (A:554) | The needle climbing in Iona's empty room (B:833) | No demonstration, so the audience can't tell the needle means something is crossing. It becomes a spooky prop. |
| The two dishes: nothing will eat the turned germs (A:616-640) | Why the flask matters, and why Saye burns the room with Iona aboard | One hedged line (B:577). The danger that prices the climax is never seen. |
| The nurse's wristband with Iona's name backwards (A:497) | "Copied from a hospital wristband" (B:1176) | The wristband is never shown. |
| The turned cage stops "exactly where it stopped before it fell" (A:285), and Iona sees it: "Same speed back up. Same height." (A:590) | The ledge escape, crossing at the top of the climb | Saye states the rule (B:551). Iona no longer works it out as the lift engineer she is. |
| Iona jams a chair under the stair door (A:115) | The gunshots at the door | Jude, at the bottom of the shaft, says "Stair door's wedged" (B:161). He can't know that, and nobody wedged it. |
| Saye: "a reserve I would rather you did not touch" (A:1022) | The reserve bar at the climax | The reserve bar is never planted. |
| The figure looks at Nell's drawing of a house before moving the beds (A:1308) | Its choice to send them home | The look is cut, so the choice has no visible cause. |

*Fix:* in B, restore these from A. Keep B's own trims of action lines. The length barely changes.

**2. The ending's biggest cost is barely on screen (both, worse in B).** Iona comes home turned twice, so she is ordinary again. Jude and Eli are still turned once. She is now the mirror image of her husband and her brother, and can't share their food.
- **A** gives this one silent beat: Jude takes his hand off her harness (A:1724). It also gives "A year… to make another" (the time they'll be apart). Its sharpest statement ("Turned once… Eli, once… It will fit her world, and not its own", A:1541-1545) is written only in the action lines, where a camera can't film it.
- **B** cuts the hand beat and leaves Jude out of the last two scenes.

*Fix (from the character check):* make it visible. For example, their hands meet on the glass, mirror images, the wedding rings on opposite hands. That pays off the kitchen ring beat and replaces A's unfilmable line.

**3. B's last image breaks its own rule, and B never settles the flask (B).**
- The container holds Jude's blood, turned in the cage and turned again at the ledge. By the story's rule, two turns make its growth ordinary again. The camera still dwells on the "cloudy growth" as a threat. A viewer who keeps track will feel cheated; one who doesn't gets a vague chill.
- B also never says the flask went with the fire. A does: Iona "looks at the flask inside the outline. Leaves it there" (A:1407), a choice that costs Eli his medicine, and Eli answers "Good".

*Fix:* if you want B's growth, give it an outline the rules allow. Bring back A's "What does it eat?" / "It used to eat what you eat" (A:1805), so the growth reads as the animal growing its own food from her name. Otherwise end on A's cloth. Either way, name the flask among the things that vanish.

**4. In B, Iona is always right and never pays (B).** A gives her a real flaw, answering trouble with force:
- "Stop it hard and there's nothing under it to catch us" (A:51). Then she stops it hard, and the cage falls.
- She watches her own elbow hit the button on the recording (A:748).
- "I put them in that cage" (A:1017).

B cuts all three. Her reason for going becomes "It came to get me. Let it finish", which is harder to follow. Her change, from striking to waiting (Nell's "Wait", the tape, the cloth), is earned in A and only half-earned in B.

**5. The middle briefs instead of pressing (both).** Two scenes each teach about ten rules (the demonstration, then the suit-up), with no clock running. Every rule pays off, so don't cut the tools. Deliver some where they first bite, and add a clock. For example, let the dawn camera footage show the ship slipping ("a man holding a door open with his back").

**Smaller points:**
- **Saye's sister** ("His medicine kept my sister out of hospital") never touches a choice. Either cut it, or let Saye watch the flask go and feel it.
- **In B, Saye's voice drifts.** In A she never shortens words ("That is your left", "I will not hear you"), which marks her as careful. B mixes in "that's", "can't" and "won't".
- **B's puck scene** breaks A's four-line rhythm ("What was it rated for?" / "One body." / "One." / "You.") with explanation. It adds a recap ("The engine could safely turn me…"), and Eli confesses before he's asked.
- **B's goodbye on the ship** cuts A's pay-off of the car question. In A, Eli asks "Are we going to be all right?", she says "I don't know", then sends him with "Go." B replaces it with a spoken apology at the end ("I should have told you").
- **B's car line** ("the way you look at a place you have been before") hints that Eli has crossed before, and nothing pays it off. A's "a result you expected" is right.
- **The Nell harness test** (both) closes an exit nobody would ask about. It can go.

## What B does better (carry these into A)

- **The paper letter:** "A paper letter won't face the other way however you slide it. You have to lift it and turn it over." The best single explanation of the turn in either version. Better still as an action, not a line: Saye slides a paper F, then lifts it and flips it, without a word.
- **The napkin ring:** a turn is cheap, a crossing is dear. It makes the charge bars readable.
- **The camera recording that turns and climbs back to the line** (B:873-875). A good visual preview of the ledge escape.
- **"Why lock you up?"**, a real question for Iona, and the bare "Eli." instead of "Eli. Leave it."
- **The ring beat is clearer to stage** in B (B:418-420).

## What I'm unsure of

- **Which draft is later, and what B's cuts were for.** If B was cut for length or pace on purpose, these notes should be weighed against that aim.
- **Whether B's growth is meant to stay unexplained.** By the test in `story-critique`, it currently reads as a gap, not a deliberate open question, because nothing on the page bears on what it is. But you may have intended it.
- **Pacing.** It was judged from the page only. Nobody has watched it.

## What this test showed about the workshop

**It helped.** Each skill found things a plain read tends to miss, and gave a reason for each:
- `plot`'s rule that a fact a turn depends on should be shown beforehand turned the comparison into a set-up audit. That is how the needle, wristband and reserve were caught.
- "Show the rules, hide the thing" (your Anticipation theory) is why the needle matters.
- The film table in your Implied World theory (an object is cheap on screen, explaining talk is dear) turned a first instinct to praise B's shorter explanation into the opposite verdict.
- The Bond theory's "always right, never pays" named what B loses in Iona.
- `dialogue`'s "how would he know?" check caught Jude's line.

**It stalled in five places**, now the workshop's to-do list:
1. The diagnosing steps expect a named complaint. With "try these", every skill had to invent one.
2. No skill has a method for comparing two drafts, which was the most useful lens here.
3. Nothing covers whose eyes the camera takes in a mirror world. Every letter is backwards only for Iona, so when is the picture flipped?
4. Nothing covers meaning written only in action lines, which a viewer never sees.
5. The reading is heavy: 20,000-40,000 words of method per check.

The worked examples built from an earlier draft of this script also primed the checks towards that draft.
