## The short answer

**The two versions share the same strong spine, scene for scene. Where they differ, A more often puts a rule on screen before the moment that depends on it.** The needle kicks when the toy carriage vanishes (A:554) before it climbs in Iona's room. The cage comes back up to exactly where it stopped (A:285), and Iona spots the same thing on the ramp (A:590). The two dishes (A:616-640) show why the flask is dangerous. B lacks these, and in places has Saye or Eli say the rule instead (B:551, B:577, B:687). This is not one-sided. B has set-ups A lacks: the camera recording that turns and climbs back (B:873-875), and the oxygen mask offered and refused (B:480, B:502). A's Eli explains the culture at greater length than B's (A:695-701 against B:632-638). And B has a handful of things done better, worth weighing for A (listed below). Most of what B lacks weakens a payoff rather than breaking it; the one that breaks is Jude's line about the wedged stair door (B:161), which nothing on B's page explains. B's last image is a strong feeling, but it breaks the story's own rules as written. This judges what a first-time audience can follow; if B's differences were made for running time, weigh them against that aim.

All five checks (my own and the four skills) reached this verdict, but their agreement counts as one check, not five: they all read the same page, through a list of differences I had picked out for them, and none of them has watched a film of it.

## What works in both, and must be protected

- **The climb earns the catch.** On the way up, her arm "settles into the shape" of the bright sill. At the catch, "her arm knows it before she does." Remove the rest on the sill and the catch becomes luck.
- **The mint.** Backwards signs, the car and the clock let the audience build the wrong theory (the world changed). "Nothing has happened to the mint" breaks it: they changed.
- **The puck reveal, spread over three scenes:** the hidden hand in the fall, the empty clip, the recording. It is fair to the camera, and it makes "Drive." land as something Eli does to her.
- **The cylinder chain.** Her fear cracks the figure's cover, her tape mends it, and "only one of them can be mended with tape" ties it together.
- **The figure turning out kind.** It was planted fairly: the cup slid within Jude's reach, the arm lifted "as carefully as a nurse", the air tank with a hand-cut fitting.
- **The toy carriage with the F.** One object shows both things a turn does, and is used four times, ending with Iona asking the animal.
- **The one question every character answers differently:** may you decide someone's fate without telling them? Eli hides his hand. Saye waits, then burns. The figure takes people, then tends them. Iona shows the animal the carriage before she turns it.
- **Detail that does no plot job and makes the world real:** "Goods only. No persons.", OSTREL, Saye's empty kitchen, the black thumbprint. And two questions left open on purpose: the engine in a field, and "partway isn't a place." Never explain them.

Only one of these breaks outright: Jude's line about the wedged stair door (B:161), which nothing on B's page explains. The rest weaken a payoff rather than break it, because B has other routes to some of them. A also has set-up links this table does not list: the collar matching the puck, with Iona's look at Eli (A:535-537; B has Eli tell it, B:687); "Most", which plants Nell (A:577); Eli's line on why Nell is thin (A:1144-1146); the leaking water that turns white, tying back to the white crust (A:1467); and Saye reaching for the flask before Eli's warning (A:439).

*Fix:* bring these into B from A, except where B already has a route to the same payoff: for the ledge escape, both versions show the ramp (A:590, B:544-553) and B also has the camera recording (B:873-875), so adding A's turned cage (A:285) as well would give three routes to one set-up: pick one set-up route. Whichever you keep, let Iona draw the conclusion herself, as she does at A:590: that does a second job, showing her as the lift engineer she is (row 4). Keep B's own shorter action lines. The added weight falls in the demonstration and suit-up scenes, which fault 5 already finds slow, so deliver some of them where they first bite.

**2. The ending's biggest cost is barely on screen (both, worse in B).** Iona comes home turned twice, so she is ordinary again. Jude and Eli are still turned once. She is now the mirror image of her husband and her brother, and can't share their food.
- **A** gives this one silent beat: Jude takes his hand off her harness (A:1724). It also gives "A year… to make another" (the time they'll be apart). Its sharpest statement ("Turned once… Eli, once… It will fit her world, and not its own", A:1541-1545) is written only in the action lines, where a camera can't film it.
- **B** has no hand beat, and Jude is not in its last two scenes.

*Fix (from the character check):* make it visible. For example, their hands meet on the glass, mirror images, the wedding rings on opposite hands. That pays off the kitchen ring beat and replaces A's unfilmable line.

**3. B's last image breaks its own rule, and B never settles the flask (B).**
- The container holds Jude's blood, turned in the cage and turned again at the ledge. By the story's rule, two turns make its growth ordinary again. The camera still dwells on the "cloudy growth" as a threat. A viewer who keeps track will feel cheated; one who doesn't gets a vague chill.
- B also never says the flask went with the fire. A does: Iona "looks at the flask inside the outline. Leaves it there" (A:1407), a choice that costs Eli his medicine, and Eli answers "Good".

*Fix:* if you want B's growth, give it an outline the rules allow. Use A's "What does it eat?" / "It used to eat what you eat" (A:1805), so the growth reads as the animal growing its own food from her name. Otherwise end on A's cloth. Either way, name the flask among the things that vanish.

**4. In B, Iona is always right and never pays (B).** A gives her a real flaw, answering trouble with force:
- "Stop it hard and there's nothing under it to catch us" (A:51). Then she stops it hard, and the cage falls.
- She watches her own elbow hit the button on the recording (A:748).
- "I put them in that cage" (A:1017).

B has none of the three. In B, her reason for going is "It came to get me. Let it finish", which is harder to follow. Her change, from striking to waiting (Nell's "Wait", the tape, the cloth), is earned in A and only half-earned in B.

**5. The middle briefs instead of pressing (both).** Two scenes each teach about ten rules (the demonstration, then the suit-up), with no clock running. Every rule pays off, so don't cut the tools. Deliver some where they first bite, and add a clock. For example, let the dawn camera footage show the ship slipping ("a man holding a door open with his back").

**Smaller points:**
- **Saye's sister** ("His medicine kept my sister out of hospital") never touches a choice. Either cut it, or let Saye watch the flask go and feel it.
- **In B, Saye's voice is uneven.** In A she never shortens words ("That is your left", "I will not hear you"), which marks her as careful. B mixes in "that's", "can't" and "won't".
- **B's puck scene** has explanation where A has a four-line rhythm ("What was it rated for?" / "One body." / "One." / "You."). It has a recap ("The engine could safely turn me…"), and Eli confesses before he's asked.
- **B's goodbye on the ship** lacks A's pay-off of the car question. In A, Eli asks "Are we going to be all right?", she says "I don't know", then sends him with "Go." In B, the goodbye is a spoken apology at the end ("I should have told you").
- **B's car line** ("the way you look at a place you have been before") hints that Eli has crossed before, and nothing pays it off. A's "a result you expected" is right.
- **The Nell harness test** (both) closes an exit nobody would ask about. It can go.

## What B does better (worth weighing for A)

- **The paper letter:** "A paper letter won't face the other way however you slide it. You have to lift it and turn it over." (B:563.) The only line in either version that says why a turn flips things, and it fits the book-and-leaf picture both versions use. It makes a rule clearer rather than setting up a later payoff: take it out and nothing later breaks. Better still as an action, not a line: Saye slides a paper F, then lifts it and flips it, without a word.
- **The napkin ring** (B:608): a turn is cheap, a crossing is dear. In B it is the main way the charge bars become readable. In A it is optional, a second route, since A already makes charge scarce (A:789, A:1022, and the bar). Like the paper letter, it is a spoken rule: carried into A as a line, it adds to the demonstration scene that fault 5 finds overloaded.
- **The camera recording that turns and climbs back to the line** (B:873-875). A good visual preview of the ledge escape, drawn in the same kind of labelled-floor diagram the visor uses (B:1425-1453). A already plants that move with the cage (A:285) and the ramp (A:590), so in A it would be a third route: add it only if it replaces some of the briefing.
- **Set-ups B shows that A does not** (added by the correction): the oxygen mask offered and refused (B:480, B:502), where A plants the cylinder on the wall instead (A:495); the second suit on its stand (B:960); and Saye's glance at Nell's photo (B:968).

**Not listed above: choices of taste, not things done better.** "Why lock you up?" (B:641), the bare "Eli." (B:174), and B's simpler ring beat (B:418-420). Nothing on the page decides between the versions here, except that a bare "Eli." early would prepare A's later bare "Eli." (A:697), and that "Why lock you up?" does a job A already does, with Eli lifting his wrist and "They found the letter" (A:710-711), and would displace or repeat A's "Like that." The ring beat is not settled by the page either way: A's rings "directly across" read correctly whichever way the camera sees them, and A's version holds Iona's look at Jude's ring (A:435), which the ending fix in fault 2 needs. Two lines in B point to things missing from B's own page (B:161, B:1176), and B carries a dated heading; the break test read these as hints that B came from a draft like A. ## What this test showed about the workshop

**It helped.** Each skill found things a plain read tends to miss, and gave a reason for each:
- `plot`'s rule that a fact a turn depends on should be shown beforehand turned the comparison into a set-up audit. That is how the needle, wristband and reserve were caught.
- "Show the rules, hide the thing" (your Anticipation theory) is why the needle matters.
- The film table in your Implied World theory (an object is cheap on screen, explaining talk is dear) turned a first instinct to praise B's shorter explanation into the opposite verdict.
- The Bond theory's "always right, never pays" named what B's Iona lacks.
- `dialogue`'s "how would he know?" check caught Jude's line.

**It stalled in five places**, now the workshop's to-do list:
1. The diagnosing steps expect a named complaint. With "try these", every skill had to invent one.
2. No skill has a method for comparing two drafts, which was the most useful lens here.
3. Nothing covers whose eyes the camera takes in a mirror world. Every letter is backwards only for Iona, so when is the picture flipped?
4. Nothing covers meaning written only in action lines, which a viewer never sees.
5. The reading is heavy: 20,000-40,000 words of method per check.

The worked examples built from an earlier draft of this script also primed the checks towards that draft.
